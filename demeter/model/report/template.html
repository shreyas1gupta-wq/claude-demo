    Data and its limits

    Return accounting

    For target leverage L on day t, the next day's portfolio return is

    r[t+1] = rf[t+1] + L·(total_return[t+1] − rf[t+1]) − max(L−1, 0)·financing_spread/252 − |L − L_prev|·cost_bps/10,000

    This is a futures-like accounting: the levered leg pays financing above the risk-free rate, the cash leg earns the T-bill. Demeter books cash at 0%, which is subtracted separately in the asymmetry rows.

    Demeter's fingerprint

    Before building anything, the published monthly returns were inverted against daily market data to ask what the underlying daily rule must have been doing. Implied exposure is the month's strategy return divided by SPY's, for months where SPY moved more than 2%.

        The central finding

        The rule that looks brilliant — until you test it earlier

        Same rule, two eras

        Buying when implied volatility has just fallen is buying after up days. In a fourteen-year bull market that is a momentum tailwind; through 2000–02 and 2008 it is a whipsaw machine. A study that started its backtest in 2012 would have shipped this model.
