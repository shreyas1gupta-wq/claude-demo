A midcap model that does not forecast anything

Seven timing families were tested on Indian midcaps and every one reduced risk-adjusted return. So this model gives up on prediction entirely. It earns its keep in two places instead: how the exposure is wrapped, and a premium the Indian option market has paid every year since 2014.

The engine of the excess return

India's variance risk premium

Implied volatility has exceeded the volatility that actually followed, in every calendar year since the India VIX series begins. That gap is what the overlay sells.

The one assumption that decides whether this works

The overlay's edge rests on midcap implied volatility being richer than Nifty's

Sensitivity to that assumption

Re-running the model with midcap implied volatility set to different multiples of India VIX. The measured ratio of realised volatilities is 1.35×, which is what the base case uses.

How to settle it in a day

Pull the MIDCPNIFTY option chain at the close for the near-month expiry, take the implied volatility of the strike nearest 6% above spot, and divide by that day's India VIX. Repeat for twenty sessions and take the median.

If that ratio comes back at 1.3 or above, the base case holds and the overlay is worth roughly three percentage points a year. If it comes back near 1.0, the overlay is worth nothing and the model collapses to the cost saving alone — still positive, but a far smaller prize.

A second check worth making at the same time: the quoted bid-ask on those strikes. The model assumes 70 basis points of premium as a half-spread. Wider quotes eat the premium directly.
