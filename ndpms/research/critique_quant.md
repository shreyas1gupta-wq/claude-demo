# Quantitative Risk & Performance Critique — NDPMS ₹500 Cr Operating Model
**Reviewer:** Quantitative risk manager / performance-measurement specialist (CFA, FRM) perspective
**Date:** 2026-09-12
**Material reviewed (in full):** design_regulatory, design_allocation, design_rebalancing, design_risk, design_performance, design_selection, design_operations, data_market, data_facts (+ spot-checked sel/tco.json, risk_calc.py outputs referenced in design_risk).
**Method:** every formula in the risk, performance, cost and turnover sections was independently recomputed by hand (parametric VaR/ES, EWMA vol, Black-Scholes hedge costs, Brinson-Fachler/Carino attribution, consent-latency cost, TCO tax-drag, Monte Carlo turnover/TE table, stress-library capture ratios) and checked for consistency against the same numbers as they reappear in other domain notes and against the two verified data packs.

## Overall assessment

The quantitative core of this operating model is unusually well engineered. Independently recomputing the parametric VaR/ES table, the EWMA vol-shock example, the Black-Scholes hedge-cost table, the Brinson-Fachler/Carino attribution worked year, the consent-latency √t cost formula (reused identically across four documents), and the SIF-vs-Cat-III post-tax comparison all reproduced the documents' own numbers to within rounding — this is genuinely strong, publication-quality quant work, not hand-waved output, and a compliance-style reviewer would not catch this from reading alone (it required redoing the math). A separate compliance-focused review of this same material (critique_compliance.md, already produced) correctly flags the TCO tax-drag anomaly and the gross-alpha-vs-fee-load gap; this review corroborates both independently and adds five further high-severity items a compliance lens would not surface: (i) the mandate has **four mutually inconsistent versions of its own strategic asset allocation / policy composite** spread across the regulatory, allocation and performance notes, with no single canonical table any of the ₹-Cr risk limits, TE bands or cost budgets can be checked against; (ii) the allocation design's **own modeled ex-ante tracking error (1.5% Moderate / 2.1% Aggressive) sits below the "green" TE band (2.5–4.5% / 4.5–6.0%) and information-ratio floor (≥0.40) that the risk and performance notes set as the governance target** — by the framework's own rules the constructed portfolio would be flagged amber/red for closet-indexing risk on day one, a fact none of the seven notes states explicitly; (iii) the two "worked" PM-fee assumptions used across documents (0.30% in regulatory/operations vs 0.50% in performance's cost/tax-drag budget) differ by 20 bps, which is enough on its own to flip the "does gross alpha cover the fee" conclusion from roughly breakeven to clearly negative depending which document a reader trusts; (iv) the stress-library's own summary sentences ("45–55% of Nifty drawdown captured," "65–75%" for Aggressive) understate the dispersion actually present in the ten-episode table — several individual episodes (Taper, IL&FS, COVID, election-day for Moderate; IL&FS, Tariff for Aggressive) sit 5–10 points outside the stated range; and (v) Cat III AIFs' own internal leverage (flagged elsewhere as "~2x NAV, unverified") is not reflected anywhere the risk framework computes look-through gross notional against its concentration/derivative limits. None of these are fatal to the design's quality, but every one of them would change a number the CIO/IC will actually rely on, and all five are exactly the kind of error that a single merge pass — concatenating seven independently-built notes — would otherwise launder into an operating fact. The rebalancing Monte Carlo is honestly caveated (independent random paths, not common random numbers) but the caveat is under-applied: the TE column, which the note calls a "reliable relative signal," moves non-monotonically with turnover in a way that is very likely the simulation-noise the note warns about elsewhere, not a real turnover/TE trade-off. Market-data provenance is mostly disciplined (India VIX, repo, gold, USDINR all verified and consistent across notes) with one exception: the specific Nifty 50 closing levels feeding the Black-Scholes hedge-cost table are asserted as verified in three documents but the data pack's own accuracy legend says the underlying 10–12 Sep 2026 close could not be confirmed.

## Top missing items for a ₹500 Cr NDPMS operating model

1. A single canonical, version-controlled SAA/Policy-Composite table that every other document (risk limits, TE bands, cost budgets, attribution benchmark) references by pointer rather than restating — the current seven-note structure has re-derived it at least four times with four different answers.
2. A model-risk governance process for the quant engines themselves: no stated back-testing plan (Kupiec/traffic-light test) for the parametric VaR model once live NAV history exists, no common-random-number discipline for the rebalancing Monte Carlo, no independent QA sign-off step for `risk_calc.py` / `sel/tco.py` before their numbers reach the IC.
3. A liquidity stress test of the illiquid sleeve (9–15% AUM in Cat II/III AIFs, 3–7-year lock-ups) against a **client-side** redemption or mandate-reduction scenario — every liquidity discussion in the risk/rebalancing notes is about market-side ADV stress, never "what if the client wants ₹100 Cr back and 12% of the book is locked."
4. Explicit uncertainty bands / downside scenarios on the illiquid-sleeve CMAs (Cat II private credit 11.5% net, Cat III/SIF 10.0% net, Pre-IPO/PE 15.0% gross) — these are point estimates with no stated confidence interval, unlike the well-caveated listed-market CMAs.
5. A quantified cost/pricing of the FX hedge the risk note gestures at ("consider partial USDINR forward/NDF hedge") — the equity tail-hedge gets a full Black-Scholes cost table; the currency hedge gets one sentence.
6. A decision on whether Ionic itself charges a performance fee on this mandate (regulatory §6 floats "0.30% fixed + 10% performance over house benchmark with HWM" as a worked illustration) and, if so, how R2/R3/DTL and the IC scorecard change — every other document's cost/alpha model assumes a pure fixed fee.
7. A stated MWRR–TWRR divergence threshold that triggers a client conversation (both are reported monthly; nothing says how large a gap is "normal" during the 8–12-week deployment window vs a red flag afterward).
8. A formal reconciliation checklist to run before the seven notes are merged (SAA weights, PM fee bps, TE bands, correlation/vol assumptions, Nifty/VIX levels as of a single as-of date) — this review found the mismatches by hand; the merge process needs an automated cross-check, not a human re-read.
9. A backtesting/validation plan for the stress-library "capture ratio" claims themselves once 12+ months of live NAV exist — right now the 45–55%/65–75% ranges are model output from a single beta/sleeve-sensitivity pass, not validated against realised drawdowns.

## What is strong (keep verbatim / as the template for the merge)

1. The Brinson-Fachler + Carino attribution worked year (performance design, Table 4 and §3.7) — independently recomputed cell-by-cell and it ties out exactly (allocation + selection + interaction = active return, by sleeve and in total); this is the cleanest, most rigorous attribution worked example in the whole set and should anchor the merged document's methodology section.
2. The parametric VaR/ES/EWMA-vol risk engine (risk design §2.3–2.4) — every VaR-95/99, 1-day/1-month, and ES-97.5 cell reproduces exactly from the stated vol/beta inputs using standard z-scores and the ES normal-tail multiplier; the EWMA vol-shock worked example is exact to the third decimal.
3. The Black-Scholes hedge-cost table (risk design §5.2) — independently recomputed via Black-Scholes with the stated S/K/r/q/σ/T inputs and reproduced the 5% OTM put premium (~0.73%/qtr) and 10% OTM put premium (~0.23%/qtr) almost exactly; genuinely rigorous options work, not a plausible-looking placeholder.
4. The consent-latency √time cost formula and its ₹79-lakh/₹35-lakh (10-day vs 2-day SLA) worked number is derived once and then reused **identically and correctly** across the regulatory, rebalancing, risk and operations notes — a rare example of real cross-document consistency in this set and the strongest evidence the NDPMS-latency framing is load-bearing rather than decorative.
5. The SIF-vs-Cat-III post-tax comparison (selection design §5.1) correctly applies the surcharge-inclusive effective LTCG rate (14.95%, not the headline 12.5%) and reproduces the stated +1.98pp post-tax/post-fee gap exactly — a subtle, correctly-handled tax-mechanics detail.
6. The rebalancing design's honesty about its own Monte Carlo's limitations (explicitly flags "independent random path, not common random numbers" and separates "reliable" cost/tax/turnover columns from "noisy" CAGR columns) — the right instinct, even though (per Finding 7 below) the caveat should extend to the TE column too.
