## Things commonly believed but wrong or outdated (relevant to this mandate)

1. ‘Non-discretionary’ just means light-touch oversight — wrong: NDPMS legally requires an evidenced client direction/consent before every trade; the PM cannot exercise independent investment discretion at all (Reg 2 definitions, Reg 24).
2. SGBs are still available to buy fresh from the government — wrong: no new tranche has been issued since Feb-2024; only secondary-market purchase or holding existing bonds to 8-year maturity is possible, and Budget 2026 narrowed the redemption tax exemption to original subscribers who hold to maturity.
3. Debt mutual funds still get LTCG/indexation benefits after a long holding period — wrong: debt/'specified' MF units bought on or after 1-Apr-2023 get NO long-term capital gains treatment at all; all gains are deemed short-term and taxed at slab rate under s.50AA regardless of holding period.
4. Any asset held long-term gets indexation-adjusted LTCG — wrong: indexation was removed economy-wide for transfers on/after 23-Jul-2024 (Finance Act 2024); LTCG is now a flat 12.5% without indexation across virtually all asset classes.
5. AIF Category III gets the same pass-through tax treatment as Category I/II — wrong: only Cat I/II get s.115UB pass-through; Cat III is taxed at the fund level, with business/F&O income taxed near the Maximum Marginal Rate (~42.7% for an indeterminate trust).
6. AIF minimum investment is always ₹1 crore — wrong: employees/directors of the AIF/manager can invest from ₹25 lakh, and Accredited Investors are exempt from the ₹1 crore floor entirely.
7. SIF is basically a mutual fund with a slightly higher ticket size — wrong: while SIF sits under the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations umbrella, it is a distinct product line permitting long-short strategies and unhedged derivative exposure up to 25% of net assets, which ordinary MF schemes cannot do; minimum entry is ₹10 lakh at the PAN level across all of an AMC's SIF strategies, not per scheme.
8. PMS clients can still enter with ₹25 lakh — wrong: the current PMS Regulations (2020, in force) set the minimum client investment at ₹50 lakh (except LVAI's ₹10 crore agreement route); ₹25 lakh was an older, now-superseded threshold.
9. There is a SEBI-mandated ceiling on PMS management/performance fee percentages — wrong: SEBI prescribes only the high-water-mark computation method for performance fees, the 0.50% operating-expense cap, and the graded exit-load schedule; the headline fee % itself is a bilateral contractual matter.
10. Block deals still have a ₹10 crore minimum — wrong: SEBI's 2026 revamp raised the minimum block-deal order size to ₹25 crore and added a same-day ±3% reference-price band with compulsory delivery.
11. T+0 settlement is now the norm for Indian equities — wrong: T+0 remains strictly optional and limited to the top ~500 stocks by market cap; T+1 is the mandatory baseline for everything else, and brokers may charge extra for T+0.
12. Mutual funds are banned from investing overseas — wrong: there is no ban, only an industry-wide USD 7 billion aggregate cap (plus USD 1bn ETF and USD 1bn per-AMC sub-caps) that is periodically exhausted, causing individual AMCs to pause fresh subscriptions rather than a permanent prohibition.
13. A portfolio manager can invest client money in another PMS's strategy or in the PM's own group PMS product freely — wrong: PMS 'units' are not classified as securities the same way MF units are, and Reg 24(10)'s bar on investing based on 'the advice of any other entity' plus the outsourcing prohibition (reinforced by SEBI's May-2026 enforcement order) makes routing client funds into another PM's discretion a live enforcement risk, not a routine option.
14. Accredited-investor status, once granted, is permanent — wrong: accreditation has a limited validity requiring periodic renewal, and both the qualifying thresholds and the certification format have changed multiple times (2021 introduction, Jan-2026 net-worth-certificate change, proposed Aug/Sep-2025 new routes) — treat it as a status to actively track, not a one-time checkbox.
15. The July 2026 SEBI consultation paper (MF-PMS category, foreign securities, 50% unhedged short via derivatives) is already the law governing PMS — wrong: as of 12-Sep-2026 it is a draft that closed for comment on 13-Aug-2026 and has not been notified; today's binding rules remain the 2020 PMS Regulations plus the 16-Jul-2025 Master Circular.
16. REIT/InvIT distributions are simply 'tax-free dividends' — wrong: a single distribution is typically a blend of interest (taxable at slab, 10% TDS), dividend, rental/other income and return-of-capital/'repayment of debt' components, each with different tax treatment reported on Form 64B; the 2026 dividend-exemption change is itself a pending Bill, not yet confirmed enacted.
17. Corporate bond RFQ routing is only an FPI/mutual-fund requirement — wrong: SEBI-registered PMS (discretionary and non-discretionary alike) must also route at least 10% of secondary-market corporate-bond trades by value through the exchange RFQ platform on a rolling basis, from 1-Apr-2022.
