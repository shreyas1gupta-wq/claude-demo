# Compliance & Regulatory Critique — NDPMS ₹500 Cr Operating Model
**Reviewer:** Principal Officer / Compliance Officer perspective (SEBI PMS/AIF, 15 yrs) · **Date:** 2026-09-12
**Material reviewed:** design_regulatory, design_allocation, design_rebalancing, design_risk, design_performance, design_selection, design_operations (+ data_market, data_facts) — all read in full, plus sel/tco.json.

---

## Overall assessment

All seven design notes and both data packs were read in full. Individually each domain note is well-organised, cites sources, and is unusually disciplined about flagging its own uncertainty ("unclear_verify", "approx.") rather than asserting invented facts — a genuine strength for a SEBI-adjacent operating model. The regulatory core (NDPMS definition, 25% unlisted cap, fee/exit-load rules, FIFO tax-lot mechanics, the 2026 draft being pipeline-not-law) is consistent across documents and matches the verified fact pack.

The problems are concentrated at the **seams between documents** — exactly where a merge into one blueprint is most likely to launder an inconsistency into an operating fact — and in a small number of **quantitative outputs that don't survive a sanity check against the documents' own inputs**. Three findings are severe enough to block sign-off: (1) the allocation design's own expected gross alpha (+45–55 bps) is smaller than the fee/cost load the performance design computes (~77 bps), meaning the portfolio as constructed is expected to show *negative* net alpha against the very targets (≥+100 bps 3Y) the performance design sets as the primary accountability metric — a client-facing promise the house's own numbers say it will miss; (2) the rebalancing/operations notes operationalise (and mark "must") a self-executing, pre-authorised sell-side de-risk trigger (1.5× band breach) that the regulatory and risk notes simultaneously flag as legally unresolved ("unclear_verify... get a legal opinion") — a live NDPMS-recharacterisation risk being built into the calendar before it is cleared; and (3) third-party PMS allocations are booked as line items *inside* the ₹500 Cr model-portfolio sleeve tables in design_allocation, while design_regulatory, design_selection and design_risk all state — correctly — that a third-party PMS must sit in a *separate* account **outside** NDPMS AUM. Only one of these can be true, and each has been treated as true in a different document. A cluster of smaller cross-document inconsistencies (which regulation number governs the 3-month reporting cycle, which fixed-fee assumption to model, whether SIF and Cat III AIF share a tax treatment) and one apparent computation bug in the TCO tax-drag model round out the findings below.

None of this is unfixable — the underlying regulatory research is sound — but the merge into a single blueprint must not simply concatenate these seven notes; it needs a reconciliation pass on the items flagged high/medium below, and the two "must"-labelled items that are still legally unresolved (standing-instruction-based de-risking; the LVAI-solves-consent-mechanics framing) should be downgraded to "should, pending legal opinion" until outside counsel or a SEBI Informal Guidance response is obtained.

## Findings

See structured findings in critique_compliance.json (same content, machine-readable). Summary below, most severe first.

1. **[HIGH] Allocation design's own expected alpha is below its own fee/cost load, implying negative net alpha against the performance design's targets.** design_allocation.md §3.3 reports "Expected alpha vs own composite — Moderate +55 bps / Aggressive +45 bps" as a **gross**-of-fee figure (the E[R] column header states "pre-tax, gross fees"). design_performance.md §3.5 computes total fees+expenses+booked-cost load at ~77 bps p.a. and states the target is **net** alpha ≥ +100 bps at 3Y (§6.1), with <0% net alpha at 3Y scored "red" (§4, Table 7). 55 − 77 ≈ **−22 bps net**; 45 − 77 ≈ **−32 bps net**. The portfolio as constructed is therefore expected, on the design's own numbers, to structurally miss (in fact fail "red" on) the primary accountability metric the performance design sets up to judge the team. This should be surfaced to the IC/client before it becomes a fee/target commitment, not discovered in year 3.

2. **[HIGH] Third-party PMS money is booked inside the ₹500 Cr NDPMS sleeve tables, contradicting the regulatory/selection/risk notes that require it to sit outside AUM.** design_allocation.md §3.1/§3.2 include "1 PMS (₹10–15 Cr)" as a line item inside the Moderate/Aggressive model portfolios, summed into the "Total 100% / ₹500 Cr" row. design_regulatory.md §1.4 and §7, design_selection.md §3.3, and design_regulatory.json's own can_do list state third-party PMS "sits OUTSIDE NDPMS AUM" (ndpms_feasibility for holding it inside AUM is explicitly "not_allowed" in the JSON) because a PMS is a service contract, not a security, and Reg 24(10) bars investing on another entity's advice. Either the sleeve tables overstate what is actually inside NDPMS custody (real AUM ≈ ₹485–490 Cr, not ₹500 Cr), or the structuring intent is to hold the PMS allocation inside the NDPMS account, which the firm's own regulatory analysis says is not permitted. Must be resolved to one position before merge.

3. **[HIGH] A self-executing, pre-authorised sell-side de-risk trigger is marked "must" while its legality is simultaneously flagged unresolved.** design_rebalancing.md §3 (event triggers table) states that a ≥1.5× band breach is "Pre-authorised in IPS; **executed**, then reported at next monthly cycle" — i.e., the trade fires without a per-instance client instruction. design_rebalancing.json's can_do list marks this "recommendation: must". Yet design_regulatory.md §5.1 point 4 and design_risk.md §7.4 both flag standing instructions of this kind as "**unclear_verify** — get a legal opinion / consider SEBI Informal Guidance before go-live," precisely because a pre-set trigger that fires without a specific client direction is the paradigm case SEBI's May-2026 First Global order (cited in the same documents) penalised as outsourced/discretionary decision-making. Labelling the mechanism "must" in the operating calendar before that legal question is resolved risks building an inspection-failing control into the house's standard process. Recommend downgrading to "should, contingent on legal clearance" everywhere it appears (rebalancing, risk, operations).

4. **[HIGH] LVAI accreditation is presented as the fix for NDPMS consent-mechanics risk, which conflates a contractual relaxation with a substantive regulatory test.** design_regulatory.md §2 ("Decision: Must... It is the only route that lets a bespoke NDPMS agreement carry the monthly-consent machinery we need without fighting Schedule IV boilerplate") and §5.1 present LVAI status as unlocking the standing-instruction/consent-latency design. LVAI relaxations (per the same document's own sourcing) waive Schedule IV's *prescribed agreement contents* and allow bilateral fee/exit-load terms — they do not, on any source cited, change what Reg 24/Reg 2 require for a trade to count as client-directed rather than discretionary. The standing-instruction legal risk (finding #3) exists identically whether or not the client is LVAI-accredited. This framing should be corrected before it is relied on operationally.

5. **[MEDIUM-HIGH] TCO tax-drag figures for several sleeves appear to be a computation error, undermining the "tax dominates cost" conclusion.** design_selection.md §9 (and sel/tco.json) report annual tax-drag of **1,914 bps (19.1%)** for the arbitrage sleeve, **2,542 bps (25.4%)** for silver, **1,624 bps (16.2%)** for REIT/InvIT, and **1,196 bps (12.0%)** for gold. Each of these exceeds or nearly consumes the entire pre-tax expected return the *same document set* assigns to that asset class in design_allocation.md's CMA table (arbitrage E[R] 6.2%, gold 8.0%, REIT/InvIT 9.5%; silver 8.5%). A 12.5%-LTCG, low-turnover, buy-and-hold sleeve cannot plausibly generate a tax bill larger than its total return under any realistic realisation schedule; the short-duration/money-market row, by contrast, checks out exactly (257 bps tax drag ≈ 6.6% E[R] × ~39% slab, fully consistent with 100% annual income recognition). This strongly suggests a units/scaling bug in the tax-drag formula for equity-taxed and distribution-heavy sleeves in sel/tco.py, which should be re-derived before the headline "All-in TCO ≈ 424 bps ≈ ₹21.2 Cr/yr" and the "tax, not manager alpha, is the biggest lever" conclusion are presented to the IC.

6. **[MEDIUM] Two different regulation numbers are cited for the same 3-month client-reporting requirement.** design_regulatory.md §1.5 and design_operations.md cite **"Reg 31"** for the ≤3-month periodic client report; design_performance.md §0.3 and §5.1 cite **"Regulation 22"** for the identical requirement, and even flags it "confirmed — Reg. 22 tightened this from the prior 6-month cycle." data_facts.md's own T2 table also uses "Reg 31" but rates the whole table only "partially_verified." Since neither primary SEBI text could be fetched this session, this is an internal citation conflict on a load-bearing clause number (it will appear in the Disclosure Document and client agreement) that must be resolved against the primary regulation text, not left as a 50/50 split across the blueprint's own sources.

7. **[MEDIUM] Inconsistent fixed-fee assumption used for the same worked economics.** design_regulatory.md §6 and design_operations.md §9.3 model a **0.30%** p.a. fixed fee (≈₹1.50 Cr/yr) as the base case used to argue the platform-cost economics are "tight but workable." design_performance.md §3.5's cost/tax budget table instead assumes **0.50%** p.a. ("typical 0.25–0.75% approx.") for the identical fee line (≈₹2.50 Cr/yr). These two fee assumptions materially change both the client-facing fee illustration and design_operations' own conclusion that "fixed operating cost (₹1.8–3.2 Cr) approaches or exceeds the 0.30% fixed-fee revenue" — that conclusion looks different at 0.50%. Pick one number before the blueprint states either as "the" fee.

8. **[MEDIUM] Cat III AIF and SIF hybrid long-short are conflated under one tax treatment in the allocation CMA table, though the selection note shows they are taxed completely differently.** design_allocation.md's CMA table (§1) has a single row "Cat III long-short AIF / SIF hybrid LS" with one note, "Trust-level tax up to ~42.7%." design_selection.md §5.1 correctly shows SIF gets **investor-level, MF-style taxation (≈12.5% LTCG)**, not Cat III's fund-level tax up to MMR, and computes a **+198 bps/yr post-tax advantage for SIF over Cat III** on identical gross returns, recommending SIF-over-Cat-III wherever both exist. A CIO reading only the allocation table would apply the wrong (much higher) tax drag to whichever wrapper is actually chosen, or conversely under-price the tax benefit of preferring SIF. Split this into two CMA rows.

9. **[MEDIUM] A stale/incorrect USDINR figure is used as the baseline for a live risk-monitoring threshold.** design_risk.md §6 (Early-Warning Indicator Board) lists "USDINR (spot ~₹88 [design_allocation.md])" as the green/amber/red baseline for the FX monitoring line (green <1% monthly move, red >3%). The verified data pack (data_market.md T6) and design_allocation.md's own §0 market-context paragraph both state USDINR was **≈95.7–95.79** as of 11-Sep-2026 — an ~8% baseline error carried into a dashboard specification that will misfire its monthly-move percentage thresholds from day one if built as written.

10. **[MEDIUM] Direct-equity name-count assumption differs between the sizing model and the construction rule for the same sleeve.** design_allocation.md's Moderate/Aggressive model portfolios use **22** and **25** direct-stock names respectively (§3.1/§3.2), while design_selection.md §6.2's portfolio-construction rule for the identical NIFTY-750 scorecard sleeve specifies "**25–35 names (30 as base case)**." A ~25–35% gap in assumed single-name diversification between the two documents that are meant to describe the same sleeve.

11. **[MEDIUM] design_allocation.md gives two non-reconciling "Moderate" unlisted/AIF breakdowns within the same file.** §3.1's costed Moderate model portfolio totals 9% unlisted (Cat II 5% + Cat III/SIF 4%). §5.3's "SAA bands... illustrative for the Moderate house model" shows a different structure (AIF Cat I/II target 5% with a 0–7.5% band; Cat III/long-short target 2.5% with a 0–5% band; "Total unlisted ≤15% IPS / ≤75 Cr"). These don't line up sleeve-by-sleeve and will confuse whichever is taken forward as "the" Moderate model in the merged blueprint — pick one canonical version.

12. **[MEDIUM] An unsupported, specific "cure window" figure is introduced without citation and not carried elsewhere.** design_regulatory.md §1.2 states an active unlisted-cap breach "cure window approx. 90 days, verify" — no source is given (contrast with data_facts.md's T1, which discusses "active" vs "passive" breach conceptually but names no cure period at all), and design_risk.md's parallel limit (#9, "Illiquid/unlisted aggregate") instead treats any active breach as "hard block... non-compliance," with no cure window at all. A specific number this consequential (it affects how much unlisted headroom the desk can safely run) should not appear in only one of seven documents with no source.

13. **[MEDIUM] The associate/related-party percentage caps (15%/25%/30%) that anchor a core group-conflict control are sourced to a single, unfetched secondary document and repeated without independent corroboration.** The 15% single-associate / 25% aggregate-per-asset-class / 30% combined figures recur in design_regulatory, design_risk, design_operations and design_allocation, all citing the same Aug-2022 APMI-hosted PDF that could not be fetched this session (data_facts.md flags this table only "partially_verified"). Because Angel One is both the group and the executing broker here, this is exactly the control most likely to be tested at inspection — it should be confirmed against the primary circular (or SEBI's website directly) before being hard-coded as an IPS/RMS limit, rather than being treated as settled because four documents happen to repeat the same unverified number.

14. **[MEDIUM] REIT/InvIT dividend "exemption from 1-Apr-2026" is used as if settled in several downstream tables despite being explicitly flagged as unenacted in the fact pack.** data_facts.md's T8 and its "commonly believed but wrong" list (#16) are explicit: the REIT/InvIT dividend exemption is a "pending 2026 Bill, not yet confirmed enacted," and real distributions are a multi-component blend (interest/dividend/rental/return-of-capital) that must be read off each unit's Form 64B. design_allocation.md's tax table and design_selection.md §8 do carry a "verify enactment" caveat, but design_performance.md's Policy Composite and cost/tax-drag sections use REIT/InvIT return and income figures without re-flagging this uncertainty at the point of use — the caveat needs to travel with every number derived from it, not just the first table it appears in.

15. **[MEDIUM] PMS fee non-deductibility risk is documented but not reflected in any cost/return model.** data_facts.md explicitly notes PMS management-fee deductibility against capital gains is "contested/fact-specific — no blanket deduction" (also echoed in design_regulatory.md §1.3). None of the TCO (design_selection §9), cost/tax-drag budget (design_performance §3.5), or worked-numbers (design_regulatory §6) tables show a scenario where the fee is *not* deductible — all implicitly treat the fee as a pure return deduction. If a tribunal disallows the deduction for this client's fee structure, realised/post-tax return (R3/R4 in the performance design) would be lower than modelled by roughly the tax rate applied to a disallowed fee add-back. Worth an explicit sensitivity line.

16. **[LOW-MEDIUM] Conflicts-of-interest framework covers Angel-One-group products but not third-party-PMS/route referral incentives.** design_regulatory.md §1.4 states Ionic "may be paid as distributor" when referring a client into a third-party PMS, and design_selection.md treats third-party PMS purely as a due-diligence exercise. The conflicts protocol built for Angel One group products (one-time Annexure A consent, IC best-selection minute, 10% IPS cap, real-time alert) is not extended to this referral-commission relationship, even though recommending a fee-paying third-party manager over a non-fee-paying alternative is a comparable conflict that a SEBI/APMI inspection would likely probe.

17. **[LOW] Citation/amendment-date drift for "the" PMS Regulations across documents.** design_regulatory.md's source [1] cites the regulations "as amended... Feb-07-2023"; design_allocation.md's source [3] cites "amended 03-Sep-2025"; design_risk.md's source [1] cites "last amended on February-10-2025." All four should be citing the single current consolidated text; as written, a reader cannot tell which amendment date the blueprint is actually built on.

18. **[LOW] No sizing analysis of how the ₹500 Cr is expected to be split across legal entities, even though several hard caps are per-entity.** The regulatory design's own "open questions" list surfaces this ("Which legal entities will hold the ₹500 Cr... each must independently qualify... and sign an NDPMS agreement ≥₹10 Cr for LVAI status") but no design note proposes or models a structure. Because LVAI status, the ₹10 Cr AIF Large-Value-Fund entry, and per-PAN SIF minimums are all entity-level, this is a load-bearing open item that materially changes almost every other number in the blueprint (fee schedule, unlisted headroom, international sleeve routing) depending on the answer.

## Top missing items (things a real ₹500 Cr NDPMS operating model needs that no design covers adequately)

1. **A completed legal opinion / SEBI Informal Guidance response on the standing-instruction and event-trigger mechanism** before any of it is labelled "must" — this single open item underwrites a large share of the consent-latency cost savings claimed across four documents.
2. **AML/KYC, PMLA beneficial-ownership and source-of-funds due diligence** for a ₹500 Cr inflow — absent from all seven notes; enhanced due diligence and STR/CTR monitoring obligations are not mentioned anywhere.
3. **A concrete legal-entity structuring proposal** (individual/HUF/trust/holding-company split of the ₹500 Cr) resolving the open question above, since it changes LVAI eligibility, AIF/SIF minimums, tax rates and FEMA routes simultaneously.
4. **SEBI SCORES/Ombudsman grievance-redressal process** (mandatory for registered PMs) is not described in the governance, operations or client-communication sections.
5. **Independent QA/sanity-check process for the quantitative models** (risk_calc.py, sel/tco.py, the rebalancing Monte Carlo) — as finding #5 shows, at least one of these currently produces implausible output that a basic cross-check against the CMA table would have caught.
6. **Professional indemnity insurance / firm capital provisioning for error trades**, beyond the qualitative error-trade policy in design_operations §8.1 — no reserve, insurance line, or net-worth-adequacy check is modelled.
7. **A reconciled, single canonical Model Master** (one Moderate model, one fee assumption, one direct-stock name count) — right now the blueprint would be merging at least three internally competing versions of "the Moderate model."
8. **Board/trustee-level governance for a corporate or trust client entity**, distinct from "authorised signatories" for e-sign — flagged only as an open question, never designed.
9. **A worked example showing net alpha actually clearing the performance targets** under the allocation design's own CMAs — as finding #1 shows, the current numbers do the opposite, and no document shows what portfolio changes (fee reduction, higher direct-equity/PMS selection weight, lower TER) would be needed to close the gap.
10. **Explicit treatment of Angel-One-as-broker execution conflicts on group-product trades** (order routing/best-execution scrutiny specifically when the instrument itself is a group product), beyond the existing brokerage-rate-card benchmarking.
11. **Data-vendor/index-licence and tax-adviser engagement letters** as actual gating deliverables in the build roadmap (design_operations §9.1) — several tables (REIT taxation, target-maturity-fund tax treatment) are explicitly marked "review by tax counsel" as an open item but no roadmap phase actually assigns and dates that engagement.

## What is strong (worth keeping largely as-is)

1. The **R0–R4 return-series hierarchy** plus Perold implementation-shortfall decomposition (design_performance §1.1, §3.6) is a rigorous, audit-ready way to isolate consent-latency cost from selection/allocation/tax effects — a genuinely good design choice, not just a compliance checkbox.
2. The **hybrid band-design Monte Carlo comparison** (design_rebalancing §2.3) that quantifies turnover/cost/tax/TE trade-offs across eight strategies and lands on a specific, defensible recommendation is decision-useful work, not filler.
3. The **consent-latency cost formula** (∝ weight × vol × √days) applied consistently across the regulatory, risk, rebalancing and performance notes to put a rupee number on NDPMS friction is a good unifying framework and should become the house standard.
4. The **FIFO/demat-account segregation strategy** (Core/Tactical/Income books) as the only real lever for approximating specific-identification given mandatory FIFO is correctly reasoned and practical.
5. The uniform **feasibility-tagging discipline** (allowed / allowed_with_consent / restricted / not_allowed / unclear_verify) applied across all seven domains is genuinely useful governance scaffolding worth keeping as the house convention.
6. The **Angel-One-group conflicts protocol** (one-time Annexure A consent + self-imposed 10% IPS cap vs SEBI's 15/25/30% + IC best-selection minute + real-time 80%-of-cap alert) is a defensible, above-minimum control design that should survive the merge unchanged (subject to finding #13's sourcing check).
