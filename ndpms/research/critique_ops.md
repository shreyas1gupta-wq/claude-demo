# Head of Operations & Client Servicing — Review of the ₹500 Cr NDPMS Operating Blueprint

Reviewer stance: Head of Ops/Client Servicing who has lived through NDPMS consent failures, settlement fails, MF cut-off misses, AIF capital-call surprises and dashboards that never shipped.
Materials reviewed in full: design_regulatory, design_allocation, design_rebalancing, design_risk, design_performance, design_selection, design_operations (.md + .json), data_market, data_facts. Also read the prior critique_cio.md and critique_compliance.md already in this folder to avoid duplicating their findings; this review focuses on consent-workflow feasibility, ops load/staffing, SLAs, reconciliation, data-feed realism, custodian/broker mechanics, team sizing and the build roadmap.

---

## Overall assessment

The seven-document set is unusually well-sourced for a first pass, and the consent-latency quantification (a ₹-cost attached to every day of delay, threaded through regulatory, rebalancing, risk and performance) is genuinely good design discipline — it treats NDPMS friction as a budgeted cost, not a complaint. The recon matrix, the R0–R4 return hierarchy and the hybrid band/event-overlay rebalancing design are strong enough to keep close to verbatim.

But read as an operator who has to run this book on Monday morning, the blueprint is optimistic in three specific ways that the earlier CIO and Compliance reviews did not fully surface. First, the team is sized for a steady-state book, not for the actual daily/weekly grind of eleven reconciliation types, AIF capital-call tracking, corporate-action processing and custodian liaison on a multi-instrument ₹500 Cr book — 1.5 Ops FTE and 0.5 Compliance FTE, with no named backup for the PM or Compliance seat, is thin enough that a single absence during a live event stalls the consent machinery. Second, the design treats the monthly RPP as the operating heartbeat but never stress-tests it against real client behaviour: a 30-minute walkthrough of 50+ trade lines every month, forever, for a UHNW family that travels, and a capital-call SLA (≈10 business days) that is tighter than the normal consent cadence with no standing pre-authorisation to fund it, are both realistic failure points that will surface in year one, not year three. Third, the build roadmap compresses custodian selection, OMS/RMS compliance-rule configuration and AP-relationship onboarding into the same 0–30-day window with no dependency gating and no BCP/DR test before the ₹500 Cr actually starts moving — exactly the sequencing that produces "we went live before the failover was ever tested" incidents. None of this is unfixable, but it needs to be fixed before the operating model is presented as ready to run, not discovered during the first tail event.

## Findings

### High severity

**1. [operations/allocation] The entire ₹500 Cr initial deployment is executed off a single week-0 consent event — a bigger discretion-adjacent instruction than anything built into the monthly RPP, and no document treats it with matching scrutiny.**
- Target: design_allocation.md §6 ("Deployment plan for fresh ₹500 Cr" — 12 weekly tranches, acceleration rules "pre-consented in the deployment schedule so no fresh instruction is needed") and §9 build sequence ("Week 0–2: Sign IPS with Model Master, bands, deployment schedule + acceleration rules... Legal opinion on standing-instruction scope"); design_operations.md §2.6 Standing Instructions Register (6 listed instructions, none of which is "deployment-ladder acceleration").
- issue_type: regulatory_misstatement. severity: high. confidence: medium.
- Detail: every other domain (regulatory, risk, compliance's own review) worries hard about whether *monthly band-restoring* standing instructions cross into discretion. But the design's actual riskiest item is bigger: 100% of the client's capital, deployed across ~50 lines over 12 weeks, with built-in acceleration logic (pull forward tranches on a −5%/−10%/VIX>22 trigger) that the PM alone monitors and fires — all authorised by one consent at onboarding. This is a much stronger "standing discretion" fact pattern than a ±5pp monthly band restoration, yet design_operations.md's Standing Instructions Register (§2.6) does not even list it as a category, so it is neither reasoned about legally nor tracked/audited the way the other five instructions are.
- Suggested fix: add "deployment-ladder acceleration" as a named, seventh Standing Instruction with its own trigger/instrument/max-₹/revocability row, subject it to the same legal-opinion gate as the band-restoring instructions, and consider re-confirming client consent at least once mid-deployment (e.g., week 6) rather than relying solely on the week-0 signature for 12 weeks of execution.

**2. [operations] Team sizing (6.7–7.2 FTE) cannot sustain eleven daily/weekly recon types, AIF capital-call tracking, corporate-action processing and custodian liaison simultaneously, and no seat has a named, tested backup.**
- Target: design_operations.md §7.1 ("Ops/fund accounting 1.5... Compliance officer 0.5 (dedicated slice of a shared role)") and §4.1 (11-row recon matrix, mostly daily or weekly) and §8.2 ("Key-person unavailability: documented delegation of authority... refreshed quarterly").
- issue_type: underestimated_cost_or_risk. severity: high. confidence: medium.
- Detail: 1.5 Ops FTE is being asked to run daily cash/holdings/MF-folio/CA/dividend/fee recons, weekly AIF-tranche and deployment-tranche checks, monthly AIF-statement recon, and custodian liaison — for a book that itself flags (§4.2) that it needs three *new* extensions (AIF ledger, bond settlement leg, third-party-PMS look-through) beyond the house's existing single-strategy automation. 0.5 Compliance FTE is expected to run pre-trade checks, associate-limit real-time monitoring, consent-audit and SEBI/APMI filing for the same book. "Documented delegation of authority" is named as the BCP answer but no backup individual, seniority level, or authority-activation test is specified anywhere — if the PM and the Compliance officer's shared-role holder are both unavailable during a live tail event (a real scenario for small teams), the consent machinery in Section 2 of the same document has no one authorised to act.
- Suggested fix: raise Ops/fund-accounting to at least 2.0–2.5 FTE and Compliance to a fully dedicated (not shared-slice) 1.0 FTE for year 1 given the build-and-run overlap; name specific backup individuals (not just "designated backup") for the PM and Compliance sign-off gates and rehearse the handover once before go-live, not "quarterly" starting after go-live.

**3. [operations] AIF capital-call funding has no standing pre-authorisation, yet its SLA (~10 business days) can be tighter than the normal monthly consent cadence — a real default-to-the-GP risk for a 9–12% AIF sleeve.**
- Target: design_operations.md §3.2 ("capital-call SLA (typically 10 business days, approx., per PPM)") and §2.6 Standing Instructions Register (cash sweep, distribution reinvestment, CA default, tranche execution, hedge auto-roll, tail de-risk — capital-call funding is absent); design_allocation.md's Moderate/Aggressive models sizing Cat II/III at 9%/12% of AUM (₹45–60 Cr).
- issue_type: missing. severity: high. confidence: medium.
- Detail: a drawdown notice from a private-credit or PE fund typically gives ~10 business days. Under this design that notice is not covered by any of the six listed standing instructions, so funding it requires either an ad hoc RPP (subject to the same 2-day-target/48-hour-escalation consent SLA used for everything else) or an emergency workaround. If the client is travelling or slow to respond — the single most common real-world reason capital calls get missed — the fund misses the call, triggering PPM default remedies (forced sale of the LP interest at a discount, forfeiture of prior distributions, or exclusion from future closes), which is a real, avoidable, money-losing failure mode entirely absent from the design.
- Suggested fix: add a seventh standing instruction — "AIF capital-call funding: pre-authorised sweep from the liquid/arbitrage sleeve up to the committed-but-undrawn amount already disclosed in the IPS, executed on receipt of a drawdown notice from an approved fund, client notified same day" — mirroring the cash-sweep instruction's mechanics, so funding never depends on the standard RPP SLA.

**4. [operations] The 30-minute monthly RPP walkthrough for 50–55 trade lines is not a sustainable operating assumption — it will either become a rubber-stamp (defeating the point of NDPMS) or consume materially more RM time than the 0.5–1.0 FTE budgeted.**
- Target: design_operations.md §2.2 (RPP template, item 3: full trade list with ISIN, side, quantity/₹, execution window, participation rule, cost/tax estimate per line) and §2.4 (T-1 "30-minute call/portal walkthrough"); design_operations.md §7.1 (RM/client servicing 0.5–1.0 FTE).
- issue_type: impractical. severity: high. confidence: medium.
- Detail: design_allocation.md §4 itself caps the line count at "50–55 lines Moderate... hard cap 60." Walking a principal or family office through 50+ lines — each with a cost/tax estimate and an unlisted/associate flag where relevant — in 30 minutes, every month, indefinitely, for a relationship the house has committed to treat with "full ownership of the portfolio and its returns," is optimistic. In practice this either turns into "approve all" click-through with no real client engagement (which is the exact discretion-recharacterisation risk the design elsewhere worries about) or the RM/PM ends up spending 2–3x the budgeted time per cycle across calls, follow-up questions and partial-rejection handling (§2.4's "PM revises, re-issues affected lines... within 1 business day").
- Suggested fix: redesign the client-facing pack as a one-page executive summary (drift, top 5–8 changes, cost/tax total) with the full 50-line detail available on click-through/appendix, size the RM walkthrough at 45–60 minutes not 30, and track actual walkthrough time/engagement depth as an operating KPI (not just SLA-to-approve) to catch rubber-stamping early.

**5. [operations] The build roadmap compresses custodian selection, OMS/RMS compliance-rule configuration and AP-relationship onboarding into the same 0–30-day window with no dependency gating, and schedules the first BCP/DR drill only after the ₹500 Cr is already live and deploying.**
- Target: design_operations.md §9.1 (Phase "0–30: Legal/compliance foundation — custodian selected and onboarded; OMS pre-trade compliance rules configured" listed alongside "consent-ledger schema live" as parallel MUST/SHOULD items) vs §9.1's own Phase "90–180: Scale-readiness... Documented BCP, completed first drill" (marked COULD); design_rebalancing.md §6.3 ("the mandate should empanel at least one AP relationship before go-live so this is available on day one" — not listed as a dated roadmap deliverable anywhere in design_operations.md).
- issue_type: impractical. severity: high. confidence: medium.
- Detail: custodian onboarding for a multi-asset book (equity + MF + AIF + bonds/G-secs + international look-through) realistically needs KYC/agreement negotiation, SFTP/API testing against the daily pipeline, and a parallel-run before it can be trusted — this is rarely a 30-day exercise even when it is the *only* thing being built that month. The same 30-day window is also asked to deliver custom OMS compliance-rule configuration (Reg-24 unlisted/associate/derivative logic, consent-FK linkage) and its own UAT. Meanwhile the AP relationship the rebalancing note says must exist "on day one" is never named as a dated roadmap item in design_operations.md, and the first BCP/DR drill — the one test that would catch a custodian-feed or consent-ledger failure — is scheduled for 90–180 days, i.e., after several live monthly cycles and the entire 12-week deployment ladder have already run without it.
- Suggested fix: sequence, don't parallelise, the custodian → OMS-configuration → AP-relationship chain (custodian selection first, since OMS/RMS integration depends on its feed format); add "AP/AMC-direct relationship signed" as a named, dated Phase 0–30 deliverable; and move a lightweight BCP/DR tabletop test (not the full annual drill) to before the first live monthly cycle, not after 90 days.

### Medium severity

**6. [operations] No professional-indemnity/error-reserve capital line exists anywhere in the cost stack despite an explicit policy committing the firm to absorb error-trade losses.**
- Target: design_operations.md §8.1 ("if the loss is borne by the firm, booked to a segregated error-account, never netted against client P&L") vs §9.3's cost table (Custodian, OMS/RMS, e-sign, data feeds, data/dashboard engineering, team, audit/legal, contingency/BCP — no PI-insurance or error-reserve line).
- issue_type: missing. severity: medium. confidence: medium.
- Suggested fix: add a PI-insurance premium (or a funded error-reserve, sized off a percentile of historical error-trade incidence at comparable books) as an explicit line in §9.3's annual cost table; without it the "segregated error-account" has no described funding source.

**7. [operations/rebalancing] Time-bound corporate actions requiring an active election (rights issues, buybacks) have no dedicated fast-track consent SLA distinct from the standard ad hoc/tail-event SLA — the design's own rules mean a rights window can close before consent completes.**
- Target: design_rebalancing.md §3 ("Corporate action requiring an active election... **No standing instruction**... Ad hoc consent required" — response time "Before the corporate-action deadline," no numeric SLA given); design_operations.md §2.4's SLA table (RPP-specific, ≤2 business days) is not explicitly extended to CA elections.
- issue_type: missing. severity: medium. confidence: medium.
- Detail: rights-issue subscription windows are commonly 2–3 weeks from announcement, and a rights subscription is also flagged elsewhere (design_regulatory §5.3) as an "active" unlisted-cap event requiring extra compliance headroom checks — adding process time on top of consent time. If the CA is announced shortly after that month's RPP has gone out, the next natural consent touchpoint is a full month away; relying on an undefined "ad hoc" channel with no committed turnaround risks missing genuine, value-accretive elections (rights at a discount, favourable buybacks).
- Suggested fix: define a CA-election-specific SLA (e.g., RM contact within 4 business hours of the CA record-date notice, client decision within 3 business days, with an explicit "no response = default no-action" clause so silence is compliant, not ambiguous) and add it as its own row in design_operations.md §2.4.

**8. [operations] Custodian selection criteria list an "SLA" as a screening factor but specify no enforcement mechanism — no penalty, credit, or escalation consequence when the custodian itself misses NAV or corporate-action deadlines.**
- Target: design_operations.md §3.3 ("Selection criteria: SEBI-registered custodian... NAV computation SLA, corporate-action processing SLA...") — no reference to SLA credits, penalty clauses, or a remedy path anywhere in the document.
- issue_type: missing. severity: medium. confidence: medium.
- Suggested fix: require the custodian contract to include SLA credits/penalties for late NAV/CA processing and specify the escalation path (Ops lead → CFO → contractual remedy) the same way the recon-break escalation ladder (§4.2) is specified for internal breaks.

**9. [operations] Blended fully-loaded FTE cost (₹18–25 lakh/FTE) looks understated for Mumbai/BKC-calibre PM, Risk-manager and Research-analyst hires, which weakens the "tight but workable" economics conclusion built on it.**
- Target: design_operations.md §9.3 ("Team (6.7–7.2 FTE, blended fully-loaded cost approx. ₹18–25 lakh/FTE) — 120–180 [₹ lakh/yr]").
- issue_type: underestimated_cost_or_risk. severity: medium. confidence: low.
- Detail: ₹18–25 lakh fully-loaded is plausible for junior Ops/RM support but is low for a dedicated PM and a Risk manager at a firm running a ₹500 Cr multi-asset mandate under regulatory scrutiny — market fully-loaded costs for those two seats alone in Mumbai typically run well above this blended average, meaning the true team cost is likely nearer the top of, or above, the stated ₹120–180 lakh band, which matters directly to §9.3's own conclusion that the business case "depends on this infrastructure serving multiple NDPMS mandates."
- Suggested fix: re-run §9.3's team-cost line with role-specific (not blended) fully-loaded salary bands before using the "≈₹184–318 lakh/yr, tight but workable" framing in front of the CIO/board.

**10. [operations] Multi-entity/family signatory complexity (HUF, trust, holding company — each requiring board-resolution-backed authorisation) is not designed into the consent workflow at all, even though the mandate's structure across legal entities is flagged elsewhere as an open question.**
- Target: design_operations.md §2.3 ("Aadhaar e-Sign... or class-2/3 DSC for HUF/trust/corporate signatories" — the only mention of non-individual signatories) vs the open legal-entity-structuring question already raised in the compliance review (finding #18) and implicit in the ₹500 Cr mandate's likely spread across "individual, HUF, family trust, holding company" (design_regulatory.md §2).
- issue_type: missing. severity: medium. confidence: medium.
- Detail: a DSC line item is not a consent workflow — for a trust or holding company, each RPP approval may need to be preceded by a board/trustee resolution authorising the specific signatory (or a standing Power of Attorney with its own scope limits), which has its own lead time and its own risk of lapsing (director changes, POA renewal). None of this is modelled in the SLA table (§2.4), which assumes a single individual clicking "approve" within 2 business days.
- Suggested fix: map the actual legal-entity structure first (per the compliance review's finding #18), then design a distinct consent sub-workflow (with its own SLA) for any entity requiring board/trustee-level authorisation, rather than treating "DSC available" as sufficient design.

**11. [operations] LRS utilisation and TCS-credit tracking across family members has no described operational process, despite sitting adjacent to the "Client consolidated view" dashboard and materially affecting the international sleeve's realistic size.**
- Target: design_operations.md §6 ("Client consolidated view (new, client-facing)... look-through across NDPMS AUM + third-party PMS sleeve + LRS/IFSC sleeve advised outside AUM") vs design_regulatory.md §4 ("LRS capacity... N=5 → ₹11 Cr/yr; TCS 20% → ₹2.2 Cr blocked till ITR credit").
- issue_type: missing. severity: medium. confidence: medium.
- Detail: LRS is a per-individual, bank-tracked annual limit; nothing in the design describes how the house coordinates *across* family members to avoid one member inadvertently double-counting capacity already used for an unrelated purpose (school fees, property purchase abroad), nor how the ₹2.2 Cr TCS-blocked-till-ITR-credit cash drag is tracked and reconciled back into the family's overall liquidity picture. This is a real client-servicing gap, not just a reporting one.
- Suggested fix: add an LRS-utilisation tracker (per family member, updated at least quarterly via RM/bank confirmation) to the consolidated-view dashboard's data model, and add the TCS-credit timeline to the cost/tax dashboard so it is visible cash, not a forgotten receivable.

**12. [operations] No change-control/UAT process is described for the Python compute engines (risk_calc, tco, rebalancing simulation) before their outputs drive daily VaR, consent-latency and TCO figures — and the CIO review already found a live 100x scaling bug in one of them.**
- Target: design_operations.md §5.2/§9.2 ("Build (Python, on top of existing house pipeline pattern)... Data pipeline design... Recon engine" — no mention of code review, test coverage, or a promotion gate from dev to production) vs the sibling CIO review's Finding #1 (sel/tco.py 100x tax-drag scaling bug) and Finding #6 (rebalancing worked-example arithmetic that doesn't foot).
- issue_type: missing. severity: medium. confidence: high (the bug itself is independently confirmed in the CIO review; the absence of a stated QA gate is directly verifiable in this document).
- Detail: this project's own design phase already produced at least one materially wrong, unreviewed number that would have shaped a client-facing "tax dominates cost" narrative had it not been caught. A production pipeline computing daily VaR, consent-latency shortfall and monthly TCO for real client money needs a described peer-review/unit-test/UAT gate before a model change reaches the dashboards Section 5–6 describe — none is specified.
- Suggested fix: add a lightweight change-control step to §9.1's roadmap ("any change to a compute engine feeding a client-facing or IC-facing dashboard requires a second reviewer and a documented reconciliation check against the prior run") before automation maturity (Phase 90–180) is declared complete.

**13. [operations] Third-party-PMS and AIF-administrator statement delivery is assumed to arrive on a schedule the house does not control, with no described data-sharing SLA or escalation path.**
- Target: design_selection.md §3.3 ("pull the third-party PMS's monthly client statement (holdings + TWRR) via a data-sharing agreement (or the client's own statement)") and design_operations.md §5.1 (AIF NAV/capital statements: "Monthly (some quarterly)... PDF/Excel, manual ingest") vs the monthly attribution/TWRR close deadline (design_performance.md §5.1: "Monthly pack... T+5 business days").
- issue_type: missing. severity: medium. confidence: medium.
- Detail: third-party PMS managers and AIF administrators routinely deliver statements 15–30 days after month-end in practice, especially for smaller or newer managers — well past the T+5-business-day monthly-pack deadline the performance design commits to. "Via a data-sharing agreement" is aspirational language, not a negotiated SLA with a named remedy (stale-flag threshold exists in the risk design, §2.10, but no contractual lever is described to actually get the data faster).
- Suggested fix: make a negotiated data-delivery SLA (with a stated day-count and an escalation contact) a condition of onboarding any third-party PMS or AIF manager, and build the monthly pack's publish logic to explicitly show "as at last-confirmed-date" for any sleeve still on a stale look-through, rather than silently holding the whole pack.

### Lower severity / verification items

**14. [regulatory, extends a prior finding] The custodian-exemption framing ("historic <₹500 Cr AUM exemption... at ₹500 Cr+ it binds anyway") understates the actual rule — the exemption was removed entirely for essentially all PMS in the 2020 Regulations, regardless of AUM.**
- Target: design_regulatory.md §1.5 ("Custodian for securities... mandatory for PMs (historic <₹500 Cr AUM exemption; at ₹500 Cr+ it binds anyway)").
- issue_type: factual_error. severity: low. confidence: medium.
- Detail: the earlier CIO review flagged this line as uncorroborated (finding #13); a web check this session confirms the pre-2020 ₹500 Cr AUM threshold was removed by the 2020 PMS Regulations, making custodian appointment mandatory for (near) all portfolio managers irrespective of AUM — so the operationally correct statement is simply "mandatory, full stop," not "binds anyway at this size," which incorrectly implies smaller PMS books might still be exempt.
- Suggested fix: correct the sentence to state the custodian requirement is unconditional under the 2020 Regulations (except for advisory-only PMs), and drop the AUM-threshold framing everywhere it is repeated.
- evidence_url: https://www.anandrathipms.com/blog/role-of-custodian-in-pms.php

**15. [operations] No described after-hours/weekend coverage for the consent-SLA clock — a 48-hour escalation trigger that lands on a weekend or market holiday has no stated adjustment rule.**
- Target: design_operations.md §2.4 (SLA table: "Not actioned by 48h → Escalation call from CIO"; "72h... Escalation call from CIO if still unreachable") — no mention of business-day vs calendar-day counting near holidays, nor an on-call rotation for the CIO/RM escalation calls outside business hours.
- issue_type: missing. severity: low. confidence: medium.
- Suggested fix: state explicitly whether SLA hours are business hours or calendar hours (the RPP SLA table elsewhere says "business days," but the escalation clock in §2.4 mixes "business hours" and plain "hours" language), and name an on-call rotation for escalation calls that fall outside standard hours, especially around the Diwali/year-end period when HNI clients are least reachable.

**16. [operations] The second-broker relationship (≥10% of listed-equity flow, for TCA benchmarking) introduces a second full settlement/custodial-instruction relationship with no described integration effort or cost line.**
- Target: design_operations.md §3.4 ("a standing option... to route a minority of flow through a second, non-group broker for benchmarking — recommended at ≥10% of listed-equity flow in year 1... reviewed after 6 months") vs §9.3's cost table (no line item for a second broker relationship, its OMS connectivity, or its own settlement-instruction setup with the custodian).
- issue_type: missing. severity: low. confidence: medium.
- Suggested fix: add the second-broker onboarding (OMS connectivity, custodian standing-settlement-instruction setup, dual contract-note reconciliation) as an explicit Phase 90–180 roadmap item and a small cost-stack line, rather than leaving it as an unbudgeted "standing option."

**17. [operations/rebalancing] The 10-trading-day RPP validity window can leave as few as one usable Mon/Thu tranche if consent lands near the 48-hour escalation point, which is not modelled anywhere.**
- Target: design_operations.md §2.2 ("validity window (10 trading days, then lapses)") and §2.4 (SLA table) vs design_rebalancing.md §6.6 ("enter the existing weekly Monday-redemption/Thursday-deployment cadence... naturally staggers execution over up to four weekly tranches").
- issue_type: inconsistent. severity: low. confidence: medium.
- Detail: "up to four weekly tranches" assumes consent lands promptly (T0, the first Thursday). If consent instead lands at the 48-hour escalation point (i.e., effectively the following Monday/Tuesday), only 1–2 Thursdays remain inside the 10-trading-day window before the pack lapses and must be reissued — a materially different execution reality than "four tranches" implies, with no documented fallback (partial execution vs full re-issue) for this common case.
- Suggested fix: model the tranche count as a function of actual consent-arrival day (not just the best case), and define a rule for what happens to unexecuted lines when the window closes before all tranches complete (auto-lapse-and-reissue vs a one-time extension).

**18. [operations] No dedicated SLA is defined for the 06:30 IST pre-market ops check if an upstream feed (AMFI NAV, custodian EOD file) is delayed past the 02:30 IST publish deadline — the pipeline's stale-file check is a detection control, not a recovery/escalation SLA.**
- Target: design_operations.md §5.2 (pipeline: "01:30 IST Validate: schema check, stale-file check... 02:30 IST Publish... 06:30 IST Ops desk pre-market check").
- issue_type: missing. severity: low. confidence: low.
- Suggested fix: add an explicit recovery SLA ("if a feed is still stale at 06:00 IST, Ops manually pulls the prior validated file with a flag and notifies Risk/PM before the 06:30 check, rather than the dashboard silently carrying a gap").

## Top missing items (a ₹500 Cr NDPMS operating model needs, not adequately covered anywhere)

1. **A standing, pre-authorised AIF capital-call funding instruction** (sweep from liquid/arbitrage up to the disclosed committed-but-undrawn amount) — currently the single biggest unaddressed "we missed the deadline and lost money" scenario in the whole design.
2. **Named, tested backup individuals** (not just "documented delegation of authority") for the PM and Compliance sign-off seats, rehearsed once before go-live.
3. **A negotiated custodian SLA with enforcement teeth** (credits/penalties, escalation path) — currently "SLA" appears only as a selection criterion, not a contractual mechanism.
4. **A distinct, faster consent sub-workflow for board/trustee-authorised entities** (HUF/trust/holding company), separate from the individual e-sign flow the SLA table assumes.
5. **A dedicated fast-track SLA for time-bound corporate-action elections** (rights issues, buybacks) distinct from both the standard monthly RPP and the tail-event ad hoc process.
6. **Change-control/UAT discipline for the compute engines** (risk_calc, tco, rebalancing simulation) that feed client- and IC-facing numbers — this project's own design phase already produced one materially wrong output that a basic review would have caught.
7. **PI insurance or a funded error-reserve** to actually back the error-trade policy's commitment that the firm absorbs certain losses.
8. **A realistic, dependency-gated build sequence** for custodian → OMS/RMS compliance-rule configuration → AP/AMC-direct relationship, with a pre-go-live (not 90–180-day) BCP/DR tabletop test.
9. **LRS-utilisation and TCS-credit tracking across family members**, feeding the consolidated client view and the cost/tax dashboard.
10. **Negotiated data-delivery SLAs with third-party PMS managers and AIF administrators**, since the monthly attribution close (T+5 business days) assumes data the house does not control arrives on time.
11. **A realistic client-facing RPP format** (executive summary + appendix) and an engagement-depth KPI, so the monthly walkthrough does not silently degrade into a rubber-stamp.
12. **Second-broker relationship onboarding** (OMS connectivity, settlement instructions, cost) budgeted and scheduled, not left as an unbudgeted "standing option."

## What is strong (worth keeping largely as-is)

1. **The consent-latency cost quantification** (₹/day-of-delay, tied to real vol and position size) threaded consistently through regulatory, rebalancing, risk and performance — turns an abstract legal constraint into a concrete, monitored, budgeted number, which is exactly the right instinct for an ops-led NDPMS design.
2. **The hybrid band design with an event overlay** (design_rebalancing.md §2–3, "Strategy H") is a well-reasoned, quantified answer to the calendar-vs-threshold rebalancing trade-off, sized specifically to the monthly consent cadence rather than borrowed from a discretionary playbook.
3. **The recon matrix's structure** (11 recon types, each with a compares/SLA/escalation row) is the right skeleton for a multi-instrument book — it just needs the staffing (Finding #2) to actually run it.
4. **The standing-instruction register's design principle** (narrow, mechanical, fully parameterised, revocable, never expanding scope) is the correct shape for the boundary between "pre-authorised" and "discretionary" even though its exact legal edge still needs sign-off — it should stay this narrow when finding #1's new capital-call and deployment-ladder instructions are added, not loosen.
5. **Reusing the existing house dashboard family** (IPS, attribution, deployment tracker) rather than standing up a parallel BI stack for NDPMS is the pragmatic, cost-efficient call, and the palette/pipeline consistency (Section 5.4) will pay off in maintenance cost.
