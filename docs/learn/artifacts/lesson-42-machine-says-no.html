The Machine That Says No

A research register can be honest on paper and still leak at the joints — a rule adopted in conversation, a paper trade nobody grades, a walk-forward promised but never built. This leg turned the program's governance from prose into tested code: the adjudicator that killed its first shortlist, a lane system the CI physically enforces, the spine that assembles the regime score without ever mistaking silence for calm, and the closing of a file the desk had emotional reasons to keep open.

PART 1

The spine that can't mistake silence for calm

The regime score R now exists as code: seat states averaged within blocks, blocks combined by the registry budgets — and AVAILABILITY-WEIGHTED, so a seat with no data shrinks the denominator and is named in the trail instead of silently reading as "no stress." Three refusals are structural, each with a test that fails if anyone weakens it: Tier-C seats never enter the score (they act only through a capped, negative-only overlay that can shrink risk permission but never add it); reduce-only seats can push toward risk-off but never pull toward risk-on; and the warm-up period returns NO bucket rather than defaulting to "normal." Run end-to-end on the vaulted index, the first real timeline put its rare crisis bucket exactly where history put the crises — COVID for 70 sessions, IL&FS, Russia — and held it into the vault's final month, the eve of the documented May-2026 episode. A demonstration, the record says carefully, never a forecast.

PART 2

The adjudicator drew blood on day one

The walk-forward harness — expanding history, disjoint test eras, an embargo scaled to the signal's half-life — was always the registered judge for de-risk value. Built and pointed at the F2-index shortlist, its first verdict was to take the shortlist away: both surviving cells were within budget in only two of four eras, their full-period costs flattered by a quiet decade. That is not the machinery failing; that is the machinery doing the one thing it exists for — separating "worked on average" from "works across eras." What survives is the honest economics: always-on insurance costs 2.5-4pp a year in whipsaw-rich eras and paid ~11pp of drawdown protection when COVID arrived. The full F2 will price that trade at book level; nothing is armed until it does.

PART 3

A law the CI enforces

lanes

Every adaptive or promotable rule now lives in exactly one lane — shadow, challenger, online — in a registry file. Six rules are laned today; none is online.

teeth

The validator REFUSES to load a rule marked online: promotion happens only at review dates fixed a year ahead, by an explicit commit citing the winning print. A test suite proves the refusal fires.

the ledger

Paper trades now have a home with pre-committed columns and a lapse policy: a grading row not filled within three months marks the trade LAPSED and resets its promotion clock. Evidence has a filing date, or it isn't evidence.

costs

The per-book turnover-cost curve is executable — registry-driven, returning the honest [lo, hi] pair, with the ADV table's PROVISIONAL stamp traveling on every number it produces.

PART 4

Closing a file the desk liked

The phase directive — states as trajectories, with direction and age — was the register's most elegant idea, and elegance is exactly what the gates exist to check. At index resolution all three gates have now spoken: direction adds nothing at matched stress levels (F7a, p=0.65); AGE adds nothing either (H68a: young versus old high-stress days differ by half a point, p=0.84); and the dead-band calibration (H67a) shows the display is stable at every registered grid point. The phase file is CLOSED — levels carry everything measurable — and the convention's own provenance now says so, with the reopening condition written down: a pre-registered pass at another band or resolution, nothing less. The trails stay, because a reader deserves to know how a state got where it is. The trades never came, because the numbers never did.
