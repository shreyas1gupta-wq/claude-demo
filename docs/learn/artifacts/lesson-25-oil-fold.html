Not All Barrels Are Alike

For a country importing 85%+ of its crude, an oil spike sounds like unambiguous bad news. It isn't — and the difference between the two kinds of oil-up year is worth eighty percentage points of Indian equity returns. This lesson teaches the Kilian decomposition, walks the OPEC-and-shale regime history, and then does the register's signature move on its own giant result: caps it, because the proxy can't prove what it suggests.

PART 1

The eighty-point split

Take every year from 1994–2015 in which real oil rose more than 10%. Split them by one crude flag: was the world equity market up (a demand-flavored year — oil rising because the world is booming) or down (a supply-flavored year — oil rising against a weakening world)? Why the desk capped its own best-looking print. The flavor flag IS the global equity sign — so "supply-flavored oil-up" years are, by construction, global bust years, and India falls in global busts whatever oil is doing (its global loading is +0.57, Lesson 22). This print is therefore CONSISTENT with the Kilian asymmetry and nearly indistinguishable from the global factor wearing an oil costume (2008's annual averaging — a first-half spike netted against a second-half crash — even sneaks a crash year into the "oil-up" set). What it licenses: the commitment that raw oil price never enters the ladder, and this table as a briefing exhibit. What it doesn't: a measured oil-specific asymmetry. That waits for the real identification machinery.
PART 2

The decomposition, and the identification ladder

Kilian's 2009 result — not all oil price shocks are alike — separates oil moves into supply shocks, aggregate-demand shocks, and precautionary-demand shocks, using the structure of the oil market (supply's short-run inelasticity, a freight-based measure of global activity) rather than the equity tape. That is the machinery L9's oil leg is committed to, and the entry's identification ladder is explicit: the equity-sign proxy (today's shadow, capped) → the Kilian activity index (the runsheet pull, where the real replication is registered — the desk expects the asymmetry to survive but SHRINK once the global factor is controlled) → the full structural VAR literature (reference, not desk machinery). A state that cannot tell a $20 symptom from a $20 tax is worse than no state.

PART 3

Regimes, not a cycle — and the fold

1973–86

The embargo/revolution era, then the quota system's slow unraveling into the 1986 collapse — the first proof that cartel floors are regimes, not physics.

1998–2008

The $10 trough, then the discipline era riding China's demand — the decade that taught everyone the wrong lesson about permanence.

2014–20

The market-share war against shale — the structural break: a supply curve with a fast short end; then April 2020's negative print as the era's absurdist coda.

2016–26

OPEC+, the 2022 invasion spike and price caps, voluntary-cut politics, and the 2026 Hormuz-tension spike that drove the rupee's May episode (Lesson 22's live exam) — regimes stacked on regimes, no clock anywhere.
