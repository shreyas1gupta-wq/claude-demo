The Market That Moves While You Sleep

The principal reopened candidate generation with a wide brief: sweep the field — India's quant PMS boom, the global systematic giants — for anything missed; find the theories 2026-2040 rewards before the herd does; then step past cycles into technical-systematic territory. PART 1

What the field is actually doing

India's systematic scene is arriving fast — five quant PMS launches in 2025 alone, AlphaGrep at multi-thousand-crore scale, factor shops advertising "300+ factors" — which is itself the finding: the STANDARD factor menu is crowding in India on schedule, and the desk's 25-35% forward haircuts were the right call before the evidence arrived. The global read was different: AQR's "trends everywhere" (trend applied to macro series and factors, not just prices) and the 2025 dispersion between good and average trend implementations are genuinely instructive; return stacking is a leverage decision we haven't made; tail hedging is our hedge grid wearing a hat; and the macro-quantamental school is convergent validation — sophisticated desks independently arrived at the genre this program has been building with harder pre-registration. Marketing numbers were consumed nowhere: every adopted idea lands as a registered candidate with bars, or it doesn't land.

PART 2

Two candidates and a registry of broken clocks

Two ideas survived the constraint filter. H60-VRP: India's variance risk premium — F5a already measured implied above realized on 85% of days — funded historically by retail lottery demand that SEBI's 2024-26 derivative curbs are actively reshaping. H61-FLOWMULT: the inelastic-markets frame (a dollar of flow moves aggregate value about five dollars, and the effect persists) applied to the most flows-rich story in world markets — India's SIP culture meeting FPI swings. Both are data-gated on the principal's pulls; both carry their priors already. And because the SEBI curbs are a dated fact that every derivatives-adjacent design must now respect, the long-referenced BREAKS REGISTRY finally became a file: five dated structural breaks, append-only, checked by the annual loop — regime changes as registered facts rather than post-hoc excuses.

PART 3

The overnight print

Track T's first registered test asked where the NIFTY's return actually accrues. The answer is extreme: over 2007-2026, the overnight session (close to next open) earned +24% a year while the intraday session (open to close) LOST 12.6% a year — a 36-point annual gap at t=7.6, robust with COVID excised, and strengthening since 2019. The desk's prior said the US signature would appear "attenuated"; it appears at full strength, and the miss is booked. The registered consumption cap does the disciplining: nobody trades this (two trades a day is suicide at Indian transaction taxes, and index opens are auction prints). What it changes is EXECUTION: staged deployment tranches now have measured evidence to default to buying at the close, not the open — across a multi-tranche program, the average overnight accrual is real money the old habit left on the table.

PART 4

A graveyard to calibrate the bar

Track T opened the way the cycle program opened. The atlas began by testing calendar clocks against ENSO — the planet's own oscillator — and letting the control group fail; Track T began with the Brock-Lakonishok-LeBaron moving-average family, the most-mined rule set in finance. Net of costs and deflated for 174 trials of selection: 0 of 10 rules survive, precisely as registered — the best two nose past buy-and-hold raw and die under deflation. That graveyard is the point. Every future T-design now faces a bar that has already refused the famous stuff, judged by a census that counts every look this program has ever taken. The track's backlog (trend-on-states, gold-equity cross-asset, an India low-volatility factor) inherits that standard on day one.
