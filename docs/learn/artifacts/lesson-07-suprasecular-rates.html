The Trend You Must Not Trade

Real interest rates have drifted down for 700 years — about one basis point a year. It's one of the most famous facts in modern macro, it's probably true, and our book does exactly nothing with it. This short lesson is about why "true" and "tradable" are different properties, measured.

PART 1

The fact, reproduced by us

A real interest rate is the rate you see minus inflation — what a lender actually earns in purchasing power. Schmelzing's celebrated study traced it back to 1311 and found a glacial decline of roughly 1–2bp per year across seven centuries. We ran the 150 years we can measure cleanly: −1.27bp/yr — independently inside his band. The fact is real.

At any horizon your capital will ever live through, the cyclical weather is fourteen times larger than the secular climate. And here is the satisfying part: after we measured this, the literature caught up with itself — Rogoff, Rossi and Schmelzing's own re-examination reports that the trend is not reliably detectable in 150-year sub-samples. Our SR2 finding, corroborated from inside the original author's team.

The rejected uses, named in the monograph: terminal-multiple assumptions anchored to "the trend"; duration bets on reversion to it; anything keyed to r-star estimates (whose confidence intervals are wider than the object they estimate); "lower forever" leverage. Each is a real fund's real epitaph. What survives: the real-rate ERA classification — which already lives in the debt monograph's repression gauge — and a written humility clause in any valuation model that embeds a terminal rate.
