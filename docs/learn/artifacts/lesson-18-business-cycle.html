The Cycle That Earns No Seat

The business cycle is the most famous cycle in economics — real, datable, persistent — and this desk gives it no weight at all. This lesson is about why that's the right design, and about the trial that produced the entry's exportable surprise: the textbook direction "credit leads growth" fails on its own home panel. India's contrarian finding turns out to be everyone's finding.

PART 1

Real, sticky — and already spoken for

Two of the three trials confirmed the object. Growth cycles on the 18-country panel space out at a median 6 years with 65% of spacings in the classic 3-to-7-year band (BC1, PASS on its pre-registered bar), and the growth state stays on the same side of neutral 77% of the time year-over-year (BC3) — a regime, like inflation eras, not a coin flip. So why no seat? Budget algebra: the macro block already holds monetary stance, credit, capex, and property states under one shared weight. A "growth state" built from IIP and GST would be their correlated shadow — a fifth claim on the same budget carrying mostly duplicated information. CONTEXT costs zero and keeps everything useful: the dated chronology becomes the desk's event clock for validating the seated states, and the nowcast surface feeds the briefing.

PART 2

The import that died at home

Indian evidence (Saini et al. and related RBI work) has long said something awkward: in India, business activity leads credit — banks lend after demand shows up — the reverse of the direction usually imported from the advanced-economy literature. Before trusting either direction we registered a test of the import on its own home panel: for each of 18 countries, where does the cross-correlation between the credit gap and the GDP gap peak?

What this does NOT say. The credit gap's seat (L10) rests on a different mathematical claim: elevated credit gaps predict CRISES — rare tail events — years ahead (our own honest AUROC of 0.62–0.65, Lesson 1). Leading a crisis and leading the business cycle are different objects: one is quantile exceedance before a tail event, the other is cross-spectral phase at cycle frequency. BC2 kills only the casual import "credit leads growth, so credit states time the cycle." L10 never claimed that; now nothing on this desk ever can. A STANDING WARNING enters the registry: every imported lead-lag direction, in any future entry, is a hypothesis to test with BC2's print as the reminder — never an assumption.
