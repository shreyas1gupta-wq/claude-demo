What Goes Up Rises Slower

"Profit margins are the most mean-reverting series in finance" is one of value investing's founding slogans. We tested it on 114 countries and seventy years: the reversion is real at extraordinary breadth — and the slogan is still wrong. High profit shares don't fall; they rise slower. That distinction redesigns the candidate seat, reframes India's earnings boom, and closes Band 2 with its thirteenth entry.

PART 2

Kalecki's discipline, and India's live question

The profits identity — profits equal investment plus dividends minus household and government saving plus net exports — is an accounting truth, not a forecast, and its job is to discipline stories: WHY a share rose determines WHICH reversion forces apply. India's listed-corporate profit share roughly tripled from its FY20 trough — tax cuts, deleveraging completed, formalization concentrating profits in listed large-caps, and the identity's fiscal leg running hot. Some of those tailwinds are exhaustible in exactly the way the US literature found for 1989-2019's margin expansion (rates and taxes did much of the work) — so the honest question isn't "when do margins crash" (PS2 says that question earns no seat) but "how much of projected earnings growth silently assumes the share KEEPS rising?" That is what candidate H56 governs: at high percentiles of the share's own history, the valuation block's earnings-extrapolation input gets a registered haircut, read jointly with the value spread so an expensive market priced off boom-share earnings gets flagged as the double-count it is. Tier C, reduce-only, no decline forecast anywhere in the interface.
