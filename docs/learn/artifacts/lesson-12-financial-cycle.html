Credit and Concrete

The financial cycle: credit and property prices inflating each other's collateral for a decade at a time, then unwinding together. The strongest pooled regularity we have measured — 17 countries out of 17 — a working module with India's data reality built into its API, and the honest weakness that keeps it a state, never a date.

PART 1

The strongest regularity we've measured

Borio's claim is mechanical: banks lend against property whose value their own lending inflates — collateral channel up, funding channel back — until debt service outruns income. If that's real, five-year changes in credit/GDP and in real house prices should move together everywhere. We measured it:

PART 2

The combined state, live on four countries

The case record's live lesson (Part B): Australia and Canada spent a decade as the famous "un-burst" controls — proof that a HIGH state carries no date. As of mid-2026, Canada is ~20% off its 2022 peak while Australia still climbs at 8× income: identical entry states, different exits, on no schedule anyone called. The seat conditions permissions — leverage, hedge floors, sector tilts through the projection principle — and refuses, with its own FC3 evidence now, to call tops.
