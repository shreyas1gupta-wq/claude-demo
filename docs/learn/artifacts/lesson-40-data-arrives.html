The Day the Data Arrived

The atlas closed with twenty-five designs waiting on data. Then two mirrors of that data turned out to be reachable from here — a full daily NIFTY history and a survivor panel of 487 stocks — and the machine got to do, for the first time at scale, the thing it was built for: run pre-registered designs on real prices, bars already written, nothing decided after the print. One afternoon produced two passes, four honest fails, one corroborated freeze, a process error caught by its own paper trail, and a discovery about the ladder's own memory.

PART 1

Authenticating a stranger's data

A mirror on GitHub is a stranger's copy of exchange data. The vault discipline treats it like one: BEFORE looking at the values, write down anchors the true data must hit — independently documented facts like "March 23, 2020 closed at 7,610, down ~13%" — then check. The index series hit six of six. The stock panel hit its two event anchors exactly (Yes Bank's −56.1% on the moratorium day; Reliance's +14.7% in the 2020 rebound) and MISSED two structural bars — both misses recorded, neither bar moved, both traced to composition facts rather than bad data. One check "failure" turned out to be a bug in the check itself (NaN cells failing a greater-than-zero test), fixed and confessed in the record. And one bias cannot be checked away: the panel's roster is the index AS OF 2021, backfilled — a SURVIVOR panel, stamped as such on everything it touches.

PART 2

What the daily resolution settled

budget vol

Lesson 33 showed February ordinary at monthly resolution. At daily resolution, the budget ±1-day window carries ~60% higher |returns| (p=0.005) — the resolution theorem now measured on BOTH sides of the same event. The L5 seat's claim finally rests on our own print. (The day-only cut failed at n=19; and the partial registration mis-copied its parent's window — caught because the parent was written down. Process note #5.)

weekdays

No day-of-week structure (p=0.587) — and India's Monday is the BEST median, the opposite sign of the famous US weekend effect. The 5.5 reject now carries evidence it never needed.

the fast layer

The L2 composite's half-life on real data: ~2.9 months — inside the ladder's registered [1,3] band. Its episode test FAILED honestly at 7 of 11: one untestable (warm-up), two misses that VALIDATE the three-leg design (2013 lived on the funding leg the two-leg run didn't have), and one discovery.

The discovery — the 2008 shadow
Once a mega-crisis enters an expanding percentile's history, a Feb-2018-size vol spike ranks low and the state stays cold.

Expanding percentiles buy no-look-ahead at the price of post-crisis de-sensitization. This is now a measured property of the whole ladder's machinery, and design F2b — expanding vs trailing-window percentiles, priced in episode drawdown terms — is registered to price the trade-off.

PART 3

A freeze corroborated, a narrative's third no-show

The one-month reversal flag (L1) has been frozen since its lesson argued the desk cannot win a race against market-makers. The survivor panel allowed a one-way test, registered as such: it could corroborate the freeze but never unfreeze it, because survivorship flatters the signal. The print: gross +0.20% a month (t=0.39 — nothing, even flattered), minus ~1% a month of transaction costs at 89% monthly turnover, equals −0.80% a month, negative in both half-samples. The freeze is corroborated on data biased in the signal's favor — the cleanest kill in the register.

And the crowding monitor got its first calibration: comomentum IS a real slow state (autocorrelation 0.65), but the 2017-18 "crowding built before the smallcap unwind" prior FAILED — late 2017 prints the lowest comomentum in the sample. That makes three independent prints where the imported factor-crowding narrative fails to appear in Indian data: no negative momentum skew, no visible 2025 unwind, and now no 2017 build. Either India's factor book genuinely isn't crowded the way America's is, or the simplified construction is contaminated by stress-correlation regimes (the series peaks in June 2020, which points that way too) — both explanations are on the record, and the full residualized run will arbitrate.

PART 4

What a mirror cannot do

None of this discharges the true runsheet. A survivor panel cannot run the real MR1 (which requires point-in-time membership precisely so that dead names count); a mirror vault is superseded the day the exchange pull lands; and India VIX, CCIL funding rates, and the flow series remain unreachable from here. The runsheet now records what the mirrors did and the exact line where their authority stops. The pattern this lesson preserves: pre-registration is what made a windfall of data SAFE to use on the same afternoon it arrived — every bar was already written, so nothing the data said could tempt the desk into deciding after the fact.
