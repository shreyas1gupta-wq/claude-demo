The Prior That Printed Twice

Most research priors are one-sided bets: "this will pass" or "this will fail." The oil fold's design registered something braver — a two-part prediction, written at the moment the data was pulled, that its own earlier result would SURVIVE proper identification and simultaneously SHRINK. Both halves printed true. This lesson is about what that kind of prediction costs, why the desk's most violent number lost half its violence honestly, and why a replication mirror and an author's own GitHub repo carry different weights in the vault.

Vaulted Kilian index 1973-2019 (5/5) · Känzig supply-news 1975-2025 (4/4) Print OL-D1a PASS: +46.0pp, shrunk from +81.2pp Sep 2026
PART 1

A proxy that graded itself

OL1's original print was spectacular: among years when oil rose more than 10%, India's factor-market return averaged +38% when the rise was "demand-flavored" and −43% when "supply-flavored" — an 81-point spread. But the desk capped its own pass on dissection, because the flavor proxy was the pooled sign of GLOBAL EQUITY returns. A year counted as demand-driven when world stocks rose — which means the split was partly measuring "did world stocks rise?", and India's returns were being sorted by a variable that contains their own biggest driver. The monograph called this its weakest rung and registered OL-D1: pull the real Kilian index — the structural measure of global real activity built from shipping rates, the variable the theory actually names — and re-run the same test with the proxy replaced. Crucially, the registration wrote the expectation down BEFORE the pull: the asymmetry should survive, and it should shrink.

PART 2

Two files, two pedigrees, one discipline

The Kilian index arrived through the weakest kind of mirror — a third-party replication workbook on GitHub — so the anchors did all the work: the sample maximum had to land in the 2007-08 boom (it did: 190.7 in May 2008, the published peak), the GFC and first-oil-shock recessions had to print in the right tails, and the series had to co-move with the separately vaulted IMF commodity index (Spearman 0.634). Five of five. The second file needed less charity: Känzig's oil-supply-news shocks came from the author's own distribution repository, vintage December 2025 — effectively a primary source. Its anchors were pure public record: the November 2014 OPEC "no cut" shock had to be negative (−10.8), the November 2016 Vienna cut positive (+8.9), the March 2020 price-war collapse negative (−10.6). Four of four. Same two-pass protocol either way — bars committed before values are read — because the discipline doesn't relax for good pedigree; good pedigree just makes misses less likely.

PART 3

Survives AND shrinks — both, exactly

OL-D1a re-ran OL1's construction verbatim, changing one thing: a year is demand-flavored when global real activity (the Kilian index's annual mean) is rising, supply-flavored when falling. The split rebalanced from 8/3 to 6/5, and the two definitions agree in only 7 of the 11 oil-up years — confirmation that the old proxy really was measuring something else. The result: demand-flavored oil-up years +36.8% for India, supply-flavored −9.1%. The +46.0pp spread clears the registered +10pp bar four times over — the asymmetry is real, structural, and survives identification. And it is 35 points smaller than OL1's spread, because the catastrophe cell deflated: −43% became −9% once 2008 and 2011 stopped being joined by every other year world equities happened to fall. The two-sided prior — survive, shrink — printed true on both halves. That almost never happens, and when it does it is worth more than any single pass: it says the desk's model of its own evidence was calibrated, not just lucky.

PART 4

What is consumed, what waits

Consumption is unchanged in kind and upgraded in quality: the L9 oil fold's shock-type briefing table — "an oil rise means different things depending on what drives it" — is now a measured claim with a structural instrument behind it, not a committed intention. It remains a briefing input, never a standalone signal: eleven annual observations promote nothing. The Känzig shocks sit vaulted for OL-D2's windfall test (were the supply-driven oil-DOWN years of 2014-16 a measurable gift to India?) whenever that tiny-n design earns its run. Still owed on the runsheet: the Baumeister-Hamilton decomposition proper and the post-2019 Kilian months. The census stands at 128 consumed looks; two of them bought this lesson.
