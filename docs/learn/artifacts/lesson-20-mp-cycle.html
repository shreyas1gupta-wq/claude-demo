The Sign That Flips

Everyone watches the central bank on announcement day. The desk's seat for monetary policy does nothing that day, by construction — and this entry measured why: the correlation between rate moves and credit growth is POSITIVE in the same year and NEGATIVE the year after. Read the stance today and you're reading the boom it reacted to; the policy is in next year's data. Also: the only imported lead-lag direction to survive our matched-legs test, and a coin-flip result that redesigned what "stance" means.

The coin flip that redesigned "stance." MP3 asked whether tightening and easing come in persistent campaigns at annual granularity: 53% — noise. The campaigns you remember (thirteen hikes in 2010-11, the 2022-23 sprint) are real narrative objects, but pauses and reversals wash out the year-over-year sign. Consequence, recorded in the ledger: L6's regime content lives in the stance LEVEL — the effective real rate against its own history, paired with the liquidity position banks actually feel — never in the direction of last year's move. A Δ-based stance variant is now formally closed off.
PART 3

India's machinery, and what the desk refuses

The India record (the monograph's case chapter walks 1997-2026 campaign by campaign) adds the engineering realities: the operating rate itself MIGRATES (Bank Rate → repo → reverse-repo-as-floor in the 2020-22 surplus era → SDF), so the desk builds a regime-tagged effective-rate series rather than a naive splice; the lending-rate regime changed four times (BPLR → Base → MCLR → EBLR), each reform an admission the last one transmitted poorly — and as EBLR share rises, transmission speeds up, so the lag itself is re-estimated annually with hysteresis. System liquidity (surplus vs deficit) is the stance banks feel, often more than the repo rate: the seat reads the PAIR.

What the desk refuses, permanently: same-day MPC event trades (the announcement is public; L2's fast layer owns event volatility mechanically), MPC-meeting prediction, OIS-curve alpha (crowded, decayed), and any "RBI put" assumption. The seat's whole edge is the unfashionable part — reading last year's stance carefully while the market re-reads this morning's adjectives.
