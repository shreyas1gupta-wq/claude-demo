Speed Is the Seatbelt

Leg one landed the data; leg two pointed the risk system at eighteen years of real Indian market history and asked the program's central question: does cutting risk on the fast-stress state actually buy drawdown protection at a price inside the budget? The answer came back with a shape nobody ordered but everything now confirms: protection lives at the FASTEST settings, and the direction of travel — the phase arrow the desk spent a directive building — adds nothing a level didn't already say.

PART 1

Three cells out of eighteen

F2's grid — trigger percentile × confirmation strictness × re-entry family — was frozen before any real data existed. Run on the vaulted index (long-only proxy, costs in), exactly three cells cleared both pre-stated bars (≥5pp less drawdown in the deep episodes, ≤2pp/yr of return drag) — and all three share the same left edge: the EARLIEST trigger (0.80) with the LOOSEST confirmation (any one leg). Requiring both legs to confirm destroyed protection everywhere, because the drawdown leg lags realized vol — by the time both agree, a COVID-speed crash is over. The ladder's "any one arms, two confirm" asymmetry, written as a design principle, is now a measured fact.

The shortlist
Trigger 0.80, one-leg confirm, calendar re-entry: −5.6pp of deep-episode drawdown for 1.23pp/yr — the best adoptable cell.

The phase-D re-entry cell printed even better (+8.4pp at 0.56pp/yr) — and is inadmissible, because phase-conditioned rules were gated behind their own test. Which the desk then ran.

PART 2

The arrow that failed its exam

F7 was frozen months ago with its consequence attached: if "falling-from-high" days don't actually differ from "rising" days at matched stress levels, the phase overlay stays display-only. The print: at high stress, forward 3-month returns are +5.62% (falling) vs +5.67% (rising), p=0.65. No difference. FAIL — and the frozen rule executed itself: phase stays display-only.

So why did the phase-D re-entry cell dominate the grid? Because it re-entered EARLIER — and at high stress levels, everything forward-looking is rebound-rich regardless of direction (the panic-state rebound is priced by the LEVEL). The dominance was earliness wearing a phase costume. The register's oldest doctrine — states, never dates — gains a sibling: levels, not directions. A direction-free early-calendar variant (F2c) is registered to prove the costume empty at the full run.

PART 3

The trend seat gets its numbers — and a humbling

TS1 calibrated L4's two legs. On the index, only the FASTEST lookback (3-month sign) passed its drawdown bar — 22% max drawdown against 47% for buy-and-hold, through 2008, at 1.1pp/yr of drag — while the 6- and 12-month rules failed on cost and the 12-month rule actually printed a WORSE drawdown than its own-window buy-and-hold. The desk's stated prior ("12m passes via the 2008 exit; 3m whipsaws hardest") was inverted — with one honest asterisk: the 12-month cell's warm-up excludes 2008 entirely, so its half of the prior was untestable, not refuted. On gold, all three lookbacks passed and the slowest was strongest (net return ABOVE buy-and-hold with maxDD 34% vs 62% across 54 years) — the literature arriving intact.

The converging lesson of the whole leg: Indian equity crashes are FAST, so every drawdown-control mechanism that works here works by being fast — the 0.80 trigger, the one-leg confirm, the 3-month lookback. Gold trends are SLOW, and slow rules win there. The seatbelt must match the speed of the crash it's built for; the CONTRACT's own prior ("shorter lookbacks, higher confidence") is now a measurement.
PART 4

What this leg cannot claim

Every number above is an index-proxy, single-path, in-sample calibration with flat costs — it shortlists cells and calibrates priors; it arms nothing. The full F2 (three legs, book-level costs, walk-forward) remains registered, now fifteen cells cheaper. Three cells printed NEGATIVE drag — de-risking that added return — and were deliberately not promoted: that is a timing-alpha claim, and this design does not adjudicate alpha. The discipline that refused those free-looking returns is the same one that made every other number in this lesson believable.
