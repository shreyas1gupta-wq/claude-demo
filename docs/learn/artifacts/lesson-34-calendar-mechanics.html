Sixteen Dates the Fast Layer Must Ignore

Some dates move prices without carrying information: tax money leaves the banking system on statutory days, results announcements gap single names overnight, expiry flows pin index closes, and index committees force billions of passive rupees to trade at a known minute. None of it is alpha for a weekly-cadence desk. All of it can make our signals lie. This entry ships the plumbing map — an ops pack whose tests literally forbid it from ever recommending anything.

PART 1

Money leaves on schedule

Four times a year — June 15, September 15, December 15, March 15 — Indian companies wire advance-tax instalments to the government, and once a month, around the 20th, GST settles. On those days tens of thousands of crore leave bank deposits for the government's account at the RBI. Banking-system liquidity drains MECHANICALLY, and overnight funding rates (call money, TREPS) spike on a schedule anyone can read off a statute.

Why the fast layer cares: L2's funding-stress trigger watches for exactly such spikes, because in 2018 a REAL funding squeeze (the IL&FS run — Lesson 17) was the earliest warning anywhere. A stress classifier that can't tell September 15 from September 2018 has its precision quietly eaten by sixteen false alarms a year.

Design point · quarantine, never veto

The rule only SUPPRESSES: a funding fire inside a drain window is flagged as "probably mechanical" and needs a second L2 leg (realized vol, VIX backwardation) to confirm. The catastrophic failure mode — a real crisis that happens to straddle a tax date — is covered by the two-of-three architecture, not by trusting the calendar.

PART 2

Gaps you don't have to buy

A staged entry builds a position in tranches across days. If a tranche date crosses the holding's results announcement, the desk is voluntarily short a straddle over the announcement — it eats whatever the gap does, for free, in the middle of building a position it hasn't finished deciding on. The pause rule defers tranches past a supplied results calendar (±1 business day). The cost is a day or two of delay; the benefit is a distribution of dodged gaps that design H58-D2 will simply COUNT once daily data lands — a frequency report, not a hypothesis test, because prudence rules don't need p-values.

Expiry days get the same treatment: pinning and rollover flows distort closes, so rebalance tranches skip them. One engineering decision matters enough to be a test: the expiry WEEKDAY is a required config parameter, never a constant — SEBI's 2024-25 derivative curbs and the exchanges' weekday reshuffles turned "expiry is Thursday" from a fact into a bug waiting to fire. The module raises an error if you call it without saying which weekday this exchange, in this era, uses.

PART 3

The index add that isn't momentum

Twice a year the Nifty family reconstitutes, and every index fund and ETF tracking it must buy the adds and sell the drops at the effective date — a forced flow announced weeks in advance. The classic literature (Shleifer's demand curves, Harris–Gurel) found large entry pops in the US; the modern literature finds the effect DECAYING as arbitrage capital crowded it. Design RC1 will measure India's version for the special-sits sleeve when the add/drop lists are vaulted.

The hygiene rule that needs no trial
An add's pre-effective pop is a one-time level shift from forced flows — not a trend. It must be excluded from momentum lookbacks.

This follows from what the pop IS, not from a measurement: feeding it to L3/L4 would have the trend legs chase a flow artifact that ends the day the index funds finish buying. RC1 measures its SIZE for the sleeve; its exclusion from momentum is adopted now.

PART 4

An entry with zero trials, on purpose

Everything here is mechanics — flows forced by statute, settlement, or index rules. The register grades mechanics by counting (how many false fires quarantined, how many gaps dodged, how big the reconstitution flow), so this entry registers four counting designs and runs nothing today: the daily data they need isn't vaulted yet. The one design with a real bar is H58-D1, because a suppression rule must EARN its existence: drain-window fires must prove ≥2× as frequent as the base rate AND ≥80% mean-reverting within five business days — i.e. the calendar genuinely attracts mechanical spikes — before the quarantine graduates from flag to rule.

The module itself ships now, tested: quant/ladder/exclusion_calendar.py — three deterministic calendars, seven exact tests, and a namespace test (test_no_alpha_surface) asserting no function named anything like signal, alpha, tilt, or score ever appears. The pack excludes and defers; it never recommends. No seat, no budget, no ladder row — the entry's product is that the fast layer stops lying to itself on sixteen known dates a year.
