Destiny Isn't a Trade

Demographics is the one macro force you can genuinely forecast twenty years out — the people are already born. It is also, by our own measurement, the force with the least stable translation into returns. The final Band-0 lesson: why the best-measured thing in economics gets zero allocation authority in our book.

PART 2

India's window, held honestly — and Band 0's closing lesson

India's working-age share rises into the mid-2030s — the demographic dividend is real as potential. The monograph holds it with three honesty clamps: the dividend cashes only through jobs absorption (an outcome free data can date only in retrospect); the country number blends aging southern states with young northern ones; and the listed market is not the street economy — buying NIFTY is not buying India's age pyramid. Every market top of the past decade has cited the dividend; that is precisely why the charter forbids demography in sizing memos.

Band 0, complete — what the slowest band taught
Seven entries, five real-data legs, two honest nulls, one full rejection — and one repeated lesson: at century scale, TRUE and TRADABLE come apart.

The debt identity is true — and classifies regimes, never dates. The dollar's drift is true — at half a point a year. The real-rate trend is true — and 14× smaller than its swings. Gold's anchor is true — with no measurable pull. Inflation runs in generations — two of them, ever. War shapes everything — on no schedule. Demographics is destiny — for populations, not portfolios. Band 0's entire harvest is insurance sizing, regime classification, and humility clauses: roughly 100–300bps of drawdown protection, zero forecasts.
