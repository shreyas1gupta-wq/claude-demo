Timing Is Law, Direction Is Surprise

India's election calendar is written in statute; what the market does with the result is not. This closing entry of the intra-cycle band folds the political cycle into the calendar seat with its number attached — the pre-election window's direction predicts the result month's direction 3 times in 8 — buries two more labels with their evidence, and tallies a band whose real work was refusal.

PART 1

The number behind the slogan

The calendar seat was designed on one sentence: timing is law, direction is surprise. PL1 makes the second half a measurement — across all eight general elections in the factor sample, the sign of the two months BEFORE the result month matched the result month's sign three times. At or below a coin flip. The record's teaching cases carry the point better than any statistic: 2004's two-day −15.5% when an expected continuity story reversed; 2009's +10.7% upper-circuit HALT when the surprise ran the other way; 2024's −5.9% as an expected sweep narrowed. Three violent moves, three different directions, one lesson: schedule the risk, never the view. That is what L5 already does, why the FP1a pre-election pass was refused promotion, and why HL-7 sits in paper-trade until roughly 2029.

PART 2

What 3.7 actually adds: reform waves as context

Elections are dates; REFORMS are waves — 1991's big bang, the GST/IBC/RERA institutional cluster of 2016-17, the corporate-tax reset of 2019 — and waves reprice specific sectors over years, not days. The entry's addition is an annotation convention: dated reform-wave cards in the briefing layer, feeding sector-projection CONTEXT while a wave is in progress. Never a forecast — reform timing is politically stochastic, and a desk that annotates waves honestly beats one that predicts them confidently.
