PART 1

A filter that finds what it was built to find

The supercycle literature (Erten-Ocampo, Cuddington-Jerrett) dates 3-4 great commodity waves since 1870 using band-pass filters that keep only oscillations between roughly 20 and 70 years. Run that filter on anything long enough — a random walk included — and smooth multi-decade waves come out, because that is what the filter passes. It is also two-sided: it needs future data to date the past, so its chronology was never computable in real time. This is the same objection our Contract codifies against the HP filter, now pointed at commodity historiography. Respect the papers as history; refuse the tool for trading.

So we ran our own machinery — expanding, one-sided, the same peak logic as Lesson 13, minimum gap 15 years, bars pre-registered before the run — on the Jacks 1850–2015 real price panel (40 commodities, vaulted and authenticated against IMF and EIA lineages at correlations 0.987–0.996):

PART 2

What passed matters most for India

CS2 asked whether commodity swings are BROAD — whether energy, metals and agriculture move on a common factor rather than on private stories. Across 451 cross-group pairs of 10-year changes, the median correlation is +0.30 and 89% are positive. That pass is the license H53 actually needs: an import-basket terms-of-trade state for India (crude, gold, coal, edible oils, fertilizer) measures one real macro object, not oil counted five times.

And for a net importer, that one state feeds two OPPOSITE-signed harvests at once: a high world-commodity state is a macro headwind (current account, rupee, WPI) and a metals/energy sector tailwind simultaneously. That is why the seat exposes the state and never a direction — each consumer applies its own sign. No date interface exists anywhere: after CS1, period language is banned from the seat outright.

PART 3

The confession, and why it's in the lesson

Near-miss #4, logged the day it happened. Drafting the vault's authentication protocol, the desk wrote plausible-looking "results" into the table BEFORE the checks ran — invented correlations, an invented pass narration. Caught minutes later, blanked, and then the checks were run for real: the invented numbers were WRONG (0.986 invented vs 0.993 measured; 3.28× invented vs 2.45× measured), even though all five bars did genuinely pass. That is exactly why the rule exists: text that looks like output must only ever be written from output. The standing rule is now two passes for every protocol file — bars first, results only after the print. A desk that trains people on this material should train them on the failure, not just the machinery.

Where atlas 1.3 lands: state yes, clock no, capacity-timing no. H53 stays exactly what the atlas designed it to be — context plus a reduce-only sector-tilt conditioner plus an enrichment input to the India macro-transfer seat, Tier C until the graduation test (its design is registered) can run on real Indian sector histories. The supercycle survives as a regime object with its own measured chronology, and nothing in the seat can ever claim to know what year the next one turns.
