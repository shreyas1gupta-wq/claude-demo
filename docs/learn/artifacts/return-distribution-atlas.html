Volatility: its path, its distribution, its memory

Rolling 21-day annualized NIFTY volatility. Median 14.3%, 95th percentile 38.6%, peak 87.9% (March 2020) — a 6× range between the calm and stressed regimes of the same index.

21-day realized vol (NIFTY)

Realized vol is right-skewed and lognormal-ish: it lives near 10–18% and visits 40%+ rarely but unforgettably.

VIX daily close, 1990–2026

Implied vol says the same: median 17.6, mean 19.4 (mean > median — the right tail), maximum 82.7 on 2020-03-16.

autocorrelation of |return| (clustering) autocorrelation of return (signal)

The defining asymmetry of markets: the sign of yesterday's return tells you nothing (orange dots ≈ 0), but its size echoes for months — |r| autocorrelation is +0.28 at lag 1 and still +0.18 thirty days out. Volatility is forecastable; direction is not. This is the license behind vol-managed sizing (F3a) and the L2 stress band.

The compression is the story: annualized dispersion falls 19.3% → 2.9% from 1y to 20y, the worst case rises −58% → −0.2%/yr, and the positive fraction climbs 69% → 100%. The US caveat from the disaster census stands: this is the shallowest-disaster country of the 16 — the 16-country median worst drawdown is −78% with an 18-year recovery.

Why horizon changes the shape: the kurtosis ladder

Excess kurtosis by aggregation step (0 = Gaussian). Fat tails wash out as returns aggregate — daily 15.8 collapses to 0.2 at annual — with one exception that proves the rule: US monthly over 152 years keeps kurtosis 18, because it contains the 1930s. Within any calm few decades, aggregation tames the tails; across a century that includes a Depression, it never fully does. (Shiller monthly prices are month-averages — the 1-month cells are smoothed; ≥ 1y horizons are unaffected.)

The 152-year real drawdown path

Drawdown of the US real total-return index from its running peak. The deepest: −77% (June 1932). The longest full recoveries cluster around the 1910s inflation, the Depression, and 1966–1982 — the last of which never printed a −77% but took sixteen years of real losses to escape.

The US verdict: small paid — +1.5 to +2.7pp/yr at every horizon for 1.32× the volatility, and at 20 years its worst case (+5.3%/yr) beats the market's (+1.9%): the small premium compounds into a higher long-horizon floor. The India verdict is the opposite: over 32 years the smallcap tilt lost 2.9pp/yr against the market while carrying 1.34× the volatility, a deeper worst month, and a −90% drawdown (the 2001 bust). At the factor level, Indian smallcap beta has not been compensated — the smallcap money in India is stock selection inside the segment, never the segment itself. Daily survivor-tercile detail (2013–21, severe survivorship — a lower bound on damage): the small basket prints daily AR(1) of +0.20 vs the NIFTY's 0.00 — the stale-price signature of illiquidity: measured smallcap volatility understates true risk, because part of every shock arrives the next day.
