The Cycles We Refuse

Empire arcs, eighty-year turnings, Thucydides traps: the grandest cycle stories in finance, given their full day in court — steelmanned, dismantled, and salvaged for the three things they get right that our book already holds through measurable channels. The series' first rejection lesson, because knowing why you say no is half of a method.

PART 1

The court record

Dalio's Big Cycle

Eight-metric empire arcs, Dutch→British→American. Steelman: the metrics are real observables. Flaw: three instances, three different monetary and technological worlds — a sequence, not a distribution.

Thucydides Trap

Rising vs ruling powers, "16 cases, 12 wars." Flaw made vivid: the author's own case count moved in print (from 11-of-15) — when the dataset can be re-dated to fit, the dataset is the narrative.

Turchin's cliodynamics

The strongest entry: a 2010 forecast, in Nature, of 2020s instability — made in advance, with stakes. Scored honestly both ways in the monograph: a genuine hit on timing, contested on mechanism, one forecast is still n=1.

Fourth Turning

Generational schedules. The unfalsifiability structure in its purest form: hedged horizons, movable definitions, retrofit-friendly stages.

The common fatal flaw is the one Band 0 taught from its first entry: three-to-five instances drawn from different processes are not draws from one process. Each "cycle instance" has a different causal story wearing the same label — and a framework that can absorb any outcome can inform no position.

PART 2

The salvage — what they get right, and where it already lives

Here is the twist that makes this a rejection rather than a dismissal: nearly everything true in the grand narratives is already in our book, through seats with data, budgets, and falsification conditions. War finance builds debt mountains — that's Atlas 0.1, where the DS1 peaks literally are the world wars. Wars end monetary orders — that's 0.2's sterling case. Wars ignite inflation regimes — 0.5's 1940s arc. Rivalry drives reserve diversification — 0.2's sanctions-and-gold leg. What the grand narratives add on top of all that is exactly one thing: a schedule. And the schedule is the part with no evidence.

The three channels conflict may enter our book (all pre-existing): the Caldara-Iacoviello geopolitical-risk index as a watched series (free, monthly, real volatility content, weak return predictability — so it watches, it doesn't trade); L15's existing tail budget (no war top-up without an annual-review case); and the measurable consequences held by 0.1/0.2/0.5. The operator hazard has a name in the register: a geopolitical scare plus a Big Cycle bestseller is the doom-cult vector squared — the same countermeasures as the debt and gold lessons apply, doubled.

One more honest note from the market record: the "war puzzle" literature finds equity returns often held up through major wars — for the countries whose markets survived. That's survivorship's sharpest lesson, and it cuts against both the doom trade and complacency: the tail that matters isn't a bad year, it's a market that doesn't reopen. That tail is insurable (gold, the tail budget) and not timeable — which is this whole lesson in one sentence.
