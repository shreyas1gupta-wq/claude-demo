The Pass We Refused to Promote

The pre-election rally is one of Indian markets' favorite folk beliefs — and our pre-registered test PASSED it. This lesson is about what a disciplined desk does next: dissect the pass, find the window design flaw and the one global rebound carrying the mean, refuse promotion, and send the claim to the heuristics lane to be scored one election at a time. Plus the political-budget-cycle literature, and why India's fiscal signature is composition, not deficits.

PART 1

The folk claim meets its bar — and clears it

Before running anything: window = the three months ending in each general-election result month, n = 8 elections (1996–2024), pass bar = window mean above the unconditional mean AND at least 6 of 8 windows positive. The print:

The three deflators, in order of force. First: the registered window ENDS IN the result month — so May 2009's +33.6% result-day shock sits inside its own "pre-election" window. The design conflates drift with surprise; that flaw is fixed in a registered follow-up design (window ends the month before; world-market control), not by re-running today. Second: the 2009 window (+18.8%/m) is mostly the global post-crisis rebound wearing an election costume. Third: eight windows is an association, not an attribution. The pass stands in the ledger exactly as registered — AND it is refused promotion. Those two sentences coexisting is the whole discipline.
PART 2

What the windows are actually for

FP1b measured the part the ladder already uses: election windows are LOUD — median absolute monthly move 5.4% inside windows against 4.2% overall, and result months are violent in both directions (2004's −17.7% and 2009's +33.6% are the same phenomenon with opposite signs). That is why seat L5 schedules risk DOWN into these windows without holding any directional view: you can know when the coin will be flipped without knowing how it lands. The folk tilt, meanwhile, goes where suggestive-but-tiny evidence belongs — the heuristics lane, carded as HL-7, teach-only/paper-trade, scored forward one election at a time. The next data point arrives around 2029. The lane can wait; that is what it is for.

PART 3

India's real fiscal signature: composition

The political-budget-cycle literature predicts exactly what India's record shows — and it isn't bigger deficits before elections. Rogoff's signaling model says incumbents shift spending toward VISIBLE items; Shi-Svensson found the cycles are larger in developing democracies; Brender-Drazen found voters learn, so the crude version decays. India's exhibits: a transfer scheme announced in an interim budget weeks before the 2019 election; waiver waves around state elections; and, in the other direction, 2013-14's consolidation INTO an election under a ratings threat — the exception that kills any mechanical rule. The desk's harvest is a composition watch (transfer outlays vs capex share from the monthly accounts) as briefing context. The fiscal-dominance REGIME — the thing that could actually re-rate everything — already has its own seat in the debt monograph's L15, where it belongs.
