The Cycle That Globalized

One factor — dollar, risk appetite, US policy — moves risky assets and capital flows across the world, and it binds India regardless of exchange-rate regime. We measured its rise on our own panel: national equity markets' co-movement nearly TRIPLED across 1990. India loads on it at +0.57. And the one bar that failed taught the seat its most useful sentence: one cycle, in most places, most of the time — never "everywhere," never assumable in advance.

A standing caveat was born in GF1. If co-movement tripled across 1990, then every analogue result computed on pre-1990 panels — diversification benefits, insulation stories, crisis-transfer sizes — must be DISCOUNTED when transferred to today's India. That caveat now travels with every cross-era claim in the register. And GF3's honest miss (69% median breadth vs a 75% bar) bounds the other side: partial insulation is real ex post — India in 2015-16 and 2022 partially proved it — but the desk never gets to assume it ex ante. That asymmetry IS the seat.
PART 2

Down years: how broad is a global down?

The seat that consumes all this, L9, is the ladder's fastest macro seat (episode half-life 3–9 months — episodes, not eras): a dollar/VIX/US-real-rate state that conditions the SIZE of the India book, with Kilian-decomposed oil inside it and FPI flows as confirm-only (flows follow returns — that finding has its own atlas row). What it refuses is the standard menu: Fed forecasting, flow-momentum trading, decoupling narratives, and dollar-cycle dating.

PART 3

Nine episodes, one growing transfer

The case record walks nine global episodes from the 1994 Fed shock to the current regime, and the through-line is the TRANSFER GROWING as India's capital account opened: barely a ripple in 1994; partial insulation in 1997-98 behind an unconvertible rupee; the full arrival in 2008 (rupee 39→52, equity −60%); the 2013 taper tantrum as the canonical anatomy; the double cycle of 2018 (global QT × domestic IL&FS); 2020's fastest-stop-and-fastest- reflation; the 2022 record FPI exodus met, for once, with relative equity resilience. The atlas names the May-2026 INR episode as L9's live test case, and the grade is honest: the rupee hit a record ~96.8 on a tariff overhang, FPI outflows and an oil spike — while the VIX/dollar/US-rate triad was QUIET going in. The classic global-risk signature did not cover a tariff-and-oil-driven INR event; that coverage gap is now written into the seat's file and routed to its oil conditioner and a registered re-grade. A seat with a named, dated, public exam — including the parts it missed — is a seat that cannot quietly fail.
