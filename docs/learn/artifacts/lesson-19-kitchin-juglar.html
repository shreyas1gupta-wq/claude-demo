Five Clocks, Zero Survivors

The 40-month Kitchin inventory cycle and the 7-to-11-year Juglar investment cycle — the two oldest names in cycle taxonomy — close out the project's clock hunt. PART 1

Kitchin on Kitchin's data

Joseph Kitchin measured bank clearings, commodity prices and interest rates from 1890 to 1922 and reported a ~40-month rhythm. The desk holds his variable class at monthly cadence — 58 years of floating-era gold and the 37-year IMF all-commodity index — so the claim gets a direct test: expanding-gap states, local maxima, spacings, with the pass window [30, 50] months registered before the run.

PART 3

What survives the funerals

bullwhip

Kitchin's MECHANISM — demand-signal distortion amplifying up supply chains — is alive; 2021-24 (glut, freight recession, restocking) is a live specimen. It enters as CONTEXT: global PMI inventory waves read through L9's existing inputs, zero new budget.

Juglar's object

His 7-11-year credit-investment cycle was the FIRST empirically dated cycle (1862) and the ancestor of the modern credit-cycle view. The object is already seated — L10 credit, L11 capex, one shared budget. The fold retires the NAME so "a Juglar signal" can never re-enter the register as new alpha.

India design

The inventory wedge (IIP vs sales proxies, PMI stocks) waits as a registered runsheet design with a stated expectation of weakness — the atlas's own "weakly measurable in India" prior, kept honest.

the discipline

Retiring names while keeping objects is register hygiene, not dismissal: every retired label carries its failing print, and every kept mechanism carries its seat address. Nothing is silently dropped; nothing gets to wear two names.
