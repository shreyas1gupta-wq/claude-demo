PART 1

Cheapness, from zero

Value investing is one sentence: pay less than a business is worth. The quant version replaces "worth" with an accounting anchor — book value, earnings, cash flow — and asks: do stocks that are cheap relative to their anchor beat expensive ones? Across nearly a century (Basu 1977, Fama-French 1992), the answer has been yes. Two schools explain it, and the fight matters: the risk school says cheap stocks are cheap because they're genuinely scarier (so the premium is payment for pain); the error school (Lakonishok-Shleifer-Vishny) says investors extrapolate glamour growth too far and flee boring franchises — and the premium is their mistake, harvested. Our design refuses to pick a winner: it's built to survive under either. What both schools agree on: the return comes from convergence — the price migrating back toward the anchor (Fama-French's own decomposition) — which takes years, and that single word is this lesson's villain and hero.

The one mechanical trap, demonstrated in our tests

Fundamentals arrive LATE. A March book value isn't public until May-June. Compute B/P with data the market hadn't seen and your backtest cheats — our test suite proves it: the "no-lag" version beats the honest version on every random seed we planted. In India the lag structure is harsher still: balance sheets are half-yearly at best (Part 5).

PART 2

The winters — value's defining property

Value's problem isn't the average; it's the path. The strategy hibernates — for years — and every hibernation produces the same two funerals: capital that quits at the bottom, and researchers who redefine the factor to fit the recent past. Here are the winters, measured by us as drawdowns of the long-short value spread:

India's own value winter — confirmed in our numbers
2015–2019: Indian value earned +0.8%/yr (Sharpe −0.39 vs the risk-free rate). Post-2020: +18.8%/yr, Sharpe 0.82.

The mania and the thaw, both in the data we hold. A desk that had abandoned value in 2019 — as many did — sold the insurance the day before the payout. A desk that had doubled down in 2016 bled for four years first. The design's answer is neither: fixed sleeve weights inside frozen caps, with the value-SPREAD state (how stretched cheap-vs-expensive pricing is) conditioning patience — a state we compute, in Part 6's designs, never a timing trade.

PART 3

The pairing — why value sits next to momentum

Here is the arithmetic that makes two flawed strategies into one good book. If two return streams have Sharpe ratios S₁ and S₂ and correlation ρ, an equal split earns (S₁+S₂)/√(2(1+ρ)) — and when ρ is negative, the denominator shrinks: the combination beats both parts. Value and momentum are natural opposites (value buys what has fallen toward its anchor; momentum buys what has recently risen), so ρ should be negative. Is it?

PART 4

Quality — the other half of the pair

Quality asks a different question: not "is it cheap?" but "is it good?" — profitable (Novy-Marx: gross profits over assets, the cleanest measure), honestly profitable (Sloan: cash earnings persist, accrual-stuffed earnings reverse), conservatively financed, and governed for shareholders. Good firms outperform more than their riskiness justifies, plausibly because big investors who cannot use leverage reach for exciting lottery stocks instead and leave boring excellence underpriced. Quality's practical role in our book is double: a return sleeve, and a floor that binds in late-boom regimes (junk rallies at the top — the credit lesson's composition input, wearing equity clothes). In India, quality has teeth the textbooks don't mention: promoter pledge percentages, auditor resignations, and rating actions are free, dated, and brutal — they enter as reduce-only demotions to a name's quality rank, the same Tier-C clamp arithmetic you saw in the credit composite.

PART 5

India's data problem — the honest constraint

Everything above needs fundamentals, and Indian fundamentals have a shape most quant literature ignores: quarterly filings carry the P&L only; balance sheets and cash-flow statements arrive half-yearly or annually. So B/P steps like a staircase, accruals are an annual signal riding in a quarterly book, and only 2 of Piotroski's 9 quality checks are genuinely quarterly-computable here. The monograph's Part C builds the full machine anyway — announcement-dated XBRL filings (mandatory since 2017) as the point-in-time store, banks parsed separately under their own statute, aggregator websites ruled exploration-only — and it sets the two validation gates our constructed ratios must pass before anything trades: tracking the academic factor library within tolerance, and matching NSE's own daily-published index P/E and P/B. Two new pullers went straight onto your machine's runsheet.
