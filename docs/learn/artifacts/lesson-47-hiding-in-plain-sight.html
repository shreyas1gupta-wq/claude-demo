Four Trials Hiding in Plain Sight

Two days after declaring the mirror frontier exhausted, a harder re-read of the register found four registered designs that never needed new data at all — they needed someone to notice the vaults already covered them. One afternoon later: the vol-managed lure is priced, a third confirmation input is refused, the insurance premium is decomposed into its true parts, and the expiry day turns out to be an ordinary Thursday. Twenty-seven pre-registered cells, four sets of priors graded in public.

PART 1

The vol-managed lure, priced (F3a)

Moreira-Muir volatility scaling is the literature's most seductive free lunch, and on the NIFTY it seduces on schedule: full-period, the scaled strategy prints +5.76%/yr of alpha with max drawdown cut from 55% to 26%. Then the two registered disciplines take turns. Cederburg's real-time protocol — estimate the scaling constant only from data you had — erases the Sharpe improvement entirely (0.60 vs 0.62; the return difference's interval spans zero), confirming on India what it showed in the US: the full-period result is hindsight wearing a formula. And the desk-feasible version (no leverage — the funding decision is still dark) delivered a genuine surprise against the registered prior: max drawdown 22%, BETTER than the fast-trigger F2 survivors — at −4.9%/yr of net drag, roughly double their premium. The prior missed and the miss is booked; the doctrine it sharpened: continuity is a more expensive seatbelt than speed.

PART 2

The third echo (F4a)

Cross-sectional correlation spikes are the crowd's favorite crash thermometer, so F4 asked the register's standard question: does the mean pairwise correlation of the top-50 names tell the stress machinery anything realized volatility doesn't? On the survivor panel (one-way reading declared: a failure to add on flattered data leans decisive), the answer is no at both windows — not rank-redundant, but a WORSE episode classifier than RV (AUROC 0.716 and 0.663 against ~0.75). That makes three offered confirmation inputs — cross-sectional correlation, domestic implied vol, global implied vol — each measured and each refused. The fast-stress composite keeps its realized-vol spine three-for-three, and the burden of proof for any fourth candidate is now visibly higher.

PART 3

Where the premium actually lives (F6a)

The walk-forward had already priced always-on crash insurance at 2.5-4pp/yr in whipsaw eras. The false-fire ledger decomposed that bill across all eighteen grid cells: the TRANSACTION cost of whipsaws — firing, paying the round trip, re-entering — is 0.02 to 0.28%/yr at index-futures rates. A rounding error. The premium is almost entirely EXPOSURE drag: the price of standing half-out while the market rises. That reframes the principal's open decision B3-1 — the budget being set is an exposure-drag budget, and transaction cost needs no line at this scale. False-fire discipline behaved exactly as registered (58% of fires false at the loosest cell, 0% at the tightest), and the one registered prior that could not be confirmed was recorded rather than rescued: with the GFC hidden in percentile warm-up, only COVID could test whether slow cells miss deep episodes — and even the slowest cell caught it. One episode is not a verdict; it is an n of one, and the ledger says so.

PART 4

An ordinary Thursday (H58-D3a), and the queue's honest end

The calendar-mechanics block always insisted its expiry-day exclusion was mechanical prudence, not alpha. The index partial agreed with unusual grace: 224 monthly expiry days against 702 other Thursdays, median absolute moves 0.67% vs 0.57%, p=0.21 — an ordinary Thursday. The rule stays because its true object (single-stock close-auction mechanics) still waits on the point-in-time data, but nothing at index level owes it justification. With that, the rediscovered queue is spent for real this time: every F-design and every ops design now has either a full run, a booked partial, or a data gate with its bars already written. The lesson under the lesson: "the frontier is exhausted" is itself a claim that wants re-testing — this batch existed because the desk re-read its own register with yesterday's certainty treated as today's hypothesis.
