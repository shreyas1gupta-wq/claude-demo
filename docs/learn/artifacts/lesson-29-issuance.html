The Signal That Shouldn't Decay

Nearly every edge in this project carries the same epitaph-in-waiting: alpha decays. The issuance cycle is the register's rare exception by construction — issuers sell paper when it's expensive because it pays them to, and nobody can arbitrage a promoter's decision not to float. India's primary market has top-ticked the secondary market four times in thirty years, a regulator has confirmed the froth institutionally, and the seat now has its machinery: two legs, volume and reception, both required for the froth signature.

PART 1

An incentive, not an information gap

Baker and Wurgler's finding — the share of equity in total new issues predicts market returns — is usually filed under anomalies. The desk files it under INCENTIVES: a promoter deciding when to sell a slice of their company is the cleanest insider trade in finance, it is perfectly legal, and there is no mechanism by which arbitrageurs can make issuers stop timing their own sales. That is why L7's decay prior differs from every other seat's: the haircut applies to the magnitude, never the existence. The honest counters ride along — pseudo-market-timing (heavy issuance mechanically clusters after run-ups) and the post-publication record of the US predictor — which is why the seat is a reduce-only confirm, not a timing engine.

PART 2

Four top-ticks and a regulator

The case record walks India's waves: the 1992-96 free-pricing flood that ended in vanishing companies; the 2004-08 great wave crowned by a 73-times-subscribed mega-IPO listing within days of the all-time top; the 2021 startup wave whose record raise fell 27% on listing day; and the 2023-25 SME frenzy that ran until SEBI stepped in — the ladder cites that intervention as the institutional confirmation, because a regulator acting on primary- market froth IS a froth reading from the one participant with the full book. Each wave's aftermath asks the Baker-Wurgler question — did heavy issuance precede weak returns? — and the record's answer, wave by wave, is the case chapter's scorecard. India adds a reception variable most markets lack: subscription books published in real time, by investor class.

PART 3

What the seat refuses

no shorts

Froth is a SIZING state (shrink the special-sits sleeve, confirm the valuation read with L8) — never a short signal. Frothy markets outlast solvency; the interface has no short path by construction.

no SME pollution

SME-board microstructure (tiny floats, circuit games) is kept as a satellite series — its 2023-25 statistics would have blinded a merged state.

no vehicle blindness

The next wave may arrive through vehicles that don't exist yet (REITs did; SME boards did) — the calendar scraper's category audit is annual, because the fragile node moves here too.

the gated tests

IS1 (the India Baker-Wurgler test, prior stated in the ledger) and IS2 (the ladder's own named validation episodes: 2018's freeze, 2023-24's frenzy) run when the primary-market vault lands — bars filled at the data, per the two-pass rule.
