The Seat Defined by What It Refuses

"FIIs are buying" is India's most-marketed market signal — and the evidence says flows FOLLOW returns, so the desk rejects flow momentum outright and enforces the rejection in code: the module has no flow output to misuse. What survives is slower and stranger: foreign ownership as a measure of exit crowding, flagged only at high extremes, only to reduce risk. This lesson teaches the exclusion, the capacity mechanism, and the structural change that may be blunting it.

PART 1

Flows follow returns — and the interface knows it

Across emerging markets, foreign flows chase past returns far more than they predict future ones (Griffin-Nardari-Stulz's daily-flow VARs; the India-specific confirmations). The desk's planted-truth fixture makes the direction visible: an economy where flows are built to chase lagged returns prints a follow-correlation of +0.97 while the lead-correlation sits at zero — and the test suite verifies the seat's module cannot even EXPRESS a flow-momentum signal, because no flow-named function exists in its interface. The atlas's §7 rejection is enforced structurally, not just registered.

PART 2

The capacity mechanism, and its denominator

Why do positioning EXTREMES survive when flow momentum dies? Because an extreme foreign ownership position is a crowded exit: unwinding it takes real capital and real time, and in a risk-off episode everyone reaches the door together (fire-sale economics — Coval-Stafford, Greenwood-Thesmar fragility). That is REGIME information — it conditions how bad a bad episode can get — not alpha. Two design details carry the honesty: the denominator is FREE FLOAT (raw ownership shares mislead in a market where promoters hold half the register), and the flag is asymmetric by mechanism — low ownership is information about absence, not about forced selling, so the seat stays silent there. Everything is Tier C and reduce-only; the seat's own India numbers (FL1/FL2) are registered designs awaiting the shareholding-pattern and NSDL vaults, with roughly twenty years of quarterly ranks needed before an "extreme" means anything.

PART 3

The structural question the record poses

The case record's five eras end on a genuine open question. In FY2022, foreign investors sold a record sum of Indian equity — and the market absorbed it with only a modest drawdown, because a domestic bid (the SIP culture, insurance and pension flows) had grown deep enough to take the other side. The capacity mechanism assumes the exit door's width is fixed; India may be widening the door. The desk's read: the mechanism is not dead — a crowded exit still costs something — but its CONSEQUENCE SIZING now carries a domestic-counterweight annotation, and each future unwind episode is one more observation on whether the flag's bite is fading. That question arrives on the market's schedule, not ours; the seat waits at its clamp, which is what Tier-C seats are for.
