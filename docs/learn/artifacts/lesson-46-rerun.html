The Re-run

A research register makes a quiet, testable promise: everything booked can be re-derived by a stranger from what is committed. Most programs never collect on that promise — the numbers sit in documents, the scripts drift, and nobody checks until a dispute forces it. With the mirror frontier exhausted and no new data to chase, the desk spent the day collecting: every analysis script re-run cold, every headline number re-checked against its booked value, every honesty flag re-examined for what the new vaults could now resolve.

PART 1

Fifty scripts, cold

The protocol was the stranger's protocol: check out the repo, point PYTHONPATH at it, run every analyze_*.py with a timeout, capture everything. Forty-eight ran clean immediately; the two that "failed" refuse by design to run without a --demo flag (their real-data modes are honestly unimplemented, waiting on gated data) and pass with it. Then the numbers: twenty-nine booked headline values — the budget-day p-values, the reversal freeze's net drag, the walk-forward's era verdicts, both half-life estimators, all of this week's prints — grepped against the fresh logs. All twenty-nine reproduce. The stochastic ones reproduce exactly, because the house rule that every bootstrap and Monte Carlo states its seed was never a formality; it is why the ledger's numbers are re-derivable at all.

PART 2

What the re-run nearly destroyed

The audit's sharpest finding was about the record itself. Many RESULTS files are written by their scripts and then extended BY HAND with the "honest read" — the interpretation the discipline requires be written only after the print. Re-running those scripts regenerates the computed part and silently deletes the hand-written part: the numbers survive, the meaning is destroyed. Nineteen files were affected; every diff was checked — the computed content reproduced byte-identically in all of them — and every file was restored from the committed record. That became process note #7: reproduction is run-diff-restore, never run-and-keep, because the record is prints PLUS the appended interpretation, and only one of those regenerates. The lone semantic divergence found anywhere: one script prints a sample-end month as a "recovery" date for a drawdown still open — and the committed file already carries the hand-corrected reading. The record was righter than its own generator.

PART 3

Flags that the new vaults could close

The sweep also re-read every [VERIFY] flag the corpus carries. Most are literature honesty markers that only primary sources can close, and they stay. Two were closable by computation against this week's vaults, and were: the climate SST file's uncertain region is now CONFIRMED as Niño 1+2 — its mean level (23.1°C) excludes region 3.4 outright, its 5.7°C seasonal amplitude is the 1+2 signature, and its anomalies correlate 0.664 with the vaulted ONI, exactly the documented cross-region relationship. And the metal-production units flag resolved from the file's own committed provenance (thousand tonnes for metals, tonnes for gold — the reading the original pass used). Neither changed a number; both changed a flag into a fact, which is the entire point of carrying flags.

PART 4

Why this is the right last leg

The mirror frontier closed yesterday: every remote-reachable series is vaulted to its limit, the dead ends are probed and recorded, and the next data event belongs to the principal's machine. A program at that boundary has exactly one high-value move left that costs no new degrees of freedom: prove that what it claims, it can re-derive. It can. The audit now lives in the register (REPRODUCIBILITY.md), re-runs on the annual loop, and the 136-cell census it protects goes into the data phase with its numbers not merely booked but demonstrated re-derivable. When the PIT bhavcopy lands and the full F2 runs, it will run on a codebase that has already survived the stranger's test.
