PART 2

The null that teaches more than a number would

We ran our bias-corrected persistence machinery on the log real gold price expecting to measure how fast departures close. The estimator answered: ρ = 1.000 — on a century of annual data, real gold is statistically indistinguishable from a random walk. (Our third logged falsification of a pre-written sentence; the register keeps them all.) Three independent approaches — Jastram's centuries, Erb-Harvey's regressions, our AR(1) — agree on the direction of reversion and none can pin its speed. So the anchor is real and utterly unusable for timing: exactly why gold in our book is sized as insurance you can carry for twenty-two wrong-looking years, never as a bet on the anchor pulling prices home.

The rejected uses, named: timing entries off "gold is cheap/dear vs the constant"; trading inflation prints (gold's short-horizon inflation beta is unreliable at every horizon tested); doom-allocation past the ceiling. What survives: the ~20–25% ceiling (far under the frozen 50% cap), the L15 insurance floor from Lessons 5–6, and India's specific legs — the INR depreciation wedge, the duty-regime breaks, and the 1962–1990 Gold Control Act as the domestic reminder that confiscation tails are also part of gold's history, on both sides.
