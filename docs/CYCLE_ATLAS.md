## 0. How to read this (the five-minute epistemics course)

**Persistence, not periodicity.** Almost nothing in markets is a clock. Slutzky (1937) showed
that merely *smoothing random noise* manufactures beautiful-looking waves; Granger (1966) showed
macro data has no spectral peak at any "cycle length" — just persistence that fades. So the
honest object is not "an 8-year cycle" but *a state variable that mean-reverts with a half-life*
(`τ½`). The **clock test**: only a phenomenon with ≥4 observed complete periods may even be
called a cycle; India's post-1991 history gives almost nothing 4 full periods above the annual
band. Consequence: long "cycles" tell you *where you probably are*, never *when it turns* —
their timing uncertainty is ±20% or worse, and news can compress or stretch any phase.

**The four harvests.** Every entry below is tagged by what we take from it:
- **REGIME** — risk permission: how much leverage, hedge, concentration the state allows.
  This is where cycles earn their keep (prior #3: the cycle stack is the risk system, worth
  only ~100–300bps/yr directly — it buys the *right to run* the return engines).
- **EDGE** — direct alpha: a return source with a decay-survival argument and a stated haircut.
  Few cycles qualify; most "cycle alpha" is regime permission wearing a costume.
- **CONTEXT** — interpretation with zero allocation authority: it shapes how we read other
  signals but never moves a rupee by itself.
- **EXECUTION** — an operational rule (when *not* to trade, what to exclude from a trigger).
- **REJECT** — examined and excluded, with the reason written down so it stays excluded.

**Tier discipline** (Contract §4): A = ≥30 independent observations; B = 4–30 (or <4 with ≥10
cross-country analogues), parameters frozen at inception; C = narrative, may only REDUCE risk.

**Learning note (what would fool you):** every Band-0 entry *looks* like it has 4+ observations
if you count sub-periods mechanically — but each "period" ran under a different monetary regime
(gold standard vs fiat, closed vs open capital account), so the realizations are not draws from
one process. That is why the design refuses Tier-B promotions here even where n nominally ≥4
(D07 §5's explicit rejection of a technical promotion for Schmelzing's series).

**Learning note:** Band 2 is where the design's one real "free lunch" lives — not prediction, but
*permission asymmetry*. A credit boom doesn't tell you when the bust comes (±years), but it tells
you the **price of being levered and concentrated has gone up**. Cutting leverage into a maturing
boom costs a little upside with certainty and avoids a catastrophic path with some probability —
under a binding 30–35% drawdown ceiling, that trade is bought every time. That is what "cycles
are the risk system" means in practice.
