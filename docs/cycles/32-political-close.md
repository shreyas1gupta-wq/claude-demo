# PART A + B + C — The calendar's fold, the reform wave's new content, the two rejects, and Band
# 3's closing ledger

## A. The India political/reform cycle (3.7): what folds and what's new

Atlas entry 3.7 packs two genuinely different objects under one name, and separating them is this
section's entire job. The first object — *when* does a general election happen, and how loud is
the market around that date — is a scheduling question with a hard, public, five-yearly answer
(Article 83(2) of the Constitution caps the Lok Sabha's term at five years) and a `config/
ladder.yaml` entry, **L5_calendar_windows**, that has already priced it. The second object — *what
does a government, once elected, actually do to the economy over the following years* — is a
different question on a different frequency, has no seat in this program's ladder at all, and is
this entry's own contribution: a reform-wave record, read as context, never as a timing signal.
Both belong in atlas row 3.7 because both are keyed to the same five-yearly electoral clock; only
one of them is a state variable this ladder can act on.

### A.1 The fold: election-window scheduling is L5's job, already seated

`config/ladder.yaml`'s **L5_calendar_windows** entry (tau_half [1,2] months, Tier B, `reduce_only:
true`, block `calendar`) already does exactly what atlas row 3.7 asks of it: schedule
leverage/volatility permission around election (and budget) windows, with its own provenance
citing "election n=8–9, budget n>>30 timing only; direction is surprise (2004/2009/2024) (D08)."
Nothing in this chapter widens that mandate, and two of this program's own completed, pre-
registered trials confirm precisely why not, cited here rather than recomputed.

**FP1a/FP1b** (`docs/cycles/21-fiscal-cycle.md` Part B.3, cross-referenced not re-derived): testing
India's own factor data (IIMA monthly, 1993–2025) against the eight usable general-election result
months, the folk "pre-election rally" claim passed as registered (+3.99%/month in the three-month
pre-result window vs +0.72% all-months base, 7/8 positive) and was then dissected and refused
promotion — the registered window contains the result month itself (conflating drift with the
result-day surprise it should be measured against), one election (May 2009's global-rebound
window) carries the pooled mean, and n=8 is an association sample with no counterfactual control.
FP1b, the measurement leg that actually underwrites L5's design, found election windows genuinely
**loud** (median absolute monthly move 5.4% vs 4.2% all-months) with result-month moves ranging
from −17.7% to +33.6% on the factor series — volatility real, direction empty.

**PL1** (`political-RESULTS.md`, this program's own pre-registered follow-on, cited in full and
not recomputed here): does the pre-election window's own *sign* predict the result month's sign?
Measured, no bar set (n=8 is too small for one): the pre-window mean and the result-month return
agree in sign on **3 of 8** elections — at or below a coin flip. "Direction is surprise" is no
longer a slogan this program repeats; it is now a number this program measured and it says the
same thing FP1a's dissection already implied. Between FP1a/FP1b and PL1, this program has now run
the fold's entire converse test twice — once on the window's *level*, once on its *sign* — and
both runs land on the same conclusion from two different angles.

**The three surprise verdicts, verified for this chapter.** Atlas row 3.7 and `ladder.yaml`'s L5
provenance carry three numbers as their founding evidence, and this chapter re-verifies each
against independent press reporting rather than taking its own prior citation on faith, per
Contract §12.

- **17 May 2004 (result day, the "two-day fall").** Multiple independent sources confirm the BSE
  Sensex closed down **15.52%** that session — its largest single-session percentage fall on
  record — after the NDA's surprise defeat and the prospect of a Congress-led government dependent
  on Left-party support outside the government raised fears of a reform reversal [Verified via
  search]. The fall itself unfolded in **two circuit-breaker stages within that one Monday
  session**: the index hit the market-wide 10% band, halting trading for roughly an hour, then on
  reopening fell a further ~5 percentage points to hit the 15% band, triggering a second, longer
  halt (roughly two hours) — the **first time the Nifty circuit breaker had ever been triggered**
  in Indian market history [Verified via search]. This chapter's own read of the record: the
  "two-day" characterization this program's provenance line carries most plausibly refers to this
  two-*stage* circuit-breaker sequence inside one trading day, not to a decline spread across two
  separate calendar sessions — every independently verified source this chapter located places the
  entire 15.52% move inside the single 17 May session, with results having been declared four days
  earlier (13 May) and an intervening trading day showing no comparable move. **−15.52% ≈ this
  program's own −15.5%, confirmed** [Verified; the "two-day" label flagged and clarified rather
  than silently repeated — VERIFY if a primary NSE/BSE archive record describes it differently].
- **18 May 2009 (the upper-circuit day).** UPA's return with a much larger, Left-independent
  mandate produced the opposite shock. The Sensex and Nifty hit their **first-ever upper circuit**
  within roughly 30 seconds of the Monday opening, freezing trading for about an hour; on
  reopening the market immediately ran up into a **20% upper-circuit band**, and trading was
  suspended **for the remainder of the day** [Verified via search]. The session's own closing
  print — 2,110.79 points, to 14,284.21 — is a gain of roughly **17.3%** on the prior close, but
  this program's own provenance figure of "+10.7%-then-halt" describes the **first** circuit-level
  print (the 10% band-triggered freeze) rather than the day's final tally, an honest distinction
  this chapter preserves rather than collapses: the index moved in two upward stages exactly as it
  had moved in two downward stages five years earlier, hitting a lower circuit band first, then a
  higher one, with trading suspended before a normal, continuous close could ever be recorded
  [Verified via search: index levels, timing, and the day's ultimate suspension all independently
  corroborated across contemporary reporting; the precise same-day percentage at the first 10%
  freeze is this program's own already-carried figure, consistent with, not contradicted by, every
  source located this pass].
- **4 June 2024 (result day).** Exit polls on 1 June had implied a large NDA landslide and markets
  rallied hard into the result; the actual count showed the BJP short of a majority on its own,
  requiring coalition partners (TDP, JD(U)). The Sensex fell an intraday low of roughly **−7.4%**
  before closing down 4,390 points, **−5.74%**, at 72,079; the Nifty 50 closed down 1,379 points,
  **−5.93%**, at 21,885 [Verified via search] — this program's own "−5.9%" is confirmed almost to
  the decimal on the Nifty print.

**Conclusion: nothing new to seat.** Three independently verified, violent, opposite-signed
result-day moves (−15.5%, an upper-circuit halt, −5.9%), a dissected pass on the pre-election
window's level (FP1a), and a coin-flip on the pre-election window's sign (PL1) are, together, the
single cleanest confirmation this program's own register has produced for the design sentence L5
was seated on at inception: **election timing is law; election direction is surprise.** L5's
`reduce_only: true` scheduling mandate stands exactly as written. Nothing in this entry's own
evidence argues for widening it into a directional bet, and the two trials that could have argued
for widening it — a level test and a sign test — have both now been run and both refuse.

### A.2 The new content this entry owns: the reform-wave record as CONTEXT

What atlas row 3.7 does *not* already have a home for is the second object bundled into "political/
reform cycle": not the date of an election, but the multi-year *policy sequence* a government
enacts once in office. This is a different frequency (years, not a single volatility window), a
different mechanism (structural/institutional change to specific sectors, not a discrete political
surprise), and — critically — a different harvest: never a timing signal, because (per §A.4 below)
reform timing is itself politically stochastic, but a genuine, dated, sector-specific **context**
input the desk annotates as it unfolds. The harvest form this chapter proposes, matching this
program's existing composition-watch and sector-projection conventions (`docs/cycles/
21-fiscal-cycle.md` Part C's composition watch; atlas §14's sector-projection principle): **dated
wave cards** in the Stage-2 briefing layer — a short, dated, sourced note naming the wave, its
enacted measures, and the sectors it plausibly re-rates, carrying zero allocation authority of its
own and never promoted past a human analyst's own judgment. Four waves earn a card.

**1991 — the liberalization big bang.** P.V. Narasimha Rao became Prime Minister in June 1991
amid a balance-of-payments crisis (itself the fiscal-dominance/oil-shock episode this program's
L15 and D08's I11 already carry — cross-referenced, not re-derived) and appointed Dr. Manmohan
Singh as Finance Minister. The sequence that followed is dated precisely: the rupee was devalued
against major currencies by roughly **9% on 1 July 1991**, then a further **11% two days later**
— a deliberate two-step devaluation Rao judged more politically palatable than one large move
[Verified via search]; a mini trade-policy package followed on **4 July**; and on **24 July 1991**,
Manmohan Singh presented the 1991–92 Budget in Parliament — raising the corporate tax rate five
points to 45% and introducing tax-deducted-at-source on some financial transactions, while opening
mutual funds to private-sector participation and relaxing non-resident investment rules — on the
same day the Industry Ministry tabled the **New Industrial Policy Resolution**, which dismantled
the comprehensive industrial-licensing regime ("License Raj") at a stroke [Verified via search].
Together this is the New Economic Policy's own stated tripod — **Liberalization, Privatization,
Globalization** — and the sector-projection content a 2026 desk reads off it is not "buy on the
budget date" (nobody could have timed 24 July 1991 in advance any more than L5 can time a result
day) but a multi-year re-rating of Indian manufacturing, financial services, and any sector whose
prior binding constraint was licensing or a closed capital account — a re-rating this program's own
credit-cycle chronology (L10, atlas 2.1) dates its own first observable India cycle from, in the
years immediately following.

**1999–2004 — the NHDP/privatization era.** The Vajpayee government (NDA-I) launched the **National
Highways Development Project** in 1998–99, with its flagship **Golden Quadrilateral** phase
starting construction in 1999 [Verified via search] — the infrastructure-supercycle chronology
atlas row 1.6 and `docs/cycles/16-capex-cycle.md` already date their own "n≈2–3" India capex-cycle
count from (cross-referenced, not re-derived: this reform wave is the seed of that later capex
cycle, not a separate object). In parallel, a dedicated **Ministry of Disinvestment** was created
in 1999 under Arun Shourie, executing strategic sales of Videsh Sanchar Nigam Limited (VSNL),
Hindustan Zinc, BALCO, IPCL, several ITDC hotels, and Modern Food Industries — strategic sales
alone raising roughly **₹6,344 crore**, with total disinvestment proceeds across the NDA-I term
reported at roughly **₹37,000 crore** (1998–2004) [Verified via search]. The sector-projection
content: a highway-construction/cement/EPC re-rating that runs into and helps seed the 2003–08
capex/credit boom this program's own L10/L11 chronology already tracks, and a metals/telecom
privatization wave whose market-structure effects (VSNL's exit from a monopoly telecom-carriage
position, Hindustan Zinc's and BALCO's transition to private strategic control) outlasted the
specific NDA-I term by years — again, a multi-year sector story, not a date to trade.

**2014–2019 — the institutional-reform cluster, and the policy-shock outlier inside it.** Three
statutes define this wave's institutional core: the **Goods and Services Tax**, rolled out
nationally on **1 July 2017** after roughly seventeen years of political negotiation and repeated
missed deadlines dating to the early 2000s [Verified via search]; the **Insolvency and Bankruptcy
Code**, enacted in **2016**, giving India its first time-bound corporate-insolvency resolution
mechanism; and the **Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (RERA)**, imposing
disclosure and escrow discipline on a previously lightly regulated developer sector [Verified via
search, all three]. The government's own characterization frames GST and IBC as complementary
halves of one design — GST "ensures that growth is visible and fairly taxed... IBC ensures that
this growth remains disciplined... by enforcing accountability in the use of capital" [Verified
via search, PMO/Government of India source — noted explicitly as a government characterization,
not an independent academic finding, and read with that caveat] — and RERA sits alongside them as
the same transparency logic applied to real estate specifically. The sector-projection content:
formalized-sector consolidation at the expense of the informal sector (GST's compliance-cost
asymmetry), a multi-year re-rating of organized lenders and asset-reconstruction plays (IBC's
resolution machinery), and a re-rating of listed, RERA-compliant developers relative to
unregistered competitors — three genuinely multi-year sector stories, still readable in listed-
company fundamentals a decade on.

**Demonetization (8 November 2016) is deliberately excluded from this cluster, and the exclusion
is the lesson.** At 8:15pm IST on 8 November 2016, the government announced the immediate
demonetization of all ₹500 and ₹1,000 banknotes, effective from midnight the same night [Verified
via search] — a single, unscheduled, overnight monetary-and-liquidity shock, not a multi-year
institutional build. Its market and macro transmission ran through this program's **fast-stress
(L2)** and **liquidity** channels (a sudden, economy-wide cash-in-circulation contraction) rather
than through any sector-repricing mechanism a reform wave produces — the honest distinction this
subsection exists to draw: a policy **shock** is dated, discrete, and (by definition) surprises
markets on a single day much the way an election result does; a reform **wave** is a multi-year
sequence a desk can only annotate once several of its pieces are already visible. Filing
demonetization under "2014–2019 reform wave" would be exactly the kind of label-shopping error this
program's own atlas §14 and the Kitchin/Juglar monograph's closing argument both warn against
(`research/cycles/kitchin-juglar/partAB-theory-cases.md` §B.3) — a genuine event mistaken for the
wrong object because it shares a decade with three genuine institutional reforms.

**2019–2026 — the tax-and-manufacturing-incentive era, current as of this writing.** On **20
September 2019**, Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman announced, via the Taxation Laws (Amendment)
Ordinance, 2019, a cut in the base domestic corporate tax rate from 30% to **22%** (effective
~25.17% with surcharge/cess), with a **15%** rate for new domestic manufacturing companies — a
$20.5bn-scale stimulus explicitly framed as a bid to revive slowing private investment [Verified
via search]. The **Production-Linked Incentive (PLI)** scheme followed, conceived and first
launched in March–April 2020 across three sectors (mobile manufacturing/electronics, pharmaceutical
APIs, medical devices) and expanded through November 2020 and after to **14 sectors** with a
combined outlay of roughly **₹1.97 lakh crore** [Verified via search] — automobiles and auto
components, electronics/IT hardware, telecom, pharmaceuticals, solar modules, metals, textiles,
white goods, drones, and advanced-chemistry-cell batteries among them. **The 2024–26 reform
agenda, current as of this document's date and explicitly flagged for refresh** [VERIFY: current
items — this wave is still unfolding and any list risks going stale within months]: a two-slab
**GST 2.0** rationalization (5%/18%) implemented through 2025, with the government's own reporting
citing the taxpayer base expanding past 1.5 crore and FY2024–25 collections of roughly ₹22.08 lakh
crore; four consolidated **Labour Codes** (wages, industrial relations, social security, worker
safety — replacing 29 prior statutes) brought into force on **21 November 2025**, which the
government's own release characterizes as India's "most significant structural reform since 1991"
[Verified via search — again, a government characterization stated as such, not adopted as this
desk's own independent judgment]; a new **Income Tax Act, 2025**, replacing the 1961 Act, alongside
a raised no-tax threshold to ₹12 lakh/year; and **100% FDI permitted in Indian insurance companies**
[Verified via search, all items]. The sector-projection content for this wave is, honestly, less
settled than the three earlier ones precisely because it is still in progress: manufacturing
sectors named under PLI (electronics, autos, solar, batteries) carry an explicit multi-year policy
tailwind; GST 2.0's rate compression plausibly favors consumption-facing sectors paying the lower
slab; the labour-code consolidation's sector effects (organized manufacturing compliance costs,
gig/platform-worker social-security extension) are, as of this writing, too new to have a dated
market record — a wave card opened, not yet closed.

### A.3 What a reform wave does that an election date doesn't

The contrast with §A.1 is the entire point of separating these two objects under one atlas row. An
election result is a single, dated, mean-reversion-free volatility event — FP1a/FP1b and PL1
together show a loud, direction-empty one-day-to-one-month object, exhausted by a scheduling rule.
A reform wave is the opposite shape on every axis: it has no single date (GST took seventeen years
from first proposal to launch; the 1991 sequence itself ran across a three-week span, not one
session), its market effect is not a volatility spike but a **multi-year repricing of specific
sectors** (organized lenders after IBC, manufacturing after PLI, developers after RERA — effects
still legible in listed-company fundamentals years after the enacting date), and — the harvest
consequence — it cannot be reduced to a leverage/vol-scheduling rule the way an election date can,
because there is no fixed clock to schedule around. What it *can* be is exactly what §A.2's wave
cards are: a sourced, dated, sector-tagged context note for Stage 2's human judgment layer, read
alongside the desk's existing sector-projection conventions (atlas §14's own finding that "almost
every named 'sector cycle' is a projection of states Part I already holds, onto one sector" applies
here too — a reform wave's sector effects run through, and are eventually absorbed by, the ordinary
factor/credit/capex machinery this program already carries, not through a bespoke reform-timing
signal of their own).

### A.4 The honest verdict: reform timing is politically stochastic

The four wave cards in §A.2 share one structural property worth stating plainly, because it is the
reason this content stops at CONTEXT and never becomes a Stage-1 signal: **none of them was
forecastable from the political calendar.** GST's own seventeen-year gestation is the clearest
proof — a reform that was politically *known* to be under discussion for a generation, and whose
actual launch date remained unpredictable until the year before it happened. Demonetization was a
surprise to markets and, by most contemporary accounts, to most of the sitting cabinet outside a
small circle. The September 2019 corporate tax cut moved markets precisely *because* it was
unannounced and unanticipated — a genuine surprise stimulus, not a scheduled event. A reform wave,
in other words, fails the same test an election's *direction* fails (§A.1's PL1 finding), for a
related reason: whether, and when, a government spends its political capital on a specific
structural reform is a function of coalition arithmetic, bureaucratic readiness, and opportunistic
timing this desk has no comparative advantage in forecasting, and no free, high-frequency data
series exists that would let it try under Contract §7's free-data rule. The desk's discipline is
therefore identical to L5's own: **annotate reform waves once several of their pieces are already
visible; never attempt to forecast one before it lands.** This is the same asymmetry the atlas
already enforces for Tier-C narrative content generally, applied here even though this entry
carries no allocation-reduce mandate of its own — it is Stage-2 sector-projection context, full
stop, with the same zero-allocation-authority status atlas §0 defines for CONTEXT and nothing more.

## B. The two rejects (3.8, 3.9), each argued properly then buried

### B.1 State-election midterm pulses (3.8): too diffuse to price

Atlas row 3.8 states the verdict in one line — "too diffuse to price; spending pulses fold into
2.7" — and this subsection earns that line rather than repeating it.

**The staggered calendar.** India's 28 states and 8 union territories each run five-year assembly
terms, but their start dates are staggered across the calendar rather than synchronized to the
Lok Sabha's own cycle, so **some state goes to the polls in almost every year** rather than in a
clean five-yearly rhythm. The recent record illustrates the point concretely: a five-state round
(Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Rajasthan, Telangana, Mizoram) in November–December 2023 — widely
read as the "semifinal" ahead of the 2024 general election, and the round this program's own
fiscal-cycle chapter already dates the Madhya Pradesh Ladli Behna cash-transfer launch inside
(`docs/cycles/21-fiscal-cycle.md` Part B.2) — was followed by Maharashtra and Jharkhand in
November 2024, Delhi in February 2025, Bihar in 2025, and a further five-state round (West Bengal,
Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Assam, Puducherry) in April–May 2026 [Verified via search] — a cadence that
confirms "staggered, continuous" as a description of the calendar itself, not merely of this
entry's own framing of it.

**The tiny per-event national-market footprint.** Secondary academic and practitioner sources
converge on the same shape, though this chapter flags the underlying papers' exact findings for
independent confirmation rather than treating a search-engine summary as a verified figure: a
2025 SAGE-published study on India's general-election volatility ("Electoral Shockwaves,"
Gayathri & Sophia, 2025 [VERIFY: full citation/findings — title and authors located, complete
methodology not independently confirmed this pass]) and separate assembly-election-focused papers
located this pass (JETIR/IJRAR working papers on Indian assembly-election stock-market impact
[VERIFY: exact authors, venues, and effect sizes — not independently confirmed]) each describe
state-level election reactions as short-lived — "for a day or two" in one practitioner summary —
rather than as a durable repricing. The live, current test case: West Bengal's April–May 2026
assembly election produced the BJP's first-ever win in that state, a genuinely historic and
largely unanticipated result by the state's own long electoral history — and yet contemporary
coverage describes the national market reaction as short-lived, with "traders... back to normalcy
thanks to feeble global cues" within the same trading window [Verified via search]. A result this
unexpected, at this scale, inside one of India's largest states, still failed to produce anything
resembling the 2004/2009/2024 general-election shocks §A.1 verified.

**Why the mechanism itself predicts this, not merely the data.** National economic policy
authority — fiscal policy, monetary policy, most taxation and regulatory law, and every reform this
chapter's §A.2 catalogs — sits with the Union government regardless of which party wins any single
state's assembly. A nationally diversified, earnings-weighted equity index (Nifty 500/750) has no
geographic revenue concentration in any one state comparable to the concentration a national
government's own mandate carries over the whole economy; there is no structural channel by which
one state's assembly result should move an index the way a Lok Sabha result does, and the record
in §A.1 and in this subsection is exactly what that reasoning predicts.

**The spending content is already homed, and no separate 3.8 trial was run.** The composition-cycle
content genuinely present in state elections — farm-loan waivers (Uttar Pradesh ₹36,000 crore and
Maharashtra ₹34,020 crore in 2017, Karnataka ~₹8,100 crore the same year), the Ladli Behna (Madhya
Pradesh, March 2023) and Ladki Bahin (Maharashtra, August 2024) cash-transfer schemes — is verified,
dated, and fully argued in `docs/cycles/21-fiscal-cycle.md` Part B.2, cross-referenced and never
re-derived here; that chapter's own verdict (CONTEXT folded into L5/L6, atlas entry 2.7) already
absorbs the spending-composition half of "state-election midterm pulses." What remains for this
entry, once that half is subtracted, is only the question this subsection has just answered — does
a state result *itself* move the national market — and the desk states honestly that no dedicated
pre-registered trial (comparable to FP1a/FP1b/PL1) was run to test this directly: the diffuseness
argument here rests on the staggered-calendar fact, the mechanism argument above, and the secondary
literature and live 2026 case cited, not on a desk-computed effect size. This is stated as a limit
of this chapter's own evidence, not papered over.

**Verdict: REJECT as signal.** State-election results carry no seat as a directional or
volatility-scheduling input in their own right. The one residue this chapter grants, matching the
atlas's own "CONTEXT at most": where a mega-state cluster's results coincide with, or immediately
precede, a national-election window L5 already schedules around — the November 2023 five-state
round ahead of the 2024 general election is exactly this case — the cluster is retained as a
calendar-awareness row inside L5's own scheduling table, never as an independent trigger, and never
carrying its own vol-scheduling authority the way the general election itself does.

### B.2 The US presidential cycle (3.9): the label may not re-enter via costume

**The puzzle, in its strongest form.** Santa-Clara & Valkanov, "The Presidential Puzzle: Political
Cycles and the Stock Market," *Journal of Finance* 58(5) (2003): 1841–1872 [Verified via search].
Using US monthly data from 1927–1998, the paper finds excess stock-market returns significantly
**higher under Democratic than Republican presidencies** — roughly **9 percentage points a year**
for the value-weighted market portfolio and **16 percentage points a year** for the equal-weighted
portfolio [Verified via search] — a spread the authors show is **not explained by business-cycle
risk proxies**, is **not concentrated around election dates themselves**, and is, if anything,
associated with *lower* realized volatility under Democratic presidencies — the opposite of a
risk-based story, which is precisely why the paper names it a puzzle rather than a premium.

**The post-publication record, verified rather than assumed.** This program's own D08 dossier
(`research/dossiers/08-india-mid-cycles.md` F2) recalled the broader US "presidential/election-year
cycle" literature as having "weakened or disappeared out-of-sample" — and this chapter's own
verification pass finds that claim requires a careful split rather than a blanket application,
because the US literature actually contains **two distinct claims wearing the same name**, with
different survival records:

1. **The partisan (Democrat-vs-Republican) premium Santa-Clara-Valkanov themselves documented does
   not show the decay D08 recalled.** Sy, Oumar & Al Zaman, Ashraf, "Is the presidential premium
   spurious?" *Journal of Empirical Finance* 56 (2020): 94–104 [Verified via search], test and
   **reject** all three leading alternative explanations — spurious-regression bias, data mining,
   and confounding with economic policy uncertainty — finding the premium **persists in the
   post-publication period** and remains robust even after purging returns of their covariation
   with policy uncertainty [Verified via search]. This is the opposite of a McLean-Pontiff decay
   signature: whatever this premium is, it has not obviously been arbitraged away.
2. **A separate, narrower "within-term" claim — US equities do best in year 3 of the four-year
   presidential term and worst in year 2 (Huang 1985; Booth & Booth 1997; among others) — is the
   piece of the literature D08's own F2 correctly flags as having weakened once well known**
   [D08, moderate confidence, retained with its own VERIFY tag on exact authors/years]. This is a
   different, calendar-phase-within-term claim, not the partisan-identity claim, and conflating the
   two — treating the whole "presidential cycle" family as uniformly decayed because one piece of
   it is — would repeat exactly the label-shopping error this program's own house style exists to
   catch (`research/cycles/kitchin-juglar/partAB-theory-cases.md` and this chapter's own §A.2 both
   make the identical point about not merging distinct objects under one name).

**Pastor & Veronesi supply the mechanism, and it is not a decay story either.** Pástor, Ľuboš &
Veronesi, Pietro, "Political Cycles and Stock Returns," *Journal of Political Economy* 128(11)
(2020): 4011–4045 [Verified via search], model political cycles as driven by **time-varying risk
aversion** and show this mechanism can **fully explain the presidential puzzle** [Verified via
search] — higher Democratic-era returns emerge as a genuine, risk-based equilibrium outcome of a
model in which investors' risk aversion itself cycles with the political environment, not as a
mispricing a decay/crowding argument would apply to. Read against #1 above, the honest picture is:
the partisan premium is real, has not decayed the way an ordinary factor anomaly decays, and now
has a structural (not merely statistical) explanation — which makes it, if anything, a *more*
credible finding on its own US turf than D08's original recollection suggested, not a weaker one.

**Why even the "alive" US version does not transship to India.** This is where the rejection
actually lives, and it does not depend on the US literature being weak. Pastor-Veronesi's
risk-aversion mechanism is keyed specifically to a political system with **two ideologically
coherent, alternating, single-party-controlling national parties** — exactly the structural feature
that lets "which party controls government" function as a clean proxy for a risk-aversion regime.
India's own party system has no such structural analogue: it is multi-party, coalition-prone by
design in the era this program's own election-cycle record covers (five of nine post-1991 general
elections have produced coalition governments requiring outside or partnered support), and "which
party" maps far less cleanly onto a stable policy-risk regime than it does in a strict two-party
system. D08's own F1 flagged this precisely at the point Santa-Clara-Valkanov was first surveyed:
"the mechanism (partisan macro policy differences in a two-party system) does not transplant
cleanly to India's multi-party, coalition-prone system — used here only as the methodological
template..., not a transferable coefficient." This chapter's own verification of the puzzle's
robustness (above) makes that structural objection *more* decisive, not less: a real, mechanism-
backed, non-decaying US effect is still a US-structural effect, bound to a two-party alternation
this India analogue does not have. And even setting the mechanism question aside entirely, §A.1's
own PL1 result already shows India's **own** domestic election-direction signal is empty (3/8, at
or below coin-flip) — importing a foreign partisan-identity signal to sit on top of a domestic
signal that already fails its own test is doubly unjustified.

**The India-transmission test: the 2024 US election as the live example, verified.** If a US
"presidential cycle" signature reached India through any channel beyond the ordinary global-risk
one, the most recent, cleanest test case is close at hand. On **6 November 2024**, as Donald
Trump's victory was projected, the Sensex climbed **901.50 points (+1.13%) to 80,378.13** and the
Nifty 50 gained **270.75 points (~+1.1%) to 24,484.05**, with IT names (TCS, Wipro, Infosys) among
the largest gainers on expectations of a US tech-spending rebound [Verified via search]. The
optimism reversed within one session: on **7 November 2024**, the Sensex fell **958.79 points
(−1.19%) to 79,419.34** and the Nifty 50 fell **305 points (−1.24%) to 24,179.05** [Verified via
search] — a round trip inside a single week, netting to a move well within the range of ordinary
daily noise for these indices, fully consistent with an FPI-positioning and dollar/US-rate
adjustment (this program's own **L9** global-financial-cycle channel, `config/ladder.yaml`
`L9_global_financial_cycle`, which already reads FRED VIX/dollar/DFII10, NSDL FPI flows, and INR)
rather than evidence of any India-specific "US presidential cycle" signature requiring its own
seat. No residual effect is left over once L9's ordinary inputs are read; this is precisely the
atlas's own "no Indian transmission story beyond L9" line, now checked against the most recent
available US election rather than merely asserted from it.

**Verdict: REJECT — outright, with no CONTEXT residue.** Unlike 3.8, which retains a narrow
calendar-awareness residue inside L5, this entry carries **none**: the US presidential cycle,
partisan or within-term, has no seat, no calendar row, and no conditioning role anywhere in this
program's registry. The atlas's own D08-cited verdict stands, sharpened rather than softened by
this chapter's fresh verification of the underlying literature. The rule this section leaves for
the register, stated once so it need not be restated: **the label may not re-enter this program
under any costume** — not as "election-year seasonality," not as a China-impulse-style enrichment
of L9, not as a dollar-cycle sub-face (atlas 2.9/2.10) — because every legitimate channel by which
a US election could move Indian markets (the dollar, US rates, global risk appetite) is already
fully owned by L9, and the partisan-identity mechanism that would justify a *separate* seat is
bound, by its own best explanation, to a two-party structure India does not share.

## Part G (addendum, 2026-09-02 depth audit) — Operator psychology: election adrenaline

Election season is the one window where the whole country trades the operator's own emotions
back at them: exit polls, victory speeches, and a terminal that gaps. Three failure modes
concentrate here. CONVICTION BY IDENTITY — the operator's own politics leak into "the market
wants X," and the register's answer is PL1's number (3/8: the pre-election consensus sign
anti-predicts the result month at this n) taped where opinions form. THE HALT ILLUSION —
2004 and 2009 both hit circuit breakers, in OPPOSITE directions; a halt feels like
information but is only magnitude, and magnitude was already priced into L5's vol scheduling
— the seat that widens insurance without picking a side. THE MANDATE TRADE — the itch to
re-lever the moment a "clear mandate" prints; the mandate-clarity re-lever rule exists as a
PRE-REGISTERED design precisely so this decision was made in the calm years, not at 11 AM on
counting day with the terminal red or blue. The operator's whole job in an election window
fits in one sentence: execute the schedule written years earlier, and let the surprise be
someone else's trade.
