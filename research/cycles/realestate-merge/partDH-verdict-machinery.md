# Parts D–H — verdict + machinery pointers (atlas 1.2; MERGE entry, no new seat)

## Part D — The mathematics of a clock claim, and what RE1 measured instead

A fixed-period ("clock") claim is a renewal-process claim: peak-to-peak spacings drawn from a
TIGHT distribution around the period. The desk's test is exactly that (pre-registered:
median inside the claimed window AND ≥50% of spacings inside it — a deliberately generous
8-to-11-year-wide window). What RE1 printed is a WIDE renewal distribution: median 14y,
IQR 10–17y, range 8–45y, per-country medians 10–20y, 45% in-window. Descriptively (same
print, no new trial): the spacing distribution's spread is of the same order as its center —
a clock whose tick varies by ±50% is a regime, not a clock. RE2 (Kuznets, investment/GDP)
fails wider still: median 11y, 25% in [15,25]y.

Two honesty notes carried from the results file: (i) the min_gap=8y construction DELETES
short spacings and therefore biases the test TOWARD the folk claim — it fails anyway;
(ii) RE2's tool measures spacing, Abramovitz measured amplitude — RE2 rejects the clock
reading of Kuznets, not Abramovitz's historiography.

What SURVIVES the math: real house-price peaks space at a decade-plus scale everywhere —
slower than business cycles, consistent with FC2's lengthening watch. That slowness is
information L12 already carries as a state; no date is extractable from a distribution this
wide (the credit monograph's AUROC framing applies unchanged: ordering, not timing).
