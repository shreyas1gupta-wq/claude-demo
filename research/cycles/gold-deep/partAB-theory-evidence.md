## PART A — Theory and evidence: two classics and a century of gold data

### A.1 Jastram's *The Golden Constant* (1977; Leyland update, 2009)

**What it is.** Roy Jastram's *The Golden Constant: The English and American Experience* (Wiley,
1977) is the founding long-run study of gold's purchasing power. He measured **England from 1560**
(Elizabeth I's recoinage year, a clean start-point on one of economic history's longest continuous
price series) and the **United States from 1800** (the earliest point with reliable wholesale-price
data). Jill Leyland — then economic adviser to the World Gold Council — extended both series and
added France, Germany, Japan and Switzerland in a 2009 edition retitled *1560–2007*, reproducing
Jastram's original text unchanged and appending new chapters covering the decades since his 1998
death **[Verified: publisher, scope, Leyland's extension, via multiple independent summaries this
session]**.

**The construct: purchasing power as "operational wealth."** Jastram's core measure — gold price
divided by a general price index, indexed to a base year — he also called gold's **"operational
wealth"**: not the nominal price, but what a fixed quantity of gold could actually buy over time. A
reader watching the nominal price sees violent swings ($20.67/oz pre-1933, $35 through Bretton Woods,
an $850 spike in January 1980); a reader watching operational wealth sees something closer to
oscillation around a level — the "constant" of the title **[Verified via LBMA *Alchemist* and
secondary summaries this session]**.

**Century vs. decades.** Jastram's headline finding, and the reason this program's atlas gives gold
a CONTEXT verdict rather than a REGIME seat: operational wealth is stationary **only over
multi-generational windows**. Over any shorter span — a decade, sometimes several decades — it can
sit far from its long-run level, for stretches that outlast a working career. He documented this for
both countries directly: extended below-average stretches (much of the 18th and early 19th centuries
in England) followed by extended above-average ones, with no clock governing the swings. **This is
the exact shape our own GC1–GC3 reproduce independently**, on a different window (US, 1915–2020) and
deflator (CPI, not Jastram's wholesale-price index): GC1's full-sample +1.31%/yr real return is
"roughly flat at century scale" in Jastram's own idiom, and GC3's **58% of years >±50% off anchor**
with a **22-year** longest stretch is a precisely-measured modern instance of what Jastram described
in prose. Two independent studies on non-overlapping data landing on the same qualitative structure
is real corroboration of the *shape*, not of any number transferring across studies.

**The deflation/inflation asymmetry.** Jastram's second, less-repeated finding runs opposite to
popular gold marketing: gold is a **poor hedge against major inflation** — purchasing power reliably
fell *during* inflationary episodes even as it recovered later — while it **appreciated in
operational wealth specifically during deflations** **[Verified via LBMA *Alchemist*, Gold-Eagle and
Irish News finance coverage, converging independently this session]**. Read peak-to-peak across a
full inflation cycle, gold often ended near where it started; read *within* the inflationary phase,
it characteristically lagged — the opposite of the "buy gold when inflation rises" retail heuristic.
This is the direct ancestor of Erb-Harvey's far more precisely measured 2013 result (A.2) that gold is
an unreliable short-and-medium-horizon inflation hedge.

**Sources.** Jastram (1977); Jastram & Leyland, *1560–2007* (Edward Elgar, 2009) **[Verified: title,
publisher, scope, via LBMA and independent summaries this session]**. Exact numeric index values not
re-extracted from the primary text; qualitative findings corroborated across multiple independent
sources.

### A.2 Erb & Harvey's *The Golden Dilemma* (2013) and the 2024 update

**What it is.** Claude Erb and Duke's Campbell Harvey, "The Golden Dilemma," *FAJ* 69(4): 10–42
(2013; also NBER WP 18706), is the modern quantitative heir to Jastram, and D06's own load-bearing
citation for treating gold as a correlation instrument, not a return-forecastable asset **[Verified:
authors, journal, volume/pages, NBER number, via NBER's own page and CFA Institute's FAJ archive this
session]**. Their central object is the **real price of gold** (nominal ÷ CPI, indexed) — Jastram's
operational wealth made regressable. Headline finding: when the real price sits above its historical
average (roughly **$780/oz** real, in their sample), subsequent real returns have tended to run below
average — mean reversion in *direction* **[Verified: direction and approximate figure via NBER's own
text and independent summaries this session]**. They frame the investor's choice as a genuine
dilemma: either the real price reverts as it always has, or emerging-market wealth accumulation is a
structural regime change and "this time is different" — nothing in their data resolves which. Their
May-2024 follow-up, **"Is There Still a Golden Dilemma?"** (SSRN 4807895), re-runs the question a
decade on and finds the same structural pattern still holds — high real price, low subsequent 10-year
real return — sharper now that gold's total market cap has grown to roughly **$9 trillion**, about
10% of combined world stock and bond capitalization **[Verified: title, date, SSRN ID, and the
$9T/10% figure via SSRN's own abstract and a hosted copy this session]**.

**The real-rate finding — the most load-bearing number in the literature.** Erb-Harvey report the
real gold price negatively correlated with the US 10-year TIPS real yield at roughly **−0.82**
**[Verified: coefficient and direction via multiple independent summaries this session; exact
sub-sample window — VERIFY]**. The mechanism is arithmetic: gold pays no yield, so its opportunity
cost *is* the real yield forgone by holding it instead of an inflation-protected bond — when that
yield falls, gold's relative attractiveness rises mechanically. This is `sleeves.yaml`'s
`real_rate` input (weight 0.15–0.20, half-weighted post-2022); this file supplies the primary-literature
number underneath a parameter already frozen elsewhere.

**Inflation-beta — sharper than a simple horizon story.** The honest finding is not "weak at 1–5
years, reliable at 10+": Erb-Harvey tested **every horizon from 1 to 20 years** and found gold an
unreliable inflation hedge at **all of them**, with no more dependability at the long end than the
short **[Verified via Fuqua School coverage of Harvey's own research and independent summaries this
session]**. What explains gold's spectacular 1973–82 stagflation run (real return roughly
**+9.2%/yr** **[VERIFY: market-commentary sourcing]**) is not the inflation print but what that
regime did to *real rates*: double-digit inflation the Fed would not fully chase drove real yields
deeply negative for years. Gold's inflation-hedge reputation is substantially a **real-rate hedge in
disguise** — inflation helps gold only when it is the kind that drives real rates negative (2022–23's
credibly-fought inflation, per `realrates-deep` A.4's 350bp reversal, did not). Mechanism, not
duration, is the load-bearing variable — (a) and (b) in A.3 below are the same channel, not two
separate hedges.

**Supply/demand red herrings, dispatched.** Global mine production has held near **3,300 tonnes/yr**
through gold's entire 2000s–2020s bull run — essentially unresponsive to price, since a new mine's
development timeline runs a decade-plus — and because almost none of the ~200,000-tonne above-ground
stock is ever consumed, the economically relevant "supply" is the whole stock, not the annual flow
GFMS/WGC-style tables emphasize **[Verified: figures and framing via independent summaries this
session]**. What moves the marginal price is investment demand with positive price-elasticity — a
small set of large, non-valuation-sensitive buyers (ETF flows, central banks) — exactly the mechanism
`sleeves.yaml`'s `gold_momentum` and `cb_buying_regime` inputs condition on, and why this program never
models gold from jewelry/mine-supply fundamentals.

**The capacity observation.** Because gold's ~$9T cap is small relative to global fixed income and
equities, and supply is inelastic, a **small institutional reallocation — a percentage point or two
of an "inflation protect" sleeve — is large relative to gold's own tradeable float**, and can move
price without any change in fundamentals **[Verified via SSRN's own abstract and secondary coverage
this session]**. This cuts two ways at once: it seeds the "structural demand keeps the real price
elevated" half of the dilemma, but it also warns that this desk's own gold sleeve is a small
participant in a capacity-constrained market — a large tactical overweight can move against its own
entry and exit at exactly the moments (crisis, or an already-crowded CB-buying wave) it most wants
liquidity.

**Reconciling with GC2 — an original synthesis, not a contradiction.** Erb-Harvey's regression uses
**overlapping decade windows**, the design that produces a statistically detectable mean-reversion
coefficient. GC2 instead fit a bias-corrected AR(1) on **non-overlapping annual** observations
(n≈105) and found rho_hat = 1.000 — no measurable reversion speed. These are not competing findings;
they answer different questions, and CONTRACT §9's own standard ("correct Stambaugh bias on
persistent predictors") explains why both can be true: an overlapping-window regression on a
near-unit-root series is exactly where uncorrected small-sample bias inflates apparent
predictability. Erb-Harvey's sign — high real price → below-average subsequent return — is real and
triple-corroborated (Jastram's prose, Erb-Harvey's regression, GC1's own near-flat return); its
exploitable *speed* is not measurable by any of the three, including our own — stronger, not weaker,
support for the CONTEXT verdict than either study alone.

Dilemma?" (SSRN 4807895, 2024) **[Verified]**. Real-rate correlation (−0.82) **[Verified; sub-sample
— VERIFY]**. Mine-production/market-cap figures **[Verified]**. 1973–82 return **[VERIFY]**.

### A.3 What gold actually hedges — evidence-graded

**(a) Long inflation regimes.** Not "weak at 1–5y, reliable at 10+" — Erb-Harvey found gold unreliable
at every horizon 1–20 years. The real finding is narrower: gold performs well in the subset of
inflationary regimes that also drive real rates deeply negative (1970s), and poorly where a central
bank credibly holds real rates up despite hot headline inflation. Century-scale protection (Jastram,
GC1) is real but unusable as a timing input. **Grade: Tier B mechanism (real-rate channel), Tier C on
any inflation-horizon claim shorter than a century** — this corrects the brief's original framing.

**(b) Real-rate declines.** The cleanest relationship in the literature — the −0.82 correlation, an
opportunity-cost identity, not a discovered anomaly. Strongest from roughly the mid-2000s (10-year
TIPS auctioned since 1997; FRED's constant-maturity `DFII10` begins 2003) through 2008–21's
near-zero-to-negative-rate era, **partially breaking down since 2022** as gold rallied through the
sharpest hiking cycle in decades — a decoupling D06 and `sleeves.yaml` already half-weight rather
than discard. **Grade: Tier B** — unchanged by this file, already load-bearing.

**(c) Equity tail events — genuinely mixed, not a clean hedge.** Baur & Lucey (2010) and Baur &
McDermott (2010) — already Tier B in D06 via the cross-country-analogue clause — define gold's
safe-haven property as conditional and **time-limited**, typically holding for the first ~15 trading
days after an extreme shock before it can revert. The record confirms exactly this, and its limits:

- **2008 GFC**: gold fell with everything else in the Sept–Oct liquidity panic — roughly a 30%
  correction — before rallying **163% over three years**, from an October-2008 trough near $700 to
  $1,917.90 by August 2011, as real rates collapsed **[Verified via independent summaries this
  session]**.
- **2020 COVID crash**: the same shape, compressed — gold fell ~12% in the "dash for cash," bottoming
  below $1,500 on **March 19**, then rallied to a new high above **$2,070 by August 2020** as the Fed's
  unlimited QE drove real rates toward zero **[Verified this session]**.
- **1980–2000 — the twenty years gold offered no help.** Real: a 20-year bear market from the
  January-1980 $850 peak to a **~$279 average in 2000**, while equities boomed uninterrupted for a
  generation. Volcker's disinflation removed the negative-real-rate conditions the 1970s run needed,
  and nothing in the subsequent regime gave gold a mechanism **[Verified this session]**.

Both extreme-day breakdowns (2008, 2020) live Baur-Lucey's own caveat that even qualifying crises can
see gold sold *with* equities on the worst days; 1980–2000 shows gold can simply sit out a bull or
bear market on its own schedule outside a real-rate-collapsing regime. **Grade: Tier B on the
mechanism's existence and conditionality; single-episode magnitudes remain single-instance facts.**

**(d) Currency/repression states.** `jst-debt-RESULTS.md` DS4 already establishes the pooled finding
this leans on: real equity returns in the **fiscal-dominance state** (high debt ∧ negative real
rates) average **+4.5%/yr** (median +3.4%, n=196) — **halved** vs. the normal state's +10.0%, never
reversed. `DESIGN.md` §4.3 prices L15's gold-floor attribution (0.40–0.50 of the floor) and its
0.3–0.6%/yr tail budget on this mechanism, not on any measured gold outperformance — that gap is
**DB2** (extending DS4 to the gold leg), which Part H of `partDEFH-math-algo-harvest-ledger.md`
records as **still pending**. Honestly: this file cannot cite a measured gold-in-repression return,
only the mechanism and the design tie-in. **Grade: Tier C on gold's own conditional performance (DB2
pending); Tier A/B(pooled) on DS4 itself, inherited unchanged.**

**(e) Confiscation/sanctions tails.** Rare, domestic-history events — Tier C, reduce-risk-only,
always:

- **US, Executive Order 6102 (1933)**: signed April 5, 1933, forbade private gold holdings above $100
  (~5oz), surrender required by May 1 at $20.67/oz — the exact price our own GC1 mirror-check
  independently confirms as historically correct pre-1933. Penalty up to 10 years/$10,000. Not
  repealed until January 1, 1975 — **42 years** of prohibition **[Verified: dates, price, penalty,
  repeal via multiple independent summaries this session]**.
- **India, the Gold (Control) Act era (1962–1990)**: Morarji Desai's 1962 measures (responding to the
  India-China war forex drain) recalled bank gold loans and banned forward trading; a 1963 order
  banned jewelry above 14-carat fineness; the **Gold (Control) Act, 1968** banned private bar/coin
  ownership outright, forcing conversion to jewelry, capping goldsmiths at 100g and dealers at 2kg.
  Repealed **6 June 1990** by FM Madhu Dandvate amid the crisis that would culminate the following
  year, liberalizing import at ₹250/10g duty **[Verified via multiple independent summaries,
  including Wikipedia's own entry, this session]**. Twenty-eight years of prohibition on the plainest
  form of gold ownership should end any assumption that free, legal gold ownership is a safe
  background condition for a multi-decade design.
- **India, the 1991 sovereign pledge — confiscation's flip side.** With reserves covering barely three
  weeks of imports, the Chandra Shekhar government pledged **67 tonnes** of RBI gold as loan
  collateral in May 1991 — **20t to the Union Bank of Switzerland, 47t to the Bank of England**
  (21–31 May), raising ~$600m via repo agreements that were **fully repaid** **[Verified: tonnage,
  split, dates, repayment via multiple independent summaries this session]**. Not confiscation, but
  the same category: a sovereign under genuine BoP duress treating gold as collateral of last resort —
  the fiscal-dominance tail L15 exists to price, demonstrated with India's own reserves.

**Grade: Tier C throughout, by construction** — fewer than four qualifying instances exist even
pooling US and India, the clock test fails outright, and CONTRACT §4 follows: Tier-C evidence may only
reduce risk, never add it.

### A.4 India — the domestic evidence (double weight)

**Cultural and portfolio role.** India may be the largest single data point in this literature.
Household gold holdings run an estimated **25,000–34,600 tonnes** depending on methodology and
vintage — the higher figure, reported mid-2025, values the stock near **$3.8 trillion**, variously
placed near 56–89% of GDP depending on the GDP-year denominator used **[Verified: range and order of
magnitude via multiple independent sources (Khaleej Times, BusinessToday, Business Standard) this
session; precise tonnage/GDP-ratio not reconciled — VERIFY]**. On the low end alone India holds
roughly **14% of the world's private gold stock**, reportedly exceeding the *combined official
reserves* of the US, Germany, Italy, France, Russia and China **[Verified via multiple independent
sources this session]**. This is not portfolio-theory investment demand; it is *streedhan* (a married
woman's customary property and historically her chief independent financial security), wedding and
dowry gifting, temple-vault accumulation, and a savings default reaching households with minimal
formal-banking access — a mechanism genuinely different from anything Jastram or Erb-Harvey model,
and why G.3 below treats it as a distinct operator-psychology hazard from the debt monograph's own
"doom cult."

**INR vs. USD returns — the currency leg.** The identity is exact: for USD gold return `r_g` and
USDINR depreciation `r_fx`, `r_INR ≈ r_g + r_fx`. INR has depreciated against the dollar essentially
continuously since 1991, at a pace varying by window but consistently mid-single-digit: **3.9%/yr
(5y), 3.4%/yr (10y), 4.3%/yr (15y), 3.5%/yr (20y)**, **3.74% CAGR since the 1991 reforms**
**[Verified: figures via multiple independent sources this session, methodologies/end-dates differ —
VERIFY exact numbers]** — close to, slightly below, the brief's "~4–5%/yr" prior. This is a genuine
structural tailwind for an INR holder distinct from gold's own value — but `golden-constant-RESULTS.md`
GC3 itself is explicit that an **INR-real** equivalent of GC1's +1.31%/yr has **not yet been
computed** ("measured separately once the principal's gold-INR pulls land"); this file inherits that
gap rather than filling it with an illustrative number. Nor is the currency leg an unconditional
rescue: the 2013 taper tantrum (INR down ~19% even as USD gold suffered its worst year since 1981)
shows it can be **overwhelmed** by a simultaneous gold bear market.

**Import duties and smuggling as a price wedge.** India is a price-taking, import-dependent market:
domestic price = international price × USDINR + duty + GST ± local premium. Duty moves as policy, not
market, and every move is a **level break** — `sleeves.yaml`'s own `series_hygiene` note already says
so:

- **2013**: raised in three steps (4%→6% Jan, →8% Jun, →**10%** Aug), responding to the record
  current-account deficit and rupee weakness, alongside the "80:20" export-obligation scheme
  **[Verified via multiple independent sources this session]**.
- **2022**: raised again to **15%** (from 10.75% **[VERIFY]**) amid renewed CAD concern after the
  Russia-Ukraine shock **[Verified: 15% figure and timing via Business Standard's own timeline this
  session]**.
- **July 2024**: cut sharply to **6%** — the sharpest cut on record, lowest since June 2013 — to
  support the domestic jewelry industry and curb smuggling **[Verified via WGC's own Goldhub
  commentary this session — materially more precise than D06's earlier unverified placeholder]**.

Smuggling is the market's own arbitrage response to this wedge, evidence it is economically real, and
one more reason 2013 and July-2024 must be treated as regime breaks, never fit through.

**Sovereign Gold Bonds — wound down.** SGBs, issued by the RBI from **2015**, were the dominant
India-specific vehicle: no expense ratio or storage cost, a **2.5% p.a. coupon**, capital-gains-tax-free
at the 8-year maturity. The government **suspended new issuance from February 2024**, and Budget 2025
confirmed **no plans to restart** **[Verified via PIB and financial-press coverage this session]**.
The stated rationale is this file's own capacity logic playing out at sovereign scale: the outstanding
liability had grown to roughly **₹1.12 lakh crore across ~132 tonnes** of gold-linked redemption
obligations, judged fiscally unsustainable to keep growing **[Verified this session]**. The state
learned, on its own books, that an unbounded gold-linked liability is not sustainable — the same
discipline this program's frozen ceiling applies prospectively. Existing bonds are unaffected — coupon
and maturity redemption continue, with NSDL still administering a premature-redemption calendar through
at least March 2026 — but this is moot for the design regardless, since CONTRACT §3 already restricts
gold to **ETF and futures only**; SGB's wind-down simply confirms D06's earlier judgment that
ETF/futures were already the load-bearing channel.

**Gold Monetization Scheme — also retrenching.** Launched **15 September 2015** alongside SGBs to
mobilize idle household gold into the formal system, its Medium/Long Term Government Deposit (MLTGD)
component was **discontinued 26 March 2025** "based on the examination of the performance of GMS and
evolving market conditions"; the Short Term Bank Deposit component continues at bank discretion
**[Verified via PIB's own release and independent confirmations this session]**. Two independent
policy retreats within a year of each other confirm the same underlying fact: **it has proven hard to
get Indian households to give up physical gold, or accept a paper substitute, at any scale that dents
the household stock** — this program's own ETF/futures sleeve is a wholly separate, professional-capital
question, not an attempt to address household behavior.

**The RBI's own buying — and repatriation.** The RBI added **72.6 tonnes** in 2024 (the largest
annual purchase since 2021, taking reserves to 876.18t, +9% YoY), then collapsed to just **4.02
tonnes in 2025** — an 8-year low, ~94% YoY decline **[Verified via WGC-sourced press coverage this
session]**. Total holdings stood at **880.52 tonnes** by March 2026. More structurally interesting:
gold held **overseas** fell from **387.26t (Mar 2024)** to **348.62t (Mar 2025)** to **197.67t (Mar
2026)**, with **64 tonnes repatriated in H1 FY26 alone** — an active, accelerating decision to bring
gold home **[Verified via secondary press coverage this session]**. This closes a historical circle:
the same Bank of England vault that received 47 tonnes of India's *pledged* gold under BoP duress in
May 1991 is, three and a half decades later, the vault the RBI is actively *withdrawing* from — not
crisis, but a considered custody choice running in the opposite direction, for the opposite reason.
For `sleeves.yaml`'s `cb_buying_regime` input (weight 0.20–0.25, zero assumed persistence), 2025's
collapse in purchase pace is exactly the state movement that input exists to observe — though it does
not trigger the parameter's own decay condition ("≥4 quarters net *selling*"), since the RBI has not
sold, only bought far less while repatriating more.

**What the sleeve can hold, free-data-wise.** Per `sleeves.yaml` and DESIGN.md §6.5, the instrument
menu is frozen to **ETF and futures only**. Indian gold ETF expense ratios have compressed
meaningfully since D06's earlier 0.4–0.8% estimate: current AMC ratios run roughly **0.25% (Nippon
India ETF Gold BeES)** to **0.50–0.59%** for several other large funds **[Verified: current range via
multiple AMC-comparison sources this session — materially updates D06's placeholder]** — the natural
venue for the structural floor across all three books. **MCX gold futures** (1kg, plus
Mini/Guinea/Petal for smaller lots) remain the natural venue for the tactical band, since embedded
leverage counts cleanly toward the 1.5x gross cap and can size up/down fast without disturbing the ETF
floor — subject to roll-cost measurement the data phase, not this dossier, must complete.

## PART B — Why the ceiling holds at 20–25%: the case made affirmatively from GC3

### B.1 GC3's own numbers are the argument

GC3 found **58% of years (1915–2020) sit more than ±50% from the century real-gold anchor**, with a
**22-year** longest continuous stretch away from it. Twenty-two years outlasts most mandates, most
manager tenures, and this program's own design timeline many times over. A desk sizing gold as though
the century anchor licenses a large standing position must accept looking "wrong" relative to that
anchor for longer than most operators will spend running the book at all — not a risk a Tier-C,
narrative-adjacent thesis earns the right to be sized heavily against (CONTRACT §4).

### B.2 The frozen 50% cap is not the target

CONTRACT §3's gold ≤50% is a **mandate ceiling** built for flexibility, never presented as
evidence-derived. The evidence assembled here — Jastram's century-only constancy, Erb-Harvey's
unmeasurable-speed reversion and unreliable short-horizon inflation hedge, A.3(c)'s mixed equity-tail
record, GC3's 22-year off-anchor stretch — supports nothing near 50%. `sleeves.yaml`'s frozen ceilings
(**conservative 20–24%, moderate 20–25%, aggressive 15–18%**) already encode this, and D06's own note
is blunt: **no non-sponsor study supports a strategic allocation materially above ~20–25%**; WGC's own
higher "optimal allocation" studies are excluded as conflicted — WGC exists to promote gold demand.
The ceiling sitting near 40–50% of the mandate's own legal maximum is exactly what the assembled,
cross-verified literature supports, no more.

### B.3 The floor is insurance, never alpha

GC1's own full-sample **+1.31%/yr real** should end any temptation to size the floor as return-seeking:
far below equities (DS4's own +9.5%/+4.5% real means), because — Erb-Harvey's own point — gold has
**no cash-flow-based valuation anchor**. Any return above the century-flat baseline is a bet on
relative store-of-value demand shifting, not a modeled expected return. DESIGN.md §4.3 prices the floor
correctly on this basis: L15 justifies the floor's existence and ~40–50% of its size, plus a
**0.3–0.6% NAV/yr** tail budget and a conditional +1–2pp lift only when debt-trajectory ∧
negative-real-rate-persistence ∧ active reserve-diversification co-occur — "nothing else. No timing
calls, no equity beta, no leverage." This mirrors DESIGN.md §5.5's rare option-buying tail hedge (a
budgeted 0.5–1.5% NAV/yr premium, never a return-seeking position) — this program's other standing
insurance line, priced the same way, for a different tail.

### B.4 Three named rejected uses

**(1) Anchor-reversion timing.** No rule may condition a position on distance from the century anchor
— GC2's honest null forecloses this: rho_hat = 1.000, no defensible half-life at any holdable horizon.

**(2) Trading gold on inflation prints.** Erb-Harvey's unreliable-at-every-horizon finding forecloses
a CPI-surprise overlay; real rates already carry the load via `sleeves.yaml`'s `real_rate` input — a
CPI overlay would double-count the same channel through a noisier proxy.

**(3) Doom-allocation.** The gold-specific mirror of the debt monograph's own G.2 "doom cult,"
foreclosed the same way: the hard-coded reduce-only ceiling means no narrative, however compelling,
can push gold toward its ceiling or the 50% mandate cap. Part G develops why this needs a structural
answer, not operator discipline in the moment.

## PART G — Operator psychology: two anchors and one cult

### G.1 The doom-cult overlap — same evidence, doubled

The debt monograph's G.2 names an operator seduced by a compelling debt-supercycle narrative into
treating the gold floor's *existence* as license for a conviction call — rotating hard into gold,
treating equities as a melting ice cube. Its own answer is DS4: **+4.5%/yr** real equities even in the
fiscal-dominance state, halved, never reversed. This file adds a second, independent piece the debt
monograph could not have cited: **gold's own long-run real return is barely positive at all** — GC1's
+1.31%/yr full-sample figure. A perma-bear exiting well-evidenced +4.5%/yr equities for gold on the
strength of the debt-supercycle narrative is trading return for safety **twice over** — once against
DS4, again against gold's own century-scale return, itself barely above zero and spent 58% of 105
years more than half-away from even that low bar. The countermeasure is identical and already
structural: CONTRACT §4's reduce-only Tier-C rule, enforced in the registry validator, not left to
discretion.

### G.2 "It always comes back" — GC2's null is the direct rebuttal

The intuition "gold always reverts eventually" is true at century scale (GC1). The failure is what it
smuggles in: an implicit "...soon enough for me to time it," which GC2 shows is not statistically
supportable. The AR(1)'s 6931-year half-life point estimate is, in GC2's own words, "decoration" — the
honest statement is the confidence interval, and it says no reversion speed is measurable from 105
annual observations. An operator overweighting gold because it looks "cheap versus anchor" is making a
timing claim this program's own machinery could not validate even in principle. This restates A.2's
synthesis point as a psychology risk: the same overlapping-window regressions that make reversion look
tradable are exactly where persistence-driven small-sample bias (CONTRACT §9) inflates apparent
predictability.

### G.3 Indian household anchoring — familiarity, not fear

India's own relationship with gold is a distinct hazard, running on familiarity rather than panic.
A.4 already gives the scale: 25,000–34,600 tonnes, streedhan, wedding gifting, Dhanteras and Akshaya
Tritiya rituals, a savings default reaching households the formal system barely touches. The hazard is
that this is *socially reinforced* — "buy gold, it's what everyone does" is transmitted culturally,
untethered from Erb-Harvey's valuation metric or GC1–GC3's anchor. `CYCLE_ATLAS.md` 4.8–4.9 already
fence this correctly (gold demand seasonality is **CONTEXT only**, "a flow fact, not a price signal,"
D06) — but the fencing must be deliberate, because the excluded intuition is not obviously irrational
the way panic-driven doom-allocation is. This is the debt monograph's own G.1 "frog-boil complacency"
(comfort masking a mechanism) applied to a savings default rather than money illusion. The sleeve's
inputs (real rate, CB-buying, INR tilt, momentum, crisis kicker) must come from mechanism-backed,
evidence-tiered series — never from "everyone I know is buying this Dhanteras."

### G.4 Narrative capture at price highs — the mirror trap

The opposite failure arrives when gold is working: D06 already notes 2024–26 has been "one of gold's
strongest runs on record," attributed to CB buying, US fiscal concern, and geopolitical risk premia.
Media and household buying rise together procyclically — the same flow pattern 4.8/4.9 already
document as real but non-predictive. The trap turns Erb-Harvey's own dilemma into conviction: "new
highs prove the structural story is different now, the anchor doesn't matter." The same mirror-error
family `realrates-deep` G.2 names for real rates ("regime-change maximalism") — a loud, real episode
extrapolated into permanent conviction neither this file's evidence nor Erb-Harvey's own 2024
re-examination resolves either way.
