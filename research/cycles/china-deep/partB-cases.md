## B1. What a China credit pulse is, and how this record reads it

Atlas 2.11 names the mechanism in one sentence: China's stimulus cadence — a roughly 3–4-year
rhythm of credit-and-fiscal easing followed by a tightening or administrative clampdown — moves
global commodity prices, EM risk appetite, and Asian trade, and that movement transmits to India
through its terms of trade and its flows. This is a **policy-pulse** object, not the decade-scale
capacity-lag mechanism `commodity-deep`'s own case 5 already documents for 2001–2011: a supercycle
is a demand shock meeting a mine or a smelter that takes a decade to build; a pulse is a stimulus
announcement, a surge in total social financing (TSF) or bank credit, a domestic construction/
infrastructure target that absorbs it, a resulting run-up in the metals and bulk-commodity prices
China imports at scale, and — inside two to four years — a tightening move (an interbank squeeze,
an administrative capacity cut, a leverage cap, a deleveraging campaign) that ends it. Both objects
run through the same economy and the same physical commodities; they are read here at two
different frequencies, exactly as the atlas itself frames rows 1.3 and 2.11 as separate entries.

**The desk's own proxy evidence, cited once before the cases rather than re-argued nine times.**
China sits in neither the JST panel nor the commodity vault (BIS China credit data untested from
this environment; no free mirror found, per `commodity-deep`'s own data-engineering note, C.6),
so the desk's pre-registered **CI1a–CI1b** trials (`china-RESULTS.md`, cited directly, never
recomputed) are proxy trials on the commodity-price channel itself, not on TSF. **CI1a** measures
whether the China era changed metals' relative behavior against agricultural commodities: the
standard deviation of the 3-year change in mean log real metals price minus mean log real
agricultural price rose from **0.145 (1950–1999) to 0.318 (2000–2015)** — a **2.19×** widening
against a 1.5× bar, **PASS** — evidence that *something structural* entered metals' relative
dynamics around 2000, consistent with (not proof of) a China-demand channel. **CI1b** checks
whether the metals-minus-agriculture signal moved in the predicted direction across two of this
Part's own named pulse windows: **2008-11 to 2010-12** (cumulative log change **+0.339**) and
**2016-01 to 2017-06** (cumulative log change **+0.161**) — **2/2 positive** against a 2/2 bar,
**PASS**, with the confounds stated at registration and repeated honestly here: the first window
also contains the global post-GFC reflation and a falling dollar; the second also contains the
supply-side reform's own capacity-*cutting* rally leg (a supply, not a pure-demand, driver). Both
passes license the metals-vs-agriculture state as an **L9 enrichment candidate** only — never a
standalone China signal, and never proof the proxy separates a China demand pulse from its fellow
travelers. `partCDEFH.md` Part E states explicitly that this Part's own pulse table is the
**validation calendar** H54's eventual real-TSF test will be graded against; §B4 below is written
with that instruction in view.

**Five pulses since 2008, read in the order the task specifies.** Atlas 2.11 counts "n≈4–5 since
2008"; this record finds five, with the fifth itself best read as a *sequence* of easing rounds
inside a single structurally different property-bust era rather than one clean pulse — a
distinction §B4's own cadence discussion returns to directly, without smuggling a date claim about
what comes next.

## B2. The five pulses

### Pulse 1 — 2008–10: THE stimulus

**The policy trigger.** On **9 November 2008**, the State Council announced a **RMB 4 trillion
(~$586 billion)** stimulus package. On **15 November 2008** it emerged that the central government
itself would fund only **RMB 1.2 trillion** of the total, with the remaining roughly three-quarters
reallocated onto provincial and local government budgets — the single design choice that produced
the pulse's most consequential side effect. Reserve-requirement policy reversed in the same window:
having raised the RRR steadily from July 2006 to 7 June 2008, the PBOC cut it **three times for
large banks (to 15.5%) and four times for small/medium banks (to 13.5%) between 16 September and
22 December 2008**.

**The credit surge.** Because China's local governments are barred by statute from borrowing
directly, they financed their roughly RMB 2.8 trillion share of the package chiefly through newly
created **local government financing vehicles (LGFVs)** — local-government-owned entities pledging
land as collateral to borrow from banks — a structure this pulse effectively invented at scale.
Bank credit grew by a cumulative **RMB 14.6 trillion between 2008 and 2009**, with new lending
rising from an RMB 4.7 trillion order in 2008 to roughly **RMB 10 trillion in 2009**
`[VERIFY: the precise actual-vs-target framing of the RMB 4.7tn/RMB 10tn figures across sources —
both orders of magnitude recur but are not fully reconciled to a single primary release this
pass]` — new loans effectively **doubling** year-on-year, the largest one-year credit expansion in
the modern Chinese record and the founding instance of the "flood-style" credit response every
later pulse in this record is measured against.

**The domestic target.** Infrastructure — rail, roads, urban utilities — absorbed the LGFV-financed
share of the package; property construction rode alongside it as bank credit flowed freely into
developers for the first time since the pre-2008 tightening cycle.

**The commodity signature.** `commodity-deep`'s own case 5 carries the full decade-scale arc
(copper low-$1,000s/tonne in 2001 to over $10,000/tonne by February 2011; iron ore roughly
$12/tonne to $187/tonne by early 2011; the 40-year annual-benchmark iron-ore pricing system
breaking down across 2008–2010 as spot sales rose from ~30% to ~60% of China's iron-ore trades),
and is cross-referenced rather than recomputed. This pulse's own contribution is dating the
2008–09 stimulus specifically as the demand engine behind the *second half* of that decade arc: the
Baltic Dry Index's 94% collapse (May–December 2008) was a liquidity air-pocket **inside** the
supercycle, not its end, and the RMB 4 trillion package is the demand event that reopened Chinese
steel-mill order books through 2009 into the run that carried iron ore and copper to their
respective 2011 peaks. Steel production itself rose from 130 million tonnes (2000) toward its
eventual 820-million-tonne (2014) plateau across this same window — the physical-output backdrop
`commodity-deep` documents, cited here as the pulse's own real-side confirmation.

**The EM/India transmission.** FIIs poured **$60.31 billion** into Indian equities from **March
2009 to November 2010**, lifting the Nifty from roughly 2,500 to 6,300 — the "2009 V"
`globalcycle-deep`'s own case B3 dates from the global-liquidity side and this Part cross-
references rather than recomputes. The metals-sector-specific mirror of the same rally is Nifty
Metal's **+232.5% return in 2009** (`commodity-deep` §B3.6, cross-ref), a materially larger swing
than the Nifty 50's own recovery — the sector-dispersion signature this pulse's commodity-price
leg produced inside the broader global-liquidity rally `globalcycle-deep` already dates. India's
own 2009–10 metals/commodity rally is thus best read as **two simultaneous, mechanistically
distinct tailwinds arriving together**: a global-liquidity FII wave (Fed zero rates, `globalcycle-
deep` B3) and a China-demand commodity-price wave (this pulse), each independently bullish for
Indian metals equities and neither one this Part's own instrument (a China-specific credit proxy)
can cleanly separate from the other — precisely the identification-gap caveat `partCDEFH.md` Part D
states for the CI1 proxy in general.

**The tightening that ended it.** Having cut the RRR through late 2008, the PBOC reversed course
and hiked it **twelve times, reaching a historic 21.5% for large banks by June 2011** — a sustained,
multi-quarter tightening rather than a single dated shock, consistent with this record's general
finding (§B4) that pulse-ending tightenings vary sharply in character from one pulse to the next.

**Pulse length.** From the 9 November 2008 announcement to the RRR-hike sequence's own 2011 peak is
roughly **two and a half years**; read against the commodity price peak itself (early 2011), the
pulse's effective demand-side life is closer to **two years** (late 2008 to early 2011).

### Pulse 2 — 2012–13: the mini-pulse

**The policy trigger.** China's 2011 GDP growth slowed to **9.2%**, and the PBOC delivered its
**first RRR cut in nearly three years on 30 November/5 December 2011 (–50bp)**, opening a modest
easing cycle through 2012 `[VERIFY: the precise count and dates of every 2012 RRR/rate move — the
2011 December cut and the subsequent 2012 easing direction are confirmed; a complete month-by-month
ledger was not independently reconstructed this pass]`.

**The credit surge — and its distinctive shape.** Unlike pulse 1's bank-credit flood, this pulse's
credit surge ran overwhelmingly through the **shadow-financing channel** — trust loans and
entrusted loans — the "golden era" the task itself names. Newly extended trust loans grew **more
than five-fold in 2012**, reaching roughly 8% of total social financing; growth accelerated further
in 2013, with trust loans rising **378% year-on-year** and trust's own share of TSF climbing from
**1.6% (end-2011) to 13.4% (Q1 2013)**. Trust plus entrusted loans together grew a cumulative
**RMB 4.6 trillion across 2008–2011** but **RMB 7 trillion in 2012–2013 alone** — a materially
faster pace concentrated into a fraction of the time. The trust industry itself became China's
largest non-bank financial sector in 2012; trust-company assets rose **65% year-on-year to RMB 8.7
trillion by Q1 2013**, en route to **RMB 16.7 trillion by 2015**. TSF as a whole grew **59%
year-on-year in Q1 2013** — the fastest pace since the 2009 stimulus-induced boom — with trust
loans, entrusted loans, and bankers' acceptances together supplying **30% of new financing in
2013**, a record share. This is the credit-surge scale figure this pulse contributes to the record,
and it is the direct backdrop `shadow-deep`'s own §B8 (the trust/WMP implicit-guarantee mechanism)
is cross-referenced against rather than re-derived here: this Part supplies the *size and dating* of
the golden era; `shadow-deep` supplies the *funding-structure* mechanism running underneath it.

**The domestic target.** With banks now more cautious after 2008–09's excesses and LGFVs facing
tighter direct-lending scrutiny, developers and local-government platforms turned to
trust-and-entrusted-loan financing as the marginal channel for exactly the same infrastructure and
property projects pulse 1 had financed through bank credit directly — a channel substitution, not a
change in ultimate use, and the reason this pulse is fairly read as a "mini" version of pulse 1's
own target rather than a distinct one.

**The commodity signature — honestly muted.** Unlike every other pulse in this record, 2012–13
produced no fresh commodity-price rally of its own. `commodity-deep`'s own CS1/CS1b trials (its
Part B1, cited not recomputed) find no distinct trough or peak dated to this window in the 165-year
metals-and-broad-commodity chronology; copper and iron ore were, through 2012–13, drifting down
from their respective 2011 peaks (pulse 1's own commodity signature) toward the base the 2014–16
collapse (`commodity-deep` case 6) would later complete. The honest read this Part owes the record:
this pulse's shadow-credit surge financed real activity and real Chinese import demand, but at a
scale and through a channel (financial intermediation more than fresh construction starts) that did
not print as a distinguishable upward break in world metals prices — the clearest case in this
record of a genuine China credit pulse whose transmission channel to global commodities was, on the
evidence available, materially weaker than the "stimulus cadence moves commodities" framing atlas
2.11 states as its general rule.

**The EM/India transmission — an honest identification limit.** This pulse's own acute window
(H1 2013) sits on top of, rather than beside, the Fed's own May 2013 taper-tantrum shock —
`globalcycle-deep`'s own case B4 (the rupee's fall from ₹55 to ₹68.85, the MSF-rate defense, the
FCNR(B) swap) and `mpcycle-deep`'s own case 4 both already own that episode's full anatomy. This
Part's own honest contribution is a negative finding, stated plainly rather than smoothed over: with
no distinguishable China-driven commodity-price move in this window (the paragraph above) and
India's own 2013 stress running through a rates-and-FX channel `globalcycle-deep` already
attributes overwhelmingly to the Fed's own taper signal, **this record cannot isolate a
China-pulse-specific India transmission for 2012–13 distinct from the Fed-driven episode occurring
in the same calendar year** — a genuine analytical limit, not a finding of "no transmission," and
directly analogous to the identification caution `globalcycle-deep`'s own case B6 (2018) states for
disentangling a global dollar squeeze from a simultaneous domestic Indian credit event.

**The tightening that ended it.** The mini-pulse's own end is precisely dated and, unusually among
this record's five pulses, **is itself the tightening signal rather than a separate policy
response to one**: on **19 June 2013**, rather than injecting liquidity, the PBOC told commercial
banks to "make full use of incremental funds and revitalize stock options," judging system
liquidity "reasonable" even as pressure built. The Shanghai Interbank Offered Rate (SHIBOR)
overnight rate, ordinarily below 3%, spiked to **30% on 20 June 2013**; the seven-day repo rate
reached a comparable **25–30%** the same day, against a **2–3%** range earlier in the year, in a
single-day high-low range of **2,329 basis points** versus a five-month average of 154bp. The PBOC
itself attributed the spike to a combination of rapid loan growth, corporate tax payments ahead of a
deadline, banks' own reserve-payment obligations, and Dragon Boat Festival cash demand — but the
deliberate refusal to ease, at the moment banks most wanted relief, is the substantive policy
signal: a central bank using a liquidity squeeze itself as a disciplinary tool against runaway
shadow-credit growth, rather than tightening through the RRR or a rate hike. Chinese equities fell
**more than 14% in June 2013** during the squeeze.

**Pulse length.** From the December 2011 RRR cut to the 20 June 2013 SHIBOR spike is roughly
**18 months** — the shortest pulse in this record, consistent with its own "mini" framing.

### Pulse 3 — 2015–16: the shantytown/supply-side pulse

**The 2015 panic backdrop, cross-referenced not re-derived.** The pulse opens against the same
backdrop `globalcycle-deep`'s own case B5 already owns in full: China's domestic equity market (the
SSE Composite) fell roughly 43% in barely over two months (June–August 2015), and the PBOC devalued
the yuan's reference rate **1.9% in a single day on 11 August 2015** — the steepest one-day move in
at least two decades, and the trigger for a global VIX/dollar episode `globalcycle-deep` dates and
transmits to India (FII outflow of a then-record ₹17,000 crore in August 2015 alone) in full. This
Part's own case is the **domestic Chinese supply-and-demand pulse** that followed directly in the
devaluation's wake — a different mechanism, transmitted through commodity prices rather than
portfolio flows, and genuinely additive to rather than a restatement of `globalcycle-deep`'s own
episode.

**The policy trigger.** Two distinct instruments launched in close succession. On the demand side,
the **Pledged Supplemental Lending (PSL)** facility, first deployed for shantytown redevelopment
funding, saw heavy use from **2015 through 2019**: China Development Bank loans financed **66% of
total shantytown-rebuilding investment in 2016** and **48% in 2017**
`[VERIFY: a precise year-by-year PSL disbursement total for 2015–2017 individually was not
independently pinned this pass; the CDB-financing-share figures above are the best-verified proxy
for the programme's scale]`. On the supply side, **supply-side structural reform**, announced in
late 2015, set multi-year capacity-reduction targets of **100–150 million tonnes of steel
(8–12% of 2015 year-end capacity) and roughly 1 billion tonnes of coal (~20% of 2015 year-end
capacity)** — the real-side capacity-cut mechanics `capex-deep`'s own case 6 documents in full and
cross-references here rather than re-derives. **2016's** actual cuts reached **65 million tonnes of
steelmaking capacity and 290 million tonnes of coal capacity**; **2017's** lowered targets of
**50 million tonnes of steel and 150 million tonnes of coal** were both fulfilled, in August and
October 2017 respectively.

**The credit surge.** New yuan lending in **January 2016 alone reached RMB 2.51 trillion ($385
billion)**, up **71% year-on-year** and well above forecasts; TSF that month reached **RMB 3.42
trillion**, nearly double December 2015's RMB 1.81 trillion. By **end-2016, TSF as a whole exceeded
200% of GDP**. The PBOC delivered at least two RRR cuts through 2015 alone — **0.5 percentage points
on 4 February** and **1.0 percentage point in April** `[VERIFY: a complete count of every 2015 RRR
move — additional cuts recur in secondary compilations but were not individually re-pinned to a
primary release this pass; the two cited are independently confirmed]`.

**The domestic target — a genuine double engine, not a single one.** This is the pulse this record's
own task correctly flags as structurally distinctive: **shantytown monetization** (PSL-funded cash
compensation to residents displaced by demolition, who then purchase homes on the open market
rather than receiving an in-kind replacement unit) is a **demand-side** stimulus specifically
targeted at lower-tier-city residential property, running **simultaneously** with supply-side
reform's **supply-side** capacity destruction in steel and coal — the same pulse squeezing supply
and subsidizing demand in the same eighteen-month window, a combination this record's other four
pulses do not reproduce in the same form (pulse 1 was pure demand-side stimulus; pulse 4 below was
pure demand-side reflation).

**The commodity signature — the double boost, verified.** China's iron-ore imports **topped 1
billion tonnes for the first time in 2016**, a **7.5% increase over 2015**, at an average import
price of **$56.50/tonne**; by **early 2017** the rally had carried futures back to **$100/tonne**,
spot prices last seen in 2014. China's own steel *imports* (a smaller, higher-grade-input flow
distinct from its much larger export volumes) rose **3% to 13.4 million tonnes in 2016** and a
further **10% year-to-date by Q1 2017**. The mechanism behind the rally's unusual strength is
precisely the double engine named above: supply-side reform was actively *removing* steel and coal
capacity from the market at the same moment PSL-funded shantytown demand was *sustaining* the
construction activity that consumes steel — a genuine joint supply-cut-plus-demand-hold combination,
rather than either force operating alone, and the reason this pulse's price rally proved sharper and
more sustained than a pure-demand pulse (pulse 2, above) or a pure-supply-cut episode would have
produced individually. The desk's own CI1b trial (§B1 above) independently corroborates the
window: the metals-minus-agriculture relative price state moved **positively (+0.161 cumulative log
change) across exactly this record's own 2016-01 to 2017-06 dating** — cited directly, not
recomputed, and registered at the time with the supply-side-reform confound named explicitly.

**The EM/India transmission.** Nifty Metal returned **+48.4% in 2016** before falling **–16.8% in
2018** as the rally's eventual unwind ran its course (`commodity-deep` §B3.6, cross-ref) — the
mid-cycle metals rally `commodity-deep`'s own India section already names, and this Part's own
contribution is dating its *cause* precisely to the PSL/supply-side double engine rather than to a
generic "China stimulus" label. India's own macro exposure to this pulse ran, as in every pulse, in
the opposite direction from its equity-sector exposure: a rallying iron-ore/steel/coal complex is a
terms-of-trade and import-bill headwind for 85%-import-dependent India even as it lifted Nifty
Metal specifically — the same double-sign structure `commodity-deep`'s own B1 framing states as the
organizing idea for every China-linked commodity episode India receives.

**The tightening that ended it.** The **2017 deleveraging campaign** targeted the interbank/
wealth-management channel directly: interbank holdings of WMPs **more than halved, from RMB 6.65
trillion (December 2016) to RMB 3.25 trillion (December 2017)**; in **August 2017**, regulators
closed a loophole that had let negotiable certificates of deposit be booked as "bonds payable"
rather than interbank liabilities, blocking a route banks had used to circumvent WMP and interbank
leverage limits; the PBOC folded off-balance-sheet WMPs into its macro-prudential assessment of
bank risk for the first time. The campaign's formal capstone — the **27 April 2018** "Guiding
Opinions on Regulating the Asset Management Business of Financial Institutions," banning implicit
guarantees on new products system-wide — is `shadow-deep`'s own §B8 subject in full and is
cross-referenced, not re-derived, here. On the shantytown side specifically, PSL's own pace slowed
markedly from 2018: contemporary reporting in **June 2018** carried claims that CDB had halted new
shantytown PSL support entirely, which CDB itself publicly denied — a disputed, rather than fully
resolved, end date for the demand-side leg specifically `[VERIFY: whether CDB's PSL support for
shantytown redevelopment was formally curtailed in mid-2018 or merely slowed gradually through
2018–19 — sources disagree and this pass could not adjudicate definitively]`, while the supply-side
capacity cuts themselves, having met their multi-year targets by October 2017, simply concluded on
schedule rather than being reversed by a discrete policy shock.

**Pulse length.** From the PSL/supply-side-reform launch (2015) to the 2017 deleveraging campaign's
own capstone (the April 2018 asset-management rules) is roughly **three years** — longer than
pulse 2's 18 months and closer to pulse 1's own length, consistent with this pulse's own dual
demand-and-supply engine sustaining the credit-and-price cycle for longer than a single-engine
pulse would.

### Pulse 4 — 2020–21: the COVID pulse

**The policy trigger — controlled reflation.** China's own domestic policy response to the COVID-19
shock ran through 2020 as a comparatively controlled reflation rather than a repeat of pulse 1's
flood: contemporary characterizations describe China's pandemic-era stimulus spending as exceeding
its prior stimulus efforts in absolute scale, directed substantially toward industrial and
export-manufacturing capacity rather than a blanket infrastructure flood
`[VERIFY: a precise, single, search-verified 2020 TSF or new-loan aggregate comparable in
specificity to pulses 1–3's own cited figures was not independently pinned this pass — the
"controlled reflation" characterization is directional, corroborated across multiple contemporary
sources, but not reducible to one authoritative headline number here]`.

**The credit surge and its target — property, once more, and its turning point.** Property
remained the credit-absorbing sector this pulse's early phase targeted, precisely the pattern
pulses 1 and 3 both already show — until the policy itself pivoted mid-pulse to end that pattern
for good. On **August 2020**, regulators introduced the **"three red lines"** — a liability-to-asset
ratio below **70%**, a net-gearing (debt-to-equity) ratio below **100%**, and a cash-to-short-term-
debt ratio at or above **1.0×** — with developers sorted into green/orange/yellow/red categories
that capped permitted annual debt growth at, respectively, unrestricted, **10%, 5%, and 0%**
(`fincycle-deep`'s own case 7, cross-ref, not re-derived). This is the single clearest instance in
this five-pulse record of a *tightening measure landing mid-pulse, before the commodity rally it
would eventually help end had even peaked* — the three red lines arrived in August 2020, while the
commodity surge documented below did not crest until mid-2021, a genuinely different sequencing
from pulses 2 and 3's own tightening-after-the-peak pattern.

**The commodity signature.** Iron ore rose **more than 260%, from $83.50/tonne in May 2020 to
$219/tonne by June 2021**; futures in Singapore reached a **record above $226/tonne**. Copper rose
to a **record $10,747.50/tonne** on the London Metal Exchange. Australia's iron-ore shipments to
China alone generated **$155 billion in 2021**, with prices sustained above $200/tonne through much
of the year. The drivers compound rather than reduce to China alone — a global reopening-demand
surge, pandemic-era supply-chain constraints, and heavy Chinese stockpiling of industrial raw
materials all contributed — but the scale of Chinese demand specifically is the largest single
component named across the sourcing for this episode, consistent with this pulse belonging in the
China-credit-impulse record rather than being read as a purely global reopening event.

**The EM/India transmission.** Nifty Metal returned **+18.0% (2020), +73.4% (2021), and +25.3%
(2022)** — outperforming the Nifty 50's own **+16.1%, +25.6%, and +5.7%** in every one of those
three years (`commodity-deep` §B3.6, cross-ref). India's own broader 2020–21 equity rally is
`globalcycle-deep`'s own case B7 subject (the fastest global stop-to-reflation round trip on
record, driven by the Fed's own zero-rate and unlimited-QE response) — this Part's own honest
contribution is that the **metals-sector-specific** leg of that rally, distinct from the broader
market's own recovery, tracks the China-driven commodity-price surge documented above more closely
than it tracks the global-liquidity factor `globalcycle-deep` already owns: Nifty Metal's own
outperformance margin over the Nifty 50 (roughly 2, 48, and 20 percentage points across the three
years respectively) is the sector-dispersion signature a genuine China-pulse commodity channel,
sitting *inside* the broader global-liquidity rally, would be expected to produce — again, an
identification the desk's own single-leg proxy (§B1) cannot cleanly separate from confounds, stated
honestly rather than overclaimed.

**The tightening that ended it.** The three red lines (August 2020) is this pulse's own dated
tightening trigger, but its consequence arrived with a lag rather than immediately: Evergrande's own
liquidity crisis became acute through **Q4 2021**, with Fitch downgrading the company to "restricted
default" on **9 December 2021** and S&P following with "selective default" the same month —
Evergrande becoming the **twelfth** Chinese property developer to default on bonds in 2021, and by
far the largest. `shadow-deep`'s own §B8 owns Evergrande's CP/WMP funding-web mechanism in full and
is cross-referenced rather than re-derived here; this Part's own contribution is dating the
**policy trigger** (three red lines, August 2020) roughly **sixteen months** ahead of the
**crisis's own visible arrival** (Evergrande's December 2021 default) — a materially longer
policy-to-crisis lag than pulse 2's own same-month squeeze-and-signal (June 2013), and consistent
with `fincycle-deep`'s own general finding (its own case-record synthesis) that a property-cycle
turning point is dramatically slower to confirm than a credit-market one.

**Pulse length.** Reading the demand-side reflation alone (early 2020 to the June 2021 commodity
peak) gives roughly **eighteen months**; reading the full arc through to the policy trigger's own
crisis consequence (three red lines, August 2020, to Evergrande's December 2021 default) gives
roughly **sixteen months** on that narrower dating, or **close to two years** counting from the
initial 2020 reflation start.

### Pulse 5 — 2022–2026: the property-bust-era pulses

**Why this is written as a sequence, not a single pulse, stated up front.** Unlike pulses 1–4, each
of which shows a reasonably clean trigger-surge-target-commodity-tightening arc, the 2022–2026
period is better described as a **continuous sequence of easing rounds** responding to one ongoing
structural bust (the property sector's post-2021 contraction, `fincycle-deep`'s own case 7 and
`credit-deep`'s own case 7, both cross-referenced not re-derived) rather than a discrete stimulus-
boom-tightening cycle of the kind pulses 1–4 each display. This distinction is itself the central
finding this section is built to support, and §B4 below returns to it as the cadence question's own
sharpest piece of evidence.

**The 2022–23 easing rounds.** As developer defaults spread through 2022 (Country Garden's roughly
**¥1.36 trillion (~$190 billion)** total liabilities as of mid-2023, a **~$11 billion** offshore
bond default in October 2023; Evergrande's own **$300 billion+** liabilities, ordered into
liquidation by a Hong Kong court on **29 January 2024** — `fincycle-deep`'s own case 7 figures,
cross-ref), regulators responded with the **16-point plan**, unveiled **13–14 November 2022**:
banks were directed to extend maturing developer loans, mortgage down-payment ratios and rates were
cut to support sales, bond issuance by "quality" developers was supported, and trust companies were
encouraged to fund M&A, rental-housing, and retirement-property transactions. Country Garden's
Hong Kong-listed shares rose as much as **52%** on the announcement — the market's own real-time
read of the plan's significance.

**The 2024 stimulus pivot.** On **24 September 2024**, PBOC Governor Pan Gongsheng, flanked by the
banking/insurance and securities regulators, announced a coordinated package: an RRR cut of
**0.5 percentage points**, releasing an estimated **RMB 1 trillion (~$142 billion)** of long-term
liquidity; a **0.2 percentage point** cut to the 7-day reverse repo rate, with a signaled
**0.2–0.25 percentage point** cut to the Loan Prime Rate to follow; a directive lowering interest
rates on **existing** mortgages toward newly issued levels; and a cut to the minimum down-payment
ratio for second-home purchases from **25% to 15%** nationwide. The CSI 300 recorded its **biggest
single-day gain since July 2020** on the announcement — the clearest market-recognized "this is a
genuine pivot" moment in the entire 2022–2026 sequence, and the closest this era comes to a single
dated trigger comparable to pulses 1–4's own announcements.

**Why this era's impulse pulses transmit WEAKER to commodities — verified, not asserted.** The
central structural fact this record can state with real evidence, not merely narrative plausibility:
property's own **credit-intensity of metals demand has fallen** across precisely this period.
Construction's overall share of Chinese steel consumption fell from **60% (2020–2021) to 52%
(2023)**; the real-estate sector's own specific share of steel consumption fell from **~32% (2021)
to ~23% (2025)**; and total Chinese steel consumption itself fell **~17%, from close to 1 billion
tonnes (2021) to 830 million tonnes (2025)**, driven substantially by the real-estate contraction —
new construction starts fell a further **~24% in H1 2024 alone**, after already-sharp declines in
2022 and 2023. This is direct, quantified evidence for the mechanism the task asks this section to
state honestly: a China credit-easing round that, in 2016 or 2020, would have flowed substantially
into property construction and from there into steel/iron-ore demand now finds a structurally
smaller share of the economy's metals appetite sitting in the sector the easing is aimed at — the
same RMB of new credit produces a smaller commodity-demand impulse than an identical RMB would have
produced in 2016 or 2020, independent of the credit surge's own headline size. `partCDEFH.md`'s own
Part H already states the resulting honest uncertainty plainly: "whether the post-property China
still pulses through commodities at all... the 2022–26 record (cases chapter) suggests weakening" —
this Part's own steel-consumption-share figures are the specific evidence behind that sentence.

**"New productive forces" and the manufacturing-export tilt.** Xi Jinping first used the phrase
**"new quality productive forces"** during an inspection trip to Heilongjiang Province in
**September 2023**, and deployed it on **more than twenty occasions between September 2023 and
March 2024** — a genuinely central, not incidental, reframing of China's own growth model away from
investment/property-led expansion and toward technology-led manufacturing, structured around three
layers (technology and innovation, future industrial development, industrial chains). The concrete
expression of this pivot is the **"new three"** export sectors: China accounts for **more than 80%**
of global solar-cell exports, **more than 50%** of lithium-ion battery exports, and **more than 20%**
of EV exports. Solar-module shipments alone reached **114 GW in H1 2023**, a **34%** increase over
the 85 GW shipped in H1 2022; battery production capacity **exceeded 2 TWh in 2024**, some **60%**
above actual global demand, with planned capacity above **6 TWh** — enough, on current projections,
to meet global battery-cell demand through **2035**. The US response — tariffs of **100% on EVs,
50% on photovoltaic products, and 25% on lithium-ion batteries**, effective **May 2024** — is the
clearest single marker of how this export tilt has reshaped China's trade friction with the
developed world; the same dynamic, differently expressed, is this record's own bridge to India.

**India's DIFFERENT transmission — competition, not complementarity, stated honestly.** Every prior
pulse in this record transmits to India through **complementary** channels: a China commodity boom
raises the price of what India imports (a terms-of-trade cost) while lifting the value of what a
narrow slice of Indian equities (metals, mining) produces — the "double sign, one macro state" idea
`commodity-deep`'s own B1 names as the organizing structure of every prior episode. The
manufacturing-export tilt breaks that structure: China's overcapacity in solar cells, batteries, and
EVs does not raise a price India profits from selling; it lowers a price India is trying to
manufacture domestically, placing India's own industrial-policy goals (the PLI-linked solar and EV
manufacturing push) in **direct competition** with subsidized Chinese export capacity rather than in
a complementary terms-of-trade relationship. The evidence is concrete and dated: India's Directorate
General of Trade Remedies finalized anti-dumping duties on Chinese solar cells and modules on
**30 September 2025**, structured in three tiers (**0%, 23%, and 30%**, valid three years); the
investigation found Chinese-origin imports made up **77%** of India's total solar imports during the
review period (**30,723 MW**), and — the single most striking figure in this record's entire
India-transmission analysis — that China's own **unutilized manufacturing capacity equaled 576% of
India's total domestic solar demand**, a scale of overcapacity no tariff schedule can fully offset.
India's own policy response carries the tension this new transmission channel creates in miniature:
the February 2025 Budget **cut** solar-cell import duties from 25% to 20% and module duties from
40% to 20% (a cost-of-solar-deployment concession) even as anti-dumping duties layered on top of the
same imports specifically to blunt Chinese pricing (a domestic-manufacturer protection). On the EV
side, China formally challenged India's EV and battery-cell subsidy measures at the WTO in
**October 2025**, alleging the measures breach "national treatment" and constitute prohibited
"import-substitution subsidies" — India, in other words, is now the **defendant** in a trade dispute
over its own industrial-policy response to Chinese export competition, the mirror image of every
earlier pulse's transmission structure, where India's own policy responses (gold-import curbs,
FCNR(B) swaps, rate defenses) answered a terms-of-trade or portfolio-flow shock rather than an
industrial-competition one. `partCDEFH.md`'s own design note (CN-D3) already flags this as an open
question — "the export-competition channel's India sign... noted, watched, never assumed" — and this
Part's own contribution is the first documented evidence that the sign has, in the solar and EV
sectors specifically, already flipped from complementary to competitive.

**Where the cadence stands, 2025–26.** China's own credit impulse — the standard operationalization
behind atlas 2.11 and `partCDEFH.md`'s own Part D construction (Δ of the 12-month credit flow over
GDP) — dipped into negative territory in late 2024 before turning up again through Q1 2025, but by
contemporary characterization with **"smaller increases than during previous credit cycles,"**
reflecting a **"more cautious stance on local government borrowing"** and a stated reluctance to
return to "flood-style" credit expansion of the kind pulse 1 exemplifies. China's own fiscal impulse
turned positive at roughly **1.4% of GDP in 2025**, up from a sharply negative starting point in
mid-2023 `[VERIFY: the precise fiscal-impulse methodology and its exact 2025 point estimate — this
figure recurs across contemporary analysis but was not independently re-derived from a primary
fiscal account this pass]`; GDP growth estimates for 2025–26 diverge sharply by source, from
sub-3% full-year 2025 growth on one reading to a 4.5%-to-4.0% (2025-to-2026) glide path on another
`[VERIFY: reconciling these materially different growth readings — both recur in contemporary
analysis and were not reconciled to a single authoritative print this pass]`, with policymakers
signaling "special actions to boost consumption" and household-income support as 2026 priorities —
a domestic-demand emphasis consistent with, rather than a reversal of, the manufacturing-export tilt
documented above. As of this writing (2 September 2026), no discrete event in this window rises to
the scale of a pulse 1/3/4-style stimulus announcement; the 16-point plan (November 2022) and the
September 2024 package are each better read as **incremental deepenings of an ongoing property-bust
support effort** than as fresh pulses in their own right — the honest characterization §B4's own
cadence discussion builds on directly.

**Pulse length.** Read as one continuous era rather than discrete pulses, 2022–2026 spans, as of
this writing, **close to four years and counting**, with no tightening-driven end date visible in
the record — itself the era's own defining departure from pulses 1–4, each of which ran its course
to a dated tightening trigger inside two to three years.

### The honest cadence read — argued from the record, no date claims

The atlas's own framing (row 2.11) states "~3–4y policy pulses; n≈4–5 since 2008." This record's
own five-pulse spacing, measured trigger-to-trigger, is **not** a clean repeating rhythm: Nov 2008
to Dec 2011 is roughly **37 months** (~3.1 years, inside the 3–4y band); Dec 2011 to the 2015
PSL/supply-side launch is roughly **42 months** (~3.5 years, still inside the band); the 2015 launch
to the COVID pulse's early-2020 start is roughly **54–60 months** (~4.5–5 years — already **beyond**
the stated band); and the COVID pulse's own start to the November 2022 16-point plan is roughly
**34 months** (~2.8 years — **inside** the band again, though on the fast side). Taken at face
value, this reads as noisy but not obviously broken: three of four inter-pulse gaps land near or
inside the stated 3–4y range, and only the pulse-3-to-pulse-4 gap runs meaningfully long.

But the more informative reading is not the spacing number itself — it is **what changed about
pulse 5's own shape relative to pulses 1–4**. Every one of the first four pulses shows a clean
trigger→surge→target→commodity-signature→tightening arc that concludes inside two to three years
with an identifiable tightening event (an RRR-hike sequence, a deliberate interbank squeeze, a
deleveraging campaign, a "three red lines" policy). Pulse 5 shows no such arc: the November 2022 and
September 2024 events are each better described, on this record's own evidence, as **incremental
deepenings of continuous property-bust management** than as fresh stimulus pulses with their own
distinct commodity-price consequence — precisely because, as §B2's own steel-consumption-share
figures document, the sector each easing round is aimed at (property) now absorbs a structurally
smaller share of the economy's metals demand than it did during any of pulses 1, 3, or 4. This is
not the same claim as "the 3–4 year rhythm has lengthened" (a claim `commodity-deep`'s own decade-
scale cycle, or `fincycle-deep`'s own FC2 lengthening finding on the *financial* cycle, might
support with different evidence) — it is the different, and more specific, claim that **the pulse
object itself is dissolving from a discrete, dateable cycle into a continuous state variable**, the
same evolution CYCLE_ATLAS.md's own epistemics section (§0) describes for exactly this kind of
transition: a phenomenon that once cleared the clock test's own bar (four-plus observed complete
periods, arguably present across pulses 1–4) can still lose that status going forward if its own
generating mechanism changes — here, because the domestic target (property) that gave each of the
first four pulses its clean commodity-transmission signature is now structurally smaller, and the
replacement target (manufacturing-export capacity) transmits to commodities and to India through a
qualitatively different channel (competition, not complementarity) that this record's own evidence
(§B2, pulse 5) shows only beginning to take shape. The honest verdict this Part owes the ladder: the
3–4 year rhythm was real and reasonably clean across pulses 1–4; whether pulse 5 represents a fifth
instance of the same rhythm running long, or the rhythm's own dissolution into a different kind of
state entirely, **cannot be settled from the record available as of September 2026** — and a design
that assumed either answer in advance would be fitting a story onto four-and-a-bit observations,
exactly the caution `partCDEFH.md`'s own Part H already states ("whether the post-property China
still pulses through commodities at all... the watch is annual").
