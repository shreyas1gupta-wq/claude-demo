## Headline findings

- **The squeeze (2016-2021) was a coherent, escalating sequence, not one policy**: the "houses are
  for living in, not speculation" doctrine (Dec 2016) framed six years of tightening that ran from
  blunt purchase/price curbs (2016-17) to balance-sheet-level developer caps (Three Red Lines, Aug
  2020) to bank-level exposure caps (Dec 2020/Jan 2021) to auction-mechanism reform (centralized
  land sales, Feb 2021) — each layer targeting a different actor (buyers, then developers, then
  banks, then local governments) `[2-SOURCE]` throughout.
- **The rescue (Nov 2022-2026) has been the mirror image but has not reversed the price/volume
  decline**: new-home prices have now fallen for essentially the whole rescue period (China's
  70-city NBS index posted its 28th/29th consecutive monthly year-on-year decline in Oct/Nov 2025)
  `[2-SOURCE]`, and 2025 new-home sales hit a 10-year low, roughly half the 2021 peak in value
  terms `[2-SOURCE]`.
- **Announced size is routinely 10-100x actual uptake on the voluntary programmes — this is the
  single most important and most under-reported fact in the whole sequence.** The 2023 100bn-yuan
  pilot re-lending scheme (8 cities) used only ~2bn yuan (~2% of the facility) by end-March 2024
  `[2-SOURCE]`; its 2024 successor, the flagship 300bn-yuan (~$41bn) facility, saw only a handful
  of cities actually transact and was independently described by two outlets as "falling flat" /
  "disappointing" `[2-SOURCE]`. By contrast, the "white list" project-financing mechanism and the
  Sept-2024 existing-mortgage-rate cut were NOT voluntary/opt-in and DID disburse or execute at
  scale — the announced-vs-funded gap is a story about which mechanism design, not a blanket claim
  that "nothing worked."
- **The property tax is the policy that never came, twice.** The 2011 Shanghai/Chongqing pilots
  generated revenue equal to roughly 3% of local fiscal income even in their best case `[2-SOURCE]`
  — essentially symbolic — and the October 2021 NPC authorization to expand the pilot to more
  cities was quietly shelved by March 2022 `[2-SOURCE]` and, as of a 2026 Nikkei/Caixin report, the
  enabling law is still absent from the NPC's legislative programme `[1-SOURCE]`. This is despite
  land-sale revenue — the tax's obvious replacement target — collapsing from a ~RMB 8.5-8.7
  trillion peak in 2021 to ~RMB 4.15-4.9 trillion in 2024/2025, a drop exceeding 50% `[2-SOURCE]`.
- **Every classic demand-side lever is now at or near its practical floor**: nationwide mortgage
  rate floor removed (May 2024), first-home down payment at 15% (the lowest ever recorded)
  `[2-SOURCE]`, and purchase restrictions lifted in all but a handful of city cores (parts of
  Beijing/Shanghai/Shenzhen/Hainan) `[2-SOURCE]`. The August 2026 policy shift — mandating
  topped-out buildings before presale and pushing a move away from the presale model entirely — is
  best read as evidence policy has pivoted from "cut the cost of buying" (exhausted) to
  "restructure how homes are sold" (a supply-side/structural lever, not a demand stimulus) `[2-SOURCE]`.
- **Assessments from named analysts and institutions are consistently more skeptical than the
  headline announcements imply**: the IMF called Beijing's measures "steps in the right direction"
  but explicitly "not sufficient," well short of its own recommended developer-support package of
  ~5% of GDP over 3-5 years `[1-SOURCE]`; Gavekal's Rosealea Yao expects the adjustment to run
  "another 2-3 years" `[1-SOURCE]`; Rhodium's Logan Wright notes housing starts collapsed 69% by
  2024 and states plainly that even if "the worst of the policy tightening is behind us," he is "not
  sure we can say the worst of the financial or economic fallout is behind us" `[1-SOURCE]`.

## Q1. "Houses are for living in, not for speculation" — origin and aftermath

Xi Jinping introduced the phrase 房住不炒 at the Central Economic Work Conference in **December
2016** `[2-SOURCE: Wikipedia's dedicated page + multiple press/secondary accounts converge on Dec
2016]`. He elevated it to a national-level slogan at the **19th National Congress of the CCP,
October 2017**, declaring "houses are built to be lived in, not to be speculated on" `[2-SOURCE]`.
What followed, directly traceable to the doctrine: the Sept-Oct 2016 wave of purchase restrictions
had actually preceded the phrase's formal articulation (it crystallized an already-underway
policy stance rather than starting one from zero), but from 2017 onward it became the standing
justification cited for essentially every tightening measure through 2021 (purchase restrictions,
price-filing caps, the "one city, one policy" framework, the Three Red Lines, and the bank
concentration caps) — it has functioned, per one industry-press characterization, as "a policy
mantra for Beijing for close to a decade" `[1-SOURCE]` and is still invoked in 2026 guidance
documents, in modified form, even as the substance of policy has shifted to rescue.

## Q2. Dated list of tightening measures, 2016-2021

See the timeline table above for the full dated list with sourcing. Summary of the six layers,
each escalating to a different actor in the chain:
1. **Buyer-level purchase restrictions and price caps**, Sept-Oct 2016 wave across 21 cities,
   tightened repeatedly through 2017-18 `[2-SOURCE]`.
2. **"One city, one policy" framework**, formalizing local discretion within the national
   "houses for living" stance `[1-SOURCE]` on exact origin timing.
3. **Developer balance-sheet caps — the Three Red Lines**, August 2020 `[2-SOURCE]`.
4. **Bank-level exposure caps**, Dec 2020/Jan 2021 (property-loan and mortgage concentration
   ceilings) `[2-SOURCE]`.
5. **Land-auction mechanism reform — the "two centralizations"**, Feb 2021, 22 pilot cities
   `[2-SOURCE]`.
6. **Presale-fund escrow tightening**, through 2021, as local governments reacted to Evergrande
   contagion fears by trapping more developer cash on-site `[2-SOURCE]` on direction, `[1-SOURCE]`
   on it being an explicit 2021 reaction.

## Q3. Did the Three Red Lines CAUSE the bust or REVEAL it?

Both sourced framings exist and are presented here without adjudication beyond what the sources
themselves say.

**The "caused/triggered it" case**: multiple outlets describe the policy in direct causal
language — the WEF's explainer states the Evergrande crisis "was sparked by a decision of the
Chinese government in August [2020] to tighten... access to credit," and a substack analysis
frames the episode as "state-led capitalism's moment of truth" precipitated by the policy itself
`[2-SOURCE]` on the causal framing existing in reputable commentary. The mechanism: firms that had
built their entire growth model on rolling over short-term debt against land banks were, within
one policy announcement, cut off from the marginal financing that kept the model solvent — a
classic liquidity-run trigger.

**The "revealed pre-existing rot" case**: CKGSB's own multi-part professor-authored series frames
the Three Red Lines as "chapter 1" of a longer, structural story, implying the policy's role was to
expose rather than create the underlying leverage problem; separate reporting on Evergrande
explicitly attributes its collapse to "its own internal management problems," with the company's
crisis merely "the tip of the iceberg" of a liquidity crisis "affecting the entire real estate
industry" that pre-existed the policy `[2-SOURCE]` on this framing appearing independently.

**A reasonable synthesis** (not itself a new sourced claim, but consistent with both sides): the
policy is very likely the **proximate trigger** of the timing and speed of the crisis — Evergrande
had been over-leveraged for years and the specific August 2020/Dec 2020-Jan 2021 caps removed the
refinancing channel that had let it keep rolling debt — while the **scale** of what was exposed
(a sector-wide dependence on presale cash and land-bank appreciation rather than operating
cashflow) was a pre-existing structural condition the policy did not create. Rhodium's Logan Wright
gives a scale marker consistent with "revealed something large": housing starts collapsed 69% by
2024, a decline of a magnitude no single 2020 regulatory tweak could mechanically cause on its own
`[1-SOURCE]`.

## Q5. Announced versus actually funded — the crux

This is answered in full in the announced-vs-funded table above. The clearest, most load-bearing,
best-corroborated finding: **voluntary, opt-in, SOE/local-government-executed programmes have
seen severe under-utilization** — the 100bn-yuan pilot's ~2% utilization `[2-SOURCE]` and the
300bn-yuan facility's single-digit-city initial participation `[2-SOURCE]` are the two cleanest
examples, and both are corroborated by named mechanism explanations (local governments already
carrying an estimated ~$9 trillion debt burden and reluctant to add low-return social-housing
exposure; banks unwilling to underwrite the credit risk of loss-making SOE purchases; CreditSights'
Zerlina Zeng quoted on exactly this point: "high execution risk given banks and local SOEs have to
fully bear the credit and investment risks" `[1-SOURCE]` for the direct quote, `[2-SOURCE]` for the
underlying mechanism being reported independently). By contrast, **administratively executed,
non-opt-in measures — the existing-mortgage rate cut being the clearest example — appear to have
been delivered close to announced scale**, because there was no local actor with discretion to
decline. The white-list mechanism sits in between: its own reported cumulative "approved loan"
figures have kept pace with or exceeded successive targets, but the desk could not verify in this
search pass whether "approved" is fully identical to "disbursed and drawn down" — flagged as an
open question, not resolved as either a pass or a fail.

## Q6. Mortgage floor, down payments, purchase restrictions — what's left to cut?

- **Mortgage rate floor**: removed nationwide, May 2024 `[2-SOURCE]`. Nothing left to cut on this
  specific lever — a floor cannot be removed twice.
- **Down payment ratios**: cut from a 30%/40% (1st/2nd home) tightening regime in 2016 to a
  unified 20%/30% in Aug 2023, then to **15%/25% in May 2024**, then second-home cut further to
  **15%** in Sep 2024 `[2-SOURCE]`. 15% is described in the sourced reporting as the lowest ratio
  in the modern history of Chinese mortgage policy `[1-SOURCE]` on the explicit "lowest ever"
  characterization, `[2-SOURCE]` on the sequence of cuts themselves. There is very little
  remaining room before a down payment ratio stops meaningfully protecting lender loss-given-
  default.
- **Purchase restrictions**: by late 2024, all cities except pockets of Beijing, Shanghai,
  Shenzhen and parts of Hainan had fully removed restrictions `[2-SOURCE]`; Guangzhou (Sept 2024)
  was the first tier-1 city to remove ALL restrictions. The remaining restricted zones are the
  most politically symbolic (the three largest cities' cores), so what is left to cut here is
  small in national-market terms but disproportionately watched.
- **Conclusion — is policy near-exhausted on this axis?** Yes, on the classic demand-cost axis.
  The August 2026 shift — mandating topped-out construction before presale, extending mortgage
  terms to 40 years, and structurally moving away from the presale model — is best read as the
  policy apparatus moving to a **different axis entirely** (supply-side/structural reform of how
  homes are built and sold) precisely because the demand-cost axis (rate floors, down payments,
  purchase eligibility) has little room left `[2-SOURCE]` on the reform itself; the "exhaustion"
  characterization is this dossier's synthesis of that fact, not itself a quoted claim.

## Q7. The "white list" mechanism — announced versus extended

Launched January 2024. The approved-loan figure grew from **RMB 2.23 trillion (~$313bn) as of Oct
16, 2024**, against a stated year-end target of **RMB 4 trillion**, to **RMB 3.6 trillion
(~$494.8bn) by end-Nov 2024**, to **over RMB 5 trillion by Jan 13, 2025**, to **over RMB 7 trillion
(~$1 trillion) by Oct 2025** per MOHURD `[2-SOURCE]` on the mechanism and general trajectory,
`[1-SOURCE]` on each individual snapshot number (each appeared in this search pass via one primary
release, echoed by wire pickups that are not independent of it). This is the one major rescue
mechanism in this dossier where cumulative "approved" figures have consistently met or exceeded
prior targets rather than falling short — a genuine contrast with the SOE-purchase programmes. The
important caveat, not resolved here: government and press releases describe these as loans
**approved**, and this search pass did not surface a clean, separately sourced disbursed/drawn-down
figure distinct from "approved" — so the white list should be read as "the best-funded of the
voluntary-adjacent mechanisms," not proven to be fully disbursed at the same scale as approved.

## Q8. 2024-2025 state-purchase-of-unsold-homes and idle-land-buyback programmes

**State purchase of unsold homes**: covered in Q5/the tables. Uptake reported as low for BOTH the
2023 100bn-yuan pilot (~2% utilized) and the headline 2024 300bn-yuan facility (only ~5 cities
transacted initially; ~29 of 200+ urged cities responded after 3+ months) `[2-SOURCE]` each.
Reported reasons for low uptake, independently corroborated across sources: (i) local governments,
already carrying large debt loads (cited informally as ~$9 trillion), are reluctant to take on
low-return social-housing assets; (ii) banks are unwilling to bear the credit/investment risk of
SOE purchases of what may be loss-making inventory; (iii) most unsold inventory sits in
lower-tier cities with weak/declining population demand, while the strongest demand for affordable
housing is in tier-1 cities that do not have much excess inventory to buy `[2-SOURCE]` across
these three mechanism explanations collectively (individual explanations vary `[1-SOURCE]` to
`[2-SOURCE]`).

**Idle-land buyback**: special-purpose bonds re-permitted for this use in October 2024 after a
5-year pause. Scale-up in 2025: 28 provinces/cities had planned to issue >RMB 770bn of such bonds,
with >RMB 335bn already sold as of the report's date, and a full-year 2025 projection of RMB
600bn-1.2 trillion `[1-SOURCE]`. Unlike the home-purchase schemes, land buyback appears to have
seen comparatively strong uptake — some Guangdong tranches saw bid-to-cover ratios above 17x
`[1-SOURCE]` — though this measures appetite for the BOND (a government security), not proof that
the underlying policy goal (clearing land inventory at economically meaningful prices) is working;
land is being bought back at a discount of 80% or less of the original developer purchase price in
most reported cases `[1-SOURCE]`.

## Q9. The property tax that never came

**The 2011 pilots**: Shanghai and Chongqing both launched property-tax pilots on **January 28,
2011** `[2-SOURCE]`. Design differed sharply: **Shanghai** taxed only newly-purchased homes (a
levy on locals buying a 2nd+ home and on non-resident purchasers), at roughly **0.4-0.6%**
depending on the source consulted `[1-SOURCE]` on the precise rate, `[2-SOURCE]` on the general
"newly-purchased, low single-digit percent" description; **Chongqing** taxed both new AND existing
homes but targeted only large/high-end properties, exempting small/low-priced homes, at rates
described as **0.5-1.2%** `[2-SOURCE]`. Effects: one study found Shanghai's tax reduced average
house prices by roughly 11-15% `[1-SOURCE]`; Chongqing's luxury-only design is reported to have
produced a substitution effect that arguably raised the prices of ordinary (untaxed) homes
`[1-SOURCE]`. Revenue: pilot-city property-tax revenue came to roughly **3% of local fiscal
income** in the best-documented case (Shanghai's first year) `[2-SOURCE]` — essentially symbolic,
not a viable land-sales replacement at the piloted design and rates.

**The 2021 expansion and its shelving**: the NPC Standing Committee authorized the State Council
on **October 23, 2021** to pilot property taxes in additional (unspecified) cities, with some
analysts expecting a start by end-2021 `[2-SOURCE]`. By **March 16, 2022**, the Finance Ministry
stated there were "no suitable conditions to pilot the property tax reform in more cities this
year," effectively shelving the expansion `[2-SOURCE]` — a reversal inside five months of the
authorization, amid the deepening real-estate downturn and the absence of any detailed State
Council implementation plan.

**State of play, 2026**: per a Nikkei/Caixin report, the enabling law that would give a nationwide
property tax a legal basis is **still absent from the NPC's legislative programme** as of early
2026 `[1-SOURCE]` — i.e., the expansion remains shelved, not merely delayed by a fixed period.

**Why it is the obvious fiscal replacement**: local-government land-sale revenue fell from a peak
of **RMB ~8.49-8.7 trillion in 2021** to **RMB ~4.87-4.9 trillion in 2024** and an estimated
**RMB ~4.15 trillion in 2025** — a decline exceeding 50% `[2-SOURCE]`. Land sales had supplied
roughly 20-30% of local government revenue nationally over 2012-2021, and reportedly up to 75% in
some individual localities `[1-SOURCE]`. A recurring, broad-based property tax is the standard
fiscal-theory replacement for a collapsing one-off land-transfer revenue stream.

**Why it is politically near-impossible**: (i) the direct policy conflict — a property tax would
itself further depress the land values and buyer demand that local governments still depend on for
whatever land-sale revenue remains, undermining the very revenue base being protected `[1-SOURCE]`
on this specific mechanism; (ii) technical/administrative difficulty — there is no comprehensive
registry distinguishing which units are owner-occupied, empty, or investment properties, which a
workable tax needs `[1-SOURCE]`; (iii) a documented misalignment between economists (who favor
reform on efficiency/revenue grounds) and local officials (who weight social and political
stability risk far more heavily) `[1-SOURCE]`; (iv) broad-based multi-home ownership among
politically influential urban households creates a natural constituency against any levy that
would be visible and recurring, unlike a one-time land-transfer fee paid by developers
`[RECALL — unverified as a directly sourced claim in this pass, though consistent with the
Lincoln Institute/Forbes framing found]`.

## Q10. Sourced assessments of effectiveness

- **IMF (April 2025)**: described Beijing's property measures as "steps in the right direction"
  but explicitly "not sufficient" — falling well short of the IMF's own recommended developer-
  support package of roughly 5% of GDP over 3-5 years `[1-SOURCE]`.
- **Gavekal Dragonomics' Rosealea Yao**: the property-market adjustment "looks far from over,"
  likely lasting "another 2-3 years"; China's correction has already been deeper and faster than
  the global historical average; local-government debt constraints and fragmented regional
  control over presale funds continue to limit the effectiveness of stimulus `[1-SOURCE]`.
- **Rhodium Group's Logan Wright**: housing starts collapsed 69% by 2024; "the worst of the policy
  tightening is behind us, but I am not sure we can say the worst of the financial or economic
  fallout is behind us" `[1-SOURCE]`.
- **S&P Global Ratings**: the measures announced are "unlikely to change the economic growth
  outlook in a major way," and given weak sentiment and a large stock of unsold housing, the steps
  taken are "unlikely to stabilize the housing market in the near future" `[1-SOURCE]`; separately,
  S&P projects primary home prices to fall 1.5-2.5% and secondary home prices 4-5% in 2026
  `[1-SOURCE]`.
- **CreditSights' Zerlina Zeng**, on the SOE-purchase schemes specifically: "high execution risk
  given banks and local SOEs have to fully bear the credit and investment risks" `[1-SOURCE]`.
- **Measured against outcomes, not sentiment**: new-home prices (NBS 70-city index) posted
  consecutive year-on-year declines through essentially the entire rescue period, including a
  28th/29th straight monthly decline in Oct/Nov 2025 (2.2%, then 2.4% y/y) `[2-SOURCE]`; new
  commercial-housing sales value fell to a 10-year low in 2025, roughly half the 2021 peak in value
  terms (floor space sold down 8.7% y/y to 881 million sqm; sales value down 12.6% y/y to RMB 8.39
  trillion) `[2-SOURCE]`. On the desk's own preferred metrics — prices, volumes, inventory — the
  sourced verdict is that the rescue has **slowed the rate of decline in places (price declines have
  moderated from earlier double-digit-adjacent y/y prints to the 2-3% range by late 2025) but has
  not reversed it**, and analysts across institutional (IMF, S&P), independent research (Gavekal,
  Rhodium) and credit (CreditSights) perspectives converge on "not yet sufficient" rather than
  either "failed completely" or "has worked."
