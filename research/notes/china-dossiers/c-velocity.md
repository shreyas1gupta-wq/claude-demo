## Headline findings

- **Official index, national, cumulative bust to date: -21.8% nominal / -24.6% real from the
  Sept-2021 peak to Q1 2026** `[2-SOURCE]`, annualizing to **-5.3%/yr nominal, -6.1%/yr real**
  over the ~4.5-year window. The unofficial/private read on the SAME cumulative question is
  **~-40%**, nearly double `[1-SOURCE, but see reconciliation in Q4]`, annualizing to **-10.7%/yr**.
- **The boom that preceded it ran hot but unevenly by tier**: the 2015-2016 tier-1 spike
  (Shenzhen +63.4% YoY at its single peak month) annualizes to roughly **+30%/yr** at the tier-1
  aggregate level over its 16-month core window `[2-SOURCE]`, while the broader national 70-city
  index's 37-month 2015-2018 upswing annualizes to a much tamer **+6.2%/yr** `[1-SOURCE
  arithmetic on a 2-SOURCE cumulative figure]`.
- **The BOOM-VS-BUST RATIO the desk needs is NOT ONE NUMBER — it inverts depending on which pair
  of official-vs-official or official-vs-private figures you compare** (full arithmetic in Q10):
  national-index-only gives **~1.17x** (boom slightly faster, close to the desk's ~0.99x base
  rate); official boom vs. private-estimate bust gives **~0.58x** (bust almost twice as fast as
  the boom); the tier-1 mania-vs-grind comparison gives **~5.5x** (an outlier, not a like-for-like
  window).
- **Sales VOLUME and VALUE collapsed faster than the price index did**: floor space sold fell
  from 1,794 million sqm (2021) to 881 million sqm (2025), and sales value fell from CNY 18.19tn
  to CNY 8.39tn over the same 4 years — both official NBS primary data `[2-SOURCE]` — annualizing
  to **-16.3%/yr (volume)** and **-17.6%/yr (value)**, roughly 3x the pace of the official price
  index's decline. This is the quantity/price asymmetry classic to a controlled-price crash.
- **The current decline streak is 37 consecutive months (MoM) as of the July-2026 print**
  `[2-SOURCE]`, but this is the SECOND leg of the downturn, not one unbroken slide from the 2021
  peak — an earlier decline phase ran 2021-2023 before a brief stabilization, and the current
  unbroken streak dates to roughly mid-2023, a distinction the source material repeatedly blurs.
- **Steepest single month (MoM): -0.7% in May 2024** (the worst since Oct 2014) `[2-SOURCE]`.
  **Steepest 12-month (YoY) window: -5.9% in October 2024** (the worst since April 2015)
  `[2-SOURCE]`. Both records stand as of the latest data found (through July/August 2026).

## Q3. Consecutive months of decline as of the latest data

**37 consecutive months of MoM decline as of the July 2026 print** `[2-SOURCE]`. The count
sourced across several prints is internally consistent and monotone: Dec 2025 = 30th, Feb 2026 =
32nd, Apr 2026 = 34th, May 2026 = 35th, Jul 2026 = 37th `[2-SOURCE for the endpoints; the
intermediate months interpolate exactly, which is reassuring rather than assumed]`. One search
result mislabeled May 2026 as the "34th" month, which does not fit the otherwise-consistent
count and is flagged as an aggregator error, not used.

**Important dating caveat, not to be blurred with Q1's "since the 2021 peak" framing**: back-
counting 37 months from July 2026 puts the START of this unbroken streak at roughly **July
2023**, not August/September 2021. The market fell for a first stretch after the 2021 peak (the
Evergrande-crisis-era decline, itself running well over a year per the "home price slump reaches
a year" Sept-2022 marker), which was followed by a partial, uneven stabilization attempt in early-
to-mid 2023, before the second, still-ongoing leg began. Sources describing "3 years of decline"
or "5 years of decline since the 2021 peak" are using the price-LEVEL framing (prices are still
below the peak) rather than the streak-of-consecutive-declining-months framing used for this
question — both are legitimate but answer different things, and this dossier keeps them separate.

## Q4. Cumulative peak-to-latest decline: official vs. private, reconciled

**Official, nominal**: BIS/NBS-tracked national index fell from 145.913 (Sept 2021 peak,
2010=100) to ~114 (Q1 2026), a **-21.8% cumulative decline** `[2-SOURCE: BIS series via FRED
description + a secondary aggregator quoting the same two index levels]`.

**Official, real (CPI-deflated)**: the equivalent BIS real index fell from ~113 (2021 peak) to
~85.1 (Q1 2026), a **-24.6% cumulative decline**, putting the real index BELOW its own 2005
starting level (100) — i.e., in real terms, 20+ years of national price gains have been given
back `[2-SOURCE: two independently-published outlets citing the same underlying BIS series]`.
A third framing from the same underlying data: "85% of the gains made between 2011 and 2021 have
vanished" `[1-SOURCE]`.

**Private/unofficial**: a Stanford SCCEI China Briefs analysis frames the gap explicitly as
**"20% (official) to 40% (unofficial)"** cumulative decline, used directly to compute a household-
consumption-loss estimate of 2-4% of GDP `[1-SOURCE — this exact "20/40" framing appeared
identically worded across two different URLs (a Substack mirror and the FSI-hosted original),
which is the SAME underlying analysis republished, not independent corroboration; flagged rather
than double-counted]`. A separately-sourced city-level claim, attributed to a property-market
columnist, states **"in many cities, prices have fallen more than 40% from their peak, and some
have dropped more than 50%"** `[2-SOURCE: the same quote surfaced in two separate search passes
via the Asia Times piece]` — directionally consistent with, but not the same measurement as, the
national 20/40 framing (this is a distribution-of-cities claim, not a national aggregate).

**Reconciling the gap** (roughly 20-22% official vs. ~40% private, i.e., private runs close to
double): the search results converge on three named mechanisms, none original to this dossier —
(1) **index construction**: the NBS/BIS national index is a same-quality-mix, 70-city AVERAGE
that is pulled up by resilient tier-1 pockets (Shanghai still posting positive YoY prints even in
2026 — see Q1) while tier-3/4 and distressed cities do the real damage; (2) **administrative price
floors**: multiple sources describe local governments limiting how far developers may officially
discount list prices, so recorded transaction prices in the official series understate actual
market-clearing prices, with the gap made up through informal mechanisms (below-the-table
rebates, "loan-backed home sales," bundled discounts) `[2-SOURCE — the floor-price mechanism and
the "actual declines are roughly double" characterization both recurred]`; (3) **distressed-
channel evidence**: court-ordered auction sales — a genuinely transacted, hard-to-fake price —
sell at ~30% below comparable secondary-market prices nationally, and 50-60% below in second-
and third-tier cities `[1-SOURCE]`, which is directly consistent with, and lends real-transaction
credibility to, the "unofficial ~40-50%" reads above.

## Q5. Sales volume velocity: CNY ~18tn 2021 peak to latest annual figure

**Value**: NBS's own 2021 communiqué gives commodity-housing sales value at **CNY 18,193
billion** (+4.8% YoY, a record) `[2-SOURCE: primary NBS release + secondary reporting quoting the
same "18.2 trillion" figure]`. The 2025 full-year equivalent: **CNY 8.39 trillion**, down 12.6%
YoY for 2025 alone and described repeatedly as "roughly half" the 2021 peak / a 10-year low
`[2-SOURCE: NBS-sourced government release + Caixin]`. Cumulative decline: **(8.39-18.19)/18.19 =
-53.9%** over 4 years, annualizing to **(8.39/18.19)^(1/4) − 1 = -17.6%/yr**. A narrower cut,
residential-ONLY sales value for 2025, is separately reported at CNY 7.33 trillion (-13.0% YoY)
`[1-SOURCE]` — this is NOT directly comparable to the 18.19tn 2021 figure, which is the ALL-
commodity-housing total (residential + office + commercial); no 2021 residential-only total was
found in this search pass, so the value comparison above uses the like-for-like all-commodity-
housing total on both ends.

**Volume (floor space)**: NBS's own releases give **1,794.33 million sqm sold in 2021** (+1.9%
YoY, itself a record) and **881.01 million sqm sold in 2025** (-8.7% YoY for 2025 alone, of which
residential floor space specifically fell -9.2%) `[2-SOURCE: both figures are primary NBS
press-release numbers, independently retrieved]`. Cumulative decline: **(881.01-1794.33)/1794.33
= -50.9%**, annualizing to **(881.01/1794.33)^(1/4) − 1 = -16.3%/yr**.

Value fell slightly faster than volume (-17.6%/yr vs -16.3%/yr) because it compounds both the
volume collapse AND the price decline — consistent with, and a useful cross-check on, the price-
index figures in Q4.

## Q7. The 2015-2016 boom specifically: fastest appreciation months and where

**Shenzhen, April 2016: +63.4% YoY** — the single fastest reading found `[2-SOURCE]`. By October
2016, YoY prints across the tier-1 cohort were still extreme: Shenzhen +31.7%, Shanghai +31.1%,
Beijing +27.5%, Guangzhou +23.6% `[2-SOURCE]`. On a longer base, Shenzhen new-build prices are
reported to have risen **+177.5% cumulative from October 2014 to September 2016** (¥22,000/sqm to
¥62,000/sqm) `[1-SOURCE]` — a ~23 month window, annualizing to roughly +73%/yr on that specific
two-point comparison (desk arithmetic: (62000/22000)^(12/23)−1 ≈ +73%/yr; flagged as a single
city's raw ASP move on an unusually long doubling-plus base, not the index-based tier-1 aggregate
used elsewhere in this dossier). October 2016 was independently described as the fastest national
new-home price growth "since record-keeping began in 2011" `[1-SOURCE]`.

## Q8. The 2016-2018 tier-3 shantytown (棚改) boom and PSL

Two DIFFERENT measures give two different growth rates for the same Jan 2016-May 2018 window —
flagged explicitly as the nominal/index conflation the corroboration rule warns against:
- **Average selling price (ASP, ¥/sqm)** for 60 tier-3/4 cities: **+28.1%**, from ¥7,008/sqm
  (Jan 2016) to ¥8,976/sqm (May 2018) `[1-SOURCE]` — annualizing to **+11.2%/yr**. ASP mixes in
  changes to the composition of what's selling (more/less floor area, unit type), so it is NOT
  a quality-adjusted price move.
- **NBS tier-3 price index proper**, same window: **+15.9%** `[1-SOURCE]`, annualizing to
  **+6.5%/yr** — the smaller, index-based (quality-held-constant) figure, closer to the national
  70-city aggregate's own +6.2%/yr for a similar span (Q6).
- A regional breakdown of tier-3/4 ASP by geography (same source as the ASP figure, so
  `[1-SOURCE]` throughout): eastern-China tier-3/4 cities close to major urban centers +54.6%,
  far from centers +22.5%; central/western tier-3/4 close +51.4%, far +32.8% — a materially wider
  spread than the national tier-3 average, meaning the shantytown-boom effect was highly uneven
  even within "tier-3."

**Mechanism (PSL)**: Pledged Supplementary Lending, funneled mainly through the China Development
Bank, financed the shantytown-redevelopment monetized-resettlement program. Monetary (cash)
compensation rose from ~9% of all redevelopment compensation in 2014 to over 50% in 2017
`[1-SOURCE]`, and one academic estimate attributes roughly **+3.4 percentage points** of 2017's
national residential-sales growth rate directly to the monetized-resettlement cash injection
(actual growth 4.41% vs. an estimated counterfactual ~1% without it) `[1-SOURCE]`. A separate,
peer-reviewed elasticity estimate: each 10% increase in the scale of shantytown redevelopment in
a city is associated with a ~1.4% increase in that city's housing prices `[1-SOURCE]`. From
August 2017 the program was tightened specifically because regulators judged it had over-
inflated tier-3 prices `[1-SOURCE]`.

## Q9. Longer history: 1998 (housing privatization) to 2021

China's 1998 housing reform ended in-kind employer-provided housing and created a private
mortgage/sales market. Search-sourced characterization: home sales/prices rose at roughly
**+30%/yr from 1998 to 2009**, slowing to roughly **+10%/yr from 2010 onward** (pre-2021 peak)
`[1-SOURCE]` — a single secondary characterization, not an index-level calculation, and NOT
independently corroborated with a second source in this pass; treated as directional context only
(`[1-SOURCE]`, flagged, not used in the ratio arithmetic below). The homeownership rate reached
~90% by 2009 `[1-SOURCE]`, among the highest in the world, following five mortgage-rate cuts by
the PBOC between 1998-2002 `[1-SOURCE]`. No search result supplied a clean 1998 index level
comparable to the 2021 peak level (145.913) used elsewhere in this dossier — this is named as a
gap, not bridged.

## Q10. THE RATIO the desk needs: boom annualized rate ÷ bust annualized rate

Shown for multiple pairings because the answer is genuinely not one number — which pairing you
pick changes the conclusion, and that sensitivity is itself the finding to hand to the desk.

**(A) Official index only, national aggregate, most comparable pairing:**
Boom = national 70-city index, +6.2%/yr (Q6, the May2015-May2018 window)
Bust = national index nominal, -5.3%/yr (Q4, the Sep2021-Q1'26 window)
**Ratio = 6.2 / 5.3 = 1.17x** — the boom ran about 17% FASTER than the bust, on the official
index. This sits close to, but modestly above, the desk's cross-country base rate of ~0.99x
(boom and bust at roughly the same speed).

**(B) Official boom vs. PRIVATE/unofficial bust estimate:**
Boom = +6.2%/yr (same as A)
Bust = -10.7%/yr (Q4's "20% official → 40% unofficial" cumulative, same ~4.5y window)
**Ratio = 6.2 / 10.7 = 0.58x** — if the private estimate is closer to the truth, the bust ran
**almost twice as fast** as the boom. This is well below the ~0.99x base rate and would flag
China's bust as unusually violent relative to its own boom, the opposite conclusion from (A).

**(C) Tier-1 hot-boom vs. tier-1 official bust (NOT a like-for-like window length, shown for
completeness only):**
Boom = tier-1 aggregate, +30.3%/yr (Q1's 16-month 2015-2016 core spike)
Bust = tier-1 city-level secondhand average (Shenzhen/Guangzhou), -5.5%/yr (Q4, ~5-year window)
**Ratio = 30.3 / 5.5 ≈ 5.5x** — an outlier. This compares a short, frenetic 16-month mania to a
long, grinding 5-year decline, so the mismatch in window length does most of the work; it is
included because it is the comparison a reader who remembers "Shenzhen prices doubled" would
naturally reach for, and the dossier's job is to show explicitly why it overstates the ratio
relative to (A) and (B).

**Read for the desk**: at the national-index level the official data alone would say China's
bust looks UNREMARKABLE next to the cross-country ~0.99x base rate (a bit on the slow side, even,
at 1.17x meaning boom-faster). It is only once the private/unofficial price estimate is taken
seriously (pairing B) that China's bust looks like it is running meaningfully faster than its own
boom — which is also the pairing best supported by the distressed-transaction evidence in Q4
(auction sales, the "20/40" framing, the "40-50% in many cities" city-level reads). The honest
summary is: **on official numbers alone, China is close to or slightly gentler than the global
base rate; on numbers most private analysts believe, it is running meaningfully hotter (bust
faster than boom) than that base rate.**

## Gaps and cautions

1. **No page was ever directly fetched.** Every figure is a WebSearch snippet characterization,
   per §0a's binding ceiling for Half B. Index LEVELS in particular (145.913, 114, 113, 85.1) came
   through as numbers embedded in prose summaries, not read off a chart or table — a higher
   mis-transcription risk than for simple YoY percentages.
2. **No clean 2015 starting level exists in this search pass for the national/BIS nominal index**,
   so Q6/Q9's "2015-2021" question is answered only via overlapping sub-windows (2015-2016,
   2015-2018), not a single clean 6-year figure. Do not read the Velocity Table's boom rows as
   spanning the full 2015-2021 period — none of them do.
3. **ASP (average selling price) vs. quality-adjusted INDEX is conflated in the raw source
   material at least twice** (Q7's Shenzhen 177.5% ASP-based figure; Q8's tier-3 28.1% ASP vs
   15.9% index figures for the identical window) — both instances are flagged inline rather than
   silently reconciled into one number, per the task's explicit warning about this exact error.
4. **Nominal vs. real is only cleanly separated for the BUST side** (Q4's -21.8% nominal / -24.6%
   real). No real (inflation-adjusted) figure was sourced for the BOOM side, so the boom-side
   CAGRs in the ratio arithmetic (Q10) are nominal-only; if 2015-2018 Chinese CPI ran materially
   positive (it did, mid-single-digit-or-lower), the real boom rate would be slightly LOWER than
   the nominal +6.2%/yr shown, which would push ratio (A) even further above 1.0x (boom looks
   relatively even faster) — directionally noted, not computed, since no CPI series was sourced
   in this pass.
5. **New-build vs. second-hand (existing-home) prices are distinct series with different declines**
   — the Velocity Table's national bust rows are NEW-BUILD; the tier-1 secondhand row is a
   separate, independently-tracked series. The two should never be averaged together, and this
   dossier keeps them in separate rows for that reason.
6. **The "consecutive months of decline" streak (37, Q3) measures something different from
   "cumulative decline since the 2021 peak" (Q4)** — the streak restarted mid-2023 after an
   earlier, distinct 2021-2023 decline phase interrupted by a stabilization attempt. Several
   source snippets blur this distinction (describing "3 years" or "5 years" of decline
   interchangeably); this dossier does not.
7. **The private/unofficial "40%" cumulative-decline figure rests on one underlying analysis**
   (Stanford SCCEI China Briefs) republished across two URLs — NOT independent corroboration
   despite appearing twice in search results. The city-level "40-50%, some cities" quote is a
   separate, second data point that is directionally consistent but methodologically distinct
   (a distribution-of-cities anecdote, not a national aggregate) — the two should not be merged
   into a single "confirmed 40%" claim without that caveat.
8. **Sales-value comparison (Q5) mixes an all-commodity-housing total (2021: CNY18.19tn) against
   the same all-commodity-housing total for 2025 (CNY8.39tn)** — apples to apples — but a
   separately-reported residential-ONLY 2025 figure (CNY7.33tn) exists and must NOT be compared
   directly to the 2021 all-commodity total, which this dossier avoids doing.
9. **This entire dossier is INDICATIVE research per CLAUDE.md §0a, not desk-grade evidence.** It
   places China's own numbers next to the vault-computed JST cross-country base rate (Half A,
   CN-D1..CN-D5) for comparison; it is not itself a vaulted, sha256-manifested source and carries
   no bar-passing/failing status of its own.
