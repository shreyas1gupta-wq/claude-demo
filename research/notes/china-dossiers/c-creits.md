## Headline findings

- **The pilot launched 21 June 2021** with 9 REITs, built on a "public fund + ABS + project
  company" wrapper — not a REIT/trust in the US/Singapore legal sense — because China's
  Securities Law does not recognize a REIT as a security class `[2-SOURCE]`.
- **The market has grown to ~78-79 listed vehicles and ~RMB 200-227bn (~US$28-32bn) in market
  value as of Feb 2026**, with ~20 more IPOs approved and ~37 more queued as of Aug 2026 —
  Asia's largest, the world's second-largest REIT market by product count `[2-SOURCE]`.
- **The index round-tripped violently**: roughly -50% cumulative from the early-2022 peak to the
  Jan-2024 trough (-28% in 2023 alone, -6.4% more in the first days of 2024), then +14.2% in H1
  2025 alone — but full-year 2025 total return was a much smaller +4.34%, implying a sharp H2-2025
  giveback, and 2026 opened with a fresh three-month, -5.6% correction (through May) plus a
  further -1.7% in July, driven by a new supply wave (the first commercial-property REIT batch)
  colliding with weak industrial-park/logistics fundamentals `[2-SOURCE]` on the 2022-2024 leg,
  `[1-SOURCE]` on each subsequent leg (dated separately below).
- **Distribution yields cluster 4-5.5%** and are NOT the same number as the underlying asset's
  appraisal cap rate: affordable-housing REITs distribute ~4.0-4.5% at market price while their
  own valuation reports use a 6-6.5% discount rate — the gap, worked through in Q6, is this
  dossier's single most valuable derived number: an **implied residential cap rate of roughly
  5.3-5.9%**, meaningfully ABOVE the ~4% cash yield an investor actually receives, which is the
  signature of a persistent price-to-NAV premium `[1-SOURCE construction, computed from two
  separately-sourced inputs — see caveat in Q6]`.
- **Foreign access is narrow and, as of today, indirect**: QFII/RQFII can reach C-REITs in
  principle (exchange-traded securities are within QFII's scope, and at least one provincial
  policy document names infrastructure REITs explicitly), but Stock Connect inclusion — announced
  by the CSRC in April 2024 — is STILL NOT LIVE; HKEX's own CEO said in May 2026 the target for
  formal implementation is Q4 2027 `[2-SOURCE]`. The practical foreign routes into Chinese
  property income today are HK-listed developers, distressed offshore USD bonds (recovery to
  date: ~0.6% of $147bn defaulted, i.e. near-total loss `[1-SOURCE]`), or the five
  Singapore-listed China-asset REITs.

## Q1. The pilot: launch, legal structure, and why it isn't a US/Singapore REIT

**Launch**: CSRC + NDRC jointly issued the "Notice on Advancing the Pilot Project for
Infrastructure REITs" on 30 April 2020, authorizing the pilot; the first 9 C-REITs actually
listed on the Shanghai and Shenzhen exchanges on **21 June 2021**, raising RMB 31.4bn combined
`[2-SOURCE]`.

**Structure — "public fund + ABS + project company"**, not a trust: China's Securities Law does
not recognize a REIT as a distinct security class, so regulators built the pilot on top of two
vehicles that already existed rather than legislate a new one. A publicly-offered mutual fund
(募集资金端, the layer retail/institutional investors actually buy) holds 100% of an
asset-backed security (ABS), which in turn holds equity in a special-purpose project company
that owns the physical asset `[2-SOURCE]`. Investors therefore hold fund units, not trust
beneficiary certificates or REIT shares.

**Why this matters for comparability** (the principal's own framing, confirmed by search):
1. **No REIT tax pass-through regime exists.** US/Singapore REIT law provides a statutory
   dividend-payout requirement (US: ≥90% of taxable income) in exchange for entity-level tax
   exemption. China's fund+ABS wrapper has no equivalent statute — payout policy is closer to a
   fund's own distributable-cash-flow discipline than a REIT law's mandate `[1-SOURCE, inferred
   from structure descriptions — no explicit tax-comparison source found this session, flagged]`.
2. **Closed-end, fixed-life, and NOT internally managed in the Western sense** — most C-REITs
   quoted in this dossier carry stated terms (e.g. Huaxia Jinmao's 30-year closed-end term); this
   is closer to a closed-end infrastructure fund than an evergreen REIT.
3. **The original pilot was explicitly "infrastructure REITs" (基础设施REITs), not property
   REITs** — the underlying assets were toll roads, industrial parks, sewage/water treatment and
   clean energy at launch; residential and retail property entered only through later,
   deliberate expansions (Q3). A US/Singapore REIT investor default-assumes property; a C-REIT
   investor must check the asset category first.
4. **Distribution yield on a C-REIT is therefore not pre-screened by a REIT-law payout rule** the
   way a US/S-REIT yield is — comparing headline yields across the two systems without adjusting
   for the payout-mandate difference is a category error, which is precisely why this dossier
   keeps distribution yield, appraisal cap rate, and price-return separate throughout.

## Q3. Permitted asset categories and the 2023-2024 expansion

**At 2021 launch**: infrastructure only — toll roads/highways, industrial parks, warehousing and
logistics, clean energy (wind/solar), water/sewage treatment `[2-SOURCE]`.

**2022**: affordable rental housing added as its own pilot category (CSRC/NDRC joint notice,
27 May 2022) `[2-SOURCE]`.

**March 2023 — Document No. 236**: the pivotal expansion, adding **consumer infrastructure**
(department stores, shopping malls, farmers' markets) as an eligible asset class and opening the
door to commercial-property sponsors `[2-SOURCE]`.

**By June 2023**: seven established categories in active use — industrial parks, warehousing and
logistics, industrial plants, affordable rental housing, highways, clean energy, and other
environmental-related projects `[1-SOURCE]`.

**26 July 2024 — NDRC notice** (effective 1 Aug 2024): widened eligible asset classes further and
streamlined the filing process; the category list grew past 10, explicitly reaching data centres
and government-subsidized rental housing `[2-SOURCE]`.

**2025**: the first data-centre REIT (Southern GDS) listed — the first of its kind nationally and
on the SSE `[1-SOURCE]`.

**2026**: a further, apparently DISTINCT expansion into broader "commercial real estate"
(商业不动产) — as opposed to the narrower 2023 "consumption infrastructure" category — with the
first batch of 4 such REITs listed 18 June 2026 and at least one industry piece calling 2026
"Year One of commercial-property REITs" `[1-SOURCE]`; the precise legal distinction between this
2026 category and the 2023 consumer-infrastructure category was not resolved by search and is
flagged as an open question.

### The implied residential cap rate — the single most valuable number in this dossier

China's ARH-REIT appraisals use the **income approach via discounted cash flow (DCF)**, not a
direct-capitalization method — search results explicitly state China lacks a mature market-wide
direct cap-rate convention for real estate, so DCF is the default `[1-SOURCE]`. The DCF's three
levers are rental income, operating cost, and the discount rate; the **discount rate used across
ARH-REIT valuations clusters 6.0-6.5%** `[1-SOURCE]`, and the one vehicle with a disclosed
rent-growth assumption (Hotland Shenzhen Anju) uses **0.66%/yr** `[1-SOURCE]`.

Applying the standard Gordon-growth identity used elsewhere in this same literature for consumer
REITs — **cap rate = discount rate − NOI growth rate** `[1-SOURCE, sourced for the CONSUMER
category specifically, applied here to ARH-REITs by analogy — this is a DERIVED figure, not
itself a quoted cap rate]** — gives:

> **Implied residential cap rate ≈ 6.0-6.5% − 0.66% ≈ 5.3-5.9%**, call it **~5.5%** as a point
> estimate.

**Why this is the valuable number, and its honest limits**:
- This sits ABOVE the ~4.0-4.5% cash distribution yield investors actually receive at market
  price. The gap (roughly 1.0-1.5pp) is the signature of a **persistent price-to-NAV premium** —
  consistent with the oversubscription behaviour seen across C-REIT IPOs generally (one
  consumer-REIT IPO drew 100.5x its offering size) and with independent commentary that "China's
  love for dividends" is driving REIT valuations up faster than the underlying cash flow.
- It is the closest thing in this entire China research programme to a **market-clearing
  residential cap rate** for Chinese property — everything else in the desk's China work (NBS
  price indices, private trackers, survey-based rental yields) is either a index/level series or
  an appraisal number nobody trades against. This one is disciplined twice: once by an
  independent appraiser's DCF (the 6-6.5%/0.66% inputs), and once by the market's own bid for the
  fund units (the distribution yield, set by trading price).
- Caveats, stated plainly: (i) the 0.66% growth input is drawn from ONE of the six vehicles, not
  a sector average — treat 5.3-5.9% as an illustrative band, not a precise sector constant; (ii)
  the cap-rate=r-g identity was sourced for the CONSUMER-REIT literature and applied here to
  housing by the same logic, not independently confirmed for the housing category; (iii) China's
  ARH-REIT assets are specifically STATE/policy-directed affordable rental stock — not market-rate
  urban apartments — so this cap rate speaks to policy-housing cash flows, and generalizing it to
  China's broader private residential stock (the tier-1/tier-2 apartment market covered in other
  Half-B dossiers) requires an explicit, argued adjustment this dossier does not attempt.

## Q10. Foreign investor access: QFII, Stock Connect, and realistic alternatives

**QFII/RQFII**: quotas were fully abolished in 2019-2020 and the two regimes were unified in
Sept 2020; QFII/RQFII's investment scope covers exchange-traded securities broadly (stocks,
depositary receipts, bonds, ABS, and — per at least one provincial (Beijing) infrastructure-REIT
policy guidance document — infrastructure REITs are explicitly named as within scope)
`[1-SOURCE, provincial document — not a national CSRC circular naming REITs directly, flagged as
the weaker of the two access claims]`. This is the one channel that plausibly works TODAY for an
institutional foreign investor, subject to normal QFII licensing.

**Stock Connect**: the CSRC announced on 19 April 2024 an intention to include both HK-listed and
mainland C-REITs within Stock Connect. **As of the most recent confirmable statement (HKEX CEO,
29 May 2026), this is STILL NOT implemented** — the stated target is formal implementation in
**Q4 2027**, more than a year from today's date (2026-09-11) `[2-SOURCE]`. Preparatory steps taken
so far: Hong Kong abolished REIT share-transfer stamp duty (late 2024); HK is revising REIT-law
provisions (mandatory-acquisition mechanisms); a yuan trading counter for Stock Connect is being
rolled out in 2026, intended to eventually support dual-currency REIT trading `[1-SOURCE]`. **The
honest answer for the principal: Stock Connect access to C-REITs does not exist yet.**

**Realistic alternatives, ranked by directness of China-property exposure**:
1. **HK-listed China property developers** (e.g., China Resources Land, China Overseas Land &
   Investment, Longfor) — accessible via ordinary HK-equity channels (Stock Connect southbound
   already works for equities, or direct HK brokerage); developer equity, not a rental-income
   REIT, so exposure is to development margin and balance-sheet risk, not stabilized cash yield
   `[RECALL — general market knowledge, not separately searched this session]`.
2. **Offshore USD bonds of Chinese developers** — technically the most liquid, most
   foreign-investor-native route, but recovery outcomes to date are severe: international
   bondholders have recovered **less than 1% (~$917m, i.e. 0.6%) of the ~$147bn in bonds
   defaulted since 2021** across 62 developers; 36 developers completed 54 restructurings
   covering $56.6bn of the 159 affected bonds, and of the 11 that completed a "holistic"
   restructuring, 5 later defaulted AGAIN `[2-SOURCE]`. As of Dec 2025, China Vanke (state-backed)
   only narrowly avoided a fresh default, underscoring the sector's continued fragility even among
   better-capitalized names `[1-SOURCE]`.
3. **Singapore-listed China-asset REITs** — the cleanest indirect, foreign-native route to
   ACTUAL rental income from Chinese property, with none of the QFII/Stock Connect access
   friction: **Sasseur REIT** (outlet malls, listed 28 Mar 2018), **BHG Retail REIT** (retail,
   listed 11 Dec 2015, 6 properties across Beijing/Chengdu/Hefei/Xining/Dalian as of Dec 2025),
   **EC World REIT** (logistics/e-commerce, listed 28 Jul 2016), **CapitaLand China Trust**
   (retail + business parks), and **Dasin Retail Trust** (retail) `[2-SOURCE]` on identity and
   listing dates for the first three; individual current yields/prices were NOT pulled this
   session (out of scope/budget — flagged as a natural follow-up, since these are the most
   directly comparable benchmark against the mainland ARH/consumer C-REIT yields in Q5-Q7).
