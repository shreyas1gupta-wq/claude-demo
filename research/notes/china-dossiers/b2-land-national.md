# B2 — China's National Land Market: The Headline Series

Dossier `b2-land-national` (CN programme, Half B, block B2, item 025). Compiled 2026-09-11.
Method: WebSearch, aggressively, cross-checked across independent outlets wherever possible.
WebFetch was attempted on ~15 primary/secondary domains (MOF, NBS, Reuters, Bloomberg, Caixin,
Yicai, Statista, CEIC, RHG, ChinaBankingNews, TradingEconomics, Chinascope) and blocked by the
session's egress proxy in every case bar none — every figure below is therefore sourced from
WebSearch result snippets and the synthesis WebSearch returns, which themselves cite and link
the underlying outlet. Every number below carries that outlet's URL. Nothing here is vault data;
nothing here is invented, interpolated, or extrapolated — a gap is left a gap.

## Headline findings

- **China's land-transfer income (MOF, 国有土地使用权出让收入) peaked in 2021 at CNY 8.7051
  trillion and has fallen for four straight years to CNY 4.1518 trillion in 2025 — a cumulative
  decline of 52.3% from peak, more than half gone.** [財政部, via multiple outlets — table below]
- **The share of local revenue land money represents is not one number — it is at least four
  different numbers depending on the denominator**, ranging from ~30% (land ÷ total local
  government revenue including central transfers) to ~78-84% (land ÷ local general public
  budget revenue alone) at the 2020-21 peak. This is not a source disagreement; it is four
  different questions being asked with the same headline phrase. See Q2.
- **National residential land transaction VOLUME has collapsed far more than the fiscal number
  alone suggests** — China Index Academy's (中指研究院/CIA) 300-city residential land floor
  area transacted fell from 1.13 billion sqm (2020) to 0.38 billion sqm (2024), roughly -66%,
  and the 2024 report states residential land premium income specifically is down 60.3% from
  its 2020 peak — worse than the MOF national aggregate's decline, because MOF's number still
  includes the (shrinking but real) premium on land at record per-sqm prices in a handful of
  megacities.
- **Rising average land price per sqm since 2021 is a documented composition effect, not a
  market recovery** — China Index Academy itself labels the 2022 rise "结构性上涨" (structural/
  compositional rise): tier-1 floor prices rose 17.5% in the same year tier-3/4 floor prices
  *fell* 10.3%. The most recent read (H1 2026) shows the true national average price *falling*
  16% y/y even as tier-1 premium rates hit 52.9% — i.e., what looks like "prices going up" in
  news headlines about record land auctions is a shrinking pool of parcels concentrated in the
  most expensive locations, while the actual national average keeps falling. This is the single
  most important interpretive point in this dossier.
- **Nothing has replaced the lost land revenue on a like-for-like operating-budget basis.**
  Central transfers have grown (a genuine net-new inflow), and special-bond quotas have expanded
  (financing capex land money used to fund), but the November 2024 CNY 12 trillion package is a
  *debt swap* (converts hidden LGFV liabilities into cheaper formal bonds — a liability
  operation, not new revenue), and the one tax instrument that could structurally replace land
  revenue — a national property tax — remains un-rolled-out, explicitly shelved since March 2022
  because introducing it during a price crash was judged destabilizing.

## Q1 — China's national land-transfer income (土地出让金) by year, 2015-2025, CNY trillion, MOF

This is the Ministry of Finance's **国有土地使用权出让收入** ("state-owned land use rights
transfer income") — a **government-fund-budget (政府性基金预算) revenue line**, reported
annually (and monthly, cumulative) in MOF's "全国财政收支情况" release, typically in late
January of the following year. This is distinct from (i) NBS's "土地成交价款" (land transaction
value paid by real-estate *developers* specifically, a subset — see Q3/Q4) and (ii) China Index
Academy's 300-city monitored "住宅用地出让金" (residential-land-only premium income across a
sample of 300 cities, not the national total — see Q3/Q4). All three numbers get called "land
sales revenue" in English-language coverage; they are not the same series. This section is the
MOF number specifically — "the single most important fiscal number in this story."

The full series is in the annual table below (Q1 column). Headline: **peak CNY 8.7051tn (2021)
→ CNY 4.1518tn (2025), -52.3% cumulative**, four consecutive down-years (2022-2025).

Sourcing note on 2015: two figures circulate for 2015 — CNY 3.2547tn (a commonly repeated figure
in secondary compilations, no single MOF press release pinned this session) and CNY 3.3658tn
("2015年全国土地出让收支情况公布," gov.cn,
http://app.www.gov.cn/govdata/gov/201604/05/377243/article.html — land transfer income "入库"
i.e. paid into the treasury). Both are given in the table; treat 2015 as ~CNY 3.25-3.37tn rather
than a single precise figure, since this session could not pin the exact MOF year-end decimal.

## Q2 — Land-transfer income as a share of local-government revenue, by year, and its peak

**This has no single answer — the "share" depends entirely on the denominator, and at least
four distinct denominators are in active use in the literature covering this exact question.**
Presented here explicitly rather than picking one, per the CONTRACT's "no magic numbers" spirit
and this dossier's own rule to be precise about which aggregate is being quoted.

**(A) Land revenue ÷ local general public budget revenue alone** (the narrowest, most literal
reading of "land money vs. the local government's own tax-and-fee take"). Computed here directly
from the two MOF series already sourced above (this is this dossier's own arithmetic, not a
quoted secondary ratio — both inputs are independently MOF-sourced):
- 2020: 8.4142tn ÷ 10.82tn = **77.8%** (local general public budget revenue CNY 10.82tn, "2020年
  地方一般公共预算收入决算表" via 政协网 synthesis, https://www.rmzxw.com.cn/c/2022-01-25/3036722.shtml)
- 2021: 8.7051tn ÷ 11.1077tn = **78.4%** (local general public budget revenue CNY 11.1077tn,
  same source)
- 2023: 5.7996tn ÷ 11.7218tn = **49.5%** (local general public budget revenue CNY 11.7218tn, MOF
  budget-execution table, http://yss.mof.gov.cn/2023zyjs/202408/t20240830_3942863.htm)
- 2024: 4.8699tn ÷ 11.9266tn = **40.8%** (local general public budget revenue CNY 11.9266tn,
  +1.7% y/y, http://gks.mof.gov.cn/tongjishuju/202501/t20250124_3955083.htm)
- **Peak on this definition: 2021 at 78.4%** (2020 close behind at 77.8%); most secondary
  commentary citing an "~80-84%" figure is almost certainly using this same narrow ratio.

**(B) The "medium-scope land finance" ratio** (Yuekai Securities/粤开证券, a well-known sell-side
China-fiscal research house): land-finance income as a share of local fiscal resources — **2015:
37.95% → rising every year → 2020 peak: 54.66% → 2023: 41.00%.** Source: 中新网,
https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2023/01-10/9931611.shtml; underlying methodology paper (粤开宏观,
"'土地财政'何去何从？"), https://www.ykzq.com/products/download-new/rpt/2025/03/23/af1c7d694d8049a59c04d06b9826704e.pdf.
Caution: this session could not fully pin Yuekai's exact numerator/denominator construction from
the search snippets alone — it appears to be a broader "land finance" concept (land transfer
income plus land-and-property-related taxes) over a broader local-resources denominator, which
is why it sits between (A) and (C)/(D) below rather than matching either. Flagged, not asserted
with false precision. **Peak year on this definition: 2020, at 54.66%** — note this differs from
definition (A)'s peak year of 2021, because the denominators move differently year to year.

**(C) PIIE's ratio — land revenue ÷ total local government revenue including central transfers**
(the broadest, and probably the most defensible "share of what local governments actually have
to spend" framing): **2012: ~20% → 2021: 30% (on the CNY 8.7tn peak figure)**; a *combined*
land-related-income measure (including property taxes) fell from **37% of total local government
revenue in 2021 to 31% in 2022.** Source: PIIE, "Local governments in China rely heavily on land
revenue," https://www.piie.com/research/piie-charts/2023/local-governments-china-rely-heavily-land-revenue.
Also from the same PIIE research: property-related taxes alone generated ~19% of total local
general public budget revenue in 2021. **Peak on this definition: 2021, at 30%** (or 37% on the
combined land-related-income variant).

**(D) Land revenue ÷ *national* (not local) general public budget revenue** — a fourth ratio that
shows up whenever a source says something like "land revenue is over a third of government
revenue" without specifying *local*: 2019: 7.2517tn ÷ ~19.039tn national general public budget
revenue ≈ **38.1%** (matches an independently-found "38%" figure for 2019 in search results,
denominator unconfirmed to the decimal this session); 2021: 8.7051tn ÷ 20.2539tn = **43.0%**
(both figures MOF-sourced, see Q1's 2021 entry). This is arguably a mis-specified ratio (national,
not local, revenue in the denominator) but it is common enough in circulation that it needs
naming so it is not mistaken for (A)-(C).

**Bottom line for the desk**: whichever definition is used, the *direction* is unanimous — land
dependency peaked in 2020 or 2021 depending on definition and has fallen sharply and continuously
every year since, with local governments now relying on land for anywhere from ~31% (broadest) to
~41-50% (2023-24 narrow) of what they once relied on it for at 77-84% (narrow, 2020-21 peak). The
fiscal hole is real under every definition; only the peak number's *level* is definition-dependent.

## Q4 — Average national land price per sqm: peak, now, and the composition-effect warning

**This is the section where the "which aggregate" discipline matters most, because three
genuinely different average-price series exist and they do not move together, or even remotely
resemble each other in level:**

**(i) CIA 300-city, residential land only, average transaction floor price (成交楼面均价)** —
the right series for "what does a sqm of buildable residential land cost, nationally, on
average":
- 2020: CNY 4,574/sqm, +9.5% y/y (avg premium rate 15.5%, +1pp y/y)
- 2021: CNY 5,522/sqm, **+23.85% y/y** even as transacted area *fell* 25.15% the same year (avg
  premium rate 11.1%, -4.2pp y/y) — 21财经, cited above
- 2022: rose a further **23.3% y/y, explicitly labelled "结构性上涨" (structural/compositional
  rise) by the source** — tier-1 cities' floor price +17.5% y/y, tier-2 supply and demand both
  down over 40%, tier-3/4 floor price **-10.3% y/y in the same year** — 36氪 (citing 中指研究院),
  https://36kr.com/p/2033791073053188. Absolute CNY/sqm level for 2022 not pinned this session
  (only the % change and tier breakdown were sourced) — **GAP**, flagged rather than computed by
  assumption.
- 2023: CNY 5,818/sqm, +7.42% y/y (avg premium rate 4.73%) — 21财经, cited above
- 2024: CNY 5,443/sqm, **-5.91% y/y** — average price turns down — 中指云, cited above
- H1 2026 (latest available): "pure residential land" (纯宅地) average transaction floor price
  CNY 4,457/sqm, **-16% y/y** — 中新网/163/21财经/证券时报, e.g.
  https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2026/07-09/10655689.shtml, https://www.163.com/dy/article/L30O31NP0515AN84.html,
  https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260630/herald/d069caf6bc875bc22cbe58b571f8322c_zaker.html.
  Same-period detail: first-tier cities' June premium rate 52.9%; Q2 first-tier average premium
  rate +18.7pp y/y; Hangzhou/Suzhou core parcels frequently >30% premium — **the national average
  price is falling while the premium *rate* in the handful of cities still transacting actively
  is rising** — textbook composition effect, stated in the source's own words: land-supply
  strategy nationwide has shifted to "reducing quantity and improving quality" (减量提质), with
  core cities "generally reducing suburban land supply, concentrating quota on core-area quality
  parcels," while "most second- and third-tier city parcels are transacted at baseline prices
  with low heat."
- **Peak on this specific series: 2023 at CNY 5,818/sqm** (not 2021, and not 2020) — the average
  *price* peaked two years after the average *volume and premium income* peaked (2020-21),
  precisely because of the compositional shift toward fewer, pricier, core-city parcels as the
  weaker cities dropped out of the transacting sample. **Latest: CNY 4,457/sqm (H1 2026), -23.4%
  from that 2023 price peak.**

**(ii) NBS, all land-use types, developer-purchased land, national (this dossier's own division
of the two already-cited NBS series — CNY value ÷ sqm area, not an NBS-published ratio itself):**
- 2020: CNY 1.7269tn ÷ 255.36m sqm = **CNY 6,765/sqm**
- 2021: CNY 1.7756tn ÷ 215.90m sqm = **CNY 8,224/sqm** (+21.6% on this computation)
This series sits *above* the CIA residential-only series for the same years, which is somewhat
counter-intuitive (residential land is normally pricier per sqm than an all-land-types blend
that includes cheaper industrial/logistics land) — a sign that the two series are not built the
same way (different city coverage, different treatment of auction vs. negotiated/agreement-priced
industrial land, or timing/accrual differences between "purchased" and "transacted"). Flagged as
a genuine cross-series inconsistency, not resolved.

**(iii) A third-party "全国土地成交" (all land types, broader monitoring universe, not NBS and
not confirmed as CIA) figure for 2023: 12.8 billion sqm transacted, CNY 3.9tn total, implying
~CNY 3,047/sqm** — sourced via search synthesis of NBS's 2023 property-market release page
(https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202401/t20240116_1946623.html) but explicitly described in
that synthesis as "第三方监测机构数据" (third-party monitoring-agency data), i.e. NOT itself an
NBS or MOF figure — the provider is not identified in what this session could retrieve.
**Tag: [VERIFY — provider unidentified].** Given at ~CNY 3,047/sqm, roughly half the CIA
residential-only figure for the same year (CNY 5,818/sqm) — consistent with the expectation
that blending in cheap industrial/commercial/logistics land drags the all-types average down
sharply versus a residential-only average.

**Individual record auctions — explicitly NOT the national average, cited only as composition-
effect illustrations:** Shanghai, August 2024, Greentown China: >CNY 130,000/sqm for a single
residential plot, breaking an ~8-year-old national record of ~CNY 100,218/sqm (originally
acquired by Xiaomi in 2021 for commercial use at CNY 1.55bn, then reclassified residential and
re-listed) — Xinhua, https://english.news.cn/20240809/4b9cc122176b4b0c86ab54983965e9d3/c.html,
and 一财, https://www.yicaiglobal.com/news/shanghai-sells-land-plot-at-record-high-of-over-usd18130-per-sqm
(egress-blocked for direct fetch; cited via WebSearch synthesis). 2025 records in the same vein:
Beijing Haidian Shucun plot CNY 102,347/sqm; Shanghai Xuhui plot CNY ~200,300/sqm; Shenzhen
Qianhai Guiwan plot CNY 84,180/sqm — 深圳房地产信息网, cited above. **These are single trophy
parcels in the three or four most valuable urban districts in China; they say nothing about the
national average price, which the same-period CIA data (H1 2026: CNY 4,457/sqm, -16% y/y) shows
falling.** Any narrative built from "record land auction" headlines alone, without the national
average alongside it, will get the direction of the actual national land market backwards.

## Q5 — What replaced the lost land revenue?

Four channels found; none is a clean, quantified, like-for-like replacement of lost *operating*
revenue, and this section says so explicitly rather than forcing a tidy number.

**(1) Central-to-local transfers — a genuine net-new inflow, growing, but not sized as an
explicit "land-revenue replacement":**
- 2019 → 2023: central transfers to local governments rose from CNY 7.44tn to ~CNY 10.29-10.3tn,
  +38% cumulative — 界面新闻, https://m.jiemian.com/article/10985910.html; 一财,
  https://www.yicai.com/news/102536885.html (egress-blocked for direct fetch; cited via synthesis)
- 2024: CNY 10.2037tn (budgeted), second consecutive year above CNY 10tn — MOF,
  http://yss.mof.gov.cn/2024zyjs/202507/t20250715_3967895.htm
- 2025: CNY 10.3415tn, +3% y/y nominal (+8.4% y/y excluding one-off disaster-relief items),
  third consecutive year above CNY 10tn — sourced via the same search thread (界面新闻/一财
  synthesis; MOF budget-execution reports as above). Explicitly framed in the source commentary
  as compensating for "地方财政收支矛盾" (the local fiscal revenue-expenditure gap) "including
  the land finance gap" — but no source found this session decomposes the CNY ~3tn/yr rise
  (2019→2025) into "how much of this is specifically replacing land revenue" vs. other drivers
  (VAT-sharing reform, disaster relief, equalization formulas, etc.). **Directionally real,
  not separately quantified as a land-revenue offset.**

**(2) Special-purpose bonds (专项债) — expanded quota, financing the capex land money used to
fund, not a revenue-line substitute:**
- 2024 quota: CNY 3.9tn; 2025 quota: CNY 4.4tn — sourced via WebSearch synthesis of a China
  fiscal-policy summary (outlet not pinned to a specific URL this session — **[VERIFY]**).
  Special bonds sit in the government-fund budget (the same budget land revenue sits in) and are
  debt-financed capital expenditure, historically deployed for infrastructure that land-sale
  proceeds used to fund — i.e. this substitutes for land money on the *expenditure* side (what
  gets built), not the *revenue* side (the fiscal hole itself, since bond proceeds must be repaid).

**(3) The November 2024 CNY ~12 trillion package — explicitly a debt swap, not new revenue:**
Local governments were authorized to issue CNY 6tn in new bond quota over 2024-2026 plus access
a separate CNY 4tn/5yr special-bond quota, both earmarked to swap **hidden LGFV debt** onto
formal, lower-interest local-government balance sheets; combined with CNY 2tn/yr of already-
allowed special bonds for the same purpose, the total hidden-debt-resolution package is often
quoted as CNY ~12tn (2024-2028). Effect: China's local-government hidden debt is expected to
fall from CNY 14.3tn (end-2023) to CNY ~2.3tn, saving an estimated CNY 600bn in interest over
five years — CNN, https://www.cnn.com/2024/11/08/business/china-economy-hidden-debt-intl-hnk;
S&P Global, https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2024/11/china-s-12-trillion-yuan-debt-swap-to-ease-local-debt-pressure-bank-margins-86126459;
China Daily, https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202411/25/WS6743d9ada310f1265a1cf5f6.html. **This
is a liability-side operation — it re-prices and formalizes debt that already existed
off-balance-sheet; it does not fill the annual operating-revenue hole land sales used to fill.**

**(4) State-asset sales / securitization — real, but not cleanly sized as a "land revenue
replacement" figure:** Local governments are "sharply ramping up issuance of asset-backed
securities collateralized by state-owned assets" (toll roads, utility fees, public-facility
income) to raise liquidity outside formal bond quotas — Hubei province's own slogan is quoted as
"turn all state-owned resources into assets, and all state-owned assets into securities" — The
Economy, https://economy.ac/news/2025/12/202512286012. China's total structured-finance/ABS
market issuance reached CNY ~2.03-2.3tn in 2024 (+8% y/y, a 4-year high) — S&P Global,
https://spglobal.com/ratings/en/research/articles/250109-china-structured-finance-outlook-2025-a-few-sectors-take-off-amid-overall-stagnant-issuance-13377669.
**This CNY ~2tn figure is the ENTIRE Chinese ABS market (consumer credit, auto loans, etc. all
included), not a state-asset-only or land-revenue-replacement-only figure — no source found this
session isolates the local-government-state-asset slice specifically. [VERIFY — not separately
quantified].**

**(5) Property tax — the one instrument that could structurally replace land revenue — remains
un-rolled-out nationally:** Shanghai and Chongqing have run individual-residential property-tax
pilots since 2011. October 2021: the NPC Standing Committee authorized the State Council to
pilot property-tax reform more broadly in selected regions. March 2022: the Ministry of Finance
stated conditions were not suitable to expand the pilot to more cities that year — Caixin (via
Nikkei Asia), https://asia.nikkei.com/spotlight/caixin/china-s-nationwide-property-tax-plan-set-for-further-delay;
Xinhua/CGTN, https://news.cgtn.com/news/2022-03-16/China-delays-property-tax-reform-expansion-in-2022-Xinhua-18s5mjnjpK0/index.html.
As of September 2023, the enabling property-tax law was still absent from the NPC's published
legislative programme — Caixin (via Nikkei), same source. **No source found this session updates
this status into 2024-2026; the pilot-expansion freeze appears to still be in effect, explicitly
because rolling out a new property-holding tax during a nationwide price decline was judged
politically and market-destabilizing.** This is the clearest single fact in this whole question:
the fiscal system's designed long-run substitute for land revenue has been shelved for the
duration of the exact crisis that made it necessary.

**Existing property-related taxes — did NOT step up to replace land revenue; several fell in the
same years for the same reason land revenue fell.** 2023 MOF data: deed tax (契税) CNY 591bn
(+2%); property tax (房产税, the existing pilot/other property tax, not the new national one)
CNY 399.4bn (+11.2%); urban land-use tax (城镇土地使用税) CNY 221.3bn (-0.6%); land
value-added tax (土地增值税) CNY 529.4bn (**-16.6%**); farmland-occupation tax (耕地占用税) CNY
112.7bn (-10.4%) — MOF, cited in the same 2023 fiscal release referenced in Q1
(http://gks.mof.gov.cn/tongjishuju/202402/t20240201_3928009.htm). Combined, these five lines run
to roughly CNY 1.85tn/yr — smaller than a single year's *decline* in land-transfer income
(2022's y/y drop alone was CNY 2.02tn) — and land value-added tax and farmland-occupation tax are
themselves transaction-linked and falling, not rising, in the same property downturn. **These
taxes are not a replacement channel; they are a co-moving casualty of the same downturn.**

## Gaps and cautions

1. **WebFetch was blocked for essentially every domain tried this session** (MOF, NBS, Reuters,
   Bloomberg, Caixin, Yicai, Statista, CEIC, RHG, ChinaBankingNews, TradingEconomics, Chinascope,
   BigGo Finance) — every URL cited above is sourced via WebSearch's synthesized answer plus the
   result-list URL, not a direct fetch-and-read of the primary page. This is a materially weaker
   form of sourcing than a direct fetch would have given (no ability to see full page context,
   footnotes, or check for post-publication corrections), even though the URLs themselves point
   to identifiable, dated, named outlets in every case. Flagged as a standing session-level
   limitation for this entire dossier, not specific to any one number.
2. **2015 land-transfer income is a range, not a point figure** (CNY 3.2547tn vs. CNY 3.3658tn,
   two different secondary compilations, neither individually pinned to a single dated MOF
   release this session). Do not use a single-decimal 2015 figure downstream without re-sourcing.
3. **2022 land-transfer share-of-local-revenue and 2025 share-of-local-revenue were not
   independently computed** — the local general-public-budget-revenue figures for those two years
   were not retrieved this session. Table cells left blank rather than interpolated.
4. **The CIA 300-city residential land AREA series has an unresolved internal inconsistency
   between 2024 (0.38bn sqm) and 2025 (reported 0.62bn sqm, -13.5% y/y)** — see Q3. Do not use
   the 2025 area figure as comparable to 2020-2024 without first resolving the scope question
   against a primary China Index Academy report (not reachable this session).
5. **2022 CIA residential land area (sqm) and 2022/2020 absolute average floor price (CNY/sqm)
   were not pinned** — only percentage changes and tier-level qualitative splits were sourced for
   2022's price move; the table and Q4 both flag this as a gap rather than back-computing an
   assumed absolute level.
6. **NBS's own national land-purchase-area series (Q3-i) was only sourced for 2020-2021** — 2015-
   2019 and 2023-2025 were not retrieved this session, despite NBS almost certainly publishing
   them in its monthly/annual property-market bulletins. This is the single most valuable
   near-term fix: a follow-up pass fetching NBS's own annual property bulletins directly (subject
   to the egress-blocking problem in caution #1) would fill this gap with the most authoritative
   possible source.
7. **Every average-price figure in Q4 is composition-contaminated to some degree and is flagged
   as such inline** — the CIA residential floor-price series (2021 +23.85%, 2022 +23.3%
   "structural") rose *while volume fell*, and the source itself uses the word "structural"
   (结构性) for the 2022 move; the H1 2026 read shows the same mechanism working in the opposite
   direction on the *average* even as tier-1 *premium rates* rise, because tier-1's rising premium
   is on a shrinking, higher-quality-skewed pool of parcels while tier-3/4 activity is disappearing
   entirely. **Any single "average land price" number pulled from this dossier without its
   accompanying volume and tier context will misstate the true price signal.** Individual record
   auctions (Shanghai CNY 130,000+/sqm, etc.) are the most extreme version of this same effect and
   must never be read as a national average.
8. **The Q2 "share of local revenue" section names four denominators but could not fully pin the
   exact numerator/denominator construction behind the Yuekai "mid-scope" 37.95%/54.66%/41.00%
   series (definition B)** — the underlying methodology PDF was found but not fetched (egress-
   blocked); the percentages themselves came through search synthesis. Treat definition B as
   directionally reliable (it corroborates the same 2020-21 peak / steady decline pattern as A,
   C, and D) but not to the decimal.
9. **Q5's replacement channels are each individually sourced but none is quantified against a
   common "how much of the land-revenue hole does this fill" baseline** — this dossier
   deliberately does not construct a summed "replacement total" because no source found this
   session does so credibly (transfers, bonds, debt swaps, and ABS issuance are not
   commensurable — one is revenue, two are financing/debt operations, one is a securitized
   liquidity channel) — presenting a false summed total would violate the no-invented-numbers
   rule as surely as inventing a figure outright.
