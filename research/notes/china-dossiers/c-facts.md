## Headline findings

- **The cement claim essentially holds up** — China really did consume roughly as much cement in
  a ~2-3 year window (2010-2013, depending on which vintage of the claim) as the US consumed in
  the entire 20th century, and a USGS mineral-commodity specialist independently confirmed the
  order of magnitude to the Washington Post. But the "3 years" figure is a snapshot from 2014, not
  a constant — by Vaclav Smil's own later reckoning, the equivalent US-20th-century tonnage
  compressed to just **2 years** (2018-2019) `[2-SOURCE]`. Also watch the word: many retellings
  say "concrete" (a cubic-metre measure including aggregate) when the underlying USGS data and
  Smil's own numbers are **cement** (a tonnage measure) — the claim is right but the noun is often
  wrong.
- **China hit European/Japanese per-capita floor space at a much lower income level than Europe or
  Japan did** — the Rogoff-Yang "Peak China Housing" finding, not merely "China has a lot of
  floor space." Urban per-capita residential floor space rose from 27.8 sqm (2010) to ~39-41 sqm
  (2018-2021), now on par with Germany/France and inside Japan's 30-45 sqm range, at a fraction of
  those countries' per-capita GDP `[2-SOURCE]`.
- **The "Shenzhen ~35x" price-to-income figure is real but methodology-specific and already stale
  by two crashes.** It traces to a 2018 vintage of a Chinese property-institute survey
  (average home price ÷ average household disposable income) that put Shenzhen at 35.9, which then
  climbed to a reported record ~48.1 by 2020 `[1-SOURCE]` on that series — but a different,
  equally legitimate domestic series (the E-house 50-city report) puts the *national average*
  at only ~13-14x for the same years, and 2026 data (Global Property Guide, outer districts) show
  Beijing/Shanghai/Guangzhou/Shenzhen all down to roughly 8-13x `[2-SOURCE]`. There is no single
  "the" China price-to-income ratio; at least three non-reconciled series circulate.
- **The 2022 mortgage boycott (停贷) scaled from 35 projects to 300+ projects in about two months**
  and was tracked largely through a crowdsourced GitHub spreadsheet ("WeNeedHome") because domestic
  media coverage was restricted `[2-SOURCE]`. The state's response prioritized **completing
  construction** (a ~200bn-yuan PBOC re-lending facility, later ~300bn more, under the "baojiaolou"
  保交楼 policy) over cash compensation — genuine refunds/contract-rescissions were rare,
  precedent-setting one-offs, not the norm `[2-SOURCE]`.
- **Estimates of paid-for, undelivered homes vary by 6x depending on definition**: Nomura's ~20
  million units (end-2022, cumulative unconstructed/delayed presold stock, ~$443bn to complete)
  is the most load-bearing figure found `[2-SOURCE]`; a separate, much larger "120 million"
  circulates in a single low-authority source and could not be corroborated — likely a
  unit/floor-area confusion, not adopted.
- **The most viral "China ghost cities" number — 64.5 million (often rounded to "70 million")
  empty homes from an electricity-meter survey — was disowned by its own source days after it was
  first reported**: State Grid Corporation confirmed the figure to one outlet, then refuted it to
  another within days `[2-SOURCE]`. It is the ancestor of most "tens of millions of empty
  apartments" claims still circulating today, and it was retracted at origin.

## Q1. The cement claim: verify/correct and find its origin

**Origin, definitively traced**: historian/ecologist **Vaclav Smil**, in his book *Making the
Modern World: Materials and Dematerialization*, built the comparison from **USGS** historical
cement-production data. **Bill Gates** read the book, called it "the most mind-blowing statistic
I've seen this year," and popularized it on his GatesNotes blog in two 2014 posts — "A Stunning
Statistic About China and Concrete" (June 2014) and the book review "Have You Hugged a Concrete
Pillar Today?" `[2-SOURCE]`. It then propagated through Forbes, the Washington Post, Equipment
World, and dozens of business-press retellings — the "single most-repeated statistic" framing in
the task brief is itself an accurate description of what search turns up: this is genuinely the
most-cited number in the whole subject area.

**The numbers, cross-checked**: sources converge on the *order of magnitude* but not the *exact
tonnage or years*:
- Smil/Gates version: China **6.6 Gt** (2011-2013) vs US **4.5 Gt** (1901-2000).
- Business-press version (citing International Cement Review): China **6.4 Gt** (2011-2013) vs US
  **4.4 Gt** (1900-1999).
- **Independent verification**: the Washington Post's fact-check quoted **Hendrik van Oss**, the
  USGS's own mineral-commodity specialist for cement, who confirmed that **China's 2010-2012
  cement consumption was about 140% of total US consumption for 1900-1999** — i.e. the claim
  checks out against the primary-data holder's own specialist, using a slightly different 3-year
  window (2010-12 rather than 2011-13) `[2-SOURCE]`.
- **The ratio is not a fixed fact — it is a moving snapshot.** Smil's own later update states that
  by **2018-2019** (two years, not three), China's ~4.4 Gt cement output alone was already
  "nearly as much" as the entire US 20th-century total (4.56 Gt per that later citation)
  `[1-SOURCE]`. A Hannah Ritchie (Our World in Data) piece independently frames the same
  comparison as "China uses as much cement in **two years** as the US did over the entire 20th
  century" `[1-SOURCE]` — consistent with the compression, though using its own vintage.

**Unit hazard, explicitly flagged**: the underlying USGS/Smil data are **cement** (a tonnage
measure of the binder). Many popular retellings (including some headline framings found in this
search) say "**concrete**" instead — concrete is a cubic-metre measure of the finished
aggregate+cement+water mixture, a different unit entirely, though it tracks cement volumes closely
enough that the qualitative claim survives the swap. Treat any version that gives a **cubic-metre**
number as a further remove from the primary USGS tonnage series.

**Verdict**: **CONFIRMED** as a real, well-sourced, roughly accurate statistic with credible
independent (USGS-specialist) verification — but it should always be stated with its vintage
(which 3-year, or increasingly 2-year, window) rather than as a timeless constant, and "cement" is
the correct noun, not "concrete."

## Q2. Floor space per capita vs Japan/Europe/US at comparable income

The real finding here is **not** "China has a lot of housing" — it is the Rogoff-Yang ("Peak China
Housing," NBER WP27697, and the follow-on "A Tale of Tier 3 Cities," NBER WP30519) thesis that
China reached **rich-country floor-space levels at a fraction of rich-country income**:

- China urban per-capita residential floor space: **27.8 sqm (2010) → ~39.0 sqm (2021)**, per
  Rogoff-Yang; a separate Chinese-government-linked figure gives **>40 sqm (2017)** and **41.76 sqm**
  in more recent domestic reporting `[2-SOURCE]` on the general 39-41 sqm current level.
- **US**: ~65 sqm per capita `[1-SOURCE]` (no primary citation surfaced for this specific number;
  treat as indicative).
- **Japan**: 30-45 sqm range — i.e. **China is now inside, not below, Japan's band**, a genuine
  convergence `[1-SOURCE]`.
- **Europe**: Rogoff-Yang state directly that China's per-capita housing space **"now exceeds that
  of any major European country, even though China's per capita GDP is considerably lower"**
  `[2-SOURCE, the claim appears in both the NBER paper and its IMF F&D companion piece]`.

**Verdict**: **CONFIRMED**, and the "at comparable income levels" framing in the question is
answered directly by the Rogoff-Yang income-adjusted comparison, not by the raw floor-space numbers
alone — China's floor space is not merely large, it is large *for its income level*, which is the
paper's entire "overbuilt" thesis and the reason a large chunk of the demand pull-forward is
structural rather than merely cyclical.

## Q3. Housing units built per year at peak vs rest of world combined

This could **not be sourced as stated**. No search surfaced a clean, dated "China's peak annual
completions exceeded [rest of world combined]" statistic from a traceable primary — this is
recorded per the task's own instruction as a finding (**"widely repeated, origin not located"**)
rather than papered over.

What **is** sourced, as partial proxies that answer adjacent but different questions:
- Newly-started residential floor area peaked around **2,217 million sqm (2019)** `[1-SOURCE]`;
  completed residential floor area was **330.84 million sqm (2019)**, already down 8.5% YoY that
  year — i.e. **completions had already turned down before 2019 ended**, a useful correction to
  any "peak = most recent year" assumption `[1-SOURCE]`.
- A **stock-vs-stock** comparison (not a peak-year flow-vs-world-flow comparison, and therefore
  answering a different question): "in the first decade of the 21st century, the number of housing
  units built in China was roughly twice the total current housing stock of Spain or the UK, or
  about the same as Japan's entire current housing stock" `[1-SOURCE]`.
- A genuinely striking **employment** proxy for scale, found in the course of this search:
  China's construction-sector employment **peaked at ~62 million jobs (2020)** `[1-2 SOURCE]` —
  more people working construction at that one peak than the entire population of Italy.

**Verdict**: the specific "vs rest of world combined" framing is **UNSOURCEABLE AS STATED**; the
underlying scale is real and enormous by every proxy actually found, but the precise comparator
claim should not be repeated without a located primary.

## Q4. Share of world cement, steel, cranes, glass consumed by Chinese property at peak

An important scoping caveat up front: **every number found below is China's share of world
consumption/production of the material overall — none of the search results isolated the
property-specific slice** (infrastructure, industrial construction, manufacturing, and — for
glass specifically — solar-panel and electronics uses are all bundled in). This is a genuine data
gap, named rather than papered over with an invented split.

- **Cement**: China's share of *world production* peaked at **59.3% (2014)**, running **57.2%
  (2020)** `[2-SOURCE]`.
- **Steel**: China's share of world *production* rose from **53.3% to 56.5-56.7% (2020)**
  `[2-SOURCE]` — but this is production, not consumption (China is also a major finished-steel
  exporter); China's own finished-steel *consumption* peaked at **~1,009 Mt (2020)**, though its
  % of world *consumption* specifically was not located in this pass — flagged as a
  production/consumption conflation risk in how this stat usually gets quoted.
- **Tower cranes**: China holds roughly **50% of the global tower-crane market** and operates an
  estimated fleet of **>100,000 units**, the largest national concentration in the world; China
  also completed **65% of the world's supertall (200m+) buildings by 2025** `[1-SOURCE each]` —
  these are commercial market-research estimates with less transparent methodology than the
  USGS/Statista cement and steel figures, so weighted lower in confidence despite being directionally
  uncontroversial.
- **Glass**: China accounts for **>50% of world flat-glass production** (as of ~2019) and
  **~50% of global flat-glass demand** (2014 vintage figure), **~40% of global glass production
  overall** (all types), and **>85% of the world's solar glass** specifically `[2-SOURCE]` on the
  "roughly half" flat-glass headline, `[1-SOURCE]` on each precise variant.

**Verdict**: **CONFIRMED, directionally**, for all four materials at the whole-China level; the
property-specific attribution the question actually asked for is **not separable from the sourced
data** and should be presented as an economy-wide (not property-only) figure.

## Q5. Price-to-income ratios — Beijing/Shanghai/Shenzhen/Guangzhou/tier-2, and the "Shenzhen ~35x" figure

This is the section where the corroboration discipline earns its keep: **there is no single "the"
China price-to-income series.** At least three non-reconciled methodologies were found circulating
for the same cities in overlapping years:

1. **A Chinese property-institute city-ranking series** (reported via Yicai and cross-cited by
   Asia Times), average home price ÷ average household disposable income: **Shenzhen 35.9 (2018)
   → 43.7 (2019) → a reported record 48.1 (2020)** `[1-SOURCE for the specific number series;
   the climb-then-peak DIRECTION is corroborated by a second, independent Asia Times piece on
   Shenzhen's 2019 and 2021 price dynamics, `[2-SOURCE]` on direction]`. Separately, one snippet
   gave "Shenzhen's ratio... exceeded a multiple of 40, compared to 22 in London and 12 in New
   York" (an international comparison, likely from the same institute's report, undated precisely
   in the snippet).
2. **The E-house (易居研究院) 50-city report**, a distinct, longer-running domestic series: national
   **50-city average price-to-income ratio of 13.9 (2018), down from 14.1 (2017)** — Shenzhen
   ranks #1 in this series too, but no exact multiple for Shenzhen specifically surfaced in this
   series in this search pass `[1-SOURCE]`.
3. **Numbeo's live index** (Sept 2026 pull): **Beijing 34.76, Shenzhen 28.53** — notably, Beijing
   now reads *higher* than Shenzhen on Numbeo's own current methodology, the reverse of the
   domestic "Shenzhen is always #1" framing `[1-SOURCE]`.
4. **Global Property Guide, July 2026** (outer-district basis): **Beijing 10.03, Shanghai 9.96,
   Guangzhou 7.75, Shenzhen 12.72** — all four tier-1 cities now sit in the **high single digits to
   low teens**, a dramatic decline from any of the 2018-2020 peak readings above `[1-SOURCE]`.

**Tier-2**: **Hangzhou ~14.5x** `[1-SOURCE]` — several sources note tier-2 cities like Hangzhou,
Chengdu and Nanjing now offer *better* affordability than the tier-1 quartet, a genuine change from
the historical tier-1-cheaper-than-nothing framing.

**Verdict on "Shenzhen ~35x": CORRECTED / CONTEXTUALIZED, not simply confirmed or debunked.** The
number is real for a specific 2018 vintage of a specific methodology; it climbed further before the
crash (to a reported ~48x by 2020); and it is now roughly 3-4x too high for 2026 conditions on every
series found. The deeper, more useful finding for the desk is the **methodology divergence itself**:
"price-to-income ratio" in Chinese housing commentary is not a standardized statistic — it varies by
income base (per-capita vs household, mean vs median, disposable vs total), assumed unit size, and
whether prices are city-average or core-district — and headline multiples anywhere from ~8x to ~48x
can all be "correct" simultaneously, for the same city, depending on which series is being quoted.

## Q6. The "six pockets" (六个钱包) phenomenon

**Meaning**: two parents plus four grandparents ("six wallets") pooling savings so a young couple
can afford a first-home down payment years earlier than their own income alone would allow.

**Origin**: **Fan Gang** (范纲/樊纲), a member of the People's Bank of China's Monetary Policy
Committee, whose remarks advocating exactly this pooling strategy went viral and sparked a national
debate around **2018** `[2-SOURCE, China.org.cn and Beijing Review both covering the 2018 debate]`.

**Prevalence**: the most concrete figure found — a **2023 survey reporting that over 70% of
first-time homebuyers received financial assistance from their parents** `[1-SOURCE]` — could not
be independently corroborated with a second distinct search result in this pass (repeat searches
surfaced the same secondary framing rather than an independent primary), so it is tagged
`[1-SOURCE]` rather than confirmed. Directionally, multiple sources agree the practice is "vast
majority"-level common among low- and middle-income first-time buyers, without giving their own
percentage `[2-SOURCE, on the qualitative "very common" claim only]`.

**A second, distinct usage worth flagging**: the same "six pockets" phrase was later reused
metaphorically by economist Liu Yuhui (Chinese Academy of Social Sciences) to describe a typical
family's *total* income/savings capacity being "overdrawn" by mortgage and consumption burdens —
a broader household-finances warning, not the literal down-payment-pooling practice. Citations of
"six pockets" should specify which sense is meant.

## Q7. The 2022 mortgage-payment strike (停贷)

**How it organized**: individual groups of homebuyers at stalled developments posted open
"mortgage repayment suspension notices" (强制停贷告知书), which spread rapidly across WeChat and
other social platforms. Because domestic media coverage was restricted, the definitive running
count was maintained by a **crowdsourced, GitHub-hosted tracker ("WeNeedHome")** — an unusual case
of a technical workaround (GitHub is accessible; domestic censored platforms less so) becoming the
primary source cited by international press `[2-SOURCE, Bloomberg and RFA both describing this
mechanism]`.

**How many projects**: the count grew fast and is reported slightly differently across snapshots
(consistent with a fast-moving, crowdsourced tally rather than a single official register):
**~35 projects/22 cities (July 12) → 200+ projects/80+ cities (mid-July) → 300+ projects (late
July) → 343 projects tracked (mid-September)**, spanning "roughly one hundred" cities within a
month `[2-SOURCE, Bloomberg + RFA both independently reporting >300 projects by
September]`.

**How the state handled it**: the response was aimed at **completing construction**, not
compensating buyers in cash:
- PBOC rolled out a **200-billion-yuan special re-lending facility** to banks specifically to fund
  stalled-project completion under the **"baojiaolou" (保交楼, "guarantee building delivery")**
  policy banner, later followed by a further **~300-billion-yuan** relending facility tied to
  government-subsidized housing `[2-SOURCE, CFR + Chinese financial press (futunn/moomoo)]`.
- By mid-2023, Yicai reported that **roughly a third of unfinished, pre-sold homes had been
  delivered** following a cumulative bailout push reported at **~$55.2 billion** `[1-SOURCE]`.
- Genuine cash-refund or contract-rescission wins for buyers existed but were **rare, precedent-
  setting exceptions**, not the norm: a Nanjing government-coordinated refund program was described
  as the first of its kind, and a Yancheng court ruling ordering contract dissolution plus refund of
  principal and interest was entered into the Supreme People's Court's own guiding-case database —
  but multiple sources state plainly that most local governments and developers **could not** refund
  buyers at scale `[2-SOURCE]`.

## Q8. Homebuyers who paid for units never built

**Scale estimates vary by an order of magnitude depending on definition** — this variance is itself
the finding:
- **Nomura, end-2022**: **~20 million units** of unconstructed/delayed presold homes nationally,
  requiring an estimated **$443 billion (¥3.2 trillion)** to complete; separately sized (by a
  different outlet) as "20 times the size of Country Garden," the largest private developer by
  sales at the time `[2-SOURCE, CNBC's direct reporting of the Nomura note, cross-cited elsewhere]`.
- **Bloomberg, mid-2022**: **225 million square metres** of stalled/unfinished floor space as a
  point-in-time flow snapshot — this is a **different measure** (floor area at one moment, not a
  cumulative unit count over the cycle) and should not be divided by an assumed unit size and
  compared directly against the Nomura figure without acknowledging the scope mismatch
  `[1-SOURCE]`.
- **An unsourceable "120 million"** figure appears in exactly one low-authority newsletter and could
  not be corroborated anywhere else in this search pass — flagged in the DEBUNKED table, not
  adopted.
- Context: **>1,660 Chinese developers had filed for bankruptcy by end-2024**, with at least 10
  major builders delisted `[1-SOURCE]`.

**What recourse buyers actually had**: in practice, very little that reliably worked. Multiple
sources describe buyers **pleading with developers, petitioning local authorities, and pursuing
civil suits — all largely unsuccessful** — which is precisely why the mortgage boycott (Q7) emerged
as an escalation tactic once conventional channels failed `[2-SOURCE]`. A minority of buyers
achieved court-ordered contract rescission and refunds (the Yancheng precedent, now a guiding case)
or benefited from a rare government-brokered refund scheme (Nanjing); the more common outcome for
buyers without such precedents, per one report, was **squatting inside their own unfinished units**
to assert and protect their claim, absent any faster remedy `[2-SOURCE]`. National policy explicitly
prioritized **delivering the physical apartment** (baojiaolou funds) over cash compensation.

## Q9. Property prices, marriage, and birth decisions — the real research

This is a genuine, converging academic literature, not folk wisdom — but it is **three distinct
mechanisms**, not one effect, and the task's corroboration standard requires keeping them separate
rather than pooling into a single headline elasticity:

1. **Entry-barrier effect on non-owners/renters**: higher house prices delay marriage and
   childbearing and reduce fertility intentions, concentrated among renters, non-property-owning
   families, and (in one specification) rural migrants without urban homes and in areas with scarce
   rural schooling `[2-SOURCE across ≥4 independent papers — ScienceDirect 2020, ScienceDirect
   2024 (regression discontinuity), SAGE 2025, a CUHK working paper, and a VoxChina summary piece]`.
   Quantified: a **10% exogenous rise in urban house prices reduces the birth rate by ~0.88 per
   mille** (i.e. a semi-elasticity of roughly -8.8‰) in the CUHK quasi-experimental design using
   capital spillovers from large cities to nearby unregulated cities `[1-SOURCE]`.
2. **Wealth effect for existing owners — opposite sign**: a housing-wealth elasticity of fertility
   of **≈+0.18** is reported (a 1% rise in EXISTING owners' housing wealth associates with a 0.18%
   *rise* in fertility) `[1-SOURCE]` — i.e. for people who already own, being richer on paper from
   their house going up in value has the opposite sign from the entry-barrier effect on
   non-owners. Effects on already-homeowning families are reported as small or statistically
   insignificant in at least one specification `[1-SOURCE]`.
3. **Migrant affordability channel**: a **100% increase in the price-to-income ratio facing
   China's mobile/migrant population reduces female migrant workers' fertility intention by
   ~4.42%** `[1-SOURCE]`.

**Correlational backdrop** (not itself causal, but consistent with the causal papers above):
national urban house prices rose **+54% (2016-2021)** while the birth rate fell from **13.6‰
(2016) to an average 9.3‰ (2017-2021)** `[1-SOURCE]`.

**Verdict**: **CONFIRMED as a real, causally-supported effect**, but any single-number summary
("house prices caused China's birth rate to fall by X%") is an oversimplification of a literature
that finds the sign itself depends on whether the household in question owns property already.
