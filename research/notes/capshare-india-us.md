## The equity link (descriptive; overlapping 10y windows inflate the p-values)

- **USA (n=61 starts, 1950-2010): corr +0.36 / Spearman +0.37.** Below-median capital
  share → next-10y equity excess +2.9%/yr; above-median → +7.1%/yr. POSITIVE — the naive
  "record margins → sell" sign is wrong even directionally here; high-share years cluster
  in the disinflation/globalization era whose margins kept RISING (the escalator).
- **India (n=22 starts, 1994-2015, IIMA): corr −0.39 / Spearman −0.42.** Below-median
  capital share → +10.3%/yr; above-median → +5.5%/yr. The high-share starts are the
  mid-2000s boom — buying AFTER the great margin re-basing paid less than buying before
  it. n is tiny and one macro arc; a suggestion, not a regularity.

## Honest read
The two countries answer the "impact on stock market" question in opposite directions,
and BOTH are what the PS1/PS2 lesson predicts: the level of the profit slice is not a
return forecast; what matters is whether the slice is still re-basing (India pre-2010,
US 1980-2019 — equity holders capture the transfer as it happens) or already re-based
(returns thereafter come from growth and valuation, not further transfer). Both countries
now sit at the 80th+ percentile of their own histories with the global distribution still
drifting up — PS2 says that is NOT a sell signal (27% vs 21% unconditional decline odds),
and the US +0.36 print shows why betting on the reversion has been the losing side.
Changes no verdict; profit share stays a context state in the earnings-cycle seat.
