## Announcement vs. commissioning — why both dates matter

Per the brief and this desk's own standing view (TECH-D1's "entry state," the general rule that
an event date is a design input, never a rule): for infrastructure, the **announcement/approval
date** (cabinet nod, foundation stone, DPR sanction) is the candidate price-moving event — the
point the market re-prices expected future connectivity — while the **commissioning date**
(public opening) is mostly a confirmation with a smaller expected residual surprise, since years
of visible construction and press coverage precede it. Every infrastructure row needs **two
separate event-time zeros**, not one, and a design should not default to assuming commissioning
is the "real" event. Where a project was announced/approved more than once (revived after being
shelved), each is a distinct, legitimate event — Bangalore's Peripheral Ring Road and Chennai
Metro Phase 2 below are pure illustrations of this, with no matching commissioning event yet.

## PART A — Policy and tax event calendar

**A1. RERA (Real Estate Regulation and Development Act, 2016).** Passed by Parliament March 2016
(Rajya Sabha 10 Mar, Lok Sabha 15 Mar — moderate confidence on both days), assent shortly after.
Commencement was staggered, not a single date: ~59 of 92 "enabling" sections (authority/tribunal
constitution, rule-framing) in force from **1 May 2016**; substantive provisions (project
registration duty, 70% escrow, carpet-area disclosure, penalties, Sections 3-19) in force from
**1 May 2017** — the date most commonly cited as "RERA came into effect" [RECALL, moderate-high
confidence on both dates]. State rollout was uneven: **Maharashtra** notified rules and had
MahaRERA operational essentially by 1 May 2017 [moderate confidence]; **Uttar Pradesh** notified
rules in 2016 but practical operationalization reportedly lagged into 2017-2019 [low-moderate
confidence]; several states were still reported without functioning authorities well into
2018-2019, prompting court prodding [low confidence, general pattern only]. *Engine
implication*: use each state's own notification date for state-level features, not the national
1 May 2017 marker; the gap can exceed a year.

**A2. Benami Transactions (Prohibition) Amendment Act, 2016.** Armed the dormant 1988 Act with
enforcement machinery. Passed ~late July/early August 2016, assent August 2016, **in force from
1 November 2016** — one week before demonetisation [RECALL, moderate confidence]. The
sequencing (benami enforcement risk armed first, cash withdrawn second) is widely described as
a deliberate real-estate-targeted combination [interpretation, not a specific citable source].
*Engine implication*: model as a distinct dummy from 8 Nov, not merged into it — different
mechanism (benami/cash-funded-deal risk vs. liquidity shock).

**A3. Demonetisation, 8 November 2016.** PM Modi announced in a televised address that Rs 500/
1,000 notes ceased to be legal tender at midnight; exchange/deposit window ran into late
December 2016 (exact final cutoff, and any extensions, not confidently recalled to the day)
[RECALL, very high confidence on the date itself]. Real estate — cash-intensive, often
under-invoiced — was widely reported as a primary intended target, with press narratives of
falling registered volumes and rising unsold inventory over 2017-2018 [RECALL, no specific
magnitude retained with confidence — get any number from a verified source, not this dossier].

**A4. GST on real estate — introduction (2017) and rate cut (2019).** GST launched **1 July
2017**. Under-construction residential: effective **12% with ITC** (18% on two-thirds of value,
one-third deemed land value excluded); affordable-housing carve-out (PMAY-CLSS linked): **8%
with ITC**. Ready property with a completion/occupancy certificate: outside GST entirely (stamp
duty only) — OC-issuance date is itself a discrete tax-treatment break, useful as a feature.
**GST Council** (33rd meeting ~late Feb 2019, 34th ~mid-March 2019, days not confident) approved
new rates effective **1 April 2019**: **5% without ITC** (non-affordable), **1% without ITC**
(affordable, redefined ~60 sq m carpet in metro cities / 90 sq m elsewhere, value cap ~Rs 45
lakh — figures RECALL-level, verify metro-city list and cap) [moderate-high confidence on
structure, lower on exact sub-figures]. Builders with live projects as of 31 March 2019 got a
one-time election (old ITC regime vs. new no-ITC regime), deadline recalled as May 2019, day not
confident — meaning the same calendar date had heterogeneous project-level effects. *Engine
implication*: 1 Jul 2017 and 1 Apr 2019 are distinct regime breaks; the 2019 cut is a real
cost-side shift for developers (lost ITC), not simply buyer-price-negative.

**A5. IBC and homebuyers as financial creditors (2018).** Following the Jaypee Infratech crisis
and Supreme Court intervention (*Chitra Sharma v. Union of India*, 2017-2018), homebuyers were
made explicit **financial creditors** via an inserted Explanation to Section 5(8)(f) — first by
**Ordinance ~6 June 2018**, then the **IBC (Amendment) Act 2018** (~August 2018) [RECALL,
moderate confidence]. A late-2019/early-2020 Section 7 tightening required joint filing by a
minimum of 100 allottees or 10% of allottees, curbing individual filings. *Engine implication*:
2018 is the year developer default risk linked to a national insolvency mechanism with
homebuyers formally at the table — a structural break for stalled-project/distress features.

**A6. Maharashtra stamp duty holiday, 2020-2021 — the cleanest natural experiment here.**
Stamp duty cut in steps: **2% for 1 Sep-31 Dec 2020**, **3% for 1 Jan-31 Mar 2021**, reverting
to the standard rate (~5% Mumbai, higher elsewhere with local-body surcharges) from **1 April
2021** [RECALL, moderate confidence on structure]. Produced a widely reported registration
surge, concentrated in **December 2020** (last month of the deepest cut) and a smaller spike in
March 2021, followed by an **April 2021 payback**. On volumes: press (Knight Frank
India/CREDAI-MCHI joint data, Propstack, CRE Matrix) reported Mumbai city monthly registrations
rising from a low-thousands run rate to a reported record in the high-teens-of-thousands for
December 2020 [RECALL, **LOW confidence on the exact digit** — do not treat any specific count
in this dossier as load-bearing; pull the series from Maharashtra IGR or Knight Frank/CREDAI-MCHI
directly]. **Confound the engine must model**: the April 2021 dip coincides with the **COVID
second wave** and its lockdowns [moderate confidence on wave timing] — some of the drop is
reduced registration-office throughput, not pure demand reversion; any elasticity design must
control for lockdown severity by month, not read the drop as clean payback.

**A7. Section 43CA/50C safe-harbour widening — the circle-rate tolerance band.** Sections 43CA
(builder stock-in-trade) and 50C (capital asset) deem the stamp-duty/circle-rate value as sale
consideration when actual consideration is lower, subject to a tolerance band — **directly the
censored-regression design's tolerance parameter, and the most load-bearing, least-confident
item on this list.** Sequence: **Finance Act 2018** introduced a **5% tolerance**, effective AY
2019-20 (from 1 April 2018); **Finance Act 2020** widened it to **10%**, effective AY 2021-22
(from ~1 April 2020); as part of **"Aatmanirbhar Bharat 3.0"** (announced **12 November 2020**),
a temporary **20% tolerance** for **primary sales of residential units by builders only** (not
resale), value cap recalled as **Rs 2 crore**, window recalled as 12 Nov 2020-30 Jun 2021, given
legislative effect via **Finance Act 2021** [RECALL, **LOW-MODERATE confidence** on the exact
cap and window end-date — the two-step process (relief announced by press conference, legislated
months later in the Feb 2021 budget) needs both dates captured separately]. *Engine
implication*: the tolerance is **time-varying** (5% 2018-2020, 10% 2020-onward, 20% for a narrow
2020-21 primary-sale window) — a single fixed band across a multi-year sample misspecifies the
design; this is the top verification priority in the whole dossier.

**A8. Budget 2024 — indexation removal on property capital gains (23 July 2024).** Budget
2024-25, presented **23 July 2024**, proposed two holding-period buckets (12 months
listed/24 months other) and a harmonized **12.5% LTCG rate without indexation** — down from 20%
with indexation for property, up from 10% for equities. Backlash on the property side (nominal
gains there are often inflation-driven over long holds) led to a **grandfathering amendment**
in the **Finance (No. 2) Bill 2024**, passed ~6-7 August 2024: **resident individuals/HUFs**
(not companies) may choose, for property **acquired before 23 July 2024**, the **lower of** (a)
12.5% without indexation or (b) 20% with indexation [RECALL, moderate-high confidence]. *Engine
implication*: post-August-2024 data needs the option value modeled, not a flat 12.5% rate, and
the grandfathering applies only to direct individual/HUF holding, not corporate/trust structures.

**A9. LTCG reintroduced on listed equities, 2018 (for contrast).** Budget 2018-19 (1 Feb 2018)
introduced Section 112A: **10% without indexation** above **Rs 1 lakh**/year, effective AY
2019-20, with grandfathering (cost stepped up to FMV as of **31 January 2018**) [RECALL, high
confidence]. Equities and property were both re-touched in the same 2024 reform wave with
different grandfathering mechanics each time — relevant to any relative after-tax
equity-vs-property feature.

**A10. DDT abolition, 2020.** Budget 2020-21 abolished Dividend Distribution Tax effective
**1 April 2020**, moving dividend taxation to the recipient's slab rate with TDS under Section
194/194K [RECALL, high confidence on the date, moderate on section numbers]. Not real-estate
specific; included as a capital-markets regime marker for the same reform period as A8-A9.

**A11. TDS on property — Section 194-IA (2013) and later tightening.** Inserted by Finance Act
2013, effective **1 June 2013**: buyer must deduct **1% TDS** where consideration is **≥Rs 50
lakh** [RECALL, moderate-high confidence]. Amended (recalled as Finance Act 2022, ~1 April 2022)
to base TDS on the **higher of consideration or stamp-duty value**, closing an under-invoicing
gap [RECALL, **low-moderate confidence on this date** — interacts directly with A7, so a
misdated amendment here would misalign two related features]. Further amended (Finance (No. 2)
Act 2024, recalled ~1 October 2024) so the Rs 50 lakh threshold applies to **aggregate**
consideration across multiple buyers/sellers, not per pair [low confidence on exact date, more
confident on the substance]. *Engine implication*: three dates (1 Jun 2013, ~1 Apr 2022, ~1 Oct
2024) each change how much of the transaction universe leaves a tax-department paper trail.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE INDIA ENGINE

1. **Use two event-time zeros per infrastructure item, never one** — announcement/approval and
   commissioning are different features with different expected effect sizes; this desk's prior
   is that announcement carries the larger move.
2. **Model repeated-announcement/no-delivery projects (Bangalore PRR, Chennai Metro Ph.2) as
   right-censored, not as missing data** — the announcement-only pattern is itself informative
   about how much value front-loads on approval alone.
3. **The 43CA/50C safe-harbour band (A7) must be time-varying in any design** — 5% (2018-20),
   10% (2020-on), 20% (narrow 12 Nov 2020-30 Jun 2021 window, primary developer sales only); a
   fixed band across a multi-year sample misspecifies the censored-regression feature directly —
   verify this item first of everything in the dossier.
4. **The Maharashtra stamp-duty holiday (A6) is the cleanest natural experiment here but its
   April 2021 payback is confounded with the COVID second-wave lockdown** — control for lockdown
   severity by month before reading the dip as pure demand reversion.
5. **GST's 2019 cut (A4) removed ITC along with lowering the headline rate** — treat it as a
   developer net-cost change, not a pure buyer-price cut, and note the same date had
   heterogeneous project-level effects via the old/new-regime election.
6. **State RERA commencement is not a single national date** — 1 May 2017 is a defensible
   national marker, but state compliance/enforcement quality varied by a year-plus; use each
   state's own notification date for state-specific features (cross-check dossier C3).
7. **Two national airports and the bullet train sit in a live, named data gap** — none could be
   confirmed as commercially commissioned as of this dossier's knowledge horizon; do not encode
   any of B4/B5/B8 as "complete" without a direct current-status check.
8. **This is the highest-value re-run candidate in the C-series** — almost every item is a
   heavily publicized public event (cabinet approvals, PIB releases, ribbon-cuttings), so the
   cost of verification is low and the expected RECALL→1-/2-SOURCE upgrade rate should be high.
