## §1 — The common architecture across (almost) every state portal

Indian state registration systems were mostly built independently between roughly 2000 and 2015 (a
few were built once centrally as NGDRS and adopted, §12), but they converge on a near-identical
pattern `[RECALL — unverified structural claim]`:

1. **Tier 0 — free, no login, no captcha:** the *notified valuation* lookup — called guidance
   value / guideline value / ready reckoner / circle rate / jantri / DLC rate / fair value /
   collector rate depending on state, thirteen labels for one instrument (already flagged in this
   project's `B2.md`/`B4.md`). Almost always public because its purpose is taxpayer-facing. Grain is
   village/ward + a rate band by property type — a **zone-level administrative price**, never a
   specific parcel's transaction.
2. **Tier 1 — free/nominal fee, login/OTP, captcha:** an index search of registered documents — by
   document/registration number, or by village+survey/CTS/khasra/plot number+year, sometimes by
   party name. Returns an Index-II-style summary (parties, property description, consideration/
   market value, duty paid, date) but usually gates the scanned deed behind further payment or
   e-KYC. Aadhaar/mobile-OTP login is increasingly the gate rather than plain username/password.
3. **Tier 2 — paid, per-document:** certified copies, and Encumbrance Certificates (EC) — a
   chronological list of every registered transaction/charge against one parcel over a stated
   period (commonly 13 or 30 years) — the closest thing India publishes online to a chain-of-title.
4. **No tier: bulk export or API.** `[RECALL — unverified, stated as a negative with moderate-high
   confidence]` No major state portal is known to this agent to offer public bulk download or an
   open API of registered-transaction microdata. Every access mode is per-record, CAPTCHA-gated,
   and UI-paginated. A locality panel therefore implies either (a) a scripted, non-bulk, respectful
   scrape of the Tier-1 index search run **on the principal's machine**, ward by ward and year by
   year, or (b) an RTI/data-sharing approach to the state registration department — some Indian
   researchers/journalists are `[RECALL — unverified, no specific citation trusted]` reported to
   have obtained registration microdata this way; flagged for a future search pass, not invented.

## §2 — Maharashtra: IGR, the Annual Statement of Rates, eSBTR, e-registration

**Portal:** `igrmaharashtra.gov.in` (as given in brief; also in this project's `_PROBE.sh`).

- **e-Search/"Free Search."** `[RECALL — moderate]` Built on the historic SARITA/iSARITA
  registration software; searches by district→taluka→village or by document number/year, returning
  village/CTS-taluka geography, parties, consideration value, the ASR market value used for duty,
  and duty paid. I recall a genuinely fee-free public window covering an older band of years (very
  roughly mid-1980s to early-2000s in places), with recent documents and certified copies gated by
  a small fee and login. **The exact free-year band and fee schedule are `[RECALL — low, VERIFY]`.**
  Geography tops out at village/CTS number; no free-text address search recalled. CAPTCHA-gated;
  full documents need a registered account and per-document fee.
- **eSBTR** (electronic Secured Bank and Treasury Receipt) replaced physical stamp paper for
  higher-value duty payments, issued through authorised banks and validated against the treasury —
  a payment-instrument record, not a price record; any published issuance-volume statistic is
  `[RECALL — low confidence, likely doesn't exist]`.
- **e-registration of Leave & License.** Tenants/landlords self-register rental agreements online
  via Aadhaar e-KYC/e-sign, no Sub-Registrar visit `[RECALL — moderate]`. This is the system this
  project's own `_TAIL-CONNECT.md` already flags for the rental/yield route (handoff §4.2) — plausibly
  the richest **rent** microdata source in India because rent-agreement duty is low, so
  under-declaration incentive is weak relative to sale transactions `[RECALL — unverified
  interpretation]`.
- **Annual Statement of Rates ("ready reckoner").** Published annually (historically effective 1
  January) at **zone and sub-zone level**; Mumbai cross-walks zones/sub-zones to individual CTS
  numbers, with separate rates for open land, RCC flats/offices/shops, non-RCC/chawl construction
  and industrial use, plus age depreciation and (I recall, low confidence) a floor-rise loading for
  some high-rise categories. **I recall rates being left frozen for an unusually long stretch around
  2020-2022 (pandemic-cited) before revisions resumed — exact frozen years, resumption year, and any
  2024-2026 revision are `[RECALL — low, VERIFY]`.** Because it republishes annually at CTS-level
  grain, the ASR is the best-documented Indian candidate for the sale-price-to-guideline-value ratio
  technique `B2.md` flags as missing from this desk's India work.

## §3 — Karnataka: Kaveri 2.0, Bhoomi, guidance value

Kaveri Online Services (domain recalled as `kaverionline.karnataka.gov.in`-pattern, **not confident
enough to assert**); the 2.0 relaunch (tighter Aadhaar/DigiLocker verification, closer Bhoomi
integration) year is `[RECALL — low, VERIFY]`. **Guidance value** is free, no login, searched by
district→taluk→village+survey number (rural) or locality/street (urban wards), giving a per-sq-ft/
sq-m band by category. Document/index search needs login; party-name and deed-number search
recalled, full images gated by payment `[RECALL — moderate]`. **EC** can be requested and paid
online, delivered as a digitally-signed PDF listing every transaction/charge over the requested
window (commonly 13 or 30 years) — the closest India gets to an online chain-of-title `[RECALL —
moderate]`. **Bhoomi** (`bhoomi.karnataka.gov.in`, moderate) is the separate, older (early-2000s)
rural Record-of-Rights/Tenancy/Crops (RTC, "Pahani") system, one of India's most-studied
e-governance land-record projects (widely cited for reducing petty bribery in obtaining RTC copies)
`[RECALL — unverified; no specific paper trusted from memory, flagged not invented]`. It is a
land-record system, not a price system, but its survey numbers key Kaveri's lookups for rural land.

## §4 — Telangana: IGRS + Dharani (and its unwinding)

The least stable entry, and that instability is itself the finding. **Dharani** (launched ~October
2020, `[RECALL — moderate]`) unified agricultural Record-of-Rights/Pattadar Passbook and
registration into one system meant to remove VRO/sub-registrar discretion. It became politically
contentious: widespread complaints of wrongly frozen or misclassified parcels, litigation over
disputed ("Part-B") lands excluded from it, farmers reportedly unable to sell/mortgage trapped land
`[RECALL — moderate on the shape of the controversy; no specific affected-parcel count asserted]`.
After the December 2023 change of government, the state announced an overhaul — **I recall a "Bhu
Bharati" name attached to a 2024 successor law/authority, but the act name, passage date and current
operational status are `[RECALL — low, VERIFY before citing]`.** Urban/non-agricultural registration
ran separately via **IGRS Telangana** (`registration.telangana.gov.in`, moderate), which also
carries guideline-value lookup and standard document/EC search. **Any Telangana time series needs a
dated break at the 2020 Dharani launch and again at the 2024 replacement's effective date**, exactly
as this desk tracks RBI HPI/NHB RESIDEX rebase breaks.

## §5 — Andhra Pradesh: IGRS + Meebhoomi

AP runs its own IGRS (`registration.ap.gov.in`, moderate) for duty/registration and guideline-value
lookup by village/ward, with land records separately on **Meebhoomi/Webland** (domain low
confidence) for Record-of-Rights/Adangal data. A vague recollection of a mid-2020s blockchain
land-records pilot exists but no vendor, date or scope is trusted from memory `[RECALL — low,
flagged for search, not asserted]`.

## §6 — Tamil Nadu: TNREGINET, guideline value

`tnreginet.gov.in` (as given in brief). Tamil Nadu was among the earliest states to computerize
registration (a distinct system name from Maharashtra's SARITA is recalled but not trusted
precisely). Exposes: **guideline value search** by street/village (free, no login); **EC search
online**, recalled covering documents from roughly 1987 onward digitized, earlier ones needing
manual application `[RECALL — low-moderate, VERIFY the 1987 cutoff]`; guideline values revised
periodically rather than annually, with a notable revision around 2017 after a freeze since ~2012
recalled, and further revisions since with no specific year/magnitude trusted. Patta/Chitta revenue
records sit on a separate e-services portal — the registration/land-records split seen everywhere.

## §7 — Gujarat: Garvi and Jantri (the 2023 revision)

**Garvi** is Gujarat's computerized registration front-end (acronym expansion not trusted, do not
quote it), giving sub-registrar-office-level index/document search. **Jantri** is Gujarat's
notified-benchmark land value flooring stamp duty. Headline: **jantri rates were fixed around 2011
and left essentially frozen for roughly a decade; a revision took effect in mid-April 2023 and was
widely reported as roughly DOUBLING rates statewide, non-uniformly by area.** `[RECALL — moderate on
the "doubled, April 2023, after a ~2011 freeze" shape; low confidence on the precise average
multiple, and on whether any category — I have a vague recollection of push-back/possible partial
relief on agricultural land — was rolled back; no specific figure trusted.]` This is exactly the
single-source-in-memory number the tag discipline exists to catch — confirm against the actual
Gujarat Revenue Department notification before treating Jantri-2023 as a dated regime break; once
confirmed it belongs in `breaks-registry.md` alongside BR7-BR9.

## §8 — Uttar Pradesh: IGRSUP and district circle rates

`igrsup.gov.in` (acronym given in brief). Publishes **circle rates** at district→tehsil→mohalla/
village level, revised on district-level review cycles rather than a fixed annual calendar as far as
recalled (no reliable year-by-year revision list trusted). Free, no-login circle-rate lookup plus a
duty calculator; document search gated by login/OTP. **UP Bhulekh** (`upbhulekh.gov.in`, moderate)
is the separate khasra/khatauni land-record portal. Whether UP has migrated any registration
function onto NGDRS is **not confidently recalled either way** — an open question, not a guess.

## §9 — Delhi: DORIS and the circle-rate categories A-H

**DORIS** (Delhi Online Registration Information System) handles Sub-Registrar appointments, duty
e-payment and document/deed search (domain not confidently recalled) `[RECALL — moderate]`.
**Delhi's circle rates are structured into eight locality categories, A through H** — Category A the
most expensive localities down to Category H the least expensive/unauthorized-colony-type areas
`[RECALL — moderate-high on the existence and A-H structure, recalled as dating to Delhi's original
~2007 circle-rate introduction; low confidence on current per-category figures, none asserted]`.
This is a **step function over ~8 bins per property type** — coarser than Maharashtra's zone/
sub-zone ASR or Karnataka's ward-level guidance value, which caps how much resolution a Delhi
guideline-value feature can carry.

## §10 — Haryana/Gurugram: collector rates

Haryana uses **"collector rate,"** notified by the District Collector and revised periodically;
Gurugram's rates are published sector-by-sector (numbered planning sectors, not village names)
`[RECALL — moderate]`. Land records sit on **Jamabandi** (`jamabandi.nic.in`, moderate) — note this
"Jamabandi" name is also the generic north-Indian term for the record-of-rights register itself, and
Punjab (§11) uses the same brand for its own, separate portal; a naming collision worth flagging so
the two are not conflated. No distinct Haryana e-registration brand name confidently recalled.

## §11 — West Bengal, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Kerala, Punjab

- **West Bengal:** own portal (recalled `wbregistration.gov.in`, low confidence), e-registration
  recalled as "e-Nathikaran"; land records on **Banglarbhumi** (`banglarbhumi.gov.in`, moderate),
  organized by **mouza** (Bengal's village-unit term) rather than "village." Guideline-value naming
  not confidently recalled.
- **Rajasthan:** notified values are **DLC (District Level Committee) rates** (as given in brief),
  searchable via the state portal (recalled `igrs.rajasthan.gov.in`, moderate) by zone/colony within
  district; e-stamping and e-panjiyan modules exist `[RECALL — low-moderate]`.
- **Madhya Pradesh:** registration software recalled as **"Sampada"** ("Sampada 2.0" upgrade, early-
  to-mid 2020s, adding geo-referencing and biometric/e-KYC), portal on the `mpigr.gov.in` pattern
  `[RECALL — moderate on the name, low on the version-2 year and domain]`. Guideline value recalled
  as a "collector guideline rate" `[RECALL — low]`. MP Bhulekh/Bhu-Abhilekh is the separate land-
  record portal.
- **Kerala:** publishes **"Fair Value" of land** (as given in brief), searchable by district/taluk/
  village+survey/re-survey number, land measured in **cents** (1/100 acre) — an area-basis trap for
  cross-state normalisation on top of carpet/built-up/super-built-up. A significant fair-value
  revision (possibly a large multiple in places) is recalled around 2023-2024 amid controversy, but
  **no percentage or date is trusted from memory** `[RECALL — low, VERIFY]`.
- **Punjab:** recalled as an **NGDRS adopter** (§12) rather than a bespoke system; land records on
  **Jamabandi** (`jamabandi.punjab.gov.in`, moderate) providing the "Fard" record-of-rights extract.

## §12 — The national layer: NGDRS, DILRMP, Bhoomi/Bhulekh/Bhu-Naksha, SVAMITVA, ULPIN

- **NGDRS**, `ngdrs.gov.in` (as given in brief; also in `_PROBE.sh`) — a NIC-built, configurable
  registration-software product under DILRMP so states without resources to build their own
  SARITA/Kaveri/Dharani-style system could adopt a common one. **Recalled adopters: Punjab, Himachal
  Pradesh, Jharkhand, Manipur, Puducherry, and several smaller UTs (Andaman & Nicobar, Dadra & Nagar
  Haveli) — `[RECALL — low confidence, incomplete, VERIFY the full current list]`.** The larger
  states named above (Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Telangana/AP, Gujarat, UP, MP, West Bengal)
  run independently-built systems. **NGDRS is a shared codebase, not a shared database** — there is
  no single national portal exposing registered-transaction data across all of India.
- **DILRMP** (Digital India Land Records Modernisation Programme, renamed from NLRMP recalled
  ~2015-16) rests on three pillars: computerising Records of Rights, computerising/linking
  registration (via NGDRS or a state's own system), and modern survey/resurvey with updated cadastral
  maps. A public **dashboard** is recalled at `dilrmp.gov.in` (also in `_PROBE.sh`) showing state-wise
  progress metrics (share of RoRs digitised, offices computerised); **no specific current completion
  percentage is trusted from memory.**
- **Bhoomi/Bhulekh/Bhu-Naksha** are the record-of-rights/cadastral-map layer, state-by-state under
  shared naming conventions: Bhoomi (Karnataka's RTC, §3); **Bhulekh** (reused by UP, Bihar, Odisha,
  MP and others — each a separate state system despite the shared brand, not one federated
  database); **Bhu-Naksha** (`bhunaksha.nic.in` or state subdomains, moderate) — the NIC-built
  cadastral **map** layer complementing text-only Bhulekh with a plot-boundary drawing per survey/
  khasra number.
- **SVAMITVA** (Ministry of Panchayati Raj, ~2020 launch recalled) uses drone surveys to map rural
  **inhabited/abadi ("lal dora")** land — historically unsurveyed even where surrounding agricultural
  land had RoR records — issuing rural residents property cards for the first time; complementary
  to, not a replacement for, Bhoomi/Bhulekh agricultural RoR data.
- **ULPIN/"Bhu-Aadhaar"**: a 14-digit alphanumeric parcel identifier explicitly analogised to
  Aadhaar for individuals, piloted from around 2021 recalled under DILRMP, meant to let one parcel be
  tracked consistently across registration/RoR/survey databases that today use inconsistent local
  keys (survey, khasra, CTS, gat number) that do not compose across systems. **This is the single
  most important national land-record fact for this project if far enough along** — a working ULPIN
  is exactly the persistent parcel identifier `B1.md` already flags as missing for a Case-Shiller-
  style repeat-sales index in India. **Current national coverage is `[RECALL — unverified, no figure
  asserted]`** and the single highest-value item for a future search pass.

## §13 — The area-basis trap, restated for this pack

Every rate above — ready reckoner, guidance value, guideline value, jantri, circle rate, DLC rate,
collector rate, fair value — is quoted per unit of land or built area, and the brief's own point
stands: **carpet/built-up/super-built-up/plot-area bases move Indian per-sqft figures by roughly
20-35% for the same physical unit**, and states are not even internally consistent on which basis
applies to land versus flats (Kerala's cents-based land measure is a further wrinkle). No figure in
this dossier is basis-normalised; that step belongs explicitly to the engine's ingestion layer,
per state.

## §14 — THE BLUNT VERDICT: best first target

**Maharashtra**, for four reasons (`[RECALL — unverified reasoning]`):

1. The only state here where this desk has prior internal evidence of use: `A5.md` states IGR-style
   monthly registration counts are "proven for Mumbai," and `fincycle-deep/partC-data.md` already
   treats Maharashtra registration counts/stamp duty as part of the standing India pipeline.
2. Its notified-valuation instrument (the ASR) publishes at the finest geography here (zone/sub-zone
   cross-walked to CTS numbers in Mumbai) and re-issues **annually** — the cadence a sale-price-to-
   guideline-value ratio series needs.
3. Mumbai/Pune/Thane/Nagpur carry among the highest registered-transaction volumes in India, reaching
   a usable locality-month sample size sooner than smaller states — directly relevant to `B2.md`'s
   hierarchical-shrinkage "how few transactions support a locality index" problem.
4. Its digital stack (eSBTR, Aadhaar-based leave-and-license e-registration) is comparatively mature
   — weak but real evidence that a future RTI/data-sharing approach run from the principal's machine
   has better odds here than in a state mid-transition (Telangana) or running a coarser valuation
   grid (Delhi's 8 bins).

**Runner-up: Karnataka** (Kaveri 2.0 + Bhoomi maturity, Bangalore volume, EC as a genuine
chain-of-title asset worth testing early). **Third: Tamil Nadu** (Chennai volume, TNREGINET
granularity). **Avoid Telangana as a first target** despite Hyderabad's size — Dharani's 2024
unwinding means any series there carries an unresolved structural break inside the exact years the
engine would most want to train on. None of this changes `_TAIL-PROBE.md`/`_HEAD.md` §5: **whichever
state is chosen, the pull runs on the principal's own machine, not from this session** — this
dossier only says where to point it first.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE INDIA ENGINE

1. **Do not script acquisition against these portals from a web/remote session.** IGR Maharashtra,
   NGDRS and DILRMP are already measured unreachable (403 at the gateway) from here; treat every
   other state portal named above as equally unreachable until proven otherwise on the principal's
   own machine.
2. **Stand up Maharashtra first**: pair IGR e-search index records (village/CTS + consideration
   value + date) with the same year's ASR at the matching CTS/sub-zone to build the sale-price-to-
   ready-reckoner ratio series `B2.md` flags as the missing technique — before any other state.
3. **Register a dated regime-break the moment any unverified date is confirmed** — Gujarat's 2023
   jantri revision and Telangana's Dharani-to-successor transition are the two likeliest candidates,
   belonging in `breaks-registry.md` alongside BR7-BR9 once pinned down.
4. **Treat "circle rate/guidance value/guideline value/jantri/DLC rate/collector rate/fair value" as
   one instrument with thirteen-plus state labels, never thirteen concepts** — but never pool levels
   across states without normalising for area basis (§13) and zoning coarseness (Delhi's 8 categories
   vs Maharashtra's fine sub-zones vs Kerala's per-survey cents).
5. **EC requests, not index search, are the closest India gets to a per-parcel panel.** Karnataka and
   Tamil Nadu both appear to offer this online; test whether EC text (unstructured but per-parcel and
   dated) parses into a structured history more cheaply than page-by-page index scraping.
6. **ULPIN/Bhu-Aadhaar rollout status is the single highest-value fact to confirm next** — it would
   resolve the persistent-identifier gap `B1.md` already flags as blocking a repeat-sales index.
7. **No state offers bulk download or an API, as far as memory can determine.** Budget every design
   around per-record, CAPTCHA-gated retrieval at ward/sub-registrar-office grain, not a city- or
   state-wide pull in one pass.
8. **Re-verify this entire dossier before anything in it is cited externally or embedded in a
   scraper.** Every domain, date and percentage above is `[RECALL — unverified]`; the zero-search-
   budget failure that produced this should itself be logged as a batch-capacity issue, as `B2.md`
   already independently found for itself.
