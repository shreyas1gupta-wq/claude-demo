## 1. China: three tiers, and the credibility problem that motivates all of them

**NBS 70-city index** (70个大中城市住宅销售价格指数) — monthly, new-commodity and secondary sales,
70 cities, MoM/YoY. A 2011 revision moved it toward a constant-quality matched-model/hedonic
design, replacing a simpler average-price approach criticized for composition bias.

**Known smoothing/underreporting mechanism**: local statistical bureaus answer to local
governments with a fiscal stake in land-sale revenue and a political stake in not showing visible
declines; recollection of 2021-2023 "window guidance" (窗口指导) and, in some cities, explicit
limits on developer price cuts — a government-enforced sticky-list-price mechanism. Financial
commentary widely described NBS prints showing single-digit YoY declines through 2021-2023 while
private secondary-market data showed larger cuts. **No confident percentage-point gap to cite** —
qualitative finding only.

**Fang, Gu, Xiong & Zhou** — "Demystifying the Chinese Housing Boom," *NBER Macroeconomics
Annual* (recalled vol. 30, 2015 annual/2016 print, U. Chicago Press for NBER). Uses loan-level
mortgage-origination data (one major bank) across **120 cities, 2003-2013**, building a hedonic
constant-quality index from the loan file itself, bypassing NBS's collection chain. Finding: true
quality-adjusted appreciation was substantially larger than officially reported, especially in the
largest, supply-constrained cities — the landmark corrective to a distrusted official index.

**Glaeser, Huang, Ma & Shleifer** — "A Real Estate Boom with Chinese Characteristics," *Journal of
Economic Perspectives* (recalled vol. 31 no. 1, 2017). Documents high vacancy in new stock
("ghost cities," some new-district estimates 20%+), local-government reliance on land-finance
revenue absent a nationwide recurring property tax (only pilots, e.g. Shanghai/Chongqing since
2011), construction-quality/depreciation concerns, and frames the boom as a rational response to
limited alternative investment vehicles (capital controls, thin bond/equity markets) rather than a
pure bubble — concluding oversupply, not speculation, is the dominant downside risk.

**Private tier**: **CREIS** (China Index Academy, historically SouFun/Fang Holdings-affiliated) —
hundred-cities index (百城价格指数) from broad listing/agent data, more responsive than NBS.
**Beike/Ke Research Institute** (贝壳研究院, KE Holdings/Lianjia) — built from the platform's own
closed deals and listings, a genuine secondary-market advantage but a platform-coverage sample, not
a census. **Centaline** (中原地产) — its Centa-City Index for Hong Kong (weekly, actual registered
secondary transactions) is a credible high-frequency series; mainland brokerage data feeds
mainland coverage separately.

**The template worth generalizing**: an administratively-compiled official series suspected of
smoothing declines, checked against a microdata academic corrective and against listing/platform
private indices that diverge in the predicted direction — recurs in weaker form below.

## 2. Brazil: an asking-price flagship, a cost index mistaken for a price index, and an appraisal series

**FipeZap** — FIPE (linked to USP) + ZAP (classifieds portal, later Grupo ZAP/OLX Brasil), since
~2011, monthly, ~20 cities (SP/RJ-weighted), sale and rent, apartment-dominated. **Built from
asking prices, not closed transactions.** Implication: structurally sticky downward — sellers cut
listings slowly — so in a correction (Brazil 2015-2017 recalled as the standard example) FipeZap
likely understates the true decline versus the negotiated closing discount, the same directional
bias as China's list-price stickiness via ordinary seller psychology rather than state pressure.
No quantified Brazilian ask-vs-close gap recalled — an honest gap, not invented.

**Abecip** (mortgage lenders'/S&L association) — recollection: primarily lending-volume/
origination data for the SBPE funding channel, not a widely-cited price index in its own right.

**IBGE** — a deliberate negative flag: I do **not** believe IBGE runs a transaction house-price
index. It runs **SINAPI** (with Caixa Econômica Federal), a **construction-cost** index, and PNAD
Contínua (tenure/rent data only) — "IBGE house prices" would be a naming error, worth not
repeating for MOSPI.

**IVG-R** — Banco Central do Brasil's Índice de Valores de Garantia de Imóveis Residenciais
Financiados, quarterly, built from **appraisal/collateral values** on financed loans, major metros,
base recalled early-2000s. Closer to real valuation than FipeZap's asks, but not a closed price,
and blind to Brazil's large cash-purchase segment (historically low mortgage penetration).

## 3. Mexico: appraisal-based SHF, and an INEGI relationship I cannot confirm

**SHF** (Sociedad Hipotecaria Federal) — Índice SHF de Precios de la Vivienda en México, quarterly,
national/state, base ~2005. Built from **mortgage-appraisal values**: SHF's own loans plus,
recalled, Infonavit (mandatory payroll-deduction fund for formal private-sector workers, Mexico's
largest originator by volume) and Fovissste (public-sector equivalent), plus private banks.
Compositional caveat: a shift in Infonavit's origination share could move the blended index for
reasons unrelated to market price — analogous to, but distinct from, India's formal/informal
housing-finance split.

**INEGI** — I recall INEGI is involved in a house-price product somehow, but am **not confident**
whether it runs an independent repeat-sales index, co-brands an "SHF-INEGI" product, or whether
I am conflating institutions. `[VERIFY]` explicitly before any citation.

## 4. Turkey: appraisal-based TCMB index, and reading nominal prices under 40-70% inflation

**TCMB Konut Fiyat Endeksi (KFE)** — Turkey's central-bank Residential Property Price Index,
monthly, base ~2010=100, city-level plus Istanbul and a national aggregate. Built from **hedonic
regression on bank mortgage-appraisal valuations** (licensed-appraiser reports submitted by banks
for underwriting) — same family as SHF/BSP below.

**Why this matters for India's own long nominal series**: Turkish CPI inflation ran from ~15-20%
(2020) to a peak recalled around **85% (Oct 2022)**, staying 40-70%+ through 2023-2024
[moderate confidence, treat as directional]. Nominal YoY house-price prints of 100%+ were common
in this window — meaningless undeflated. TCMB also publishes a **real** (CPI-deflated) series
alongside the nominal one, telling a far more modest story. **Mechanical lesson: any long Indian
nominal series spanning a high-inflation era (WPI-dominant 1970s-1990s, or any future one) must be
deflated before cross-period comparison, exactly as TCMB does.**

**A sharper, second-order lesson**: Turkey also has a recalled dispute between TÜİK's official CPI
and an independent group (recalled as ENAG, `[VERIFY]` name) publishing materially higher
inflation estimates through 2021-2023. If real, this is the *same shape* as China's NBS problem
one level removed — not "is the house-price index honest" but "is the deflator itself honest."
Any India parameter assuming CPI/WPI is a trustworthy long-window deflator should note this failure
mode exists in a major EM peer.

## 5. Russia: a genuine registry-based secondary series, plus a bank's own platform

**Rosstat** — primary market (developer-reported, via regional statistical offices) and secondary
market, quarterly, by federal subject/largest cities, average price/sqm. The secondary-market leg
ultimately rests, as recalled, on **Rosreestr** — Russia's title-registration and cadastre
authority, a direct structural analogue to India's IGR. A real registration base, but a simple
average, not hedonic/repeat-sales, so composition can move the print independent of true price
change.

**SberIndex** — Sberbank's big-data initiative, using its own mortgage book and DomClick
(Sberbank's real-estate/mortgage marketplace) listing and closed-deal data; higher-frequency,
more granular than Rosstat. Sberbank's mortgage share has been recalled as 50%+ historically,
helping representativeness but still a single-institution sample.

**Dating caveat**: independent verification of Russian data became substantially harder after
February 2022 (sanctions, access restrictions); recent-vintage reads deserve elevated caution
beyond the standard EM caveats here.

## 6. South Africa: the deeds-office analogue done well, bank-book vs full-census

**The Deeds Office** system is the direct analogue the brief names, and is widely regarded as one
of the more complete, reliable registries in Sub-Saharan Africa — worth flagging as a **relative
best case**, not a "weak registry" example, when weighing it as a template.

- **ABSA House Price Index** — Absa Home Loans, built from **Absa's own financed transactions
  only**, by house size/price band, monthly, very long history (components recalled to the
  1960s-70s, modern methodology far more recent).
- **FNB House Price Index** — same structure, FNB's own book, alongside its Property Barometer.
- **Lightstone Property** — a private analytics firm aggregating, as recalled, the **full Deeds
  Office transfer register** including cash purchases — the closest thing here to a true
  transaction census on registered deeds, and the benchmark the narrower bank-book series are
  checked against.

**India template**: the clean illustration of single-lender bank-book proxy (buildable today from
RBI/bank home-loan sanction data) vs full registered-transaction census (only once IGR/NGDRS/
DILRMP data is digitized and linkable). Build the former first, target the latter.

## 7. Poland: the one series designed to measure its own asking-vs-transaction gap

**NBP BaRN** (Baza Rynku Nieruchomości) — National Bank of Poland's regional branches, quarterly.
Uniquely, collects **both** offer/asking prices (from listings and agent submissions) **and**
transaction prices (from banks, developers, notaries, intermediaries) for the **same** cities and
periods — Warsaw, Kraków, Wrocław, the Tricity (Gdańsk/Gdynia/Sopot), Poznań, Łódź, the Katowice
conurbation, plus wider voivodeship capitals, split primary/secondary. Free, in NBP's quarterly
real-estate market report.

**The gap**: recalled as offer prices typically above eventual transaction prices by a
single-digit to low-double-digit percent in ordinary conditions, narrowing or flipping in tight
seller's markets (recalled ~2021-2022). **No specific citable NBP figure held — approximate,
unconfirmed range only, not a number to plug into any model.**

**Why disproportionately valuable**: the only series here **designed** to answer the
asking-vs-transaction question directly, rather than leaving the gap to be inferred. If India ever
pairs a listing aggregator with a registered-transaction proxy, BaRN's paired-collection design —
not FipeZap's or HassConsult's single-construction design — is the template to copy.

## 8. Thailand, Indonesia, Vietnam, Philippines: a fourth construction type

**Thailand** — Bank of Thailand's Residential Property Price Index, recalled as combining (i) the
Treasury Department's (กรมธนารักษ์) official appraised valuation roll (a government-set floor used
for registration-fee/tax purposes, structurally like India's circle rates) with (ii) bank
mortgage/collateral data. The Government Housing Bank's Real Estate Information Center (REIC)
separately runs a more granular, practitioner-used index plus launch/take-up data. `[VERIFY]` on
the precise construction split.

**Indonesia** — Bank Indonesia's **Survei Harga Properti Residensial (SHPR)**, a quarterly survey
of **developers/marketing offices** in major metros (Jakarta/Jabodebek, Surabaya, Medan, Bandung)
on primary-market **list prices** by house-type segment, producing the IHPR index — a genuinely
distinct fourth construction type (developer-reported primary list price, not agent-ask,
appraisal, or closed deal). BI's survey also yields a weighted-net-balance expectations indicator.

**Vietnam** — no robust, long-running, free official index recalled. Ministry of Construction
issues periodic commentary; Savills/CBRE Vietnam and batdongsan.com.vn give asking-price city
reports for HCMC/Hanoi. **Stated as a genuine data-poor negative finding**, not a recall gap
papered over.

**Philippines** — BSP's **RREPI**, quarterly since ~2016, built from **administrative bank data on
newly granted residential loans** (appraisal/loan-file price) — same family as SHF/TCMB. Splits
NCR vs Areas Outside NCR, by housing type. Blind spot: financed transactions only, missing the
cash segment likely large at the high end and in less-banked areas.

**Kenya** — **HassConsult Property Index**, quarterly since ~2000, covering houses (Nairobi

large, distinct Kenyan asset class). Built from HassConsult's own listings/sales/agent network —
asking-adjacent, similar in spirit to FipeZap, though as a transacting agency it likely has some
closed-price visibility. Ask-vs-close mix not confidently known.

## 10. Recurring measurement lessons

**Four construction types, not one**: (a) **asking/listing** (FipeZap, HassConsult, partly Beike/
CREIS); (b) **appraisal/collateral** (SHF, TCMB, BSP, Absa/FNB, IVG-R); (c) **registered
transaction/deed** (Lightstone, Rosstat's secondary leg via Rosreestr, Chile's IPV if recalled
correctly, BaRN's transaction half); (d) **developer primary-market survey** (Indonesia's SHPR,
NBS China's new-build leg partly). Any India taxonomy should use this exact four-way split — each
type carries a different structural bias.

**Government-credibility risk is not China-only.** Turkey's TÜİK-vs-ENAG dispute is a second,
independent case of the official *deflator* itself being distrusted — contaminating any real-price
calculation one layer upstream of the index's own construction. Any long-window CPI/WPI deflator
assumption in the India engine should carry this caveat explicitly.

**Bank/appraisal indices share a structural blind spot: cash transactions** — SHF, TCMB, BSP,
Absa/FNB all miss unfinanced purchases. This compounds, in India, with the distinct IGR
under-recording mechanism (stamp-duty-minimizing understatement toward the circle rate) — two
separate biases that must never be netted against each other without an explicit argument.

**Composition/mix effects dominate naive index error nearly everywhere.** NBS's 2011 revision,
Rosstat's simple regional averaging, and every survey-based series (BaRN, SHPR, HassConsult) carry
mix risk absent an explicit hedonic/repeat-sales/matched-model adjustment — most series here do
not clearly state one is applied.

**Area basis is a live risk in every country here, not just India.** Brazil (m² útil vs m²
privativo), Turkey (net alan vs brüt alan), and regional condo-unit conventions plausibly carry
the same order-of-magnitude ambiguity as India's carpet/built-up/super-built-up split. None
confirmed this session — treat as a design principle (record area basis at ingestion, always),
not a quantified correction.

**The documented asking-vs-transaction gap.** Of everything in this file, **only Poland's NBP
BaRN is explicitly designed to measure this gap**, and even there the recalled magnitude
(single-digit to low-double-digit percent, narrowing/inverting in tight markets) is approximate,
unconfirmed. No other asking-based index here (FipeZap, HassConsult) has a published, quantified
companion transaction series — the desk cannot currently size their bias except directionally, an
honest gap to carry forward rather than a number to invent.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE INDIA ENGINE

1. **Adopt the four-way taxonomy (asking / appraisal / registered-transaction / developer-survey)**
   and classify every Indian source into it before pooling any two: Magicbricks/99acres/Housing.com
   = asking; RBI bank home-loan sanction data = appraisal; IGR/NGDRS = registered-transaction; RERA
   project filings = developer-survey (Indonesia's SHPR is the closest foreign analogue).
2. **Place India's circle-rate/ready-reckoner system against Thailand's Treasury valuation roll,
   not South Africa's deeds register.** Both are administrative floors, not market-clearing prices
   — IGR should not be assumed to behave like a clean transaction census; it behaves like a
   valuation roll with a compliance floor.
3. **Deflate any long Indian nominal series spanning a high-inflation window**, following TCMB's
   real-HPI practice — and check the deflator's own credibility (CPI/WPI vintage/revisions), given
   the TÜİK/ENAG precedent that an official deflator can itself be the contested variable.
4. **Do not import a foreign asking-vs-transaction haircut into India.** Only Poland has even an
   approximate figure; measure any Indian listing-vs-registration gap directly, BaRN-style, rather
   than borrowing a plug-in number from FipeZap or HassConsult.
5. **Build the bank-book proxy first, the full-census index second** — South Africa's ABSA/FNB
   (buildable today from scheduled-bank data) vs Lightstone (only once registry data is digitized
   and linkable) is the staging path as IGR/NGDRS/DILRMP mature.
6. **Watch for China-style local suppression of recorded declines at Indian state/municipal
   level** wherever stamp-duty or land-monetization revenue is fiscally material — cross-check any
   single state's registration series against a private comparator, as China analysts do between
   NBS and CREIS/Beike/Centaline.
7. **Treat RERA project-level disclosed prices as India's version of Indonesia's BI developer
   survey** — a cheap, primary-market-only proxy, useful for new-launch dynamics, not a substitute
   for an asking or registered-transaction index.
8. **Benchmark any future NHB RESIDEX-type series against a listing-based comparator across a full
   down cycle**, not a snapshot — the diagnostic, per China, is whether the two diverge
   specifically during declines, the signature of official smoothing rather than ordinary noise.
