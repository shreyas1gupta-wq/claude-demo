## 0. Why this dossier is load-bearing

Every sibling dossier describes data or mechanism; this one describes the referee. Get the
evaluation discipline wrong and a locality engine will report confident numbers that are pure
artifact of (a) an autocorrelated target beaten against too-soft a benchmark, (b) hundreds of
localities producing lucky winners by chance, (c) folds that leak future or neighboring
information. This desk has already lived this failure once: the ER-arc's pooled equation looked
like it "survived" at 26.7% in-sample at 10y, then failed OOS at both horizons once purged and
honestly benchmarked, and single-country kitchen sinks exploded to -389% OOS. Goyal-Welch already
binds this desk's equity work. House prices need a **stricter** version of the same standard,
because the target is far more autocorrelated than equity returns — which makes naive benchmarks
far easier to beat by accident.

## 1. The central trap: autocorrelation makes every naive benchmark too soft

Goyal-Welch's benchmark (the historical mean) is *hard* for equities because returns are close to
unpredictable. Locality house-price indices are the opposite case. **[RECALL, high confidence]**
Case, K.E. & Shiller, R.J. (1989), "The Efficiency of the Market for Single-Family Homes,"
*American Economic Review* 79(1), pp. 125–137 — regional appreciation is **positively serially
correlated**, last year's change predicts next year's with the same sign. **[RECALL, moderate
confidence]** Geltner, D. (1989), on appraisal-based real estate return risk (recalled as *Real
Estate Economics*/AREUEA Journal, volume/pages uncertain) — repeat-sales/appraisal indices are
mechanically **smoothed**, manufacturing *additional* spurious serial correlation beyond any
genuine persistence, from sparse-transaction interpolation.

Consequence: an R² against the unconditional mean is close to meaningless for a target this
persistent — a model that just echoes last period's value scores high against that benchmark
without forecasting anything. The correct, harder null is the **random walk / no-change**
forecast. This is exactly Clark-West's nested-model logic (§2): a random walk is nested inside a
richer AR(1)+covariates model, and the challenger's extra parameters add estimation noise that
biases the comparison *toward* the simpler nested model even when the extra structure helps.
**Every India engine deliverable must report accuracy against both benchmarks, labeled, with the
RW/no-change comparison treated as binding** — an unconditional-mean R² alone would read exactly
like the in-sample equity R²s Goyal-Welch spent a paper debunking.

## 2. Pairwise accuracy tests: the Diebold-Mariano family

**[RECALL, high confidence]** Diebold, F.X. & Mariano, R.S. (1995), "Comparing Predictive
Accuracy," *JBES* 13(3), pp. 253–263. Loss differential `d_t = L(e_1t) − L(e_2t)`; test
`E[d_t]=0` via `d̄/sqrt(V̂/T)` with a Newey-West-type HAC variance (loss differentials are
serially correlated at multi-step horizons). Asymptotically N(0,1).

**[RECALL, moderate-high confidence]** Harvey, D., Leybourne, S. & Newbold, P. (1997), "Testing
the Equality of Prediction Mean Squared Errors," *International Journal of Forecasting* 13(2),
pp. 281–291 — DM over-rejects in small samples/longer horizons; propose scaling the DM stat by
≈`sqrt[(T+1−2h+h(h−1)/T)/T]` and using a Student-t(T−1), not normal, critical value. With short
India histories per locality, HLN should be the default, not asymptotic DM.

**[RECALL, high confidence]** West, K.D. (1996), "Asymptotic Inference about Predictive Ability,"
*Econometrica* 64(5), pp. 1067–1084 — general theory for when parameter-estimation noise is
asymptotically negligible in a forecast comparison; it is not, under MSE loss, when one model
**nests** another. **Clark, T.E. & West, K.D. (2007)**, "Approximately Normal Tests for Equal
Predictive Accuracy in Nested Models," *Journal of Econometrics* 138(1), pp. 291–311 — the fix:
for nested comparisons (random walk nested in a richer model — this project's central test), the
ordinary DM statistic is biased against the larger model even under equal-accuracy null; their
adjusted statistic subtracts an estimate of that noise, giving an approximately-normal one-sided
test. **This is arguably the single most important citation here and the top verification
priority** — it governs the model-vs-random-walk comparison the whole engine rests on.

**[RECALL, moderate confidence]** West, K.D. (2006), "Forecast Evaluation," in *Handbook of
Economic Forecasting* Vol. 1 (Elliott, Granger & Timmermann eds.) — survey tying DM/HLN/West(1996)
together with real-time-data and general-loss-function caveats; chapter/pages not confident.

**[RECALL, high confidence]** Giacomini, R. & White, H. (2006), "Tests of Conditional Predictive
Ability," *Econometrica* 74(6), pp. 1545–1578 — DM/CW ask an *unconditional* question; GW asks a
*conditional* one: given today's state (high-vol regime, post-bear year, tier-1 vs tier-3 city),
which model is expected to forecast better going forward. Regresses the loss differential on
current-information instruments, tests joint significance; also permits comparing estimation
*methods* (e.g. rolling windows) directly. The right tool whenever a claim is state-conditional —
"beats the RW only in low-liquidity or post-bust states" needs GW, not pooled DM.

## 3. The Goyal-Welch precedent, mechanically

**[RECALL, high confidence]** Goyal, A. & Welch, I. (2008), "A Comprehensive Look at the Empirical
Performance of Equity Premium Prediction," *Review of Financial Studies* 21(4), pp. 1455–1508 —
virtually every textbook equity predictor fails OOS against the historical mean. The headline
diagnostic: the **cumulative sum-of-squared-error-differences plot** — at each OOS date `t`, plot
`Σ_{s≤t}[e²_bench,s − e²_model,s]`; rising = cumulative outperformance, falling = the reverse. It
shows *when* outperformance occurs; Goyal-Welch found much of it concentrated in one or two
episodes (their sample: largely the 1970s oil shocks) and erasable in a short subsequent stretch —
invisible to a single end-of-sample average. **Mandatory exhibit for every locality model this
desk reports** — a "beats the RW" number carried by one boom/bust quarter is not skill.

**[RECALL, high confidence]** Campbell, J.Y. & Thompson, S.B. (2008), "Predicting Excess Stock
Returns Out of Sample," *Review of Financial Studies* 21(4), pp. 1509–1531 (same issue as
Goyal-Welch, a paired response) — imposing sign restrictions and a plausibility floor/ceiling on
the forecast restores positive OOS R² for many predictors GW show failing unrestricted. Analogue
here: sign constraints on supply/rate-sensitivity coefficients, floors/ceilings on forecasted
locality growth (a −40%/+60% YoY forecast is a model red flag, not a delivered number).

**[RECALL, moderate confidence]** Rossi, B. & Inoue, A. (2012), "Out-of-Sample Forecast Tests
Robust to the Choice of Window Size," *JBES* 30(3), pp. 432–453 — DM/OOS-R² results can flip
purely from the rolling-window length choice; trying several and reporting the best is
data-snooping over window size. Fix: a sup/average-type statistic over a window-size range with
adjusted critical values. **Actionable**: this desk's walk-forward window length must be fixed by
pre-registration before seeing any locality's result — the same discipline the ledger already
applies to bars.

**[RECALL, LOW confidence — VERIFY before any use]** A ~2024 Goyal-Welch update extending the
original sample to roughly 2021–2023, re-confirming the OOS collapse largely persists; recalled
author list possibly Goyal, Welch, and a third name I cannot confirm (possibly "Zafirov"), title
close to "A Comprehensive 2022 Look..." — **weakest citation in this dossier**, must not be
repeated with names until confirmed. Substantive takeaway even without the exact cite: the
collapse is not a one-decade fluke that later data reversed — an argument for treating it as
binding, not provisional.

## 4. Many models, many localities: the multiple-testing problem

Worked arithmetic, not a citation: testing 300 localities independently at α=0.05 under a global
null of no genuine skill gives an *expected* 15 "significant" localities by chance; at α=0.10,
expected 30 — and positive cross-locality correlation (shared regional shocks) makes the realized
count more volatile and clustered than this binomial baseline.

**[RECALL, high confidence]** White, H. (2000), "A Reality Check for Data Snooping," *Econometrica*
68(5), pp. 1097–1126 — bootstraps the sampling distribution of the *best*-performing model across
a whole candidate universe, correcting the overstated significance of testing only the winner.
**[RECALL, moderate-high confidence]** Hansen, P.R. (2005), "A Test for Superior Predictive
Ability," *JBES* 23(4), pp. 365–380 — studentizes and adaptively trims dominated models before
building the null distribution, more powerful than White's Reality Check for the same FWER
control. Related: **[RECALL, moderate confidence]** Hansen, P.R., Lunde, A. & Nason, J.M. (2011),
"The Model Confidence Set," *Econometrica* 79(2), pp. 453–497 — returns a *set* of statistically
indistinguishable-from-best models rather than one winner, arguably the more honest output here.

**[RECALL, moderate confidence]** Romano, J.P. & Wolf, M. (2005), "Stepwise Multiple Testing as
Formalized Data Snooping," *Econometrica* 73(4), pp. 1237–1282 — stepdown bootstrap FWER control
across many simultaneous comparisons, more powerful than single-step (Bonferroni) correction.

**[RECALL, very high confidence]** Benjamini, Y. & Hochberg, Y. (1995), "Controlling the False
Discovery Rate," *JRSS-B* 57(1), pp. 289–300 — sort p-values, find largest `k` with
`p_(k) ≤ (k/m)·α`, reject all ≤ `p_(k)`. **The right lens for "which localities beat the
benchmark" given hundreds of tests** — controls expected false-discovery proportion among
flagged winners (less conservative than FWER control, appropriate for exploratory scanning, less
so for a single headline claim the principal will act on).

## 5. Cross-validation on a spatial panel: why random k-fold is lookahead

**[RECALL, high confidence — brief's own title, strongly suggesting correct recall]** Roberts,
D.R., Bahn, V., Ciuti, S., Boyce, M.S., Elith, J., et al. (2017), "Cross-validation strategies for
data with temporal, spatial, hierarchical, or phylogenetic structure," *Ecography* 40(8), pp.
913–929 — random k-fold on spatially/temporally dependent data is overly optimistic because a
held-out point's neighbors remain in training, testing interpolation, not extrapolation. Fix:
**blocked CV** — contiguous spatial blocks (whole cities/regions held out) and/or contiguous
temporal blocks, with an optional buffer gap. For this panel: never shuffle locality-months
randomly; hold out **whole cities/states** for spatial claims and **whole later time windows**
for temporal claims, never both randomized at once.

**[RECALL, high confidence on substance]** López de Prado, M. (2018), *Advances in Financial
Machine Learning*, Wiley — **purged k-fold CV**: any training row whose forward-looking label
window overlaps a test fold's window must be purged, not just the fold's own dates. **Embargo**:
a further buffer immediately after each test fold is also excluded, since serial correlation in
features can leak forward beyond literal label overlap. Also proposes **combinatorial purged CV**
(CPCV) for a distribution of OOS performance across path combinations. Both purging/embargo and
spatial blocking are needed together — they close different leakage channels.

**[RECALL, high confidence]** Bailey, D.H. & López de Prado, M. (2014), "The Deflated Sharpe
Ratio," *Journal of Portfolio Management* 40(5), pp. 94–107 — adjusts a Sharpe-like statistic's
significance for the number of variants tried, track-record length, and return skew/kurtosis. Any
risk-adjusted locality-timing statistic this project reports should be deflated, honestly counting
every variant tried, exactly as this desk's own optimization grids (OP-D3b/OP-D7/sweep-2) were
reported refusing their train winners OOS.

## 6. Hierarchical reconciliation: localities must sum to city and state

**[RECALL, moderate-high confidence]** Hyndman, R.J., Ahmed, R.A., Athanasopoulos, G. & Shang,
H.L. (2011), "Optimal Combination Forecasts for Hierarchical Time Series," *Computational
Statistics & Data Analysis* 55(9), pp. 2579–2589 — independent base forecasts at each hierarchy
level (locality/city/state/country) don't sum coherently; an OLS-based linear combination restores
exact coherence under an equal-variance error assumption.

**[RECALL, high confidence]** Wickramasuriya, S.L., Athanasopoulos, G. & Hyndman, R.J. (2019),
"Optimal Forecast Reconciliation for Hierarchical and Grouped Time Series Through Trace
Minimization," *JASA* 114(526), pp. 804–819 — **MinT**: generalizes to a full estimated
error-covariance matrix, finding the cross-level linear combination that satisfies aggregation
constraints exactly while minimizing reconciled-forecast error trace. **Top verification
priority** alongside Clark-West and Roberts et al. Forecast locality, city, state and national
levels independently, then reconcile via MinT — dominates both locality-only forecasting (hoping
sums work out) and naive top-down proportional allocation. Bonus: a locality forecast wildly
inconsistent with its own city/state aggregate is flagged by construction, a free check against
some of the §4 overfitting problem.

## 7. Distributional forecasts: proper scoring, calibration, sharpness

This project's mandate is distributional, not point, forecasts — consistent with this desk's ER-
arc doctrine (valuation/inflation as states, never point forecasts) and SNAPSHOT-1's refusal to
give a 1y/3y point forecast.

**[RECALL, high confidence]** Gneiting, T. & Raftery, A.E. (2007), "Strictly Proper Scoring Rules,
Prediction, and Estimation," *JASA* 102(477), pp. 359–378 — defines propriety (a scoring rule is
proper if the forecaster's expected score is optimized by reporting their true belief). Covers
**pinball/quantile loss** (`(y−q̂)τ` if `y≥q̂`, `(q̂−y)(τ−1)` otherwise, per quantile `τ`) and
**CRPS** (integral of squared forecast-CDF-vs-outcome-indicator difference; reduces to absolute
error for a point forecast; the continuous integral of pinball loss across all quantiles). Both
strictly proper.

**[RECALL, high confidence]** Gneiting, T., Balabdaoui, F. & Raftery, A.E. (2007), "Probabilistic
Forecasts, Calibration and Sharpness," *JRSS-B* 69(2), pp. 243–268 — **calibration** (an 80%
interval should contain the outcome ~80% of the time) vs. **sharpness** (how concentrated the
distribution is, subject to calibration): "maximize sharpness subject to calibration." Formalizes
the **PIT histogram**: transform each outcome through its own forecast CDF; under perfect
calibration PIT values are uniform on [0,1]. U-shaped = intervals too narrow (overconfident);
hump-shaped = too wide (underconfident); sloped = biased. **Every locality distributional forecast
should carry a PIT histogram on its walk-forward OOS history**, not just a point-accuracy number —
excellent mean accuracy can coexist with badly miscalibrated intervals, and only PIT reveals that.

## 8. THE RECOMMENDED EVALUATION PROTOCOL

1. Pre-register the benchmark set before seeing results: unconditional mean (soft, labeled as
   such), random-walk/no-change (binding), and a naive own-history AR(1) where relevant.
2. Pre-register the CV scheme before seeing results: spatial-blocked (whole cities/states held
   out) crossed with purged-and-embargoed walk-forward temporal folds — never random shuffling.
   Fix the rolling-window length by pre-registration, or use a Rossi-Inoue sup-statistic if
   window-size sensitivity must be examined.
3. Test each model against its benchmark with Clark-West, HLN-corrected, whenever the benchmark is
   nested (always true for the random-walk comparison).
4. Use Giacomini-White, not pooled DM/CW, for any regime-conditional claim.
5. Correct for multiple testing across the locality universe: Benjamini-Hochberg q-values as the
   exploratory screen; Romano-Wolf or Hansen SPA/White RC-level family-wise control before any
   single locality is promoted to a headline claim.
6. Plot the cumulative SSE-difference curve for every promoted model, not just its summary
   statistic, to catch episode-concentrated "skill."
7. Apply Campbell-Thompson-style sign/plausibility restrictions before evaluation, not as a
   post-hoc rescue.
8. Report pinball loss across a quantile grid, CRPS, and a PIT histogram (with a uniformity test)
   for every promoted model — point accuracy alone is not an acceptable deliverable.
9. Reconcile all levels via MinT before any number is published as this project's forecast.
10. Report a deflated Sharpe ratio, not a raw one, for any risk-adjusted/timing statistic, counting
    every variant tried before the reported winner.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE INDIA ENGINE

1. **Never report accuracy against the unconditional mean alone.** House prices are far more
   autocorrelated than equity returns; the random-walk/no-change forecast is the binding, harder
   benchmark.
2. **Default to Clark-West, not plain DM**, with the HLN small-sample correction, given short
   per-locality India histories and the always-nested random-walk comparison.
3. **Ban random k-fold on the locality panel.** Spatial-blocked crossed with purged-and-embargoed
   walk-forward folds is the minimum; anything else is lookahead dressed as validation.
4. **Pre-register CV window length and the benchmark set in the trial ledger before any locality
   number is computed** — window-size and benchmark snooping are the same sin the ledger
   discipline already forbids, on a new axis.
5. **Build a Benjamini-Hochberg/Romano-Wolf gate into the locality-scan pipeline from day one**,
   and publish the expected false-discovery count alongside any "N localities beat the benchmark"
   headline.
6. **Require pinball loss, CRPS and a PIT histogram for every promoted model**, not a point number
   — this project's mandate is distributional, and only proper scoring/calibration diagnostics can
   hold it to account.
7. **Reconcile locality/city/state/national forecasts via MinT before publication** — free
   diagnostic against overfitting, not just a coherence nicety.
8. **Re-verify this dossier's citations before use.** Clark-West, Roberts et al., and
   Wickramasuriya-Athanasopoulos-Hyndman are top priority (load-bearing choices); the ~2024
   Goyal-Welch update is lowest-confidence and must not be repeated with author names unconfirmed.
