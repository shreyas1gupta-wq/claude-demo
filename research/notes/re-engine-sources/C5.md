## 1. RBI's own house-price research and the HPI

**RBI House Price Index.** [RECALL — unverified, moderate confidence on shape, low on specifics]
RBI compiles a quarterly All-India HPI from property-registration/valuation data supplied by
state registration authorities, in a panel that I recall starting around 6 cities (Mumbai, Delhi,
Chennai, Kolkata, Bengaluru, Lucknow; launch ~2010-11, base 2010-11=100) and expanding toward
~10 (adding Ahmedabad, Jaipur, Kanpur, Kochi). Disseminated via RBI's DBIE and a short quarterly
RBI Bulletin note. This is the only methodologically documented, transaction-based, non-vendor
Indian HPI — but coverage is metro-only (~10 of 4,000+ urban settlements), it's a level index with
no property-type/size split, and I cannot confirm whether the underlying registration feed
handles carpet vs built-up area consistently across states (registration documents vary state to
state in what area they record) — flagged as an open question rather than answered.

**RBI Working/Occasional Papers on housing.** [RECALL — unverified, low-moderate confidence] I
recall **Snehal M. Herwadkar** (RBI DEPR) as having published on Indian real-estate/household
balance-sheet topics, plausibly including house-price determinants, but cannot state an exact
title/year/co-authors. RBI's Financial Stability Report and Monetary Policy Report routinely carry
boxes on housing-loan growth and GNPA in banks' housing books, and I recall DEPR-adjacent work on
house prices vs. bank credit / the credit-to-GDP gap in the BIS macroprudential tradition — no
specific paper citable. I will not fabricate a title-author-year triple to fill this gap.

**RBI Bulletin on the housing-loan portfolio.** [RECALL — unverified] RBI Bulletin/FSR regularly
cover scheduled-bank housing-loan growth and asset quality, and NBFC/HFC exposure post the
2018-19 IL&FS/DHFL stress — a recurring credit-side data source, no specific article named.

## 2. NHB RESIDEX — the older, parallel official index

[RECALL — unverified, moderate confidence on description, low on current status] National
Housing Bank's **Residex** predates the RBI HPI (I recall launch ~2007) and was for years India's
only official house-price index. Widely discussed weakness: methodology was inconsistent city to
city — some cities used registration/valuation data, others reportedly leaned on housing-finance-
company loan/disbursement data as a price proxy, a materially different and more selection-biased
construction (only financed transactions, embedding LTV/appraisal conventions rather than sale
price). At least one base-year revision and coverage expansion occurred over the 2010s. **For the
engine**: Residex and the RBI HPI are not interchangeable, and any splice across the two (or
across a Residex base-year revision) needs the same composition-effect caution as an index
revision elsewhere on this desk (CN-D1 precedent). I cannot confirm whether Residex is still
actively updated in 2026 or has been effectively superseded — a direct, checkable open question.

## 3. NIPFP — property taxation and land as public finance

[RECALL — unverified, low-moderate confidence] NIPFP has a long municipal-finance programme with
property tax as a recurring subject. I recall **Simanti Bandyopadhyay** (NIPFP-associated) on
property-tax systems and municipal fiscal health across Indian cities, including Kolkata
specifically, plus NIPFP work on **land value capture instruments** (betterment levy, development
charges, TDR) as underused urban-infrastructure financing tools, and on **stamp duty** as a
high-rate, evasion-prone transaction tax that — jointly with under-stated circle-rate/guideline
values — distorts registered prices downward relative to true transaction prices, directly
relevant to using registration prices as ground truth. No specific working-paper titles/numbers
recalled with confidence; flagged for direct archive verification.

## 4. IGIDR / the Ajay Shah–Susan Thomas ecosystem

[RECALL — unverified, moderate confidence on institutional facts, low on specific papers] This
NIPFP-IGIDR/xKDR Forum network's direct output on housing/land prices is thinner than on
securities markets and household finance broadly. Most relevant artifact recalled: the **RBI-
appointed Household Finance Committee report (2017)**, chaired by Tarun Ramadorai with this
network's researchers involved in the underlying survey work — headline finding: **Indian
household wealth is overwhelmingly concentrated in physical assets (real estate, gold), with a
strikingly low financial-asset share** versus comparable-income countries, with direct
transmission implications for house-price shocks. Ajay Shah's own commentary (blog/op-ed)
repeatedly argues Indian real estate is distorted by (a) a cash/black-money component tied to the
stamp-duty/circle-rate gap (§3, §9), (b) the historical absence of REITs/a liquid listed
real-estate asset class, and (c) land-title uncertainty (no Torrens-style conclusive title)
raising transaction costs — opinion/policy commentary, not peer-reviewed papers. **Distinct from
this ecosystem**: Sahil Gandhi and Vaidehi Tandel (§7) are not, as I recall, IGIDR-affiliated —
closer to IIM Ahmedabad/IDFC Institute — so the brief's grouping should be read as two adjacent
but organisationally distinct clusters.

## 6. CPR — urban land, master plans, and FSI

[RECALL — unverified, moderate confidence on people/themes, low on titles] **Partha Mukhopadhyay**
is the leading CPR figure here, with work (including with Marie-Hélène Zerah) on **"subaltern
urbanisation"** — a large share of India's urban growth occurring in small/medium towns and
"census towns" (meeting Census urban criteria but lacking statutory urban-local-body status)
outside the planning/finance apparatus built for large metros — directly relevant since official
price series (RBI HPI, Residex, RERA) are metro-concentrated and structurally miss this tier. CPR
and adjacent researchers have also produced critical analysis of Delhi's **Master Plan** process
(persistent gaps between planned land-use and actual use/density) and Delhi's FAR/FSI increases
and TOD zoning along Metro corridors. **Land Conflict Watch** (a land-conflict tracking database,
associated in my recall with journalist Kumar Sambhav Shrivastava) sits in this same milieu but I
am not confident it is CPR-housed rather than independent — flagged, not asserted.

## 7. The Mumbai FSI/rent-control cluster: Gandhi, Tandel, Tabarrok, Bertaud

The most economically rigorous cluster in this checklist, and the one I recall in outline with
most confidence (though unverified this session). **Alain Bertaud** (ex-World Bank principal
planner) wrote what I recall as the foundational paper, commonly cited as **"Mumbai FSI
Conundrum"** (title/year/byline uncertain, early-to-mid 2000s). Central claim: Mumbai combined (i)
an extraordinarily low permitted FSI in the island city (I recall ~1.33 for decades, against
5-15 in comparable dense global CBDs — Bertaud compared Shanghai, Manhattan), (ii) rent control
freezing a large stock at nominal rents (removing redevelopment incentive — §9), (iii) large
under-utilised land tracts (textile mills, railways, port trust, government), and (iv) slow
discretionary approvals — producing an **inverted density gradient**: unlike a normal monocentric
city, density does not peak near Mumbai's core, pushing development to the periphery (longer
commutes) while low-rise slum redevelopment absorbs demand the FSI cap blocks formal supply from
meeting. Elaborated later in Bertaud's 2018 MIT Press book **"Order Without Design,"** using
Mumbai as a principal case study.

**Alex Tabarrok**, with the **IDFC Institute** (Mumbai think tank), produced an accessible
synthesis (~2015-16, exact title uncertain) arguing raised FSI plus impact fees/infrastructure
charges was the highest-leverage affordability lever available, contrasting Mumbai's regime with
Hong Kong's/Manhattan's; also covered on his Marginal Revolution blog.

**Sahil Gandhi and Vaidehi Tandel** are, as far as I recall, the most directly relevant empirical
authors here for a price-prediction engine — closer to conventional applied urban economics than
the Bertaud/Tabarrok policy pieces, quantifying Mumbai rent control's effect on redevelopment
incentives and the welfare/supply cost of FSI restrictions, I believe with co-authors including
**Richard K. Green** and possibly **Ashwini Kulkarni**. Institutional homes I recall: IIM
Ahmedabad and/or IDFC Institute, possibly later a UK institution — not confident of current
affiliation. **I cannot reconstruct specific titles, journals, or coefficient magnitudes with
confidence** — exactly the kind of number the brief wants and exactly the kind I must not invent.
What I am confident of is the shape: rent-controlled stock shows systematically lower
redevelopment rates than comparable uncontrolled stock, and FSI relaxation (periodic base-FSI
increases, the TDR market) has been associated with measurable new-supply response where it
occurred — consistent with, not refuting, Bertaud's mechanism.

**Shirish Patel**, structural engineer and 1960s co-proponent of **Navi Mumbai** as a counter-
magnet, has written for decades (EPW pieces) critical of Mumbai's low-FSI-plus-slum-redevelopment
paradigm. **Bimal Patel**, architect of Ahmedabad's Sabarmati Riverfront, is the figure I associate
with Gujarat's **Town Planning (TP) Schemes** — a land-pooling/readjustment mechanism where
landowners pool land, a portion is deducted for infrastructure, and serviced higher-value plots
are returned — an acquisition alternative that has drawn separate World Bank interest as a land
value capture instrument superior to eminent domain for infrastructure-linked expansion.

## 8. Isher Judge Ahluwalia's HPEC report

[RECALL — unverified, moderate confidence] The **High Powered Expert Committee Report on Indian
Urban Infrastructure and Services**, chaired by Isher Judge Ahluwalia (Ministry of Urban
Development, ~2011), estimated a large 20-year urban-infrastructure investment need (water,
sewerage, drainage, roads, lighting) — I recall an order of magnitude near ₹35-40 lakh crore over
2012-2031, held at low confidence — plus recommendations on service-level benchmarks and municipal
finance. Not a house-price study, but directly relevant to supply-side/infrastructure-lag
variables an engine would want as a "where does future appreciation concentrate" prior.

## 9. Sanjoy Chakravorty, "The Price of Land"

[RECALL — unverified, moderate-high confidence on central theses — a well-known book] **"The
Price of Land: Acquisition, Conflict, Consequence"** (Oxford University Press, ~2013) is the
closest thing here to a unifying theory of why Indian land/property prices behave as they do
structurally. Central argument: land value is set less by an efficient market than by an
**administered-price gap** — official circle-rate/guideline value (used for stamp duty and, pre-
LARR, for compensation) versus true market price — kept wide by chronic under-revision, by the
state's dual interest as both acquirer (wanting low compensation) and taxer (wanting stamp-duty
revenue), and by cash/undisclosed-consideration practice. Chakravorty uses high-profile conflicts
(Singur and Nandigram, West Bengal; POSCO, Odisha; Bhatta Parsaul, UP) as symptoms of this mismatch
rather than one-off failures, and argues the thinness of India's land market (little agricultural
land changing hands formally) magnifies the political stakes of each acquisition absent a liquid
price-discovery mechanism. **For the engine**: the strongest available argument that registered/
circle-rate prices are a systematically biased (generally low) signal of true value, bias
magnitude varying with how stale the last official revision is.

## 10. Land Acquisition Act 2013 (LARR) as an administered price

[RECALL — unverified, moderate confidence on statutory mechanics] The **Right to Fair
Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 2013**
replaced the 1894 Act. Compensation = a multiple of registered/circle-rate value (I recall ~1x
urban, up to 2x rural depending on urban distance) plus 100% solatium (yielding the commonly
cited "2x urban/4x rural" headline), plus separate R&R entitlements, plus a consent requirement
for private/PPP acquisition (~80%/70% of affected owners) and mandatory Social Impact Assessment.
The consent/SIA provisions were contentious; I recall a 2014-15 ordinance route (repeatedly
re-promulgated, ultimately lapsing) attempting exemptions, plus state-level dilution amendments.
**The tie to Chakravorty**: because compensation is legally pegged to the registered value — a
value this literature says is chronically understated — the multiplier is in part an explicit
legislative correction for a known distortion, meaning registered/circle-rate values near a
compensation event should be treated as a lower bound with an unquantified (in my recall) offset.

## 14. Rent control and the pagdi system

[RECALL — unverified, moderate confidence] The **Bombay Rents, Hotel and Lodging House Rates
Control Act, 1947** froze rents for a large Mumbai tenancy stock (especially pre-1940s buildings)
at low nominal levels indefinitely, switching off landlord incentive to maintain or redevelop —
the first-order contributor, as I understand it, to Mumbai's stock of decayed "cessed buildings"
(subject to a MHADA repair cess precisely because market upkeep incentives were absent). The
**pagdi system** is the informal response: an incoming tenant pays a lump-sum premium to acquire
the tenancy right itself (not ownership), with nominal rent staying frozen — an unofficial,
non-fully-recorded secondary market reflecting true scarcity even as recorded "rent" data would
not. The **Maharashtra Rent Control Act, 1999** replaced the 1947 Act but grandfathered existing
controlled tenancies, so the distortion persists today. Directly the empirical setting several of
the §7 Gandhi/Tandel redevelopment papers study.

## 15. Informal/unauthorised colonies and housing outside the formal register

[RECALL — unverified, LOW confidence on figures, moderate on the qualitative picture] Two distinct
categories. **Slums** (Census/NSSO-enumerated): I recall Census-2011-era figures on the order of
**65-70 million people, roughly 17% of urban population**, held at low confidence given
definitional variation (notified/recognised/identified) across sources. **Unauthorised colonies**
are a distinct, largely Delhi-specific category — settlements without statutory approval, often
on illegally-converted agricultural land — I recall on the order of **1,700** such colonies in
Delhi housing a population commonly cited in the 30-50% of Delhi's population range (uncertain,
possibly stale). The **NCT of Delhi (Recognition of Property Rights of Residents in Unauthorized
Colonies) Act, 2019** granted conditional ownership/registration rights to residents of DDA-
notified colonies — an implicit admission of a very large housing stock existing for decades
entirely outside formal registration. **For the engine**: any registration-based price model
silently excludes whatever share of housing never enters the register at all — slums,
unauthorised colonies, informal peri-urban land — a coverage bias in the denominator of "Indian
urban housing," not a numerator data-quality issue.

## 16. What's genuinely established versus consultant assertion

**Reasonably established** (independent conceptual sources converge, though none search-verified
this session): (a) Mumbai's low FSI + rent control + fragmented land ownership plausibly explains
its inverted density gradient and high prices — Bertaud, Tabarrok/IDFC Institute, and the
Gandhi/Tandel applied-economics register all reach the same mechanism independently. (b)
Registered/circle-rate land prices are a systematically biased (generally low) proxy for market
value — Chakravorty (political-economy) and LARR's own compensation-multiplier design (a
legislative admission of the same gap) agree. (c) A large share of Indian urban housing sits
outside formal registration/planning entirely, a coverage problem for every official price
series. **Consultant assertion pending verification**: specific metro/infrastructure hedonic
premium percentages (§11) — no confident academic citation found, only unsourced consultancy
patterns. **Documented institutionally but with unverifiable construction detail**: the RBI HPI's
exact city panel/base year/area-basis handling, and whether NHB Residex remains actively maintained.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE INDIA ENGINE

1. **Treat every claim in this file as a hypothesis needing verification, not a parameter to
   hard-code** — the whole dossier is unverified recall; re-run the search pass before anything
   here enters `config/`.
2. **Do not build price ground-truth on registered/circle-rate values without a bias correction**
   — Chakravorty's thesis and LARR's multiplier both say the gap is real, directionally low, and
   time-varying with staleness of the last revision; treat registered price as a floor, not a level.
3. **Read the RBI HPI methodology note directly before use** — city panel, area-basis convention
   (carpet vs built-up) are unresolved here and could move any level comparison 20-35% per the
   brief's own area-basis warning.
4. **Model a coverage hole where slums and unauthorised colonies sit** — very roughly a fifth to a
   third of urban housing, depending on city/definition, never enters the formal register the
   engine's official inputs are built from; a structural undercount, not noise.
5. **Parameterise FSI/FAR regime and redevelopment-incentive regime (rent-control status) as
   city-specific supply-elasticity inputs** — the Mumbai cluster (Bertaud/Tabarrok/Gandhi-Tandel,
   and Bimal Patel's Gujarat TP Scheme alternative) is the most transferable structural insight
   here; do not assume a uniform national supply curve.
6. **Source metro/infrastructure premium coefficients from a fresh hedonic estimate, not the
   consultancy literature** — no defensible peer-reviewed magnitude was recoverable from memory,
   and circulating consultancy figures lack visible methodology.
7. **The highest-value re-runs once search returns**: RBI DEPR's working-paper archive, NIPFP's
   publication list, CPR's urbanisation-programme page, and a scholar search on "Gandhi Tandel
   Mumbai rent control FSI" and "Bertaud Mumbai FSI conundrum."
8. **Confirm NHB Residex's current operational status before using it at all** — if discontinued
   or superseded, any historical splice against the RBI HPI needs the same composition-effect
   caution applied to every other index revision on this desk (CN-D1 precedent).
