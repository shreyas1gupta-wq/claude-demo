## 1. RERA — what the statute requires and why it matters for a supply engine

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 ("RERA") [RECALL — unverified, high
confidence on the broad shape] came into force in stages, with most states notifying their
state-level Real Estate Regulatory Authority and rules during 2017. The Act requires every
promoter of a real-estate project above a size/unit threshold (commonly cited as projects over
500 sq m or more than 8 units — [RECALL — unverified, low confidence on the exact cutoff]) to
register the project with the state authority BEFORE advertising, marketing, booking or selling
any unit. This is precisely why RERA project registers are, in principle, a near-census of
formally launched organized-sector residential (and some commercial) supply since 2017 — a
genuine forward-supply indicator with no obvious equivalent in most emerging markets, and free
to query. The catch, elaborated state-by-state below, is that every portal I can recall is a
**search/lookup interface for one project at a time**, not a bulk database — no
government-published CSV of "all projects, all fields, all states" that I am aware of, and no
state I recall exposing a documented REST API for researchers. This absence is exactly why a
private reseller layer (Propstack, CRE Matrix, PropEquity, and RERA-specific "compliance filing"
services aimed at promoters) exists to scrape and resell structured RERA data — see §4-5 below.

**Fields every state RERA project page is built to carry** [RECALL — unverified, but a statutory
schema, so should be consistent nationally]: registration number; promoter/company name and
address; project name and precise location (survey/gat/plot number, village/taluka, district,
pincode); land area; sanctioned FSI/plan-approval reference; number of towers/wings; total units
by type/configuration and their **carpet areas** (RERA's defining reform was mandating carpet
area, not super-built-up, as the disclosed unit-size metric — directly relevant to the brief's
area-basis warning); the **proposed completion date** and any **revised completion date** (each
extension is itself a filing, so extension history is a directly observable delay signal); the
mandatory 70%-escrow bank account details; architect/engineer/CA Form 1/2/3 certifications; and,
most useful to a forward-supply model, a **Quarterly Progress Report (QPR)** stating percentage
physical completion (sometimes by activity stage) and units booked/sold to date. A separate
**complaints/litigation tab** lists consumer complaints and orders — a promoter-risk signal.
[RECALL — unverified] Not confident whether QPR history is retained as a browsable time series
on the public page or only the latest QPR is shown — an open, engine-relevant question needing a
direct portal check.

## 2. State-by-state: Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, UP, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu

**Maharashtra (MahaRERA).** [RECALL — unverified] Portal recalled as
`maharera.mahaonline.gov.in` (later possibly migrated to a `maharera.maharashtra.gov.in`-style
domain — I am not confident which is current in 2026). MahaRERA was among the first and, by
reputation, the most active and best-administered state authority — Maharashtra (Mumbai/MMR,
Pune, Nagpur) is the country's largest organized real-estate market, so its RERA register is
disproportionately important for any all-India supply model. Search is by project name,
promoter name, or district/division; I do not recall a bulk CSV or documented API. MahaRERA
periodically issues aggregate compliance statistics (counts of registered projects, QPR
compliance rates, complaints disposed) in annual reports/press notes, but I cannot confirm a
structured machine-readable version of these aggregates exists. [RECALL — unverified] Also
recall it holds authority-level data on registered real-estate **agents** (brokers), a
separate register from projects.

**Karnataka (K-RERA / RERA Karnataka).** [RECALL — unverified] Portal recalled as
`rera.karnataka.gov.in`, covering Bengaluru (the dominant share of Karnataka's organized
launches) plus other cities. [RECALL — unverified, low confidence, not asserted as fact] A vague
recollection of press commentary describing the portal's usability/uptime as weaker than
MahaRERA's and QPR compliance as less consistently enforced. Same field set as §1 expected;
same search-only structure assumed.

**Telangana (TS-RERA / TG-RERA).** [RECALL — unverified] Portal recalled as

**Uttar Pradesh (UP-RERA).** [RECALL — unverified] Portal recalled as `up-rera.in`, notable

## 3. IGR / property registration data — the transaction-truth layer RERA doesn't provide

RERA registers **launches** (proposed supply) and **QPR-stated construction progress**, not
**transactions**. The actual record of a completed sale — buyer, seller, exact area,
consideration value, stamp duty paid, registration date, survey/CTS/plot reference — sits with
each state's **Inspector General of Registration (IGR) / Stamps and Registration Department**,
which is a wholly separate government body from RERA. [RECALL — unverified] I recall
Maharashtra's IGR portal (domain recalled as `igrmaharashtra.gov.in`) exposing a document-search
tool (sometimes called "e-search" or an "Index-II" search) letting a user query registered
documents by village/survey number/party name/date range, again one document at a time, with no
bulk export; other states' IGR/stamps departments are assumed structurally similar but
unconfirmed here. IGR also publishes the annual **Ready Reckoner (RR) rate** tables — the
state-set minimum valuation-for-stamp-duty rate by locality/zone, used as a stamp-duty floor,
not a market price, but nonetheless a genuinely free, granular (down to street/zone level in
dense cities like Mumbai/Pune), annually revised administrative price grid that several private
vendors and researchers use as a floor/reference series. [RECALL — unverified] This RR series
is, to my recollection, one of the very few India real-estate price-adjacent numbers with
locality-level granularity that is genuinely free and government-published, which makes it worth
the India engine's specific attention even though it measures an administered floor, not a
transacted price.

The friction of IGR's search-only interface is exactly what a cluster of private "registration
data" resellers monetizes — see Propstack and CRE Matrix in §7. **That these companies can sell
structured, queryable databases of registration-level detail (buyer name, area, consideration,
stamp duty, date, document type) is itself the clearest evidence of what IGR actually contains**:
the underlying government record is granular and transaction-level, and the bottleneck is
interface/bulk-access, not missing data. The *data exists*; what's missing is a
government-sanctioned bulk channel — so an engine wanting registration-level ground truth will
likely have to license it from a reseller or replicate the scraping/licensing approach itself
(the vault's "free data only" rule bites here: IGR is free-to-query but not free-to-bulk-acquire,
and the resold product is explicitly commercial).

## 4. Municipal building-permission systems

**Mumbai (BPMS / AutoDCR).** [RECALL — unverified] Maharashtra rolled out an "AutoDCR" (Auto
Dwelling Control Rules) engine — a rules-based automated building-plan scrutiny system — across
multiple ULBs (Mumbai, Pune, Nagpur, Thane) as part of a mid-2010s e-governance push, partly
credited with India's improvement in the World Bank's now-discontinued "Doing Business"
construction-permits ranking (figures not cited — would need inventing without a search). In
Mumbai, the current-generation system is recalled as branded **BPMS (Building Proposal
Management System)**, run by MCGM, for online plan submission and approval tracking. [RECALL —
unverified, low confidence] Not confident whether BPMS exposes any public bulk list of
approved/pending proposals (a valuable permit-stage forward-supply signal, earlier than RERA
registration) or is purely a submitter-facing workflow tool — high-value item for a follow-up
search.

**Bengaluru.** [RECALL — unverified, low confidence] I recall BBMP (Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara
Palike) operates an online building-plan-approval system, though I am not fully confident of
its exact current branding/acronym (something in the family of an "Online Building Plan
Approval System" — I will not guess a specific acronym I am not sure of). Flagging this as an

**Hyderabad (TG-bPASS).** [RECALL — unverified, moderate confidence] Telangana launched
**TG-bPASS (Telangana State Building Permission Approval and Self-Certification System)** around
2020: below a size threshold it grants **deemed/self-certified approval** (architect/owner
self-certifies compliance, system auto-issues permission, post-facto audit) rather than manual
review — built explicitly to cut approval time. The most distinctive, best-regarded system of
the four named in the brief, and plausibly most amenable to a structured feed if one exists,
though no confident recall of a public bulk data view.

**Pune.** [RECALL — unverified, low confidence] Assumed to run on the same Maharashtra
AutoDCR lineage as Mumbai, administered through Pune Municipal Corporation (PMC); no
differentiated recall of Pune-specific features or any public data view beyond that assumption.

**Cross-cutting read** [RECALL — unverified]: none of these four systems is recalled to publish
an open bulk dataset of permit applications/approvals — they read as submission/workflow portals
for architects and developers, not public research datasets. Building-permission data sits even
further upstream of RERA registration (permit -> RERA registration -> launch -> QPR -> OC/CC)
but is likely the LEAST accessible rung of the pipeline, more so than RERA itself.

## 5. Occupancy Certificates (OC) and Completion Certificates (CC)

[RECALL — unverified] The OC/CC, issued by the municipal building-permission authority (not
RERA), formally certifies that construction matches the sanctioned plan and is fit for
occupation — the legal threshold separating "under-construction" from "ready to move" (a
project cannot legitimately market "ready" possession without one; marketing ready possession
without an OC has, I believe, been a recurring RERA-complaint category, frequency unconfirmed).
OC/CC records sit with the same municipal departments as §4 (MCGM/BBMP/GHMC/PMC), typically a
certificate-status lookup by property/CTS/survey number, not a structured public database — no
recall of any of the four cities publishing a machine-readable bulk OC/CC-issuance feed. For a
supply-pipeline model, OC/CC issuance date is arguably the cleanest "pipeline-to-completed-stock"
event — cleaner than a RERA revised-completion filing, which is stated intent, not a certified
fact — but appears to be the least digitized, least bulk-accessible datapoint in the chain.

## 6. Municipal property tax databases (MCGM, BBMP, GHMC)

[RECALL — unverified across this whole section] All three — Mumbai's MCGM property-tax portal,
Bengaluru's BBMP Self-Assessment Scheme (SAS) portal, and Hyderabad's GHMC portal (queried by
PTIN, Property Tax Identification Number) — read as **per-property lookup and payment systems**,
not open ward-level statistical datasets: a researcher can query one known property/account
number and see assessed value, tax demand and payment history, but none is recalled to expose a
bulk ward-level assessment roll or a self-serve time series of ward-aggregated collections. Where
ward aggregates circulate (municipal budget-book tables, ward-/zone-wise demand-vs-collection),
they come from the **budget document**, not a queryable database — annual, backward-looking,
format-inconsistent year to year; usable for cross-checking, not a live feed. [RECALL —
unverified, speculative] Property-tax **assessed value** in Indian municipal systems is typically
a capital-value or unit-area/rateable-value formula tied to locality zone and declared built-up
area at assessment — NOT a live market valuation — so even bulk-accessible ward data would be a
lagging, administratively-set proxy (similar in spirit to the IGR Ready Reckoner), needing
explicit area-basis and assessment-vintage disclosure before use.

## 7. The private data estate

**Consultancy/brokerage bloc — Anarock, Knight Frank India, JLL, CBRE, Colliers, Cushman &
Wakefield.** [RECALL — unverified across this bloc] All six publish periodic (mostly quarterly
or half-yearly) **free PDF research reports** covering the top organized-market cities (a
"top 7" recurring across most — MMR, NCR, Bengaluru, Pune, Hyderabad, Chennai, Kolkata — with
Knight Frank and some others extending to a "top 8" by adding Ahmedabad). Content typically
includes new launches, sales/absorption, unsold inventory, a "quarters-to-sell" (QTS)/
months-of-inventory metric, and a city-level weighted-average price figure. **Measurement
basis**: built from each firm's own tracked-project universe — a proprietary sample of
organized/branded launches, not a census — so city totals from these five diverge for the same
city-quarter because tracked-universe coverage of smaller/regional developers differs by firm
(general pattern, not a verified figure). **Bias direction** [RECALL — unverified, a
reputational pattern, not a documented study]: as fee-earning brokerages/consultancies (leasing,
capital markets, advisory income), their free research has a structural incentive toward
constructive/optimistic "recovery" and "healthy inventory" framing — a contrast Indian business
press periodically draws against bearish independent voices like Liases Foras (below). Deeper
project-level or micro-market data is a paid subscription/advisory product; the free PDFs are
top-line summaries. Sectoral emphasis differs: Knight Frank and Anarock read as the two with the
deepest **residential** practice; JLL, CBRE, Colliers and Cushman & Wakefield read stronger on
**office/retail/industrial-logistics** (Grade-A absorption/vacancy, retail rents, warehousing),
residential coverage thinner. Knight Frank's flagship half-yearly **"India Real Estate" report**
is the one the brief names — recalled as including a residential price index by city, and Knight
Frank separately co-publishes registration-based Mumbai data with CREDAI-MCHI (below), a cut
above its own launch-tracking data since it rests on actual IGR filings, not a tracked sample.

**PropEquity.** [RECALL — unverified, low confidence on corporate/ownership detail, not guessed
further] A dedicated real-estate data/analytics company tracking projects across many Indian
cities, selling **project-level primary-sales database subscriptions** to lenders, PE funds and
developers rather than running a free-report marketing business like the brokerages. Sold to
capital-allocators who need the data right, not given away to generate deal flow — a mild,
unverified argument that its discipline may be less narrative-biased than the consultancy bloc.

**Liases Foras.** [RECALL — unverified] Founded by Pankaj Kapoor, known for a methodologically
conservative, primary-survey-based "unsold inventory"/"months-to-sell" discipline and a
long-running, publicly bearish stance on Mumbai/MMR oversupply and price-correction risk — cited
in Indian financial press as the counter-narrative to brokerage optimism. Data collection
reportedly combines site surveys with RERA and registration data; its subscription-research (not
brokerage) model removes the fee-generation incentive, making it a reputationally more
independent voice, though this is not verified against a documented conflict-of-interest study.

**CREDAI-MCHI.** [RECALL — unverified] CREDAI is the developer industry body itself; MCHI
(Maharashtra Chamber of Housing Industry) is its Mumbai chapter. Periodically co-publishes
registration-based Mumbai/MMR data with Knight Frank. **A promoter-interest body, not a neutral
research house** — read with an explicit developer-advocacy bias flag, more direct than the
brokerage bloc's fee-driven optimism.

**Propstack and CRE Matrix — registration-data resellers.** [RECALL — unverified across both]
Both are analytics platforms built substantially on **scraped/licensed IGR registration data**,
resold with search/analytics tooling to brokers, developers, banks and funds — Propstack
combining this with RERA data and its own listings/comps layer; CRE Matrix recalled as
particularly associated with Mumbai/MMR/Pune/Bengaluru registration analytics, periodically
releasing free press snippets (e.g. monthly registration counts/ticket sizes, widely quoted
around the 2020-21 stamp-duty-cut period as a real-time recovery barometer). As argued in §3,
the mere existence of these businesses evidences IGR's underlying richness: what they sell
(buyer/area/consideration/stamp-duty/date-level records) indexes what the government record
actually contains, monetizing interface friction, not data scarcity.

**Listing portals — 99acres, Magicbricks, Housing.com/PropTiger, NoBroker.** [RECALL —
unverified across all four] All are ultimately **asking-price** (or asking-rent) based, derived
from active listings, not transacted prices — the key measurement-basis caveat for this cluster,
compounding the area-basis risk in the brief (listings often state built-up/super-built-up area
inconsistently, carpet area often absent). **Magicbricks (Times Internet)** publishes the
**Magicbricks PropIndex** quarterly as a free PDF, covering ten-plus cities with both city- and,
notably, **locality-level** price/rent for "prime" micro-markets — one of the few genuinely
sub-city, free, regularly-updated series in the India data landscape, valuable despite the
asking-price caveat. **Housing.com/PropTiger** (same ownership umbrella, historically tied to
REA Group/Elara) publish a comparable quarterly "**Real Insight**" report via **PropTiger
DataLabs** — city-level launches/sales/inventory/price, sourced from listing activity
supplemented, I believe, by some transaction visibility through PropTiger's advisory arm (an
unconfirmed partial claim to being closer to transacted reality than a pure listings index).
**99acres (Info Edge)**, India's largest listings inventory, publishes research less
systematically than Magicbricks' PropIndex cadence, more marketing-oriented — low confidence,
check directly. **NoBroker** (owner-direct, no-brokerage niche) samples a self-selected
population of cost-conscious owners/tenants avoiding agent fees — a sample-selection bias
distinct from the asking-vs-transacted bias shared by the other three, likely skewed toward
specific tech-hub/salaried-tenant segments rather than the full rental universe.

**Bias summary** [RECALL — unverified]: asking-price listing indices lag/overstate versus
eventual transacted price in falling markets and can be noisy in thin-listing localities; the
brokerage bloc carries narrative-optimism plus sample-coverage bias; Liases Foras leans the
opposite (bearish) way; CREDAI-MCHI carries explicit developer-advocacy bias; only the
IGR-registration-sourced products (Propstack, CRE Matrix, Knight Frank/CREDAI-MCHI joint
reports) claim actual transacted consideration values, making them the closest private-sector
analogue to ground truth, subject to whatever area-basis and reporting-lag distortions are
native to the raw IGR filings.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE INDIA ENGINE

1. **Treat RERA as a launch/completion-intent register, not a transaction feed** — its highest
   engine value is the registered-vs-revised completion date gap (a directly observable delay
   metric) and the QPR percentage-complete trajectory, if QPR history proves to be retrievable;
   confirm retrievability before designing around it.
2. **Build the forward-supply feature as a five-stage funnel** (building permit -> RERA
   registration -> QPR progress -> OC/CC issuance -> IGR-registered sales) and expect access
   quality to DEGRADE moving right to left along this chain, opposite to naive intuition —
   OC/CC and permit-stage data look to be the least accessible, RERA registration the most
   accessible free layer, and IGR the richest but least bulk-accessible layer of all.
3. **Do not average city-level "launches/sales/inventory" figures across Anarock, Knight
   Frank, JLL, CBRE and Colliers as if they measured the same thing** — each rests on a
   different proprietary tracked-project sample; pick one provider per series and hold it
   fixed, and log the choice as a stated methodology decision, not an incidental convenience.
4. **Prefer registration-sourced (IGR-derived) price/volume data over any asking-price listing
   index whenever a licensing or scraping path is legally available**, since it is the only
   private-estate layer claiming actual transacted consideration; treat Magicbricks/99acres/
   Housing.com asking-price series as a sentiment/liquidity proxy, not a price-level input.
5. **Any blend of RESIDEX-style "assessed/loan-sanction" price data, IGR IGR/registration data,
   and listing asking-price data must carry an explicit area-basis tag (carpet/built-up/super-
   built-up) per series** — per the brief's own 20-35% warning, this is likely the single
   largest source of spurious cross-source disagreement the engine will encounter.
6. **Recognize CREDAI-MCHI and, more mildly, the whole brokerage-consultancy bloc as
   promoter/fee-interest-aligned sources** — usable as data but not as neutral narrative;
   Liases Foras's independently-sourced bearish counter-read is a useful adversarial check on
   the consultancy consensus, not a replacement for it.
7. **Treat the RERA litigation/complaints tab as a promoter-risk and possible-completion-delay
   signal**, complementary to the revised-completion-date filing — a project accumulating
   complaints is a leading indicator worth weighting alongside the extension-filing history.
8. **This entire dossier is unverified and needs a dedicated search-and-confirm pass before any
   engine work is built on it** — treat every URL, portal name, acronym and structural claim
   above as a hypothesis list for that pass, not as settled input.
