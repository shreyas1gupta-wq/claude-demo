This is the project's intellectual core: a registered sale price is not a market price.
`observed = max(true_price, circle_rate)`, subject to an evasion decision, where `circle_rate`
is a real, **observed**, ward-and-time-varying administrative schedule (Sub-Registrar-notified,
revised on its own irregular calendar) — not a latent, unknown threshold. That the censoring
point is observed rather than estimated is what makes this problem tractable, and is the thread
connecting every method below.

## 1. The Tobit foundation

**Tobin (1958)**, "Estimation of Relationships for Limited Dependent Variables," *Econometrica*
26(1), pp. 24–36 [RECALL — unverified, high confidence on venue/substance, moderate on pages].
The founding paper: durable-goods expenditure censored at zero because desired expenditure can
be negative while actual expenditure cannot. `y*_i = x_i'β + u_i`, observe `y_i = max(y*_i,0)`
— structurally identical to our problem once the threshold moves from a universal zero to an
observed, ward-time-specific `c_it`. Tobin's own estimation predates modern MLE.

**Amemiya (1984)**, "Tobit Models: A Survey," *Journal of Econometrics* 24 [RECALL —
unverified; volume recalled with moderate confidence, pages low]. The standard taxonomy (Type
I–V), distinguishing single-equation censored models (Type I — our case) from two-equation
selection-flavoured models (Types II–V, bordering Heckman, §3). Amemiya's Type I is explicit
that the censoring point need not be zero or constant across observations — the textbook anchor
for a unit-varying threshold.

**The exact likelihood with an individual-specific, observed censoring point.** Let `y*_it =
x_it'β + u_it`, `u_it ~ N(0,σ²)` i.i.d., `c_it` the ward-time circle rate observed without
error, `y_it = max(y*_it, c_it)`. Then

```
ln L(β,σ) = Σ_{uncensored} [ −ln σ + ln φ((y_it − x_it'β)/σ) ]
          + Σ_{censored}   ln Φ((c_it − x_it'β)/σ)
```

— the textbook Tobit likelihood with the fixed limit `0` replaced everywhere by `c_it`. This is
a direct corollary of Amemiya's Type I classification, worked through in **Maddala (1983)**,
*Limited-Dependent and Qualitative Variables in Econometrics*, Cambridge University Press
[RECALL — unverified; a chapter, roughly ch. 6, treats non-zero/variable limits, page numbers
not confident], and in the censored-regression chapters of **Greene**, *Econometric Analysis*
(multiple editions) [RECALL — unverified; edition/section not recalled — textbook material I'm
confident exists, not a page I can cite]. Adjacent: **Rosett & Nelson (1975)**, "Estimation of
the Two-Limit Probit Regression Model," *Econometrica* [RECALL — unverified, lower confidence
on exact title] — worth flagging since Section 56(2)(x) creates a buyer-side floor/ceiling
structure (§6) that may eventually need the two-limit rather than one-limit case.

## 2. Why plain Tobit MLE is fragile, and the semiparametric alternatives

Tobit MLE is efficient only if `u_it` is truly i.i.d. Normal and homoskedastic given `x_it`.
Real-estate errors almost certainly are not: dispersion scales with price level, and the
true-price-vs-circle-rate gap is exactly what we're trying to recover — a bad thing to assume
the shape of upfront.

**Powell (1984)**, "Least Absolute Deviations Estimation for the Censored Regression Model,"
*Journal of Econometrics* 25(3), pp. 303–325 [RECALL — unverified, moderate confidence].
Censored LAD (CLAD) needs only a **conditional median restriction** `median(u_it|x_it)=0`, not
full normality, because the median commutes with censoring: `median(max(y*,c)|x) =
max(median(y*|x), c)`. Consistency at a given `x` requires the true conditional *median* of
price to lie above the local circle rate — if over half a ward-period's distribution sits at or
below its own circle rate, the median is itself censored and unidentified there. A first, sharp
version of the "honest ceiling" theme running through this dossier.

**Powell (1986)**, "Censored Regression Quantiles," *Journal of Econometrics* 32(1), pp.
143–155 [RECALL — unverified, same caveat]. Generalises CLAD to any quantile θ: whenever
`Q_θ(y*|x)` exceeds the local circle rate, `Q_θ(y_obs|x) = Q_θ(y*|x)`, identifying a
quantile-specific slope `β_θ` via check-function loss with the censoring point substituted in.
The single most useful tool here — it does not force one slope on a heteroskedastic, fat-tailed
price distribution; different quantiles can have different coefficients on location, size,
floor. The catch: only quantiles clearing the *local* censoring probability are identified — a
heavily-evading ward-period may only yield the upper tail, the opposite of a "typical price."

**Chernozhukov & Hong (2002)**, "Three-Step Censored Quantile Regression and Extramarital
Affairs," *Journal of the American Statistical Association* 97(458), pp. 872–882 [RECALL —
unverified; moderate-high confidence on paper/application, lower on volume/pages]. Powell's CQR
objective is non-convex (max(·,c) sits inside the check function). Their fix: (1) classify
observations as "very likely uncensored," (2) a first-pass quantile regression there for a
consistent start, (3) a one-step refinement on the now-convex local problem — what makes CQR
usable at realistic scale (many wards, years, covariates) rather than a one-cross-section proof
of concept.

**Honoré (1992)**, "Trimmed LAD and Least Squares Estimation of Truncated and Censored
Regression Models with Fixed Effects," *Econometrica* 60(3), pp. 533–565 [RECALL — unverified;
moderate confidence on volume/issue, lower on pages]. The paper the brief correctly flags as
crucial. Ward fixed effects (`α_i`, unobserved neighbourhood level) plus censoring is a textbook
**incidental parameters problem**: with fixed `T` and `N→∞` wards, dummying out every ward in a
Tobit MLE is inconsistent, because more wards adds no information about any single `α_i`.
Honoré's solution is semiparametric: using **pairs of periods within the same ward**, assuming
only that `u_it` is i.i.d./exchangeable across `t` within `i` given `(x_i,α_i)` — not normal, not
a known distribution — he builds a trimmed objective (comparisons of censored/uncensored pairs)
whose expectation depends on `β` but not `α_i`, differencing the fixed effect away without
estimating it. Needs ≥2 transactions per ward across different circle-rate regimes; no
closed-form standard errors (bootstrap is standard practice); and, to my recollection, the
canonical exposition normalises the censoring point to a constant for simplicity — extending it
cleanly to a genuinely period-varying `c_it` is a natural but nontrivial extension of the same
symmetry logic, not something I recall the 1992 paper stating in exactly that form. **Whether
the original result already covers a freely time-varying threshold, or needs an explicit
extension, is one of the highest-value items for a follow-up search pass** — it decides whether
the ward-fixed-effects design can be lifted directly or needs its own methods note.

## 3. Selection is a different animal from censoring

**Heckman (1979)**, "Sample Selection Bias as a Specification Error," *Econometrica* 47(1), pp.
153–161 [RECALL — unverified, high confidence on venue/substance, moderate on pages]. Classical
Tobit and Heckman selection are often conflated but answer different questions. In Tobit, one
latent index determines both whether we observe the uncensored value and what it is — the same
error does both jobs. In Heckman selection there are two equations — a participation/selection
index and a separate outcome equation — linked through the joint distribution (typically
bivariate normal, correlation `ρ`) of their errors.

The mechanical `max(true,c)` structure is pure Tobit only if the decision to under-report is
driven by the *same* unobservables as true price. In reality the evasion decision plausibly
depends on things that do not determine true price at all: seller liquidity needs, buyer's
unaccounted funds, local enforcement intensity, or whether the sale is a RERA-disclosed
builder-to-first-buyer deal (hard to under-report) versus a private resale (easy). If evasion
propensity depends on such variables and they correlate with the true-price error, the honest
structure is Heckman-type — a selection/evasion equation plus a true-price equation — needing an
exclusion restriction (e.g. RERA-disclosure status, or an enforcement-regime change, as a
determinant of evasion but arguably not of value). This is a genuinely open design choice: every
estimator in §1–2 implicitly assumes the simpler one-equation structure, and if that's wrong,
none of their own diagnostics will flag it.

**Wooldridge**, *Econometric Analysis of Cross Section and Panel Data* (MIT Press, 2002 and/or
2010 editions — I do not recall which edition's numbering to cite) [RECALL — unverified]. Two
points to carry forward. First, Wooldridge frames Tobit as a **"corner solution" model**: the
usual justification is that a utility-maximising agent's *optimal choice* genuinely sits at a
corner (zero hours worked). That justification does not apply here — no seller optimally
chooses "the circle rate"; it's a legal/reporting artifact, not an economic corner. We borrow
Tobit's likelihood mechanics, not its behavioural justification, and the Heckman-vs-Tobit
question above is exactly where that mismatch becomes consequential. Second, the standard
warning that **raw Tobit coefficients are not marginal effects on the observed variable**: `β_j`
is the effect on latent `y*`; the effect on observed `E(y|x)` needs the **McDonald & Moffitt
(1980)** decomposition [RECALL — unverified; "The Uses of Tobit Analysis," *Review of Economics
and Statistics* — year/venue moderate confidence, pages not recalled], `∂E(y|x)/∂x_j =
Φ(x'β/σ)·β_j`, splitting into extensive (probability of being uncensored) and intensive
(conditional value) margins. Favourably for us: if the target is *true* price (latent `y*`), the
raw unscaled `β_j` is exactly what's wanted, and it's the *scaled* version that would be wrong
to report — the trap only bites if a fitted Tobit is reused to predict *registered* price (e.g.
a stamp-duty-revenue forecast) without rescaling.

## 4. Bunching: the mass at the floor is itself an estimator

**Saez (2010)**, "Do Taxpayers Bunch at Kink Points?," *American Economic Journal: Economic
Policy* 2(3), pp. 180–212 [RECALL — unverified, high confidence]. Using US tax data around
kinks (jumps in the *marginal* rate), Saez shows an excess density spike right at the kink
identifies the elasticity of taxable income to the marginal rate without an external
experiment — the kink is the experiment. Method: fit a flexible polynomial to bin counts,
**excluding a window around the kink**, to build a counterfactual density; excess mass = observed
minus predicted in that window. Bunching is large among the **self-employed** (control their own
reported income) and small among wage employees (third-party W-2 reported, hard to manipulate).
India analogue: private resale (full control over declared consideration) versus RERA-disclosed
builder sale (closer to third-party reported) should bunch very differently — a segmentation to
build in from the start.

**Chetty, Friedman, Olsen & Pistaferri (2011)**, "Adjustment Costs, Firm Responses, and Micro
vs. Macro Labor Supply Elasticities: Evidence from Danish Tax Records," *Quarterly Journal of
Economics* 126(2), pp. 749–804 [RECALL — unverified, moderate-high confidence]. Formalises the
counterfactual-density recipe (§5) and shows **optimisation frictions** mean observed bunching
understates the frictionless response. Analogue here: a seller may not know the exact current
circle rate, or the deal is struck in round numbers only approximating the threshold — the
observed spike is a lower bound on the true propensity to price at the floor.

**Kleven & Waseem (2013)**, "Using Notches to Uncover Optimization Frictions and Structural
Elasticities: Theory and Evidence from Pakistan," *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 128(2), pp.
669–723 [RECALL — unverified, moderate-high confidence]. Critical distinction: a **notch** (a
jump in *total* liability) creates a theoretically empty **dominated region** just above it —
anyone locating there could strictly improve by moving down to the notch. Kleven & Waseem show
this region isn't empty in practice and use (bunching mass) + (dominated-region occupancy) to
separate elasticity from frictions. **Circle-rate registration is a notch, not a kink** —
43CA/50C/56(2)(x) impose a discrete liability jump once price falls far enough below the circle
rate, not a smooth marginal change — so notch machinery is the right frame, and any occupancy
just above the danger zone reads as frictions, not "no evasion."

**Kleven (2016)**, "Bunching," *Annual Review of Economics* 8, pp. 435–464 [RECALL —
unverified, moderate confidence]. Ties the above together and discusses **heterogeneous
notches/kinks** (thresholds varying by bracket, jurisdiction, year) and pooling by
**normalising the running variable relative to each unit's own threshold** — exactly what a
ward-year-varying circle rate needs: `log(observed/circle_rate_it)` or `(observed −
circle_rate_it)/circle_rate_it`, re-centring every ward-year's notch to zero before pooling.

**Best & Kleven (2018)**, "Housing Market Responses to Transaction Taxes: Evidence from Notches
and Stimulus in the UK," *Review of Economic Studies* 85(1), pp. 157–193 [RECALL — unverified,
moderate confidence]. The closest real-world precedent: UK Stamp Duty Land Tax's tiered notches
produce sharp bunching just below each threshold; a temporary stimulus-driven threshold change is
used to separately identify the reporting elasticity and the distortion's efficiency cost. House
prices, transaction taxes, notches, a policy shock to the threshold — structurally the single
closest external analogue in this toolkit, and the first thing to re-read in full once search
returns.

**Kopczuk & Munroe (2015)**, "Mansion Tax: The Effect of Transfer Taxes on the Residential Real
Estate Market," *American Economic Journal: Economic Policy* 7(2), pp. 214–257 [RECALL —
unverified, moderate confidence]. NY's mansion-tax notch ($1m) generates a bunching spike below
threshold, documented **transaction splitting** (personal property carved into a side contract
to bring real-property consideration under the line), and reduced volume near the notch, not
just relocated prices. The splitting behaviour closely parallels Indian practice of separate
fixtures/development-agreement side arrangements keeping the registered deed near the circle
rate while value transfers off-register — a mechanism that biases *any* registered-price-only
source, not just the exact-floor bunching mass.

## 5. Counterfactual-density mechanics, and the safe-harbour notch-to-plateau

The standard recipe [RECALL — unverified as description, high confidence in substance as
widely-replicated methodology]: (1) bin the threshold-normalised running variable `z` around
`z*=0`; (2) choose an excluded window `[z_L,z_U]` wide enough to contain the spike (and, for a
notch, the dominated region above it); (3) fit a flexible polynomial (commonly order 5–7) to
counts *outside* the window; (4) predict counterfactual counts inside it from the polynomial
alone; (5) excess mass = observed minus counterfactual, summed over the window; (6) normalise by
average counterfactual density near `z*` to get `b` (bin-width units, convertible to an
elasticity under preference-structure assumptions); (7) bootstrap the residuals for standard
errors.

**The India twist.** 43CA/50C deem the circle-rate value the consideration for tax purposes
when actual consideration is lower, **unless** actual consideration is not less than a tolerance
band below the circle rate, in which case actual consideration stands [RECALL — unverified,
moderate confidence on mechanism, lower on figures]. I recall a tolerance introduced around
Finance Act 2018 at roughly 5%, raised around Finance Act 2020 to roughly 10%, with a temporary
COVID-era relaxation (possibly to ~20% for a limited window, certain residential sales) —
**every percentage and effective date here needs search-based confirmation before use.** Section
56(2)(x) mirrors the threshold on the buyer's side.

Mechanically: a **hard, zero-tolerance notch** (Kopczuk-Munroe's mansion tax, or a simplified
circle-rate telling) predicts a single spike at the threshold and an empty dominated region
above. A **proportional tolerance band** — the actual 43CA/50C design — predicts no tax
consequence anywhere in `[circle_rate×(1−tolerance), circle_rate]`, so the shape is not a spike
but a **plateau**: excess mass spread across the band, with the notch (empty dominated region,
if frictions are low) sitting below the band's lower edge. Consequence: the excluded window must
span the **entire band**, not a point — a notch-width window will contaminate the counterfactual
with real plateau mass and understate excess mass. It also implies a clean quasi-event study: if
the tolerance percentage genuinely changed, the plateau's width should shift with it, band edge
for band edge, around each change date. **This tax-parameter history is not currently in this
desk's `breaks-registry.md`** (BR1–BR9 cover LTCG/DDT/buyback-STT, not the circle-rate safe
harbour) — a concrete gap, a new BR entry should be registered before pre-registering any
bunching design on this variable, per this desk's own pin-the-notification precedent.

One further, uncited risk: Indian circle rates are set in round per-sqft units, and transacted
prices independently cluster at round numbers (₹50 lakh, ₹1 crore) for unrelated reasons. Where
a ward's circle rate sits near a round number, round-number heaping and circle-rate bunching
become hard to tell apart in a simple density plot [RECALL — unverified, general inference, no
specific real-estate citation recalled]. A placebo — bunching intensity in wards whose circle
rate is *not* near a round number — is the natural test, not yet designed anywhere.

## 6. Classical measurement error versus non-classical censoring, and the honest ceiling

**Classical measurement error in the dependent variable** — `y_obs = y_true + ε`, `ε` mean-zero
and independent of `X` — is, by a standard result, **free** in terms of bias: OLS of `y_obs` on
`X` recovers the same slope probability limit as OLS on `y_true` would; the cost is only a
larger residual variance [RECALL — unverified as citation, standard textbook material, e.g.
Wooldridge's introductory treatment — section not recalled]. This is why intuitions about "noisy
data" are too optimistic when applied to circle-rate data.

**Circle-rate censoring is not this.** `y_obs = max(y_true, c(X,t))` is (i) **one-sided** — the
gap is never negative — and (ii) **mean-dependent on X in a structured way**:
`E[y_obs−y_true|X] = E[max(0, c(X,t)−y_true)|X]`, largest exactly where `X` implies a true price
far below its own ward's circle rate. Concretely, a model trained naively on `y_obs`:

- Gets a fitted relationship **flattened and shifted toward `c(X,t)`** exactly in the segments
  furthest below the local circle rate — a directional bias, not uniform noise, worst precisely
  in the fast-moving, under-assessed micro-markets the desk most wants right.
- Inherits a second `X`-correlated distortion from circle-rate **staleness**: schedules revised
  on a lagged, irregular calendar (some wards reportedly untouched for years — [RECALL —
  unverified], needs its own confirmation).
- **Looks best exactly where it is worst**: low residual variance and high apparent fit in the
  heaviest-bunching wards, because the floor mechanically compresses observed variance there — a
  self-congratulating failure mode for any automated model-selection step rewarding in-sample
  fit.

**What is recoverable, and by what.** (a) The conditional distribution of true price *above*
the local threshold, at whatever quantiles clear the local censoring share — Powell CQR/CLAD,
Honoré once fixed effects are added. (b) Aggregate excess bunching mass, and, under a
Kleven-Waseem-style structural assumption, an implied evasion elasticity or off-register value
fraction — without ever observing an individually-evaded true value. (c) Ward-fixed-effect-purged
slope coefficients via Honoré, again without any censored observation's true value.

**What is not recoverable without an external anchor.** Any *individual* censored transaction's
true price — these estimators identify a function (a quantile, a slope), never an individual
latent value. Where local censoring exceeds 50%, the median and below are simply unidentified by
CLAD/CQR — only quantiles clearing the local censoring share are recoverable, and in the most
evading or most compliant markets that may require reaching far up the tail, not the "typical
price" the engine wants. Genuine individual-transaction truth needs an anchor outside this
toolkit entirely — matched bank/HFC loan-sanction valuations (their own bias, per this pack's
C-series RESIDEX/RAPMS notes), RERA-filed builder price lists for the same unit, insurance
declared values — or a fully structural evasion-decision model (Heckman-style, §3) with its own
untestable exclusion restriction.

**The one failure mode nothing here solves**: circle rates are typically *set* by extrapolating
from *past registered* (already-censored) transactions — a potential floor-chasing-floor
dynamic, making the threshold endogenous to the very series being modelled. This is a harder,
different problem than anything Tobit, Powell, Honoré, or the bunching estimators assume away
(all treat the threshold as exogenous once observed). Before trusting any of this for genuine
forecasting (versus same-period description), the engine needs its own design testing whether
circle-rate revisions Granger-cause, or merely lag, past bunching mass and past registered-price
growth in the same ward.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE INDIA ENGINE

1. **Never fit plain OLS or a naive ML model on registered price as if it were true price.** The
   bias is directional and worst exactly in the fast-appreciating, under-revised-circle-rate
   wards the engine most needs to get right — the single most important warning here.
2. **Build the running variable as `(observed − circle_rate_it)/circle_rate_it` from day one** —
   this is what lets bunching mass, censoring shares, and quantile cutoffs pool across wards and
   years with structurally different threshold levels (Kleven 2016's normalisation).
3. **Segment by evasion opportunity before pooling**, using Saez's self-employed/W-2 split:
   private resale vs. RERA-disclosed builder-to-first-buyer sale is plausibly as large a
   bunching-intensity gap in India as self-employment is in US tax data.
4. **Register the 43CA/50C/56(2)(x) tolerance-band history as a new breaks-registry entry before
   pre-registering any bunching design** — the excluded window must span the full proportional
   band, and the band's own historical changes are a free, undesigned identification opportunity.
5. **Use Powell CQR/Chernozhukov-Hong across a grid of quantiles, not one CLAD/Tobit fit**, and
   report per ward-period which quantiles actually clear the local censoring share — an honest
   "how much of the true distribution is recoverable here" map beats a point estimate that
   quietly fails in the most-evading markets.
6. **Reserve Honoré's trimmed estimator for the ward-fixed-effects specification**, budgeting for
   bootstrap standard errors and no closed-form marginal effects — but confirm first (search pass
   owed) whether the 1992 result needs an explicit extension for a period-varying threshold.
7. **Decide and document, before building anything, whether this is one-equation (Tobit-family)
   or two-equation (Heckman-family)** — if evasion propensity is driven by variables that don't
   also drive true price, every estimator above is misspecified in a way its own diagnostics
   won't reveal.
8. **Test for threshold endogeneity (circle-rate revisions chasing past bunching) before trusting
   any of this for forward-looking forecasting** — an open, undesigned risk, arguably the
   highest-value design to pre-register once circle-rate schedule data lands.
