## 0. The one caveat that applies to almost everything below

Almost none of these products measure the same physical quantity as an Indian real-estate
transaction's quoted area. A satellite "built-up surface" pixel or ML building-footprint polygon
measures **plinth/roof coverage (2D footprint)**, sometimes scaled to a volume/height proxy — not
carpet, not built-up, and not super-built-up/saleable area as used in RERA filings and broker
listings. Going from a footprint to a high-rise apartment's saleable area multiplies by floor count
(FSI/FAR) and then by the carpet-to-super-built-up loading factor (a commonly cited rule of thumb of
~1.2–1.4x, unsourced here) — a *compounding*, not additive, distortion, larger than the 20–35%
area-basis swing the brief flags for price data itself. State which physical quantity each such
feature proxies and never read it as "built-up area" in the RERA sense.

## 1. OpenStreetMap + Overpass API

OSM (crowdsourced buildings/roads/POIs/land use) is licensed **ODbL** — free to use and derive from;
ODbL's share-alike attaches to the *database*, and produced statistical/analytical outputs are
generally treated as an exempt "produced work," but that is a licensing nuance, not legal advice —
get counsel before redistributing derived extracts. **Overpass API** is the standard free query
interface; heavy bulk pulls are better served from periodic dumps (Geofabrik publishes a daily-ish
India `.pbf` extract) than live Overpass calls, which throttle.

**India coverage is uneven, not uniformly good or bad.** I recall Barrington-Leigh & Millard-Ball,
"The world's user-generated road map is more than 80% complete" (PLOS ONE, 2017) as the standard
global OSM-completeness benchmark, but not its India-specific figure — unconfirmed, not invented.
More generally I recall: (a) HOT (Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team) mapathons tied to disaster
response (2018 Kerala floods, flood-prone Bihar districts) locally densified tagging around
specific regions/years — a "mapathon halo," not uniform improvement; (b) metro cores (Bengaluru,
Delhi-NCR, Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad, Pune) are visibly denser-tagged than tier-2/3 towns and rural
India, because OSM density tracks mapper population, not settlement density; (c) studies comparing
OSM building counts against ML footprint datasets (§4) generally find OSM under-counts outside
mapped urban cores, though I can't cite a specific paper. **Implication**: OSM density is usable as
a feature but is partly a mapper-effort artifact — pair it with an independent built-up measure
(GHSL, §2) rather than reading it alone as "how built-up is this place."

**Point-in-time**: a dated `.pbf` extract is a genuine snapshot and a real panel is buildable from
successive extracts, but a feature's OSM edit date can lag its true construction date by years —
never treat "first appears in OSM" as "was built" without cross-checking an imagery-based series.

## 2. JRC Global Human Settlement Layer (GHSL) — the anchor recommendation

Produced by the EU Joint Research Centre. **GHS-BUILT-S** (built-up surface, m²/cell) is the
flagship time series: **100m**, 5-year epochs **1975–2030** (the trailing 2025/2030 epochs are
partly model-projected, not satellite-observed — exclude them from historical "as-of" features), plus
a newer ~10m Sentinel-2-derived layer for one recent epoch only (recalled ~2018), not the full
series. **GHS-BUILT-V** gives an analogous volume/height-proxy grid — closer to "how much floor
space" than BUILT-S, still not registered saleable area. **GHS-POP** (100m/1km, same epochs) is
*derived from* the built-up layer to disaggregate census counts, so it is not an independent
population source, and pairing GHS-BUILT-S and GHS-POP as separate regressors risks collinearity.
**GHS-SMOD** gives the EC-OECD-UN "Degree of Urbanisation" classification (Urban Centre / Cluster /
Rural) — a clean international label independent of India's own inconsistent urban-area definitions
(municipal limits vs. urban agglomeration vs. census-town criteria). **GHS-UCDB** is a table/vector
Urban Centre Database, one row per detected urban centre per epoch — a city-level India panel can be
built from this alone without touching a raster.

Licence: **CC BY 4.0**, no share-alike — the cleanest licence in this dossier. Download: JRC's GHSL
portal (recalled domain `ghsl.jrc.ec.europa.eu`), Copernicus mirrors, and inside Google Earth
Engine's public catalogue.

**Why this is (my own synthesis, not a citation) the single best free built-up-change series for
India**: free, unrestricted licence, a genuine multi-decade time series rather than a snapshot,
methodologically consistent nationwide (unlike OSM's mapper-density artifact or Bhuvan's access
friction, §9), fine enough for locality-level change yet tractable at national scale.
**Point-in-time**: each epoch is a distinct, dated raster — legitimate for a panel provided only
epochs at or before the prediction date are used.

## 3. Population grids: WorldPop, Meta/CIESIN

**WorldPop** (University of Southampton): gridded population, typically 100m/1km, multiple product
lines (unconstrained and building-footprint-informed), generally CC BY 4.0, `worldpop.org`, no
registration. **Meta/CIESIN High Resolution Settlement Layer (HRSL)**: ~30m population density from
Meta's building-detection models combined with CIESIN (Columbia) census disaggregation, via the
Humanitarian Data Exchange under Meta's Data for Good program; India recalled as covered. **Both are
model outputs, not censuses** — they inherit whatever Census-of-India vintage underlies them
(almost certainly 2011, §11) even under a recent-looking "vintage year" label, so a 2026-dated
raster may still be extrapolating 2011 counts via a growth proxy. Treat the *level* as
stale-anchored; the embedded *growth pattern* is the more trustworthy signal.

## 4. Building footprints: Google Open Buildings, Microsoft Global ML Building Footprints

**Google Open Buildings**: ML-derived footprint polygons with a confidence score, from
high-resolution satellite imagery. Initial release covered Africa (~2021) with a later (~2023)
expansion to South Asia/Southeast Asia; **India should be in that expansion but I could not confirm
current inclusion or vintage** — the brief asked me to confirm this and I cannot without search;
flag directly for verification. I also recall a later "temporal" variant giving multi-year
presence/height, weak confidence on the year range. Licence recalled as CC BY 4.0; access via Cloud
Storage/BigQuery/Earth Engine.

**Microsoft Global ML Building Footprints**: CNN-derived polygons from Bing imagery, released on
GitHub (`microsoft/GlobalMLBuildingFootprints`) country-by-country, India recalled as included.
Licence **ODbL** — matters more here than for OSM because this is a machine-generated dataset a desk
might feed directly into a model; the same produced-work nuance from §1 applies and needs a real
legal read before external redistribution of derivatives. **Vintage is mixed and
imagery-source-dependent** — different tiles come from different years of Bing imagery, so "the
India footprint layer" has no single as-of date. **This is the dossier's strongest lookahead-trap
warning**: do not date this dataset by its GitHub release date; the underlying imagery is older and
heterogeneous tile-by-tile.

## 5. Nighttime lights: VIIRS, DMSP-OLS, and the India literature

**VIIRS Day/Night Band**: Suomi NPP (operational since ~2012), annual/monthly cloud-free composites
at roughly 500m, produced by the Earth Observation Group, now at Colorado School of Mines/Payne
Institute (recalled domain `eogdata.mines.edu`). Predecessor **DMSP-OLS** (1992–2013, ~1km-ish,
saturates in bright cores) is not directly comparable without intercalibration — harmonized
DMSP-to-VIIRS series exist in the literature, but I can't cite a specific paper. A 30-year India
nightlight history needs an explicit calibration model across the 2012–13 sensor splice, not raw
concatenation.

**Literature**: the canonical cross-country paper is Henderson, Storeygard & Weil, "Measuring
Economic Growth from Outer Space" (American Economic Review, 2012). For India specifically, the
most useful resource I can recall is **SHRUG** (Socioeconomic High-resolution Rural-Urban Geographic
Platform — recalled authors Asher, Lunt, Matsuura, Novosad, Development Data Lab), which links
Indian administrative units (villages/towns) across Census years under a stable identifier and
bundles nightlight series with Census/economic indicators — close to a ready-made answer to the
brief's crosswalk problem, if it checks out; possibly the single most useful item in this dossier.
The brief's named "Bhandari" literature I could not confidently place — possibly Laveesh Bhandari
(Indicus Analytics), who has written on district-level income estimation in India, but I have no
citable paper/title/year and won't invent one.

**Point-in-time**: annual composites are clean, dated, essentially non-revised snapshots — one of
the cleanest true series here — except that any *harmonized* pre/post-2013 value necessarily uses
information from both sensor eras to fit the calibration, so flag it if used across a hard
pre/post-2013 split.

## 6. Imagery platforms: Sentinel, Landsat, Google Earth Engine — and a licensing flag

**Sentinel-1** (SAR, ~10m) and **Sentinel-2** (optical, 10–20–60m, ~5-day combined revisit) are free
open Copernicus (ESA/EU) products; the original Copernicus Open Access Hub was decommissioned and
replaced by the **Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem**, exact transition date unconfirmed. **Landsat**
(30m, continuous since Landsat 4/5 in the early 1980s) is free via **USGS EarthExplorer** since the
2008-ish US free-and-open policy change.

**Google Earth Engine** hosts most of the above plus GHSL/WorldPop in its public catalogue with a
JS/Python API. **Licensing flag for a proprietary desk**: I recall GEE introduced a commercial-use
tier (~2021 onward) — non-commercial/research stays free, commercial entities are expected to hold a
paid licence. The Cycle Program is proprietary capital; building production infrastructure on GEE
compute (versus downloading the same CC-BY rasters directly from JRC/Copernicus/USGS) could count as
commercial use — get a real licensing check before committing the architecture to GEE.

## 7. Elevation: SRTM, ASTER GDEM, Copernicus DEM

**SRTM**: a single radar mission flown February 2000 — not a time series, one static global
elevation snapshot, later released at 30m globally (initially only over the US at that resolution).
**ASTER GDEM**: ~30m, stereo-optical, multiple refinements (v2/v3), known void/artifact issues
especially in earlier versions. **Copernicus DEM** (GLO-30/GLO-90): TanDEM-X derived (imagery
~early-to-mid 2010s), generally the best-quality open global DEM; GLO-30 recalled as becoming fully
open only after an initially restricted period. **All three are static** — fine as an "as-of"
feature for any date, but none detects new construction or grading; that job belongs to §§2/4, not
the DEMs.

## 8. Land cover: ESA WorldCover, Dynamic World

**ESA WorldCover**: 10m, 11 classes, Sentinel-1+2 derived; recalled as only **two epochs (2020,
2021)**, not annual, CC BY 4.0. **Google/WRI Dynamic World**: near-real-time 10m, 9-class
probabilistic land cover updated on essentially every Sentinel-2 scene (~5-day cadence) back to
around June 2015, via Earth Engine (same §6 licensing flag applies). For frequent land-cover
transitions (agricultural-to-built conversion at the urban fringe, a classic leading indicator),
**Dynamic World is functionally the dense series and WorldCover the two-snapshot comparison** — plan
features accordingly.

## 9. ISRO Bhuvan

India's official geoportal (NRSC/ISRO, recalled domain `bhuvan.nrsc.gov.in`), recalled to host a
Land Use/Land Cover family (national ~1:250,000, finer for select cities), an Urban Sprawl layer, a
Natural Resources Census, panchayat-level thematic layers, and a high-res imagery viewer. **Access
has historically been the weak point, not content**: much is exposed only as web-map/tile viewing,
not bulk download; higher-resolution or urban-sensitive layers have required registration with a
stated purpose and approval delay — a legacy of the pre-liberalization security posture (§10). I
cannot confirm how much this changed post the 2021–22 liberalization without checking the current
site — moderate confidence meaningful friction remains; verify before assuming bulk downloadability.

## 10. Survey of India and the National Geospatial Policy liberalization

Historically, large-scale Survey of India topo sheets (1:50,000 and finer) were security-restricted;
only coarser Open Series Maps (~1:250,000) were unrestricted under the 2005 National Map Policy.
Two liberalizing steps I recall, **both dates needing verification**: (i) around **February 2021**,
DST's "Guidelines for acquiring and producing geospatial data" removed prior security-clearance
requirements for most domestic geospatial collection/mapping/publication, replacing blanket
restriction with a narrower negative list, and opened domestic commercial mapping to private
(including foreign-invested) companies; (ii) around **December 2022**, a "National Geospatial
Policy 2022" superseded the 2005 policy with a broader industry-growth/"National Geospatial Data
Infrastructure" framing. If right, it should now be legally permissible for a private Indian desk to
commission, hold, and use higher-resolution geospatial products domestically — worth a direct
compliance check rather than assuming the older restrictive regime still applies.

## 11. Census of India: boundaries, Town Directory, and the elephant in the room

The decadal Census's **District Census Handbook** series publishes a Town Directory and Village
Directory (population, amenities) — the closest thing to an official village/town geography, but
clean machine-readable boundary shapefiles are not, to my recollection, cleanly self-served from
`censusindia.gov.in`; most usable boundary files in circulation are third-hand (DataMeet, §13; SHRUG,
§5).

**The load-bearing fact**: **Census 2011 is still the last decadal Census actually conducted.**
Census 2021 was postponed (COVID-19) and, to my knowledge, had not been conducted as of my training
cutoff — a weak, low-confidence recollection of "2027" floated as a revised reference year in some
reporting, not asserted as settled. Consequence: **every Census-of-India demographic figure —
village/town population, household counts, amenities — is frozen at 2011 vintage, now 15 years
stale.** Ward boundaries are a separate, worse problem: municipal wards are redrawn by state-level
delimitation tied to *local body* elections, unsynchronized with the decadal Census, so a
Census-era "ward shapefile" may not match a city's currently governing ward geometry — a genuine,
likely-irreducible crosswalk risk.

## 12. data.gov.in

India's Open Government Data platform, under the National Data Sharing and Accessibility Policy
(NDSAP, 2012), run by NIC/MeitY, with a companion API (`api.data.gov.in`) requiring free
self-registration for a rate-limited key (exact quota not recalled with confidence). **Treat any
given dataset as a static, one-time upload** unless its own metadata states a recurring cadence —
freshness is extremely uneven by ministry, and this is exactly the "single revised snapshot
masquerading as a live feed" risk the brief warns about.

## 13. Community/civic-tech: DataMeet, OpenCity.in

**DataMeet**: an Indian open-data/civic-tech community (since ~2011) with a GitHub org (`datameet`)
hosting community-compiled state/district boundary shapefiles (built because official ones were
historically hard to get), a pincode-boundary repo explicitly built as an approximation (§14), and
reformatted Census tables. **OpenCity.in**: recalled as a related India civic-data initiative,
possibly linked to Bengaluru open-data/transit work, but current scope/ownership/maintenance status
is a weak recollection — flag for direct verification before relying on it.

## 14. Pincode datasets and the pincode-to-ward crosswalk problem

India Post publishes an official Pincode Directory (post office, pincode, delivery status,
district/state — a flat table, no geometry) via data.gov.in and its own channels. **India Post has
never published official pincode polygon boundaries.** Every pincode "boundary" file in circulation
(DataMeet's included) is a derived approximation — Voronoi tessellation around post-office points,
aggregation of finer administrative units believed to fall inside a pincode, or commercial
geocoding-vendor hulls — and these methods disagree at the margins. This is exactly the crosswalk
problem the brief names: pincodes are postal delivery catchments, not nested within municipal
wards, revenue villages, or even reliably a single district, so joining a pincode-level price panel
(how most Indian property portals report locality prices) to ward-level infrastructure features
carries irreducible boundary-mismatch error whose size depends on which vendor's approximation was
used.

## 15. Transit: city GTFS feeds, OpenTripPlanner/r5

Low-confidence recall of partial, historically inconsistent GTFS publication: **Bengaluru (BMTC)**,
**Chennai (MTC)**, **Kochi** (recalled as relatively progressive on open transit data); **Delhi**
and **Mumbai (BEST)** less consistently open as standalone static feeds (their presence in Google
Maps transit directions implies *some* feed exists/existed, not a stable public download). This
needs a direct, current, city-by-city recheck — Indian municipal open-transit programs change often
and feeds go stale silently; the **Mobility Database** (`mobilitydatabase.org`, successor to the old
transitfeeds.com aggregator) is one place to check current India availability. **OpenTripPlanner**
and **R5 (Conveyal)** are free/open-source engines that ingest GTFS + OSM roads to compute
travel-time isochrones — usable wherever both a current feed and complete-enough OSM roads exist for
a city; quality is bounded by the weaker input, and any isochrone feature should carry an explicit
feed-vintage stamp, since a stale GTFS understates accessibility as transit expands.

## 16. Ward-level election, air quality, water, electricity

**Elections**: municipal/panchayat elections run through **State Election Commissions**, one per
state, separate from the national ECI — so there is no single national ward-election dataset; each
SEC publishes independently, often only as static PDFs, on its own delayed calendar. This is a
fragmented, largely non-machine-readable category, not a download-link problem.

**Air quality**: **CPCB** runs continuous monitoring (CAAQMS) under the National Air Quality
Monitoring Programme with a public dashboard (recalled domain `app.cpcbccr.com`) and periodic
data.gov.in mirrors; **OpenAQ** mirrors CPCB and other Indian monitors into one free unified API.
Coverage skews to larger cities/state capitals; rural/tier-3 coverage is sparse.

**Water and electricity, ward-level**: no confident recall of any systematic, free, ward-granular
DISCOM or water-utility dataset at national or broad multi-city scale; a few Smart Cities Mission
SPV dashboards may expose limited city-specific indicators, none nameable with confidence. Honest
characterization: **effectively not available as a routinely usable free feed today** — a gap, not
a locked door.

## Point-in-time vs. single-snapshot — quick reference

| Product | True time series? | Lookahead risk if used naively |
|---|---|---|
| OSM/Overpass (dated extract) | Yes, from dated extracts | Edit date ≠ construction date |
| GHSL (BUILT-S/V, POP, SMOD) | Yes, 5-yr epochs 1975–2030 | Trailing epochs are model, not observed |
| WorldPop / Meta HRSL | Nominal yes; level census-anchored | Level looks current, is stale-anchored |
| Google Open Buildings / MS Footprints | No — mixed-vintage patchwork | Do not date by file/release date |
| VIIRS nightlights | Yes, clean annual composites | Only at the 2012–13 sensor splice |
| Sentinel/Landsat via GEE/EarthExplorer | Yes, scene-dated | None if scene date used correctly |
| SRTM / ASTER / Copernicus DEM | No — static historical snapshot(s) | Cannot detect new construction/grading |
| ESA WorldCover | Two epochs only (2020/2021) | Not a dense series |
| Dynamic World | Yes, near-continuous since ~2015 | None if scene date used correctly |
| Bhuvan layers | Unclear, view-only for much content | Vintage often unstated |
| Census (village/ward/town) | No — frozen at 2011 | 15-yr-stale level; wards redrawn independently |
| data.gov.in datasets | Usually no — static upload | Assume one-time unless stated otherwise |
| Pincode boundaries | No — derived approximation | Method-dependent polygon disagreement |
| GTFS feeds | Nominally yes, but frequently stale | Feed silently goes stale as routes expand |

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE INDIA ENGINE

1. **Adopt GHSL as the backbone built-environment time series.** It is the only free, India-wide,
   multi-decade, cleanly-licensed (CC BY 4.0) series in this stack — build "built-up change" from it
   first, treat everything else as supplement.
2. **Never label a footprint/built-up measure "built-up area" in the RERA sense.** Document whether
   a feature is footprint, volume-proxy, or floor-count-proxy, and note the assumed FSI/FAR and
   carpet-loading conversion wherever one is used — a multiplicative distortion, not the additive
   20–35% the brief flags for price data.
3. **Treat OSM density as a mapper-effort signal, not a pure built-environment signal** — pair it
   with GHSL so "densely mapped" and "densely built" don't get conflated in a locality score.
4. **Do not build features on Census-of-India ward/village geography without checking whether
   Census 2021 has actually run by the time the engine ships** — my recollection is it has not,
   meaning every such feature is 2011-anchored; source current municipal ward boundaries from each
   city's own delimitation, never a Census-era shapefile.
5. **Try SHRUG first for the pincode/ward/village crosswalk**, before a hand-rolled
   Voronoi-on-pincode-centroids approach, if its coverage and licence check out on verification.
6. **Get a licensing check before building production infrastructure on Google Earth Engine
   compute** — the desk is proprietary capital and GEE's commercial tier may apply; prefer direct
   downloads of the same CC-BY/public rasters (JRC, Copernicus Data Space, USGS EarthExplorer),
   keeping GEE for exploratory use.
7. **Treat ward-level election, water, and electricity data as ungated-but-unavailable** — the
   machine-readable dataset does not exist at national scale; only worth a RUNSHEET line as a
   narrow, city-specific pilot, not a national design.
8. **Stamp every isochrone/transit-accessibility feature with the GTFS feed's vintage date** — a
   stale feed understates accessibility as a city's transit network expands, biasing a locality
   score against newly-transit-served areas right when they are becoming more valuable.
