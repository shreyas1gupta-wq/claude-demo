## 1. The bunching/notch toolkit — the methodology this whole dossier rests on

**Saez (2010), "Do Taxpayers Bunch at Kink Points?", American Economic Journal: Economic Policy.**
[RECALL — unverified, high confidence on core content] The foundational paper. Uses U.S. income
tax return data around convex kinks (marginal rate jumps, e.g. EITC phase-in/phase-out kinks) to
show that if the elasticity of taxable income with respect to the tax rate is positive, mass
should visibly bunch at a kink. Method: fit a flexible polynomial to the empirical density
*excluding* a window around the kink, use that fitted counterfactual to compute "excess mass" at
the kink, and back out an implied elasticity from the size of the bunching relative to the kink's
size. This is the ancestor of every notch/bunching estimator used below. Saez's own empirical
finding was that bunching at U.S. kinks is much smaller than a frictionless model predicts,
motivating the "optimization frictions" literature (#3 below).

**Chetty, Friedman, Olsen & Pistaferri (2011), "Adjustment Costs, Firm Responses, and Micro vs.
Macro Labor Supply Elasticities: Evidence from Danish Tax Records," Quarterly Journal of
Economics.** [RECALL — unverified, moderate-high confidence] Extends the bunching estimator to
account for **optimization frictions** (adjustment costs, inattention) that mute observed
bunching relative to the frictionless prediction: the *fraction of people "unresponsive" despite
being able to gain from bunching* can be estimated and used to bound the true elasticity — a weak
bunching response does not necessarily mean a weak underlying elasticity; it can mean large
frictions. Relevant to circle-rate under-declaration, where the friction (a bank unwilling to
lend against an under-declared price, buyer/seller trust in a cash side-payment, capital-gains
basis complications) plausibly varies enormously by deal size and financing structure.

**Kleven & Waseem (2013), "Using Notches to Uncover Optimization Frictions and Structural
Elasticities: Theory and Evidence from Pakistan," Quarterly Journal of Economics.** [RECALL —
unverified, moderate-high confidence] The single most structurally relevant paper here, even
though its setting is Pakistani **income** tax, not property. A notch (total tax liability jumps
at a threshold, unlike a kink where only the marginal rate changes) creates a "dominated region"
just above it — a range strictly dominated by locating exactly at the notch, which in a
frictionless world should be **completely empty**. Method: the mass actually surviving in the
dominated region estimates the size of optimization frictions separately from the frictionless
elasticity, via the requirement that missing mass above the notch equals excess mass at/below it
(the "integration constraint"). **This maps almost exactly onto the circle-rate problem**: since
duty is charged on the *higher* of declared price and circle rate, declaring below the rate buys
no additional saving, so any mass strictly between the circle rate and the true market price is
evidence of friction (a bank unwilling to lend against an under-declared value) rather than
unconstrained evasion choice.

**Kleven (2016), "Bunching," Annual Review of Economics, Vol. 8.** [RECALL — unverified,
moderate confidence on exact volume/year] Survey consolidating the kink/notch toolkit —
counterfactual density estimation, the integration constraint, bounding elasticities under
frictions, applications across labor supply, retirement, corporate taxation, tax evasion. The
reference implementation guide for the counterfactual-density method this whole dossier's
methodology (§6) depends on.

**Slemrod & Weber (2012), "Evidence of the Invisible: Toward a Credibility Revolution in the
Empirical Analysis of Tax Evasion and the Informal Economy," International Tax and Public
Finance.** [RECALL — unverified, moderate confidence] Methodological survey of how economists
measure evasion when the evaded quantity is by construction unobserved. Three families: (i)
**discrepancy methods** — two independently generated measures of the same transaction where only
one faces the evasion incentive (the Montalvo-Piolatto-Raya design, §3); (ii) **bunching/notch
methods** (above); (iii) **audit-based methods** (random audits, e.g. the U.S. National Research
Program), unavailable for property transfer taxes in most jurisdictions. Adjacent discrepancy-
method paper, not property-specific: **Fisman & Wei (2004), "Tax Rates and Tax Evasion: Evidence
from 'Missing Imports' in China," Journal of Political Economy** [RECALL — unverified, moderate
confidence] — compares Hong Kong's reported exports to China against China's reported imports for
the same product codes, with the gap rising in the tariff rate (I recall an elasticity on the
order of a few points of missing-trade gap per point of tariff, but do **not** trust this
magnitude without a check). The general principle for India: **any two independently-sourced
value estimates for the same asset, where only one faces the evasion incentive, is a usable
discrepancy instrument** — registered price vs. bank loan amount, insurance valuation, or a
rental-yield-implied value.

## 2. The flagship property-transaction-tax notch and evasion papers

**Best & Kleven (2018), "Housing Market Responses to Transaction Taxes: Evidence from Notches and
Stimulus in the UK," Review of Economic Studies.** [RECALL — unverified, moderate-high confidence
on core findings, lower on exact coefficients] Studies the UK Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT), a
**slab/notch** system pre-December-2014 (I recall approximately 0% to £125k, 1% to £250k, 3% to
£500k, 4% to £1m, 5% above, though the exact schedule shifted over the sample period). Two
results recalled with reasonable confidence: **(a)** sharp bunching in the price distribution
**just below** each notch (£250k, £500k especially), interpreted mostly as genuine **price
adjustment** — sellers/buyers bargaining the recorded price down, partly by reallocating value
into non-dutiable "fixtures and fittings" side-agreements, a reporting-margin channel structurally
similar to circle-rate under-declaration — rather than real quantity/quality response; and **(b)**
a **house-price semi-elasticity with respect to the notch** estimated via the missing-mass-above/
excess-mass-below pattern (à la Kleven & Waseem), implying a large price distortion right at the
threshold. The paper's "stimulus" half uses the UK's 2008-09 stamp-duty holiday (see Besley/Meads/
Surico) to separately identify a demand-side volume elasticity. **No specific numeric elasticity
from memory is trustworthy** — the qualitative architecture (notch bunching ≈ reporting/
price-adjustment, not real response) is the safe takeaway.

**Besley, Meads & Surico (2014), "The Incidence of Transaction Taxes: Evidence from a Stamp Duty
Holiday," Journal of Public Economics.** [RECALL — unverified, moderate confidence] Studies the
same 2008-09 holiday (0% threshold temporarily raised £125k→£175k) via regression discontinuity
around the new threshold, to estimate how much of the tax cut passed through to prices vs. stayed
buyer surplus. Recalled headline: a **partial pass-through** — sellers captured part of the
relief via higher prices — consistent with standard incidence theory under imperfectly elastic
supply, but no specific percentage is trustworthy from memory.

**Kopczuk & Munroe (2015), "Mansion Tax: The Effect of Transfer Taxes on the Residential Real
Estate Market," American Economic Journal: Economic Policy.** [RECALL — unverified, moderate-high
confidence on design, lower on magnitudes] Studies New York's "mansion tax" — an additional ~1%
transfer tax applying, as a notch, to the **entire** transaction value once price crosses
$1,000,000 (in place since 1989, recalled with moderate confidence). Deed-record data shows
pronounced bunching just below $1,000,000 and missing mass just above; the paper argues a
meaningful share reflects **genuine price adjustment** rather than reporting manipulation,
because U.S. deed prices are third-party-verified (mortgage underwriting, title insurance,
public recording) and harder to falsify than self-reported income. The paper's most quotable
structural point for this dossier: **whether notch bunching in property data is real repricing or
fraudulent under-reporting depends on how verifiable the recorded price is in that jurisdiction**
— tight in the U.S. mansion-tax case, but in a circle-rate/DC-rate/valor-catastral jurisdiction
the recorded price *is* the number under the evasion incentive, so the balance should tilt much
further toward reporting manipulation. No specific missing-transactions or elasticity figure from
memory is trustworthy.

## 3. Discrepancy-method evasion estimates: Montalvo, Piolatto & Raya on Spain

**Montalvo, Piolatto & Raya (~2020, "Transaction-tax evasion in the housing market," Regional
Science and Urban Economics — journal/year lower confidence than the authors/topic).** [RECALL —
unverified] Authors: José G. Montalvo (Universitat Pompeu Fabra), Amedeo Piolatto (applied
public-finance/tax-evasion economist, IEB/Autonomous University of Barcelona), Josep Maria Raya
(Spanish real-estate/housing economist). **Design**: compares an independent, less-evasion-
exposed valuation — recalled as a bank mortgage **appraisal** value ("tasación," from licensed
appraisal firms, used for loan-to-value) — against the **declared transaction price** that is the
base for Spain's transfer tax (ITP, on resales) or stamp tax (AJD, on new-builds). The logic
mirrors Fisman-Wei: the appraisal serves a different purpose (securing a mortgage) and faces a
different, partially offsetting incentive (inflating an appraisal helps a buyer get a bigger loan,
so it is not a clean ground truth either — a confound I recall the paper addresses explicitly)
than the tax-exposed declared price. Recalled headline: a quantified estimate of the **share of
transactions with a material appraisal-declared gap** and the **average gap size**, used to infer
population-level transfer-tax evasion — but **no specific percentage from memory is trustworthy;
both numbers need a primary-source check.** What *is* safe without verification: the **design
template** — pair the tax-exposed declared price with an independently-incentivized valuation of
the same asset, and read the whole **distribution** of the gap (not just its mean), since a
floor/notch problem is about how much mass sits exactly at the floor vs. spread just above it.

**Spain's 2022 "valor de referencia" reform — a live circle-rate convergence case.** [RECALL —
unverified, moderate confidence on mechanism, lower on the exact law citation] Spain reformed
transfer-tax valuation effective January 2022 (a law recalled as "Ley 11/2021, de medidas de
prevención y lucha contra el fraude fiscal," title/number uncertain), replacing the litigation-
heavy "valor real" standard with a cadastre-computed **"valor de referencia"** per property (from
a periodic market-zone study by the Catastro), with ITP/AJD charged on the **higher of** declared
price or reference value — structurally almost identical to an Indian circle rate or Pakistani
DC/FBR rate. If confirmed, this is a **recent, well-documented natural experiment in the reverse
direction of most of this literature**: a government **introducing** a circle-rate-like floor
where one barely existed, searchable (once egress allows) for 2022-2024 transaction-volume and
declared-price responses — the closest available "before/after a floor is introduced or
tightened" event study to what India could see from a ready-reckoner methodology change.

## 4. Country-by-country administrative floor/reference-value systems

All entries `[RECALL — unverified]`; mechanisms carry moderate confidence, any specific
percentage/date carries low confidence unless noted.

**Greece — "αντικειμενικές αξίες" (antikeimenikes axies, "objective values").** Zone-based
assessed values (Ministry of Finance) floor property transfer tax and the annual ENFIA tax. The
historically documented pattern ran in **both directions**: objective values in prime Athens/
island zones sat **below** market (inviting under-declaration, as circle rates do), while the
post-2008 crash left market prices falling faster than objective values in depressed areas,
temporarily flipping the gap to objective-value-**above**-market (over-taxation, not
evasion-enabling). I recall an objective-value update/alignment exercise around 2021-2022 drawing
on notary/bank transaction data to narrow both directions at once. No percentage should be
trusted from memory; the safe takeaway is that **an administrative floor drifts stale in either
direction depending on the price cycle's phase**, relevant since Indian circle rates are revised
unevenly across states and plausibly show the same bidirectional staleness.

**Italy — valore catastale and the 2006 "prezzo-valore" reform.** Cadastral value (valore
catastale, from official cadastral income/rendita × a multiplier) sits well below market price. A
2006 Finance Law ("Bersani"/"Visco-Bersani") introduced **"prezzo-valore"**: for residential
sales between individuals, the buyer may elect to have *registration tax* computed on the lower
cadastral value **instead of** the declared price, conditional on the true price being honestly
stated in the deed — deliberately **removing** the incentive to under-declare by decoupling the
tax base from the transaction price, on the theory that honest reporting (capital-gains basis,
mortgage sizing) is worth more than the foregone revenue. I recall this being credited with
materially reducing under-declaration in the affected segment, but no before/after rate is
trustworthy from memory. Separately, Agenzia delle Entrate's **Osservatorio del Mercato
Immobiliare (OMI)** publishes semi-annual zone-level €/sqm min-max bands used as an **audit-risk
trigger**, not a hard floor — structurally distinct from a binding circle rate and worth keeping
as a separate category in any India floor typology.

**Portugal.** IMT (transfer tax) is charged on the higher of declared price or "valor
patrimonial tributário" (VPT) — the same floor logic as a circle rate. No empirical
under-declaration estimate recalled; mechanism only.

**Turkey.** Title-deed transfer fee (tapu harcı, historically ~4% combined) has a long-documented
pattern of parties declaring the municipality's minimum assessed value ("rayiç değer") rather than
true price — a close structural twin of India's problem. Enforcement tightened over the 2010s
(declared value barred from falling below the municipal assessment), narrowing but not closing
the gap; no specific gap-size trusted from recall.

**Egypt.** Historically very low formal registration rates, with informal ("orfi") contracts
common instead of registered transfer — partly attributed to ad-valorem registration fees. A
~2006 reform replacing the percentage fee with small fixed fees aimed to raise registration
compliance. Egypt's evasion margin was arguably less "under-declare the price" and more "don't
register at all" — a distinct failure mode (informality, not just under-valuation) worth flagging
as a possibility for parts of India's transaction universe too.

**Pakistan — the DC rate / FBR valuation three-tier system, the closest single analogue to
India's circle rate.** Provincial **District Collector (DC) rates** floor stamp duty/registration
fees and are widely documented as sitting far below market (informal estimates I recall put them
at roughly a third or less of market value in major cities, but no specific fraction is trusted
from memory). From ~2016 the **Federal Board of Revenue (FBR)** introduced separate **FBR
valuation tables** for federal taxes (withholding, capital gains) — closer to market than DC rates
but still below true prices, creating a **three-tier stack** (DC rate < FBR value < market price),
phased closer to market through the late 2010s/early 2020s. Useful because it is the clearest
documented case of **two different administrative floors moving at different speeds** —
structurally close to how Indian circle rates vary sub-district/sub-locality and can be stale in
one direction in one micro-zone and current (or above-market) in another.

**Colombia — catastro multipropósito.** Cadastral value ("avalúo catastral," historically IGAC,
now partly decentralized) long sat well below "avalúo comercial." A World-Bank-supported reform
("catastro multipropósito," under 2018-2019 National Development Plan legislation) aimed to
update coverage and raise assessed values toward market nationwide through the early-to-mid
2020s. No specific gap figure trusted from recall.

**Mexico.** Municipal "valor catastral" (annual property tax, predial) commonly sits well below
"valor comercial." State-level transfer tax (ISAI) is generally computed on the **greater of**
declared price, cadastral value, or a professional appraisal — the same floor-on-the-maximum
logic as circle rates, Pakistani DC rates, and Portugal's VPT.

## 5. Adjacent transfer-tax literature (rate variation rather than notches)

**Fritzsche & Vandrei (~2019, "The German Real Estate Transfer Tax: Evidence for Single-Family
Home Transactions," possibly FinanzArchiv).** [RECALL — unverified, low-moderate confidence]
Germany's Grunderwerbsteuer rate is set by each of the 16 Länder and has diverged since a 2006
federalism reform (states raised it at different times from a uniform 3.5% baseline to rates I
recall reaching 6-6.5% in several). A **rate-variation-across-jurisdiction** design (not
bunching), used to estimate capitalization of the tax into prices — I recall the qualitative
finding as close to full capitalization, but no coefficient is trustworthy from memory.

**Dachis, Duranton & Turner (~2012, "Sand in the Gears: Evaluating the Effects of the Land
Transfer Tax," possibly Journal of Economic Geography).** [RECALL — unverified, low-moderate
confidence] Toronto's 2008 municipal land transfer tax (layered atop Ontario's provincial tax),
using untaxed neighboring municipalities as comparison, finds reduced transaction volume/mobility
— the general "transaction taxes reduce turnover, itself a welfare cost" literature; distinct from
under-declaration but relevant context for reform reluctance even where evasion implies effective
rates are already low.

## 6. The methodological payoff: recovering the true price distribution under an administrative floor

This is the section the brief asks for explicitly, and it is worth being precise about a
distinction the literature above supports but that is easy to blur.

**A notch (UK/NY-style) and a floor (circle-rate/DC-rate-style) are not the same shape, and the
recovery method differs accordingly.** A notch (tax liability jumps at a threshold) produces
**excess mass just below** the threshold and a **hole just above** it — a two-sided, threshold-
local distortion the Kleven toolkit (§1) reads via the "integration constraint" (missing mass
above = excess mass below, against a fitted counterfactual density). A binding **floor** (duty
charged on the higher of declared price and the assessed floor) instead produces a **single mass
point exactly AT the floor**: transactions whose true price is below it are mechanically censored
there, **and** — the part unique to floors, and central to why India's number is hard to
recover — transactions whose true price is comfortably *above* it also collapse onto the same
point, because declaring exactly the floor is the evasion-maximizing choice whenever the private
benefit (duty saved, capital-gains basis understated, unaccounted cash generated) exceeds the cost
(harder mortgage financing, legal risk). **The floor mixes two populations at one observed point —
a non-fraudulent censoring group and an active-evasion group** — and no amount of staring at the
registered-price distribution alone can separate them; unlike a notch's two-sided hole, the mass
point has no internal shape to read an elasticity off.

**This is exactly why every credible recovery method imports an external, differently-
incentivized value estimate**, following the Montalvo-Piolatto-Raya/Fisman-Wei discrepancy
template (§1, §3): a bank appraisal, insurance valuation, rental-yield-implied capitalized value,
hedonic index built off non-tax-incentivized characteristics, or a repeat-sales/matched-pair
comparison. The estimation problem then resembles a **Tobit-style censored/mixture model** rather
than a bunching-density decomposition: a smooth latent true-price distribution, a **corner
solution at the floor for the mechanical-censoring group** (standard left-censoring), and
**separately** a discrete **evasion-choice mixture** for the group whose true price exceeds the
floor but reports it anyway — with the mixing probability rising in the size of the true-price-
minus-floor gap. That gap-dependent mixing probability **is** the tax-wedge elasticity of evasion
the brief asks about, and it is identified from variation in the floor-to-true-price gap across
space/time crossed with the external benchmark — not from the registered-price shape alone.

**On the tax-wedge-elasticity relationship itself**, the consistent qualitative regularity across
this literature (Best & Kleven's notch bunching larger at higher-rate notches; Kopczuk & Munroe's
missing mass scaling with the mansion tax's size relative to price; Fisman & Wei's tariff-rate/
missing-trade-gap result; the Allingham-Sandmo/Yitzhaki intuition Slemrod & Weber's survey
organizes) is that **under-declaration rises with the size of the tax wedge being evaded** —
evasion scales with what there is to gain, not a fixed fraction of price — so a circle-rate-vs-
market gap that is larger in some zone/year should mechanically predict *more* under-declaration
there, not a constant proportional discount. India's own uneven circle-rate revision cadence
should therefore leave a time-varying "evasion intensity" signature, recoverable **if** an
external valuation benchmark can be brought to bear zone-by-zone. **No specific elasticity from
any paper above should size this without a primary-source check** — the direction (bigger wedge →
more evasion) is the load-bearing takeaway; every coefficient is a lead to verify.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE INDIA ENGINE

1. **Registered/circle-rate-anchored Indian transaction prices are a censored-and-selectively-
   mixed series, not "market price with noise"** (mechanical floor censoring + active evasion
   choice piled on the same mass point, per §6). A model that regresses outcomes on registered
   price alone, with no external benchmark, is modeling a mixture, not a market price.
2. **The engine needs at least one external, differently-incentivized value proxy per locality**
   to separate true price from the circle-rate floor — priority candidates: bank home-loan
   sanctioned/appraised amount, rental-yield-implied capitalized value, broker/portal asking
   prices, repeat-sale pairs where available. This is the Montalvo-Piolatto-Raya/Fisman-Wei design
   template, not a bespoke India method.
3. **Do not build a single-elasticity "evasion discount %" constant.** Under-declaration scales
   with the tax wedge (§6), so model the circle-rate/true-price gap as **zone-and-time-varying** —
   keyed to time since that zone's last circle-rate revision and the local stamp-duty-plus-
   capital-gains rate — not a flat national haircut.
4. **Any "recovered true price" series built this way is Tier-B/C evidence at best per
   CONTRACT.md §4** (no country here gives ≥30 independent, India-specific observations of the
   evasion-gap-to-wedge relationship) — a state variable to condition on, not a calibrated
   parameter to plug in with false precision.
5. **Default to assuming most circle-rate-edge bunching is reporting manipulation, not real
   repricing** — because the registered price is directly under the evasion incentive (unlike the
   U.S. mansion-tax's third-party-verified deed price), India sits closer to the UK "fixtures and
   fittings" mechanism than the NY mansion-tax mechanism (§2).
6. **Watch for a Pakistan-style multi-floor structure inside India itself**: circle rates revise
   on different, often long, irregular cycles by state/municipality, so expect the floor-to-market
   gap to vary by time-since-last-revision — a candidate feature, not a constant.
7. **Spain's 2022 valor-de-referencia reform and Italy's 2006 prezzo-valore reform are both live
   "introducing/redesigning a floor" natural experiments** worth a dedicated future search pass —
   the closest available analogue to "what happens if India changes how circle rates are set."
8. **This entire dossier is unverified and must be re-run** before any coefficient, share, or
   citation is used to size a parameter or appear in a published document. The safe, reusable
   content today is the **design templates** and **qualitative regularities**, not any single
   number.
