## 1. S&P CoreLogic Case-Shiller Home Price Indices

**Producer/lineage.** Index calculated and published by S&P Dow Jones Indices; underlying
repeat-sales transaction data supplied by CoreLogic (predecessor vendor Fiserv); methodology
traces to Case, Shiller & Weiss's original repeat-sales work.

**Methodology.** A weighted repeat-sales regression on pairs of arm's-length sales of the *same*
single-family home (Bailey-Muth-Nourse family, refined by Case & Shiller): stage 1 regresses
log price-change on time dummies; stage 2 models how residual variance grows with the interval
between the two sales in a pair; stage 3 re-runs weighted by the inverse of that estimated
variance, down-weighting pairs with unusually long gaps or extreme appreciation. Non-arm's-length
transactions and first sales of new construction (no second sale yet) are excluded. A known,
structural bias: the method cannot separate pure appreciation from renovation value added
between sales, likely a modest upward bias — a standard repeat-sales critique. Composite
(national/10-city/20-city) indices are value-weighted aggregates of component metros. Each
monthly print is itself a **3-month trailing average** of pairs closing in the current and prior
two months — so the headline print for month *t* already blends *t-2* through *t*.

**Geography.** US National index; 20-City Composite (Atlanta, Boston, Charlotte, Chicago,
Cleveland, Dallas, Denver, Detroit, Las Vegas, Los Angeles, Miami, Minneapolis, New York, Phoenix,
Portland OR, San Diego, San Francisco, Seattle, Tampa, Washington DC — list from memory); 10-City
Composite (the original subset); each of the 20 metros standalone; low/middle/high price-tier
sub-indices for a subset of the 20 (split by the first sale's price tercile); condo indices for a
handful of metros (roughly five recalled: Boston, Chicago, LA, NY, SF — count uncertain). **No
free county, ZIP, or tract-level Case-Shiller index exists**; CoreLogic sells more granular
proprietary indices commercially, not free.

**Start date.** National monthly history back to 1987; 10-city composite similarly to 1987/1988;
the 20-city composite's ten added metros were backfilled to January 2000 when it launched
(recalled 2006), so the 20-city series is effectively a 2000-onward product even though
component cities have longer individual histories.

**Frequency/lag/revision.** Monthly; released roughly two months after the reference month
(e.g., July data in late September, recalled "last Tuesday of the month"). Combined with the
3-month trailing-average construction, the freshest information in any release is effectively
2-4 months old relative to contract dates. The prior 1-2 months revise at each release as
late-recorded deed data arrives; S&P describes revisions as generally small but larger in
volatile periods or thin metros.

**Licence/access.** Index **levels** (not microdata) for the national, 10-city and 20-city
composites and each metro are free via FRED — series recalled: `CSUSHPINSA`/`CSUSHPISA`
(national NSA/SA), `SPCS20RSA`/`SPCS20RNSA` (20-city SA/NSA), plus a ticker per metro. S&P's own
site republishes the same levels and methodology PDF free. The underlying home-level repeat-sales
database is **not free** — CoreLogic's proprietary deed-derived database, licensed commercially;
no free bulk download of the parcel/transaction records behind the index.

## 2. FHFA House Price Index

**Producer.** Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA), successor to the OFHEO index after FHFA's
2008 creation.

**Methodology family.** Weighted repeat-sales/repeat-transactions regression, same BMN lineage as
Case-Shiller but with FHFA's own weighting (precise divergence from the Case-Shiller three-stage
weighting not confidently recalled). Three product variants:

- **All-Transactions Index** — pools repeat *sales* of homes financed by conforming
  Fannie/Freddie mortgages **and** repeat *appraisals* (e.g., at refinance), broadening sample
  size and geographic depth at the cost of appraisal-based observations that may embed appraiser
  smoothing/anchoring bias — believed to make this series smoother and slower at turning points
  than a pure transaction-based measure.
- **Purchase-Only Index** — restricted to actual arm's-length purchase transactions of
  conforming, conventional (non-FHA, non-jumbo) mortgages bought or guaranteed by Fannie Mae or
  Freddie Mac; FHFA's preferred headline transaction-price series.
- **Expanded-Data Index** — blends Enterprise (Fannie/Freddie) purchase data with FHA loan
  records and county-recorder deed data (recalled sourced via CoreLogic) to widen the sample
  beyond GSE-conforming loans, capturing more of the lower price segment and smaller
  metros/counties where GSE-only data is thin.

**Census-tract and ZIP-code annual HPIs — the free-granularity frontier.** FHFA separately
publishes an **experimental/developmental annual** HPI at 5-digit ZIP-code level and, more
recently, **census-tract level** — flagged by FHFA as developmental given thin-sample noise at
that granularity. No confident start year for either series (candidates in memory range from the
mid-1970s, paralleling the national all-transactions start, to a later date such as 1989 or the
mid-1990s) — flagged `[start-year uncertain]`, precisely the fact needing a direct check before
treating it as the finest free official US granularity. Both are nonetheless free public-domain
downloads (no login, no fee), consistent with FHFA's general posture on all HPI products.

**Geography overall.** National, 9 census divisions, state, roughly the 100 largest CBSAs/metro
divisions, essentially all ~3,000 US counties (all-transactions; very small counties suppressed
for thin samples), and the experimental ZIP/tract layers above.

**Frequency/lag/revision.** Quarterly for the flagship national/state/CBSA all-transactions and
purchase-only series; a separate monthly (SA and NSA) purchase-only series at
national/census-division/state level; annual for the ZIP/tract experimental layer. Quarterly
releases lag roughly two months after quarter-end; monthly purchase-only lags roughly six weeks.
Recent quarters revise as more repeat-sales pairs are recorded late — the same real-time
revision problem as Case-Shiller, modest but largest in the most recent 2-4 quarters.

**Licence/access.** Fully free, public-domain federal statistical product; bulk CSV/Excel
download directly from FHFA's HPI page (`https://www.fhfa.gov/hpi` — attested by a sibling
agent's reachability probe in `_PROBE.md`, though that probe call itself returned blocked/000
under WebFetch, confirming only that the URL was used, not that it currently resolves), no
registration; top-level series also mirrored on FRED.

## 3. Zillow Research Data

**Producer.** Zillow Group, via Zillow Research.

**ZHVI (Zillow Home Value Index).** Not a raw median of sales or listings — a "smoothed,
seasonally adjusted measure of the typical home value," derived from Zillow's own home-valuation
model (the family underlying the consumer-facing Zestimate) applied across the estimated values
of the *stock* of homes in a geography, not only homes that transacted that month, then
smoothed/seasonally adjusted; commonly reported for the 35th-65th percentile band (to damp
luxury/distressed skew) plus separate bottom-third and top-third tier series. Zillow's valuation
model moved from classical hedonic regression toward machine-learning/neural-network methods —
recalled a substantial rebuild around 2019 and a further Zestimate neural-net rebuild around
2021, but whether the 2021 rebuild applied to ZHVI itself or only the individual-home Zestimate
is **not** confidently recalled and is flagged as unresolved.

**ZORI (Zillow Observed Rent Index).** A "repeat-rent" analogue of repeat-sales logic applied to
observed asking rents on the same unit over time, smoothed similarly; shorter history than ZHVI
because Zillow's rental-listing density built up later.

**Other free series.** ZHVI split by home type (single-family, condo/co-op); for-sale inventory
count; new listings; median list price; median days to pending; share of listings with a price
cut and the cut's median size; months of supply; sale-to-list ratio.

**Geography.** National, state, metro/CBSA (Zillow's own metro definitions, not always identical
to OMB CBSAs), county, city, ZIP code, and — for a subset of large metros only — neighbourhood
level, the finest free geography Zillow publishes.

**Start date.** ZHVI (smoothed, SA, all-homes) commonly charted from around 2000 for most
metro/national series, with some series/tiers extending into the mid/late 1990s; no single
confident start date across every geography/tier combination. ZORI's history is much shorter,
plausibly starting around 2014-2015 for the earliest-covered metros.

**Frequency/lag/revision.** Monthly for essentially all series; roughly a one-month lag. Recent
months revise as the underlying valuation model refreshes and more current listings/estimates
data arrives — a "nowcast" property shared with any model-based (non-transaction) index.

**Licence/access.** Genuinely free bulk CSV download, no login, from Zillow's public Research
Data page; the file path prefix `files.zillowstatic.com/research/public_csvs/zhvi/...` is
independently attested in this pack's own `_PROBE.md` (e.g.
`Metro_zhvi_uc_sfrcondo_tier_0.33_0.67_sm_sa_month.csv`), raising confidence in the URL pattern
specifically, though not in the surrounding methodology claims. Zillow's terms of use, as
recalled, permit non-commercial/research reuse with attribution but restrict commercial
redistribution or embedding in a competing product — re-read directly before any commercial use,
since these terms have changed before and are not confirmed current this session.

## 4. Redfin Data Center

**Producer.** Redfin Corporation — a licensed real-estate brokerage with direct MLS access in
most markets it covers, not merely a listings aggregator, giving it first-party access to raw MLS
contract/closing data rather than only public deed records.

**Methodology.** Aggregated MLS listing and closed-transaction rollups: median sale price, homes
sold, new listings, active listings/inventory, median days on market, sale-to-list price ratio,
share of homes sold above list, and a proprietary "Homebuyer Demand Index." A materially
different lineage from Case-Shiller/FHFA/Zillow — not repeat-sales, not a valuation model, but a
direct MLS-activity rollup, so it inherits MLS mix/composition effects the way NAR's median does.

**Geography/frequency.** National, state, metro, county, ZIP, and neighbourhood-level for some
metrics in core coverage areas; weekly at the national/metro headline level, monthly for
county/ZIP/neighbourhood detail.

**Start date.** Weekly series starting roughly 2015-2017 (recalled, not confident); monthly
county/metro/ZIP series extending further back, plausibly 2008-2012 for many geographies.

**Lag/revision.** Weekly data released within about a week of the reference week — unusually
fresh relative to every other series in this dossier; monthly data lags roughly 3-5 weeks. Recent
periods are explicitly preliminary and revise as MLS closings finalize.

**Licence/access.** Free bulk TSV/CSV download from Redfin's Data Center, no login; reuse
permitted with attribution, data "as is." Exact current licence text not confirmed this session.

## 5. Realtor.com Research (residential inventory core metrics)

**Producer.** Realtor.com (operated by Move, Inc.), NAR's officially designated listing portal,
via its Economic Research team.

**Methodology.** Built from realtor.com's own aggregated MLS listing feed — an inventory/listings
census rather than Redfin's broker-side contract data. Core metrics: median listing price, active
listing count, new listing count, median days on market, price-increased/price-reduced
counts/shares, and a pending-listing ratio.

**Geography/frequency.** National, state, metro, county, ZIP code; monthly historically, plus a
coarser weekly "Housing Trends" report at national/regional level.

**Start date/lag.** Core metrics commonly start around 2016-2017 for granular (ZIP/county)
geographies, national/metro sometimes longer; monthly lag roughly 2-4 weeks; recent months can
revise slightly as listing capture reconciles.

**Licence/access.** Free CSV download from realtor.com's research/data page, no login for the
core metrics files; commercial vs. non-commercial reuse terms not confirmed this session.

## 6. US Census Bureau construction and housing series

**Building Permits Survey (BPS).** Monthly survey of essentially all US permit-issuing
jurisdictions; finest free published geography is **place** (city/town) and **county**, not
census tract; reports units authorized by structure type (1-unit, 2-4 unit, 5+ unit). Free,
public domain, `census.gov/construction/bps` (URL independently attested by the sibling
`_PROBE.md`). National/regional release lags a few weeks; the detailed annual place/county file
lags further into the following year.

**New Residential Construction / New Residential Sales (starts, completions, under construction,
sales).** Published **only at national and 4-region (Northeast/Midwest/South/West) level** —
Census does **not** publish starts or new-home sales at state or metro granularity because the
underlying Survey of Construction sample is too small to support reliable sub-national estimates.
A genuine granularity ceiling: anyone wanting state/metro-level starts must fall back to BPS
permits (a proxy, not actual starts) or a private/derived series. Monthly, released roughly 3-4
weeks after month-end, preliminary/revised/final vintages; starts figures carry well-known large
sampling-error confidence intervals, frequently larger than the month-over-month change itself.

**American Community Survey (ACS) housing tables.** Median home value (table B25077), median
gross rent (B25064), tenure, housing-cost burden, occupancy/vacancy, structure age/type —
available down to **census tract and block group** via the 5-year ACS estimates; 1-year
estimates only for larger geographies (population ≥65,000), so tract-level detail is 5-year-only.
ACS 1-year estimates began in 2005 (replacing the old decennial long form); 5-year estimates
began with the 2005-2009 vintage, released December 2010, rolling annually since. Free, public
domain, via `data.census.gov` and the Census API. **Caveat**: ACS home values are self-reported by
the homeowner respondent, not a transaction or appraisal figure — a distinct bias from every
transaction-based series above, and the finest small-area, systematically collected US
housing-value indicator that exists precisely because it trades transaction-grounding for
coverage.

## 7. HMDA (Home Mortgage Disclosure Act) loan-level register

**Producer/administrator.** Collected under HMDA, historically administered by the Federal
Reserve, moved to the CFPB post-Dodd-Frank (recalled 2011); public loan-level files published via
the FFIEC/CFPB HMDA Platform.

**Content.** Mortgage application/origination records from essentially all sizable US lenders
above a reporting threshold: loan amount, purpose, action taken, lender identity, and geography
geocoded to **census tract**. Since the post-2018 field expansion (implementing Dodd-Frank's
broadened HMDA rule), records also carry property value, combined LTV, DTI, interest rate, and
detailed demographics; pre-2018 vintages have a narrower field set without property value/DTI/
rate.

**Start date/frequency/lag.** Modern loan-level public files available from roughly 2007 forward
(older, coarser aggregate HMDA data exists back into the 1990s in different formats); the
expanded field set (incl. property value) starts with 2018 activity. Annual collection, with a
"snapshot" loan/application register released mid-following-year and a more complete "final" file
some months after — roughly a one-year lag overall.

**Licence/access.** Free, bulk-downloadable (CSV/pipe-delimited) and API-accessible via the
CFPB's HMDA Platform/data browser; public-domain federal data, no registration for the public
loan/application register.

## 8. NAR Existing-Home Sales

**Producer.** National Association of Realtors — a private trade association, not a government
agency.

**Methodology.** A straightforward **median** of closed MLS-reported transaction prices,
seasonally adjusted and annualized for the sales-pace headline (SAAR) — not repeat-sales, not
hedonic. Because it is a simple median of whatever mix of homes happened to sell that month, it
carries meaningful compositional/mix bias: a shift toward more high- or low-price homes selling
moves the median even with no change in any individual home's value. This is the best-known
"mix bias" critique among the major US series and the direct analogue of every India median-price
tracker's own composition problem.

**Geography/frequency.** National and the 4 census regions monthly; a quarterly metro-area median
existing single-family price series covering roughly 200 metros.

**Lag/revision.** National/regional release about 3-4 weeks after month-end — one of the freshest
"official" nationwide indicators, though methodologically the weakest of the major series here on
mix-adjustment grounds. NAR's underlying counts have periodically been subject to larger benchmark
revisions; I recall an episode of a significant downward benchmark correction to sales counts at
some point in the 2010s but do not have confident particulars and flag this specifically as
`[RECALL — unverified, episode/date uncertain]`.

**Licence/access.** National/regional headline release (press tables) free on `nar.realtor`; the
more granular metro-level quarterly tables and any underlying microdata are generally reserved
for NAR members or paid research products — so NAR data is only **partly** free.

## 10. County assessor and recorder open-data portals

- **Los Angeles County** — the Assessor's Office publishes parcel-level assessed values,
  characteristics, and assessment-triggering transfer events; California's Proposition 13 regime
  means the arm's-length sale **price** is not always uniformly stated on the deed's face the way
  it would be in a full-disclosure state, though the deed's transfer tax stamps (proportional to
  price in most CA counties) let a price be approximately backed out — a disclosure mechanic worth
  a direct check before building a pipeline on it.
- **Cook County (Chicago)** — the Assessor's Office and the county's broader open-data portal
  publish detailed parcel-level records including sales, assessed/market values, and property
  characteristics, bulk CSV plus an API; a frequently-cited example of genuinely open county-level
  real-estate data in academic AVM research.
- **King County (Seattle)** — Washington is a full-disclosure state, so the Assessor's
  real-property sales data (eReal Property/open-data portal) includes actual recorded sale prices
  alongside characteristics, free to download.
- **New York City** — ACRIS (Automated City Register Information System) provides free
  searchable/downloadable deed and mortgage recordings including sale price for all five boroughs;
  the Department of Finance separately publishes "Rolling Sales" files (borough-level, address,
  sale price, building class, square footage, year built) — a very commonly used free dataset for
  NYC hedonic/repeat-sales work.
- **Texas — the closed counterexample.** Texas is one of a small set of US **non-disclosure
  states**: sale price is not required on the publicly recorded deed, so records show a transfer
  occurred but not its price; appraisal districts, researchers, and even the taxing authority rely
  on voluntary disclosure, MLS access (not public), or third-party brokers for price — a
  **legally structural** gap, not a technology/portal one. Roughly a dozen non-disclosure states
  besides Texas are recalled, without a confident, current, complete roster (the list also shifts
  via state legislation) — needing a direct check before it decides which US counties can even
  serve as comparison/training geographies for a repeat-sales methodology transfer.

## 11. Composition, mix, and area-basis cautions

Every product above sits somewhere on a spectrum between **unit-price** indices that sidestep the
area-basis problem entirely by construction (Case-Shiller, FHFA, Freddie Mac all price *the same
house against itself* across two sales, so square footage never enters the calculation) and
**per-unit or mix-based** measures that carry a real area/composition problem (NAR's and Redfin's
medians move with the mix of homes that happened to transact; any hedonic or per-square-foot cut
of Zillow/Redfin/Realtor.com data depends on how "square footage" was defined by the reporting
MLS, and MLS practice on above-grade vs. total vs. gross living area is notoriously inconsistent
across US markets — the closest domestic analogue to India's carpet/built-up/super-built-up
ambiguity, though no single US source poses it as starkly as India's 20-35% swings). Repeat-sales
indices carry the opposite structural bias: they cannot separate pure appreciation from
renovation value added between the two sales in a pair, a documented critique of the
Case-Shiller/FHFA family generally. And ACS home values are self-reported by the occupant, not a
transaction or appraisal figure, and should never be silently pooled with a transaction-based
series as if the two measured the same thing.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE INDIA ENGINE

1. **Copy FHFA's three-tier product family as the design template, not any single US index.**
   All-transactions/purchase-only/expanded-data maps onto India's natural split: a broad but
   circle-rate-contaminated IGR "all-transactions" analogue, a cleaner "purchase-only" analogue if
   a verified-cash-price subset can ever be isolated, and an "expanded" analogue blending IGR with
   RERA project data and a private listing source.
2. **The real US-India analogue is by construction, not brand name: county recorder/assessor
   portals (Cook County, King County, NYC ACRIS), not Case-Shiller or Zillow.** Both are deed-
   level legal records of transfer that live or die on whether the recorded consideration is
   honest — exactly India's IGR question. Texas's closed, non-disclosure status is the direct
   cautionary parallel for any Indian state where circle-rate under-reporting is severe.
3. **Do not import Case-Shiller's repeat-sales design uncritically.** It needs a persistent
   property identifier across decades, real re-transaction volume, and a reliable arm's-length
   filter — all harder in India (weak IDs pre-DILRMP, lower turnover, common related-party
   transfers). That NHB RESIDEX and RBI's HPI already lean SPAR (sale-price/appraisal-ratio)
   rather than repeat-sales is a design choice worth studying before assuming repeat-sales is
   automatically the better target.
4. **Zillow's ZHVI is the wrong first ambition, not the right one.** It needs a live automated
   valuation model over near-total housing-stock coverage no Indian source can support today. Its
   free, bulk-CSV, ZIP-level, monthly shape is the aspiration to build toward, not day-one method.
5. **HMDA is probably the hardest US capability to ever replicate in India — build to not need
   it.** No Indian register discloses loan-level mortgage originations with geocoded property
   value; India's credit-channel features must come from aggregate RBI sectoral bank/HFC data.
6. **The US's own granularity ceiling is informative, not just a US fact.** Even Census caps
   starts at 4 regions and caps small-area price detail at ACS's self-reported tract estimate.
   India's engine should not chase a finer construction-pipeline geography than the US achieves;
   RERA project-level registrations are plausibly already at parity with the BPS place-level
   ceiling — worth checking explicitly rather than assuming.
7. **Area-basis discipline is a vault-ingestion rule, not a footnote, even in a US comparison.**
   The MLS above-grade/gross-living-area inconsistency is the direct US analogue of India's
   carpet/built-up/super-built-up problem; every source series must record its area convention at
   ingestion, or per-unit-area figures drift silently across sources.
8. **Publication lag and revision behaviour set the honest floor for India's "real-time."** Even
   Case-Shiller is 2+ months stale with a trailing 3-month smoothing on top; FHFA/Freddie carry
   their own vintage-revision noise. Assume Indian registration lag and revisions are at least as
   bad, likely worse — the backtest must never assume same-quarter registration data was actually
   available same-quarter.
