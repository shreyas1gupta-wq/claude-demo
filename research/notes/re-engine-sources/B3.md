## 1. Japan — MLIT Real Estate Information Library, and three price concepts that must not be confused

**The product.** MLIT operates a public portal I recall as **不動産情報ライブラリ** ("Real Estate
Information Library," `reinfolib.mlit.go.jp` per `_PROBE.md` — attempted, connect-rejected, not
attested live) [RECALL — unverified]. My recollection is that this is an early-2020s
consolidation/successor to the older **Land Comprehensive Information System** (土地総合情報システ
ム), which for years hosted the underlying **Transaction Price Information Search**
(取引価格情報検索) — same data, new front end with a real API. Whether it is literally the same
pipeline rebranded, or two systems running in parallel, is the single least confident claim in
this section. [RECALL — unverified]

**What the "transaction price" data actually is — the key methodological fact.** It is **not**
built from registration records. MLIT sends a **questionnaire survey** to parties who just
completed a transaction; the sampling frame comes from registry filings (ownership transfer *is*
registered), but **price, structure, floor area, distance-to-station and usage** come from a
**voluntary post-hoc mail/web questionnaire**, not the deed. Response rates are, by recollection,
well under 50% (I recall figures discussed in the 30s of a percent in some periods, but do not
trust any specific figure) [RECALL — unverified]. This directly disconfirms the intuitive read
that "Japan has an open transaction-level API" implies a transaction census — it is a free,
survey-based *sample*, generalized for privacy.

**Granularity.** Geocoded only to **city/ward (市区町村) plus a rough district label (地区)**,
never a street address or parcel; land/floor area given in **bands**, not exact figures for some
fields; nearest-station distance as a **walking-time band**; transaction date to **quarter**.
Other recalled fields: price, unit price, land shape/frontage, zoning, structure (wood/RC/SRC/
steel), build year, floor-area ratio, renovation flag. Deliberate privacy design, not an oversight.

**The API.** I recall a **REST API returning JSON**, documented in Japanese and **English**,
parameterized by prefecture/municipality code (JIS X 0402), year-quarter, and transaction type
(land, land-and-building, condo unit, agricultural/forest land). Free registration for an API key
is required; I have no confident recall of whether approval is instant or reviewed, or of any rate
limit. [RECALL — unverified, all API mechanics]

**Three other price concepts — do not blend with the above.** (a) **Koji Chika** (地価公示,
official Land Price Publication, MLIT, since 1970) — licensed appraisers value roughly the low
tens of thousands of standard points nationwide as of Jan 1, published in March; appraised
benchmark values at fixed points, not transacted prices. (b) **Kijun-chi** (基準地価, prefectural
land price survey, valued July 1) — fills gaps between Koji Chika points/cadence. (c) **Rosenka**
(路線価, roadside land price, National Tax Agency, not MLIT) — annual, per linear meter of road
frontage, denser than Koji Chika's point sample, base for inheritance/gift tax; rule of thumb is
roughly **80% of Koji Chika** [RECALL — unverified, widely repeated, not sourced by me tonight].
None of (a)-(c) is a transacted price; conflating any with the survey series would repeat the
NHB-RESIDEX two-concept error `partC-data.md` already flags for India.

**Private overlays.** **JREI** (Japan Real Estate Institute) publishes the **Urban Land Price
Index** (市街地価格指数), semi-annual, appraiser-valuation-based, recalled dating to the 1950s.
**Tokyo Kantei** publishes a widely media-quoted existing/used condominium (中古マンション) resale
series from agent-network listing/contract data — faster than MLIT's survey-lagged quarterly.
**REINS**, the MLS-equivalent, is member(agent)-only with public summaries only — Japan's closed
counterpart to the open-but-thin government survey.

## 2. Korea — RTMS: the cleanest "opaque-to-transparent by statute" case after Taiwan

**2006.** Before mandatory actual-price reporting, a registered sale price could differ from the
real consideration — "down-contracting" (다운계약서), under-reporting to cut taxes, structurally
identical to Indian circle-rate arbitrage. Following the **August 2005 ("8.31") stabilization
package**, an amendment I recall as the **Act on Report of Real Estate Transactions, Etc.** made
**actual-price reporting mandatory**, apartments first from around **January 2006**, within a
window of contract (I recall roughly 30-60 days, not confident). [RECALL — unverified on exact
statute name/date/window] The fix tied registration itself to the reported real price.

**RTMS.** MOLIT-run (domain family I recall as `rt.molit.go.kr`, unconfirmed), publishing
transaction-level records at **apartment-complex (danji) name, dong, floor, exclusive area
(전용면적), exact date** — by recollection **the most granular regime in this dossier**, since
named-building + floor + date is near unit-level even without a literal unit number. Distinctively
covers **jeonse/wolse (deposit-based lease) transactions**, not just sales — no analogue elsewhere
here, reflecting how central jeonse deposits are to Korean housing finance. Also covers officetels,
row/multi-family housing (연립·다세대), detached houses, land, some commercial. **Free API**: on
**data.go.kr**, datasets I recall named around "국토교통부_아파트매매 실거래가," requiring a free
account plus a per-dataset "utilization application" (활용신청), which for open datasets I believe
is usually fast, not confidently. [RECALL — unverified, all mechanics]

**Area-basis note.** RTMS records **exclusive/private area (전용면적)** — Korea's carpet-area
analogue — specifically because the older convention of quoting **supply area (공급면적)**, which
folds in a common-space share, created the same per-unit ambiguity India has. If accurate, this is
a directly actionable precedent: standardize on the smallest, least-gameable area concept.
[RECALL — unverified]

**Overlays and a live controversy.** **KB Kookmin Bank's Liiv On** (formerly "KB 시세") is a
bank-appraised price reference long used as the **mortgage LTV benchmark** — a private estimate,
not RTMS's transacted price, driving lending decisions for a long stretch. **Korea Real Estate
Board** (한국부동산원, K-REB, renamed from KAB ~2020) publishes the official **National Housing
Price Trends survey**, weekly/monthly. I recall a public controversy around **2020-2021** in which
critics argued the official K-REB index **understated** price increases versus KB's private index
and raw RTMS prints — official and private indices visibly diverging in a hot market, itself
evidence that index-construction choices matter as much as the underlying data source. [RECALL —
unverified, flagged prominently per instruction #5]

## 3. Taiwan — 實價登錄 (2012, strengthened 2021): the closest analogue to an Indian reform

**Before 2012.** Tax was computed off **government-announced assessed values** — 公告現值 for
land, 房屋評定現值 for buildings — set periodically, and by long-standing commentary running well
below market prices (no specific ratio trusted enough to publish). No statutory requirement
existed to disclose the true price to any public body; analysts relied on private broker
databases (**Sinyi Realty**'s Sinyi Index, dating to the 1990s; a **Cathay/國泰** index) or the
structurally-low assessed-value trend. This is the clean "opaque, by design, for tax reasons"
starting condition, structurally identical to India's circle-rate/stamp-duty problem.

**2012 reform.** An amendment (I recall to the **平均地權條例**, Equalization of Land Rights Act,
moderate confidence on the statute name) took effect **1 August 2012**, requiring the agent — or
the parties directly — to register the **actual transaction price** with the local land
administration office within a window after signing (I recall **30 days**, not confident).
Published free via a Ministry of Interior portal (recalled domain family `lvr.land.moi.gov.tw`),
with the **specific street number masked to a range** (e.g., "15-20號") — transaction-level but
address-fuzzed, more precise than Japan's district-level fuzz.

**2021 — "實價登錄2.0."** Effective around **1 July 2021**, the main gap closed was **pre-sale
(預售屋)** housing sold directly by developers/sales agents (代銷), previously unregistered until
final deed transfer, sometimes years later — a loophole for invisible pre-sale contract flipping
(合約轉讓). 2.0 required developer registration of each pre-sale contract within a window of
signing, registration of subsequent assignments, and moved completed-building disclosure from a
masked range to the **actual building number**. [RECALL — unverified on all dates/thresholds]

**Effects — a distinction not to blur.** 2012 is, as I recall it, a **disclosure/transparency**
reform, not a tax-base change: transaction taxes continued for a time to be computed off the old
assessed values, not the newly disclosed price. The tax-base fix came **separately**: **房地合一稅**
("House and Land Transactions Income Tax," an actual-gain-based capital gains tax), **2016**
("1.0"), strengthened **2021** ("2.0," raising short-holding-period rates to discourage flipping —
I recall a figure in the 35-45% range for short-term brackets, not trusted). **This
disclosure-vs-tax-base distinction is the single most important nuance in this dossier**: India's
own circle-rate system already separates a stated valuation (for stamp duty) from whatever the
deed says, so Taiwan is close to a natural experiment on which lever moves measured prices,
reported volumes and tax revenue — and they need not move together. I have no trustworthy specific
post-2012/post-2016 volume or revenue figures and decline to invent any; this is the first thing a
budget-available search pass should chase.

**How researchers likely handled the break (inference, not citation).** I believe the standard
practice is to treat 2012 Q3 onward as the "clean" actual-price series and either splice it onto
the older Sinyi/Cathay private series with an acknowledged structural break, or start the
hedonic/academic series at 2012. No specific author-year-journal citation is trusted enough to
give — deliberately not invented. [RECALL — unverified, weaker than the institutional facts above]

**Area-basis note — the sharpest cautionary case here.** Area is registered in **ping (坪,
≈3.3058 sqm)**, and the **title-registered ping (權狀坪數) includes an allocated share of
common/public facility area** — stairwells, lobby, clubhouse — the **虛坪** ("phantom ping")
phenomenon. The **public facility ratio (公設比)** on newer buildings is widely discussed as having
risen with building-code changes, figures **on the order of 30-35%+** repeated in general
commentary but not trusted to a specific number. A headline price-per-ping can therefore look
meaningfully cheaper than the true usable-area price — arguably a **worse** version of India's
carpet/built-up/super-built-up problem, baked into the legal title area itself.

## 4. Singapore — free at the flat level (HDB), paywalled at the top (full REALIS)

**URA REALIS.** Holds **caveat-level private-property transaction records** — a caveat is
typically lodged at Option-to-Purchase/S&P stage, so REALIS can reflect a deal well **before**
completion, unlike registration-triggered systems elsewhere here. Fields recalled: project, district,
floor-level **band** (not exact floor), area, price, tenure, sale type (new/sub-sale/resale),
property type. **Full detailed/API/bulk access has historically been a paid subscription** (a
figure on the order of low-thousands of SGD/year recalled, not trusted); the public website offers
a **free but rate-limited basic search**. I am **not confident** how much of this is mirrored free
on **data.gov.sg** — I recall a URA-sourced private residential transactions dataset there but
cannot confirm whether it is caveat-level/near-real-time or a coarser extract. **This is the most
operationally important open question in this section.** [RECALL — unverified]

**HDB Resale Flat Prices — the strongest free product in this dossier.** Genuinely and completely
free, at the **flat level**: town, flat type, block, street, a **storey-range band** (e.g. "10 TO
12," milder generalization than Japan's or Taiwan's), floor area sqm, flat model,
lease-commencement/remaining lease, resale price, transaction month. History splits across several
vintages from changing flat classifications, reaching back to **around 1990**. No API key needed
for bulk CSV; data.gov.sg also exposes a general query API. On the order of **three-quarters or
more** of Singapore's resident population lives in HDB flats [RECALL — unverified,
order-of-magnitude only], making this an unusually representative median-home series — the closest
thing here to "the true median transaction."

**URA Private Property Price Index (PPPI).** Official, free, **aggregate** quarterly index by
CCR/RCR/OCR and landed/non-landed; a flash estimate roughly a month after quarter-end, finalized a
month later.

**Area-basis note.** Milder ambiguity than Taiwan/Hong Kong given government-defined typologies,
but not immune: a controversy around **2012-2013** over private condo developers counting **bay
windows and planter boxes** toward saleable/marketed area, addressed by a URA/BCA circular capping
or requiring separate disclosure. [RECALL — unverified] A second concrete instance of constraint
#6's warning, in an otherwise strong-disclosure jurisdiction.

## 5. Hong Kong — excellent free aggregate indices, no free transaction microdata

**Rating and Valuation Department (RVD).** Official **monthly** private domestic price/rental
indices by **Class A-E size bands** and by region (HK Island/Kowloon/New Territories), plus
office, retail and flatted-factory indices. **Not confident** whether methodology is
transaction-based (from stamp-duty-linked consideration data) or appraisal-based (RVD valuers
pricing representative units) — I hold genuinely conflicting impressions and flag this as open
rather than guess. Free, aggregate only; **no transaction-level microdata released.**

**Centa-City Leading Index (CCL).** Private, Centaline Property Agency, from Centaline's own
brokered-transaction book — a large agency's internal sample treated as broadly representative.
**Weekly**, free to view, widely treated as the **most timely** secondary-housing read, ahead of
RVD's slower release. Not government data, not microdata — an aggregated index atop a private
firm's own book.

**Land Registry.** Maintains the land register; **Land Search** lets anyone pull a specific deed
including stated consideration, but **fee-per-record** (a modest per-document charge recalled, not
trusted), via online self-service — **not** free bulk/API. Free **monthly aggregate** statistics
(volumes, total consideration by district/type) are published separately. **Weakest of the five
regimes on free/bulk/transaction-level microdata specifically** — strong on free aggregate indices,
essentially absent on free microdata, fee-per-record where microdata exists at all.

**Area-basis note — a genuine statutory precedent.** I recall developers historically advertising
price-per-square-foot on **gross/lettable area** (apportioning common-facility share per unit),
inflating apparent affordability, addressed by a statute I recall as the **Residential Properties
(First-hand Sales) Ordinance, around 2013**, requiring first-hand-sale brochures to quote price per
precisely-defined **saleable area** excluding common areas. [RECALL — unverified on exact ordinance
name/year] If accurate, this is the most **directly transferable regulatory precedent** here: a
statute forcing one defined area basis onto advertised prices — the same idea as India's RERA 2016
carpet-area mandate (`partC-data.md`), independently corroborated as workable elsewhere in Asia.

## 6. Ranking as a template for an Indian registration-data product

1. **Taiwan 實價登錄** — best template for the *reform mechanism*: a statute overriding a
   tax-driven under-declaration incentive, address-masked but transaction-level, free, clean
   pre/post break; its disclosure-vs-tax-base distinction is the dossier's single most useful
   conceptual lesson for India's circle-rate options.
2. **Korea RTMS** — best template for *granularity plus a genuinely working free open API*, the
   longest clean post-reform history (2006-), and a mandatory-actual-price-reporting mechanism
   almost identical in spirit to what an Indian circle-rate reform would need to do.
3. **Japan MLIT Library** — best template for *methodological honesty and multi-concept
   reconciliation*: openly a survey, never confused with two coexisting appraisal-based benchmarks
   — a discipline India's registration/circle-rate/broker-price triad badly needs.
4. **Singapore HDB resale flat prices** — cleanest, purest administrative provenance, fully free,
   but generalizes only to India's much smaller public-housing stock; the private-market analogue
   that would generalize better (REALIS) is not free.
5. **Hong Kong** — weakest on free transaction microdata specifically; valuable to India mainly as
   a cautionary/complementary case (strong free aggregates and a strong area-disclosure statute,
   zero free bulk transaction data).

## 7. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE INDIA ENGINE

1. **Treat "free transaction-level API" and "transaction census" as different claims.** Japan's
   flagship product is a voluntary survey, not a registry extract — audit any future free Indian
   source's collection mechanism (survey vs. mandatory filing) before assuming complete coverage.
2. **Separate disclosure reform from tax-base reform as two distinct, independently testable
   levers**, per Taiwan's 2012 (disclosure) vs. 2016/2021 (房地合一稅, tax base) sequence. A future
   Indian circle-rate reform's effect on measured price dispersion and its effect on actual tax
   revenue should be modeled and tested separately, not assumed to move together.
3. **Always store and label the area basis explicitly, defaulting to the smallest/most-standardized
   concept (carpet-equivalent).** Korea's exclusive-area standardization, Hong Kong's 2013
   saleable-area ordinance, Taiwan's 虛坪/公設比 problem and Singapore's bay-window controversy are
   four independent instances of the same failure mode — the modal East Asian property-data bug,
   each eventually addressed by statute or engineering rather than left to buyer literacy.
4. **Coarsen location deliberately for privacy, but consistently and with a documented rule** —
   Japan's ward/district band, Taiwan's address-range mask, Singapore's storey-range band — rather
   than an ad hoc fuzz that would silently bias any later India-side geocoding attempt.
5. **A private, high-frequency brokerage index can legitimately beat a slower official series on
   timeliness** (Tokyo Kantei vs. MLIT; CCL vs. RVD) — treat Indian portals (magicbricks,
   housing.com) as a genuine complementary fast signal to registration/RERA data, provided their
   own coverage is separately audited, not as a lesser substitute.
6. **Official-vs-private index divergence is itself informative, not noise** — per the K-REB-vs-KB
   controversy, a persistent gap between a future India registration-based index and an existing
   private index (e.g., NHB RESIDEX vs. a portal series) should be treated as a finding to explain.
7. **"Free to search" and "free in bulk" are different deliverables** — Singapore's REALIS (paid
   bulk, free throttled search) and Hong Kong's Land Registry (free aggregates, fee-per-record
   microdata) show the more likely outcome for Indian IGR-style portals is "free aggregate +
   throttled single-record lookup," not a Korea-grade free bulk API — plan ingestion design around
   the pessimistic case.
8. **Every number, date, URL and statute name in this file is unverified** — this file's own state
   argues for a documented verification/confidence tier in the engine's source layer (mirroring
   this pack's `[2-SOURCE]/[1-SOURCE]/[RECALL]` discipline) rather than trusting any single
   ingested claim on first sight.
