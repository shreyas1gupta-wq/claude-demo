## 1. England & Wales: HM Land Registry Price Paid Data (PPD)

HM Land Registry publishes **Price Paid Data**: every residential sale **for full market value**
in England and Wales that has been lodged for registration, since **1 January 1995** [RECALL,
high confidence on scope and start date]. It is the closest real-world analogue to what India's
registration data could in principle become: address-level, transaction-by-transaction, free,
bulk-downloadable, sourced from the legal act of registering title rather than a survey or portal.

**Row count**: on the order of **~29-30 million rows** as of the mid-2020s, growing by roughly
1-1.2 million rows/year [RECALL — unverified, order-of-magnitude only, not a citable figure].

**Fields** (best recall): transaction ID; Price; Date of Transfer; Postcode; Property Type
(**D**/**S**/**T**/**F**/**O** — Detached/Semi/Terraced/Flat/Other; "O" is a known noise source
because some new-build flats get coded O rather than F [moderate confidence]); an **Old/New**
flag (Y=newly built, N=established — PPD's only vintage marker); **Duration** (F=Freehold,
L=Leasehold — no lease-length or ground-rent field at all, a recurring complaint given how much
that matters for London flats); PAON/SAON, Street, Locality, Town, District, County; a **PPD
Category Type** flag (**A**=standard transaction, **B**=residual "additional" data — repossessions,
buy-to-lets identifiable via lender data, transfers to non-private/corporate entities, and other
sales HMLR does not fully vouch for as clean market comparables [unverified on exact composition
— this A/B split is effectively HMLR's own confidence flag and the single most important PPD
caveat to re-verify]); and a Record Status flag (Addition/Change/Delete) for monthly updates.

**What it lacks / caveats**: (i) **no floor-area field at all** — the reason for the EPC-linkage
trick in §3; (ii) **commercial/non-residential property excluded entirely** — PPD is a
residential-only extract, and the separate commercial disclosure regime is far less complete
[low confidence on specifics]; (iii) **non-arm's-length transfers under-represented** — gifts and
nominal-consideration family transfers generally do not appear as priced market sales, and
Category B is where the ambiguous ones get parked; (iv) **registration lag** — because the
triggering event is registration, not completion, the most recent 1-3 months of any extract are
materially incomplete at first release and revise upward — the same mechanism driving UK HPI's
own revision behaviour (§2).

**Licence/access**: Crown copyright, **Open Government Licence v3.0** — free, no registration, no
API key, attribution-only. Download route recalled as CSV files on gov.uk's HM Land Registry
statistical-data-sets pages: a full combined file since 1995, year-by-year files, and a monthly
update file [unverified on exact current URL/packaging — do not cite it]. Bulk-downloadable, not
API-gated, not captcha-gated, not paywalled — the strongest access mode in this dossier.

## 2. The UK House Price Index (UK HPI)

A joint product of **HM Land Registry, Registers of Scotland, Land & Property Services NI, and
ONS**, unifying earlier separate ONS/Land Registry indices around **2016** [unverified year]. I
recall it as a **hedonic-regression, mix-adjusted index** — regressing price on characteristics
(property type, new/old, a floor-area proxy) each period so a shift in *what* sold doesn't read
as a price change [moderate-high confidence on the "hedonic" label; no confidence in the exact
functional form without the methodology paper].

**Geography**: down to **local authority district**, plus type/new-build/first-time-buyer splits
where sample size allows; thin local authorities are believed smoothed rather than reported raw
month to month.

**Revision behaviour — the operationally important fact**: because registration lags completion,
**the latest 1-2 published months are provisional and revise upward**. This is exactly why the UK
also runs fast **lender-based indices** (Halifax, Nationwide) off mortgage-approval data —
quicker, less revised, but missing cash buyers (a materially different population at both market
extremes). **Registry-based = complete but lagged/revised; lender-based = timely but
sample-limited.** This trade-off recurs, in different institutional clothes, in Norway and Sweden
(§9) and is a first-order design fact: a registration-based India index will inherit the UK-HPI
profile, not the Halifax profile.

## 3. The transferable trick: Price Paid Data linked to EPC for floor area / price-per-sqm

PPD has no size field. **Energy Performance Certificates (EPCs)** — mandatory in England/Wales on
sale, letting, or construction of most buildings since roughly 2007-2008 — carry a floor-area
field (total/gross internal area, sq m) plus efficiency rating, property type and age band, and
the EPC register itself is published as open data [unverified on exact domain/bulk-access terms].
Because both datasets carry address/postcode, they can be **linked by address matching** (fuzzy —
UK address strings vary in flat-numbering/building-name conventions) to attach a floor area to a
PPD row, yielding price-per-sqm directly. I recall ONS has published exactly this kind of linked
median-price-per-sqm-by-local-authority analysis [unverified — cannot confirm exact title or
current continuation].

**Caveats worth carrying to India**: an EPC is generated only on sale/let/construction, so
never-transacted stock is under-represented relative to the full housing stock even though PPD is
close to a full transaction census; floor-area conventions vary and are self-reported, unaudited;
address-match rates sit well below 100%, so a per-sqm series built this way is a **selected
sub-sample of PPD** with unclear bias by type/age. Structurally the same problem India would face
linking deeds (which typically state area directly — arguably better than the UK's two-dataset
need) to a valuation base; see §11.

## 4. The Valuation Office Agency (VOA): a universal-but-stale valuation layer

VOA values property for **council tax** (residential) and **business rates** (commercial), plus
other public-body valuations. Two structurally interesting facts: **council tax bands** in
England are based on a capital valuation as of **1 April 1991** (Wales revalued to 2003; Scotland
runs its own separate banded system) [unverified exact dates] — a **universal but badly stale**
value marker, structurally close to India's stamp-duty "circle rate"/"guidance value"/"ready
reckoner rate": universal, address/locality-level, periodically-but-rarely revised, publicly
notified. **Business rates** are revalued periodically (a 2023 revaluation recalled among the more
recent). VOA also historically supplied market-rent data setting **Local Housing Allowance** by
**Broad Rental Market Area**, and I recall underpinned the older ONS rental release before the
newer methodology in §5 [low confidence on the exact VOA-to-ONS data lineage].

## 5. ONS Private Rental Market Statistics and the Price Index of Private Rents (PIPR)

ONS has published rental statistics in at least two forms recalled: an older **Private Rental
Market Statistics** release (mean/median rents by region and bedroom count, historically leaning
on VOA-collected data), and a newer, more rigorous rental price index — recalled as the **Index of
Private Housing Rental Prices (IPHRP)**, later renamed/relaunched as the **Price Index of Private
Rents (PIPR)** around 2023 [unverified naming history and date]. Methodology recalled as broadly
analogous to UK HPI's hedonic/repeat approach; geography extends to region and, for some releases,
finer breakdowns. **The hardest part of any rental index anywhere, UK included, is the absence of
a universal rent registry** — unlike sales, most English/Welsh tenancies trigger no central
registration event, so the series leans on some mix of letting-agent/portal data, tenancy-deposit
scheme records, and/or VOA rent-officer data [unverified exact current input mix]. Directly
relevant to India, where a rent registry is even less developed than the UK's.

## 6. Scotland and Northern Ireland: the other two UK registers

**Registers of Scotland (RoS)** runs Scotland's Land Register (which progressively replaced the
older deed-based General Register of Sasines, transition believed complete by the mid-2010s
[unverified date]) and publishes its own transaction statistics (believed quarterly, by local
authority and property type) feeding the Scotland leg of UK HPI. **Northern Ireland** is run by
**Land & Property Services (LPS)**, jointly with **NISRA**, producing the **NI House Price Index**
on a **quarterly** cadence — materially lower frequency than the monthly England&Wales/Scotland
legs, meaning the "monthly" UK HPI figure for NI is effectively held over between quarters
[unverified — but if right, this matters a great deal for anyone treating "the UK HPI" as one
homogeneous-frequency benchmark]. One more interesting NI fact: domestic rates there are recalled
as based on **capital value** (a full per-property valuation, from around a 2005 valuation date)
rather than the banded council-tax system used elsewhere in the UK — a closer structural cousin of
the Netherlands' WOZ (§8) and of India's guidance value than anything else in the UK [unverified
exact dates; moderate confidence on the capital-value-not-bands structural fact].

## 7. Ireland: the Residential Property Price Register (RPPR) and the CSO RPPI

Ireland's **Residential Property Price Register**, recalled as administered by the **Property
Services Regulatory Authority (PSRA)**, publishes every residential sale in Ireland since
**1 January 2010** registered for **stamp duty** with the Revenue Commissioners — so, unlike PPD,
the originating legal event is a **tax filing**, not a title registration. Free, address-level,
searchable/downloadable without login [unverified current administering-body name/URL; fairly
confident on the ~2010 start]. Fields recalled: date, address, county, price, a "Not Full Market
Price" flag (RPPR's analogue to PPD's Category B), a VAT-exclusive/inclusive flag, and new/
second-hand description. **Eircodes** (Ireland's postcode, national from ~2015) and a **floor-area
size band** were both added as enhancements at some later point — if right, Ireland's own native
version of the UK's EPC-linkage workaround, done inside the register itself rather than via a
second dataset [unverified addition dates].

The **CSO Residential Property Price Index (RPPI)** is built from RPPR, recalled as monthly, with
a **Dublin vs. Ireland-excluding-Dublin** split and house-vs-apartment/new-vs-existing
breakdowns — a hedonic/mix-adjusted construction in the same family as UK HPI. Ireland's RPPR/RPPI
pairing is, on this recall, the **cleanest single template for an India analogue** here: a
tax-filing-triggered, free, address-level register — India's stamp-duty registration offices
already generate an almost identical originating event — feeding a national statistical agency's
official index.

## 8. Netherlands: Kadaster, the WOZ valuation, and the SPAR method

**Kadaster** records transactions because Dutch law requires transfers via **notarial deed**,
registered with Kadaster — near-universal, high-confidence coverage. Aggregate/statistical use is
believed free/open (feeding CBS statistics and the open buildings/addresses register, BAG), but
**individual deed extracts may carry a small per-record fee** through Kadaster's own online
service — Netherlands sits partway between PPD's free-bulk model and a purely commercial
per-record model [unverified fee amount/product name].

**WOZ (Wet waardering onroerende zaken)** requires municipalities to produce an **annual assessed
value for every property** (recalled ~8 million dwellings), used for local property tax, water-
board levies, and imputed rental value in income tax. Individual WOZ values became publicly
searchable by address via a national portal around **2016** [unverified year]. **This is the
single most important structural fact in this dossier for the India engine**: a universal,
annually-updated, address-level, publicly-disclosed government valuation — exactly the combination
India's guidance value/circle rate already approximates, but has never paired with the technique
below.

**The SPAR method (Sale Price Appraisal Ratio)**: recalled as CBS (Statistics Netherlands),
working with Kadaster, building its official price index by taking, for every sale, the ratio of
**sale price to that same property's most recent WOZ value**, and tracking the average/weighted
ratio over time. **The single most transferable idea in this entire assignment** — it needs no
repeat sales (unlike Case-Shiller-style indices) and no rich hedonic attribute set (unlike UK
HPI/RPPI), only (a) a transaction price and (b) an existing universal official valuation for that
same property. [RECALL — unverified; I recall an associated CBS/Kadaster-linked academic
literature on SPAR, possibly involving an author named de Vries among others, roughly 2000s-era,
but I am not confident enough in the author list/title/year to state it as a citation — flagged
as **[VERIFY: SPAR method original methodology paper, CBS/Kadaster-associated, authors/year
unconfirmed]**, not attributed further.] CBS statistics generally believed published via an open
**StatLine** portal under a permissive licence [unverified exact licence name].

**Why this matters for India, plainly**: India already has both SPAR ingredients — a registered
price at every stamp-duty deed, and a universal, periodically-revised guidance-value valuation for
essentially every parcel. What's missing is systematic pairing of the two into a
sale-price-to-guidance-value ratio index — reframing the core data problem from "we need repeat
sales of the same flat" (rare) to "we need the guidance value in force at each sale" — a
linkage problem, not a scarcity one.

## 9. Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Finland

**Denmark**: deeds registered digitally and, recalled, compulsorily through **Tinglysningsretten**
(the Danish Land Registration Court), e-registration (**e-tinglysning**) live since roughly 2009 —
one of the fastest, most complete official records here. **Statistics Denmark (Danmarks
Statistik)** publishes sale-price statistics via its open **StatBank** portal; **Boliga** and
**Boligsiden** are the well-known consumer portals with free individual sold-price look-up,
ultimately sourced from the same public register plus industry data [unverified exact table
names, e-tinglysning start year, and portal sourcing chain].

**Sweden**: **Lantmäteriet** (mapping/cadastral/land-registration authority) records title
transfers (**lagfart**) — authoritative but recalled to lag completion by weeks. **SCB (Statistics
Sweden)** publishes a **Fastighetsprisindex**, believed quarterly for houses, with municipality
detail. Faster private data: **Mäklarstatistik** (agent-association sold-price stats) is recalled
as quicker than the official series; **Booli** (recalled acquired by **Hemnet**) is the free
consumer sold-price look-up portal [institutional relationships among these three are the
weakest-confidence part of this section].

**Norway**: **Kartverket** operates the land register (**tinglysing**), also recalled to lag
completion. Because of this, Norway's fastest statistics — a monthly release associated with
**Eiendom Norge** (agents' association), **Eiendomsverdi AS** (data/analytics firm), and
**Finn.no** (listing portal) — are built from agent-reported completed sales rather than the
registry feed, and **SSB's** own index (**Boligprisindeksen**) is believed to lean on this same
faster data rather than wait on registration [unverified exact institutional split]. Same
completeness-vs-timeliness trade-off as UK §2, different institutional clothes.

**Finland**: structurally the most different of the four — most dwellings, especially apartments,
are held as **shares in a housing company** (**asunto-osakeyhtiö**), so a "sale" is legally a share
transfer, not a real-estate deed transfer. Transaction data has historically come from **mandatory
real-estate-agent reporting** to **Statistics Finland (Tilastokeskus)** rather than a land-registry
feed. The **National Land Survey of Finland (Maanmittauslaitos, NLS)** launched a digital
housing-company share register (**huoneistotietojärjestelmä, HTJ**) around **2019**, replacing
paper share certificates. Statistics Finland publishes "Prices of dwellings in housing companies,"
believed quarterly with a monthly preliminary figure [unverified exact HTJ year and cadence;
moderate-high confidence on the core share-based-tenure structural fact — a genuinely distinctive
feature, not one to assume maps onto the other three Nordics or onto India's direct-title model].

## 11. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE INDIA ENGINE

1. **The Dutch SPAR method, not a UK-style hedonic index, is the right first target for India.**
   India already has both ingredients — a registered price at every stamp-duty deed, and a
   universal, periodically-revised guidance-value valuation for essentially every parcel — and
   lacks only the systematic pairing of the two. No repeat sales, no rich attribute set needed.
   This should be the primary design candidate ahead of any hedonic or repeat-sales approach.

2. **Indian deeds likely already state a floor-area figure, an advantage over the UK.** The UK
   needed the EPC-linkage workaround only because PPD has no size field; if Indian deeds state
   built-up/carpet area directly (confirm from India-side dossiers), the engine can skip the UK's
   two-dataset problem for a per-sqft series — but must still solve the UK's other problem: which
   **area basis** is stated, since Indian deeds/builder documents are known to mix bases
   inconsistently, and this is exactly the 20-35% swing factor the brief flags.

3. **Build an explicit not-fully-reliable-comparable flag into any India transaction feed from day
   one.** Every register here marks a subset of sales as questionable (PPD Category A/B, Ireland's
   "Not Full Market Price"). India's data will contain an analogous population — related-party
   transfers, distress sales, consideration under-declared relative to guidance value — and should
   pre-register a flagging rule rather than discover the problem after the fact.

4. **Expect a registration-based India index to behave like UK HPI, not Halifax/Nationwide, on
   timeliness**: complete but lagged and revised upward for the most recent months, because
   registration trails the transaction by a variable interval. Any India backtest needs
   registration-lag/vintage-stamping discipline (Track P's `quant/pipeline` machinery is the
   natural consumer) rather than treating a "latest month" count as final.

5. **Norway/Sweden show a fallback if India's registration lag proves severe and state-variable**:
   run a faster, private, portal-sourced completed-transaction feed in parallel with the slower
   official register, understood explicitly as a timeliness/completeness trade-off. India's
   private portals are the candidate analogue, with the key caveat that they typically show
   **asking prices**, not registered/closed prices — a materially more optimistic population than
   Norway's or Sweden's agent-reported *completed*-sale feeds.

6. **A universal-but-stale banded valuation (UK council tax bands, NI capital value, WOZ) is
   structurally the same instrument as India's guidance value/circle rate** — "how far behind
   market reality the official valuation runs, and how it revises" is a directly comparable,
   citable cross-country question, not a UK/NL-specific curiosity. The guidance-value-to-price gap
   is itself a candidate India state variable, distinct from the price level.

7. **Rental data is the weakest link in every country here, India included.** No country in this
   dossier has a UK-HPI-quality rental register; all lean on administrative proxies because
   tenancies are not a registrable legal event the way sales are. India will not get a
   registration-quality rental index; the yield/rent leg of the engine should be designed around
   a deliberately lower evidentiary bar than the sale-price leg, not discover the asymmetry later.

8. **This dossier is itself a demonstration of an operational failure mode the engine must design
   around: shared-resource exhaustion mid-pipeline.** Every claim above is RECALL-only purely
   because WebSearch calls ran out before this dossier's turn — mundane, foreseeable, not
   topic-specific. Any India engine depending on live scraping/API pulls (state registration
   portals, RERA sites, NGDRS) should assume the same class of failure recurs operationally and
   should checkpoint/preserve partial results from the outset, exactly as this desk's vault/
   manifest process already does for data it can reach.
