## 1. RBI House Price Index (HPI)

The Reserve Bank of India publishes a quarterly all-India House Price Index and city-level indices. [RECALL — unverified] My recollection is that the index is built from **transaction-level data supplied by the registration authorities (Stamps and Registration Departments) of state governments** — i.e., actual recorded sale-deed transactions, not asking prices or valuer opinions. This is the single most important fact in this dossier for the India engine's purposes: it is the only official series that is genuinely transaction-based rather than survey-, valuation-, or loan-based, which is exactly the construction a price-prediction engine would want to anchor against.

**City coverage.** [RECALL — unverified, moderate confidence] I recall the panel covers ten cities: Mumbai, Delhi, Chennai, Kolkata, Bengaluru, Lucknow, Ahmedabad, Jaipur, Kanpur and Kochi. This list should be treated as a first guess to confirm, not a settled fact — I am not fully confident all ten names or their being exactly this set is right, and RBI has periodically discussed (without my being sure it acted on) expanding the panel.

**Base year and start.** [RECALL — unverified, low-moderate confidence] I recall a base of 2010-11 = 100, with the underlying quarterly series running back to roughly 2008-09 (Q4). RBI may have rebased since; do not treat this base year as current without confirmation.

**Publication cadence and lag.** [RECALL — unverified] Published quarterly, as a standalone RBI press release ("House Price Index") a few months after quarter-end — my recollection is a lag on the order of one quarter (e.g., Q1 numbers appearing around the start of Q3), but I do not have a confident exact day-count.

**Revision behaviour — the crucial operational fact.** [RECALL — unverified but high plausibility given the source mechanism] Because the index depends on registration filings flowing in from state Sub-Registrar offices, and registration of a sale deed can lag the underlying transaction/agreement by weeks to months, and different states' registration authorities transmit data to RBI on different cadences, the most recent 1-4 quarters of the published series should be expected to be **provisional and subject to upward revision** as later-arriving registrations backfill the transaction count for those quarters. I could not confirm RBI's exact stated revision policy (whether it marks quarters "P" for provisional, or how many quarters remain open), but the mechanism (registration-lag-driven backfill) is inherent to any registration-sourced series and should be assumed true until checked. **For engine design: never treat the latest 2-3 published quarters of RBI HPI as final; either lag your own use of it by a couple of quarters or explicitly model the backfill.**

**Access.** [RECALL — unverified] RBI publishes HPI through two channels: (i) a quarterly press release (PDF, with a data table) on rbi.org.in, and (ii) the DBIE portal (Database on Indian Economy, at a domain I recall as dbie.rbi.org.in) under a Real Sector / Prices statistics category, where the series is downloadable as CSV/XLS through the portal's table-browser UI. I do **not** have confident recall of the exact DBIE menu path or table/serial number, and I am explicitly not going to invent one — a fabricated table number would be worse than none. I also do not recall DBIE exposing a programmatic REST API for this series; my best guess is UI-driven export only (no API), but this needs confirmation. Geography: city-level plus an all-India composite; no sub-city (locality/ward) granularity in this series as far as I recall — that is a structural ceiling for a builder wanting hyperlocal India price signals.

## 2. RBI Residential Asset Price Monitoring Survey (RAPMS)

[RECALL — unverified, lower confidence than the HPI section] RAPMS is a survey RBI runs by collecting **loan-level data from scheduled commercial banks and, I believe, housing finance companies (HFCs)** on residential property financed through housing loans — capturing, at minimum, the loan amount, the assessed/transacted property value at sanction, and hence the loan-to-value (LTV) ratio, city location, and plausibly EMI-to-income or similar affordability ratios collected at underwriting. This is a fundamentally different construction from the HPI: it is a **valuation-at-loan-sanction** series (bank/HFC appraised or transacted value tied to a financed purchase), not a registration-transaction series, and it only covers the financed (mortgaged) segment of the market — cash purchases and loans from unregulated/informal lenders are invisible to it.

**Publication form.** [RECALL — unverified] My recollection is that RAPMS results surface mainly as **aggregated charts/boxes inside RBI's twice-yearly Financial Stability Report (FSR)** — e.g., all-India or city-grouped average LTV ratio trends over time — rather than as a standalone downloadable time series, and I do not believe RBI publishes loan-level RAPMS microdata publicly (that would be confidential supervisory data by nature). I am not confident whether a separate, citable RAPMS-derived house-price/valuation index (sometimes referenced in commentary as a "valuation-based" HPI companion to the registration-based HPI) is published on its own cadence outside the FSR, or whether such references I'm recalling are actually just describing the same FSR boxes. **This entire sub-section needs a dedicated search pass**: confirm (a) whether RAPMS produces any public time series at all beyond FSR chart snapshots, (b) survey frequency, (c) city list and count of reporting institutions, (d) whether "property price" in RAPMS means transacted price or bank-assessed price (these frequently diverge, and the gap itself would be a usable feature for the engine if published).

## 3. NHB RESIDEX — original (2007) and relaunch (2017-18)

**Original RESIDEX (2007).** [RECALL — unverified] Launched by the National Housing Bank in 2007 as India's first official residential house price index. I recall it started with a small city set (on the order of 5 cities) and was later expanded; a commonly cited eventual figure is **26 cities**, built from an "assessed price" concept — property valuations gathered from a mix of banks/HFCs, registration/valuation authorities, and possibly builder/developer inputs, rather than pure open-market transaction prices. [RECALL — unverified] My understanding is that this original RESIDEX suffered credibility and timeliness problems (infrequent updates, inconsistent city coverage, data-quality concerns) and was effectively **discontinued/suspended** for a period before being redesigned — I recall this happening somewhere in the low-to-mid 2010s, but I don't have a confident specific year.

**Relaunch (2017-18).** [RECALL — unverified] NHB relaunched RESIDEX with a new methodology, expanding to a much larger city panel — I recall **50 cities** — and splitting the product into two parallel indices with different source data:
- **HPI@Assessment Prices**: built from property valuation data reported by banks/HFCs at the time of home-loan sanction (conceptually close to what RAPMS captures on the RBI side) — the easier data source to scale across many cities because it rides on existing loan-processing data flows.
- **HPI@Market Prices**: built from actual transacted/market prices gathered via primary market surveys — resource-intensive to run, so [RECALL — unverified] I recall this leg launching for a smaller subset of the 50 cities (a figure on the order of ~18 cities sticks in memory, but treat that number as a guess, not a citation).

**Base year.** [RECALL — unverified, low confidence] I have a weak recollection of the relaunch base being anchored to FY2017-18 (e.g., "2017-18 = 100" or a similar recent base), but I am not confident enough to assert an exact quarter/base value, and there may since have been a further rebasing I am not aware of (my training data cutoff is January 2026, and this program is dated September 2026 — an intervening rebasing is entirely plausible and should be checked first).

**Access.** [RECALL — unverified] I recall NHB runs a dedicated RESIDEX web tool (a domain along the lines of residex.nhbonline.org.in, or a RESIDEX section under nhb.org.in) where a user selects a city and quarter and the tool renders the index value/table, with an Excel/CSV export option per query; I do not recall a public API. Geography: city-level only (no intra-city zone/locality breakdown that I can confirm, though the original 2007 version experimented with sub-city zones in some cities — unverified). **Area basis is a live risk here**: "assessed price" indices in India are typically anchored to whatever area convention the reporting bank/HFC used for its own loan-to-value calculation, which in Indian retail mortgage practice is very commonly **built-up or super-built-up area**, not carpet area — and this convention is not guaranteed to be uniform across the reporting banks/HFCs feeding RESIDEX, let alone comparable to the sale-deed stated area used in RBI's transaction-based HPI. Any model blending RESIDEX and RBI-HPI levels without adjusting for this is at real risk of a spurious 20-35% basis wedge, exactly the failure mode flagged in this program's constraints.

## 4. MoSPI CPI — Housing sub-index

The Consumer Price Index (Combined), base 2012=100, published monthly by MoSPI's National Statistical Office (NSO), contains a **Housing** group — but [RECALL — unverified, moderate-high confidence] this group is computed **for urban areas only**; there is no rural housing sub-index in CPI (rural CPI weights are typically shown with Housing weight = 0, reflecting a methodological choice that owner-occupied rural housing's imputed cost is not captured the way urban rents are). This is precisely why the brief is right to insist this is a rent index, not a price (capital-value) index: the Housing group is constructed from **actual reported house rents** collected from a sample of rented dwellings in the urban price-collection centres, tracked over time — it captures what a representative urban renter pays, and says nothing directly about sale/asset prices, though rent and capital value are of course economically linked (via cap rates) in ways an engine could try to exploit indirectly.

**Weight.** [RECALL — unverified, moderate confidence] My recollection of the 2012-base CPI weighting diagram is that Housing carries roughly a **21% weight within CPI-Urban** and, because it is zero-weighted in rural and the urban/rural combination weight for the overall "Combined" index is roughly 40-45% urban, works out to roughly a **10% weight in CPI-Combined**. Treat both percentages as approximate recollections to confirm, not exact figures to publish.

**Cadence/lag/revision.** [RECALL — unverified] Monthly release, with recent-month data initially released as provisional figures and revised (typically once) in the following month's release alongside the new provisional month — a standard "provisional-then-final" CPI revision pattern, though I do not have confident recall of MoSPI's exact revision-window policy for CPI specifically. **Geography**: state/UT and all-India, urban/rural/combined splits, and by item-group; I do not believe city-level Housing sub-index values are separately published at the same granularity as RBI's HPI cities (CPI centres are numerous but the published Housing index is typically at state/all-India level, not per-city) — this is a real granularity ceiling relative to what the brief wants. **Access**: monthly press release PDF from mospi.gov.in, with the underlying group/sub-group indices also available as downloadable tables; I recall MoSPI or its NSO wing also mirrors CPI series onto data.gov.in in CSV form, and data.gov.in in general exposes a registered-API-key REST interface for many of its mirrored datasets — but I cannot confirm CPI-Housing specifically is one of the API-exposed series without a search pass.

## 5. Labour Bureau CPI-IW House Rent Index

The Labour Bureau (under the Ministry of Labour & Employment, a different agency from MoSPI/NSO) separately compiles the **Consumer Price Index for Industrial Workers (CPI-IW)**, current base [RECALL — unverified] 2016=100 (succeeding an older 2001=100 series), released monthly. It has its own Housing group, built from a **House Rent Index (HRI)** collected across a panel of industrial centres. [RECALL — unverified, low-moderate confidence on the exact count] I recall the centre count is in the high-70s to high-80s (a figure like 78 or 88 centres surfaces in memory; I am not confident which, or whether it changed with the 2016 rebasing from a smaller count under the 2001 series).

**A key operational quirk worth flagging**: house rents are collected by Labour Bureau **only periodically via dedicated house-rent surveys** (rents do not need monthly re-enumeration the way a vegetable price does), and the HRI is then **interpolated/held between survey rounds** and combined into the monthly all-item CPI-IW. [RECALL — unverified] I do not have confident recall of the survey periodicity (my weak guess is roughly annual or biennial rent collection rounds, interpolated monthly in between) — this matters a great deal for anyone using CPI-IW housing as a "live" rent signal: for most months the housing print may be a mechanical interpolation, not a fresh observation, and the true information content arrives in discrete jumps at survey dates. **Access**: monthly PDF press notes from labourbureau.gov.in with index tables by centre and by group; I do not recall an API; some mirroring to data.gov.in is plausible but unconfirmed. Geography: centre-level (the ~80-ish industrial centres, which are specific towns/localities tied to labour-intensive industry clusters, not a general urban sample) plus state and all-India aggregates — note this is a working-class/industrial-labour cost-of-living index by design (it also drives dearness allowance calculations), so its Housing/rent basket is not representative of the broader urban housing market an India-wide price engine would care about; it is a useful triangulation input, not a primary series.

## 6. MHUA / PMAY-U data

The Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (MHUA) runs Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana – Urban (PMAY-U), and maintains a public MIS/dashboard (I recall the domain as pmay-urban.gov.in) reporting scheme **physical and financial progress**: houses sanctioned, grounded (construction started), and completed/occupied, broken out by state/UT and by the scheme's four verticals (In-Situ Slum Redevelopment, Affordable Housing in Partnership, Beneficiary-Led Construction, Credit-Linked Subsidy Scheme). [RECALL — unverified] This is **administrative/scheme data, not a price series** — its value to a price-prediction engine is indirect: as a proxy for affordable-housing supply flow by state/city over time, and as a demand-side signal (subsidy disbursement, beneficiary counts) rather than a price observation.

**Important caveat for the vault's WORM/point-in-time discipline**: [RECALL — unverified but structurally near-certain] a live government MIS dashboard of this kind is a **continuously-updated administrative database**, not an immutably published statistical release — the same "as of" query run on two different days can return different historical figures as backlog entries get updated/corrected in the underlying MIS. Any ingestion of PMAY-U data into this program's vault needs to snapshot-and-hash a specific pull date (per the desk's existing manifest discipline) rather than treat the numbers as a stable point-in-time series the way a press-release PDF is. Geography: state/UT, and I believe project/scheme-level detail is at least partially exposed (individual project records), which would be unusually fine relative to everything else in this dossier — unconfirmed. Access: dashboard UI with downloadable summary reports (PDF/PPT-style progress reports are what I recall MHUA regularly circulating); I do not have confident recall of a bulk CSV export or API for the underlying project-level MIS.

## 7. Census of India housing tables

The decennial Census's House-listing and Housing Census (the "H-series" tables) is the finest-grained official housing-stock dataset that exists for India: village- and ward-level detail on number of census houses, their use, construction material of roof/wall/floor, number of dwelling rooms, tenurial status (owned/rented/any other), household amenities (drinking water source, electricity, latrine, drainage), and a subjective condition classification of the structure (good/livable/dilapidated). [RECALL — high confidence on structure, unverified by search] This is stock/condition data, not a price series, but it is the closest thing to a nationwide, exhaustive (not sampled) housing-stock baseline, at a geography no other source in this dossier reaches (down to village/ward).

**The critical, program-relevant fact**: Census 2011 is the most recent completed Census; **Census 2021 was postponed** (originally attributed to the pandemic) and, as of this dossier's knowledge cutoff, I am not aware it has been conducted on any revised schedule — meaning the housing-stock and condition tables an India engine might reach for are, as of 2026, **roughly 15 years stale**, a structural gap no other series in this dossier fills at the same geographic granularity. [RECALL — unverified, this status needs an explicit search check: has Census 2021/2027 happened, been scheduled, or released any Housing tables since?] Access: censusindia.gov.in, tables downloadable as XLS/PDF per state; no API that I recall; no revision (a decennial snapshot is fixed once released, though enumeration/classification methodology can change release-to-release in ways that break strict comparability).

## 9. NHB Report on Trend and Progress of Housing in India

[RECALL — unverified] NHB publishes an **annual** "Report on Trend and Progress of Housing in India" (a mandate I recall arising from the National Housing Bank Act), broadly analogous in spirit to RBI's own annual Trend and Progress of Banking report. Content, from recollection: sector overview and policy developments, **HFC-level statistics** (resource mobilisation, loan disbursements/outstanding, asset quality/NPAs across NHB-regulated housing finance companies), and **state-wise** data on housing investment/credit flow and possibly housing shortage estimates. This is a **free PDF** on nhb.org.in (consistent with the brief's description), each edition a point-in-time annual publication (no revision after release, though a later year's edition may restate/update prior-year figures in its own comparison tables — worth cross-checking multi-year editions against each other rather than trusting a single year's retrospective columns). Access: PDF only as far as I recall, with statistical annex tables that may or may not be separately available as Excel; no API. This is a genuinely useful, under-exploited source for the India engine's *credit-supply* side (HFC disbursement growth, NPA stress) as a leading/coincident indicator alongside whatever price series the engine ultimately builds on, since housing-credit growth and house-price cycles are mechanically linked (this desk's own DB-battery and CN-programme findings on debt/credit and asset-price cycles are the natural cross-reference, though those were run on JST cross-country data, not this India-specific HFC series).

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE INDIA ENGINE

1. **Treat RBI HPI as the anchor series, not RESIDEX** — it is the only one of these built from actual registered transactions rather than valuations/surveys, but its last 2-3 published quarters must be discounted or lagged for registration-backfill revision before being trusted as "final."
2. **Never blend RESIDEX (assessment) and RBI HPI (transaction) levels without an explicit area-basis and construction-basis adjustment** — one is bank-valuation-at-sanction on a probably-built-up-area convention, the other is sale-deed transaction value on whatever area the deed states; a naive merge risks a spurious 20-35% wedge that will look like a real regime break in a model.
3. **CPI Housing (MoSPI) and CPI-IW Housing (Labour Bureau) are rent proxies, not price proxies** — usable only to construct an implied cap-rate / price-to-rent framework alongside a price series, never as a substitute for one, and CPI-IW's rent input is likely a periodic survey held constant between rounds, not a live monthly signal.
4. **PMAY-U is a live administrative MIS, not a citable point-in-time release** — if ingested, it must be snapshotted and hashed on a specific pull date per this program's WORM manifest rule; do not treat two pulls on different dates as comparable without checking for MIS backfill.
5. **Census housing-stock data is structurally stale** (2011 vintage, 2021 round status uncertain) — do not lean on it for anything current-cycle; its value is as a slow-moving stock baseline / cross-check, and its staleness should itself be logged as a named data gap on the RUNSHEET, the same way this program already tracks the NSE-sectoral-TR gap.
6. **RAPMS may not exist as a citable public time series at all beyond FSR chart snapshots** — before designing anything around it, a dedicated verification pass must establish whether a downloadable RAPMS series exists, or whether the engine should instead treat FSR LTV-ratio charts as periodic qualitative readings only.
7. **None of these nine sources has a confirmed public API** — every one is, at best, a UI-driven bulk CSV/XLS export or a static PDF; the engine's ingestion layer should assume scheduled scrape-and-manifest jobs against fixed publication calendars, not live API polling, consistent with how this program already treats RBI/NHB-style sources elsewhere in the vault.
