## 1. The problem

An India ward/locality index must be built where a locality sees **tens of transactions per
quarter**, not thousands. Every classical method below was designed for national/metro markets
with orders of magnitude more liquidity and degrades in a specific way as N falls. The question
is which failure mode is least damaging at India's actual sizes, and how to borrow statistical
strength across space/time to survive them.

## 2. Repeat-sales methods

**Bailey, Muth & Nourse (1963)**, *Journal of the American Statistical Association* [RECALL —
unverified], introduced the BMN estimator: take only properties that sold twice, regress the log
price-relative on time dummies spanning the interval between sales. Differencing two sales of the
*same unit* nets out unobserved, time-invariant quality without ever measuring characteristics.

**Case & Shiller (1987 working paper / 1989 *American Economic Review*, "The Efficiency of the
Market for Single-Family Homes")** [RECALL — unverified exact year/venue split] extended BMN into
the three-stage **Weighted Repeat Sales (WRS)** procedure underlying the S&P CoreLogic Case-
Shiller Index: OLS BMN regression → regress squared residuals on time-between-sales (noise grows
with holding period) → re-run by GLS using the fitted weights.

**Failure modes, all binding on a thin Indian locality:**
- **Sample-selection bias** [RECALL — unverified]: the repeat-sold sample over-represents
  flipped/renovated/distressed units and under-represents buy-and-hold and top-end stock — it
  answers "how did prices move for homes that resell," not "how did the locality move."
- **Revision problem**: a fresh third sale re-anchors and revises past published values — never
  fully final, bad for a stable backtestable series.
- **Renovation/quality-drift contamination**: any change between the two sales (floor added,
  society upgraded, unauthorized extension regularized — common in Indian resale stock) breaks
  the "same unit" assumption with no way to detect it.
- **Small-N starvation, the binding one here**: repeat pairs are a subset of an already-small
  count. At ~30 total sales/quarter in a ward, the repeat-sold subset within-window is plausibly
  near zero — this is why the method is used at metro level or above, never at ward granularity.

**Clapp & Giaccotto (1992)**, I recall in *JASA*, "Estimating Price Indices for Residential
Property: A Comparison of Repeat Sales and Assessed Value Methods" [RECALL — unverified, low-
moderate confidence on exact citation] — the bridge to SPAR (Section 6). Where an **official
assessed value exists for every property** (not just resold ones), sale-price/assessed-value
ratios give a far larger effective sample without needing a repeat match. They found the
assessed-value method tracked repeat-sales closely while avoiding some idiosyncratic small-sample
noise, bounded by the assessment roll's *currency* — a stale assessment reimports repeat-sales'
lag/bias problem into the denominator. This currency caveat is the single most transferable
lesson for India, where circle rates/ready reckoner rates are revised far less regularly than the
US property-tax context Clapp & Giaccotto studied (Sections 6, 10).

## 3. Hedonic regression

**Rosen (1974)**, *Journal of Political Economy*, "Hedonic Prices and Implicit Markets: Product
Differentiation in Pure Competition" [RECALL — unverified citation particulars, high confidence
on substance] — a differentiated good's price is a function of its characteristics bundle, and
implicit/shadow prices are recoverable by regressing price on that bundle.

A survey I recall as **Hill, "Hedonic Price Indexes for Housing"** (possibly an OECD working
paper, later a survey, possibly *Journal of Economic Surveys* ~2011-2013) [RECALL — unverified
venue/year] taxonomizes three families: **(i) time-dummy hedonic** — one pooled regression with
period fixed effects, assuming shadow prices are constant through time (parsimonious, low data
need, but rigid); **(ii) hedonic imputation** — a fresh cross-section regression each period,
pricing a fixed representative bundle, ratio-chained (flexible, but needs enough transactions
*per period* to identify a regression); **(iii)** average-characteristics/re-weighted variants
in between.

**Failure mode in a thin locality**: running imputation-style hedonics separately per ward per
quarter is exactly the demanding case — with "tens" of transactions and a characteristics vector
including size, floor, age, amenities, road width, transit distance, and (critically) **area
basis** (Section 10), degrees of freedom run out fast, correlated characteristics create severe
multicollinearity, and a rare dummy (one gated-community sale) can perfectly separate a small
sample. The standard compromise: estimate characteristics' shadow prices on a **pooled larger
geography** (city/zone-wide) and let only a **ward-level intercept** vary — the logic behind
hybrid methods (Section 4) and small-area shrinkage (Section 8).

## 4. Hybrid methods

**Quigley (1995)** — "A Simple Hybrid Model for Estimating Real Estate Price Indexes," possibly
*Journal of Real Estate Finance and Economics* or *Journal of Housing Economics* [RECALL —
unverified venue] — combined a hedonic run on **all** transactions (repeat and single-sold) with
a repeat-sales time effect on the repeat-sold subsample: the large single-sale sample disciplines
characteristics pricing while repeat-sales differencing still quality-checks the time effect. A
related Case-and-coauthors hybrid paper from around the same period is often cited alongside it
(possibly Case, Pollakowski & Wachter, or a Case & Quigley pairing) — I hold genuine uncertainty
on the correct author set/year and explicitly decline to assert a specific citation I can't back.
[RECALL — unverified]

**Relevance to India**: hybrid is the right family given the actual constraint — repeat-sales
alone starves at ward level, independent per-ward hedonics starve too — a hybrid pooling hedonic
pricing citywide while using whatever repeat pairs exist as a secondary check uses all the sparse
information generated, discarding neither the single-sale majority nor the pooling opportunity.

## 5. Median, mix-adjusted median, and how composition corruption happens

A raw **median/mean** is robust to a single outlier but **not** to a shift in the *mix* of what
transacted. If larger/higher-tier units transact more in one quarter with zero underlying
appreciation, the median rises anyway — composition mistaken for price change. **Mix-adjustment/
stratified median** strata by type/size-band/sub-tier, takes medians within strata, and
recombines with **fixed** weights (stock shares or base-period mix), not the current period's own
mix.

I recall the UK's official House Price Index (ONS/HM Land Registry) historically used mix-
adjustment before migrating toward a hedonic-regression methodology (I believe mid-2010s), and
Australia's ABS Residential Property Price Index using a **stratified median** (sub-region ×
dwelling type), with private Australian providers (CoreLogic/former RP Data/Residex lineage)
instead running hedonic "hybrid" models. [RECALL — unverified, moderate confidence; exact
transition dates and current 2026 methodology need a check I cannot run.]

**A concrete, desk-verified illustration of this exact failure, from this program's own work
[internal, first-party]**: this desk's China Property Crash Atlas (row 64, CN-D programme) found
China's national land-price series fell only **~-23% from a 2023 peak**, materially shallower
than land-transaction *volume* (**-66%**) or *revenue* (**-52.3%**) over the same crash — flagged
in that atlas explicitly as **"a composition artifact"**: as distressed, lower-tier parcels
stopped transacting, the surviving mix tilted toward higher-quality land whose prices held up
better, flattering the average even as like-for-like value fell further. Same mechanism as a
locality median moving on mix, at national scale, in the crash direction — the clearest internal
proof that an **unadjusted median/average is not safe** for the India engine, since Indian ward
markets are thin enough for exactly this adverse-selection mix shift quarter to quarter.

## 6. SPAR — Sale Price Appraisal Ratio (the method of choice for India's circle-rate situation)

**Mechanics**: for each transaction i in period t, r_i,t = SalePrice_i,t / AssessedValue_i (fixed
at a base date); aggregate ratios across period-t sales for the index level. Since the assessed
value exists for the **entire stock**, not just transacted units, SPAR needs no repeat match, and
gets automatic per-property quality control (the ratio nets out size/location/quality already
encoded in the assessment), with in-principle full-stock aggregation.

**Origin/evidence**: I recall **Statistics Netherlands/Kadaster** as the primary adopter, using
the Dutch **WOZ** annual property-tax valuation (notably revised annually — the precondition that
makes SPAR work well there) as denominator. Reference I recall: **de Vries, de Haan, van der Wal
& Mariën**, *Journal of Housing Economics*, ~**2009**, "A House Price Index Based on the SPAR
Method" [RECALL — unverified, moderate confidence] — establishing SPAR's ratio-estimator
properties and finding it tracks a full hedonic index closely at far lower cost, conditional on
the assessed value being current and unbiased. I also recall **New Zealand** (Quotable Value /
council rating "CV," periodically revalued, feeding REINZ/QV statistics) running a SPAR-like
approach, at lower confidence on exact current arrangement. [RECALL — unverified]

**Failure modes**: (1) **assessment staleness is the whole ballgame** — if assessments are
revised every 3-10 years or, as in much of India, on no fixed schedule, the denominator stops
moving while transaction prices keep moving, reimporting the repeat-sales vintage-drift problem
via assessment-date heterogeneity; a workable India SPAR needs an explicit assessment-vintage
control. (2) **Circularity risk** if assessed values were themselves partly set from comparable
transactions. (3) **Record-linkage, not the math, is the real bottleneck** — SPAR needs matching
each transaction to its assessment-roll entry, which without a unified parcel ID or clean
property register (much of India) is itself the primary engineering problem, the RPPI Handbook's
own first-order practical constraint on assessed-value methods (Section 7).

**Why this is the right template for India, in depth as instructed**: circle rate / ready
reckoner rate / guidance value (Maharashtra's Ready Reckoner, Karnataka's guidance value, the
generic "circle rate" elsewhere) is functionally an assessed value existing, at least nominally,
for essentially every registrable parcel — and it is already the denominator every stamp-duty
registration is implicitly checked against. **This means the raw SPAR ingredient already exists,
at the individual-transaction level, inside every state's Inspector-General-of-Registration (IGR)
stamp-duty database** — reframing "how do we index a ward with only tens of transactions" into
"how do we get transaction-level IGR data matched to the circle-rate schedule cell," a data-access
and record-linkage problem, arguably more tractable than a pure small-sample statistics one. The
one large India-specific complication with no Netherlands/NZ analogue: **under-invoicing** —
declared prices have historically clustered at/near the circle-rate floor with a cash component
absorbing the gap, especially pre-RERA/pre-demonetization — so the ratio's *level* is partly a
compliance/enforcement signal (shifting discretely with RERA 2016, demonetization, later
tightening) rather than pure market pricing, and must be tracked **separately**, never folded
silently into the price index. [RECALL — unverified institutional/enforcement detail; the general
under-invoicing phenomenon I hold at moderate confidence, no specific magnitude asserted.]

## 7. The RPPI Handbook (Eurostat/ILO/IMF/OECD/UN/World Bank, ~2013)

I recall the **"Handbook on Residential Property Price Indices"** as the standard multi-agency
reference (Eurostat, ILO, IMF, OECD, UN, World Bank, coordinated via the Inter-Secretariat
Working Group on Price Statistics, ~2013) [RECALL — unverified exact body list/year]. As I recall
its guidance: (a) unadjusted median/mean should never be the sole method; stratification is close
to a floor; (b) hedonic imputation is preferred with rich-enough per-period data, time-dummy
hedonic an acceptable fallback when too thin to re-estimate each period; (c) repeat-sales is a
supplementary cross-check, not a sole method, given selection/noise problems, best in large
liquid single-family markets; (d) **SPAR is explicitly endorsed as low-data-cost where a
comprehensive, current assessed-value register exists** — close to the ideal case for a country
with valuation rolls but thin/heterogeneous transactions, arguably the closest published
international template to India's situation; (e) at fine spatial resolution, aggregation or
hierarchical smoothing is recommended over publishing noisy small cells directly, with explicit
minimum-sample thresholds for suppression/roll-up. [RECALL — unverified throughout; low-to-
moderate confidence on exact wording, flagged for re-check before any quotation in a published
page.]

## 8. Small-area estimation and hierarchical Bayesian shrinkage

**Fay & Herriot (1979)**, *JASA*, "Estimates of Income for Small Places: An Application of
James-Stein Procedures to Census Data" [RECALL — unverified particulars, higher confidence on
substance as a widely-cited SAE paper] — the right mathematical object here despite being built
for small-area income. Two layers: a **sampling model** (direct small-area estimate = true value
+ area-specific sampling error) and a **linking model** (true value = regression on covariates +
area random effect). The combined empirical-Bayes/James-Stein estimator **shrinks the noisy
direct locality estimate toward the model-based prediction**, weighted by between-area variance
over total variance — wards with fewer transactions (higher sampling variance) get shrunk harder
toward the city/zone mean, exactly the behavior needed here.

I recall hierarchical/multilevel Bayesian partial-pooling has been applied to fine-geography
(ZIP/postcode) house-price indices in some academic/agency work — mathematically Fay-Herriot in
spirit — but cannot recall one specific citable paper with confidence and decline to invent one.
I recall, only generally, that **Zillow's ZHVI** applies geographic/temporal smoothing at fine
ZIP/neighborhood granularity for this exact reason (raw small-area values too noisy). [RECALL —
unverified both] — recommend the build/synthesis agent search "hierarchical Bayes house price
index small area estimation" and "Zillow ZHVI methodology" directly rather than trust recall here.

## 9. Spatio-temporal and spatial-econometric methods

I recall **Pace and collaborators** (often cited as **Pace & Gilley**, survey work sometimes
cited as **Pace, Barry & Sirmans**, late 1990s-2000s) [RECALL — unverified particulars]
developing **spatial autoregressive (SAR)**/spatial-error hedonic extensions: residuals are
spatially autocorrelated (shared unobserved micro-location amenities), so a spatial-lag/error
term from a distance/nearest-neighbor weights matrix W improves efficiency and cuts
omitted-spatial-variable bias. **Geographically Weighted Regression (GWR)** — core reference I
recall as **Fotheringham, Brunsdon & Charlton**, ~2002 book, *Geographically Weighted Regression:
The Analysis of Spatially Varying Relationships* [RECALL — unverified particulars] — lets hedonic
coefficients vary smoothly over space via locally-weighted regression with a kernel bandwidth.

**Relevance**: both are strength-borrowing devices, letting a ward's price level/coefficients be
informed by nearby wards via distance-decay weights rather than requiring enough transactions
within the ward alone — the same goal as Fay-Herriot shrinkage, implemented geographically.
**India-specific failure modes**: (a) need reliable point-location/ward-polygon data per
transaction — parcel geocoding quality is itself a serious separate India problem; (b) the
weights-matrix specification and GWR bandwidth are free parameters needing pre-registration with
stated rationale (this desk's no-magic-numbers rule applies directly — an un-pre-registered
bandwidth is exactly the researcher-degrees-of-freedom problem the process notes guard against);
(c) computational cost scales unfavorably with panel size, a lesser concern than (a).

## 10. Minimum-sample rules of thumb, and the area-basis warning

I hold only low confidence on any specific agency's minimum-cell-size number without checking its
current methodology, and decline to state one as fact. The general logic I can state with
confidence: a mean/median/ratio's standard error shrinks ~1/sqrt(n), so at n in the tens a
locality-quarter figure has a wide confidence interval, and standard agency responses are (a)
widen the window (annual/rolling instead of quarterly), (b) aggregate adjacent localities until N
clears a threshold, or (c) apply the Section 8 shrinkage logic rather than publish raw small
cells. [RECALL — unverified as to any agency's exact stated threshold.] **Treat "what N-threshold
do ONS/ABS/Statistics-NZ/Statistics-Netherlands actually publish" as an open, specifically
searchable question**, not inferable from this dossier.

**Area basis, restated as a hard warning**: carpet, built-up, and super-built-up area are
systematically different denominators; the carpet/super-built-up loading factor in Indian
apartment stock is commonly discussed around a **70-80%** range [RECALL — unverified figure],
meaning an unstated basis switch alone can move a per-sqft figure ~20-35%, matching the brief's
own caution. RERA (2016) mandated carpet-area pricing disclosure for RERA-registered primary
projects — a partial fix; resale listings and pre-RERA stock still commonly quote super-built-up.
[RECALL — unverified exact scope/date] **Any India build must carry a mandatory area-basis field
on every transaction record before any computation runs**, not as downstream cleanup.

I also recall **NHB RESIDEX**, published by India's National Housing Bank, as an existing
multi-city Indian price index, likely built partly from housing-finance-company/bank loan-linked
data — which, if correct, would skew toward financed, salaried, formal-sector, mid-market
transactions, away from cash-heavy/luxury/informal deals, the same generic bias any mortgage-data
index carries elsewhere. Low confidence on current specifics (city count, base year, frequency,
exact source) — flagged as a priority verification-search item. [RECALL — unverified]

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE INDIA ENGINE

1. **Do not build repeat-sales as the primary ward-level series** — at tens of transactions/
   quarter the repeat-sold subsample will be too small standalone; reserve it for a city-level
   cross-check only.
2. **Treat SPAR (sale-price/circle-rate ratio) as the primary candidate construction** — the
   assessed-value ingredient already exists per transaction via state circle-rate schedules,
   turning the core problem into data-access/record-linkage (matched IGR data), not small-sample
   statistics.
3. **Never fold the ratio's level silently into the price index** — track it separately as a
   compliance/under-invoicing signal, since it shifts with enforcement regime independent of true
   price moves.
4. **Mandate an area-basis field on every transaction record** before any computation — never
   consume a per-sqft figure without knowing carpet/built-up/super-built-up, given the ~20-35%
   distortion risk.
5. **Apply Fay-Herriot-style empirical-Bayes shrinkage of every ward-quarter estimate toward its
   city/zone mean**, shrinkage strength tied to each ward's own N — the direct answer to "tens of
   transactions," layered on top of whichever base method is used, not a competing alternative.
6. **Pre-register a minimum-N publication threshold** for any ward-quarter cell (no-magic-numbers
   discipline); check published agency practice first (Section 10 is an open question, not
   settled) rather than inventing a number.
7. **Always publish transaction volume/count alongside every price figure** — the China land-
   price case is a desk-verified proof that a mild headline price move can be a pure composition/
   adverse-selection artifact of thin, shifting liquidity, exactly Indian ward markets' risk.
8. **Where matched circle-rate data can't be obtained, fall back to a Quigley/Case-style hybrid**:
   hedonic shadow prices pooled citywide, only a ward-level fixed effect varies, repeat-sales pairs
   used purely as a secondary check — never an independent full hedonic re-estimation at ward
   level alone.
