## 1. Asset classes × cycle phase

**The central methodological trap, stated up front:** almost every popular version of this
literature dates the phase *ex post* (NBER turning points announced 6–18 months late;
practitioner phase labels assigned retrospectively). A claim "equities return X% during
recovery" is close to meaningless for trading unless the label was knowable in real time.
Flag this on every asset-class-by-phase claim — none of the classic sources are real-time
honest by construction.

- **Merrill Lynch "Investment Clock"** (Greetham et al., ~2004) [LIT]: growth × inflation
  quadrants — Reflation→bonds, Recovery→equities, Overheat→commodities, Stagflation→cash.
  NOT real-time honest (output-gap/inflation-gap inputs heavily revised); practitioner
  post-mortems note underperformance since ~2009 (QE-era decoupling). TEST: replicate the
  quadrant grid on IIP/PMI × CPI with a publication-lag stamp; compare real-time vs
  revised quadrant assignment — exactly what FUN-D4 must do.
- **Fidelity business-cycle sector framework** (2000s+) [LIT]: early/mid/late/recession
  phases; bonds/cash late-cycle and recession, equities early/mid. Same ex-post dating
  problem, conceded in their own methodology notes.
- **Fama & French (1989), "Business Conditions and Expected Returns on Stocks and Bonds"
  (JFE)** [LIT]: the credible academic ancestor — uses CONTINUOUS conditioning (dividend
  yield, term spread, default spread), finds expected excess returns on stocks AND bonds
  are higher near troughs, lower near peaks. Real-time constructible; converges with
  FUN-D1. TEST: replicate on NIFTY + Indian G-sec term spread.
- **Chen (1991, JF)** [LIT, LOW CONFIDENCE on magnitudes]: lagged industrial-production
  growth negatively related to subsequent expected returns — convergent with the booked
  growth-negative doctrine.
- **Commodities/cash by phase**: little academic-grade real-time work isolating
  commodities-by-phase; the inflation-regime literature (§5) is the credible frame.

## 2. Sectors × phase

- **Conover, Jensen, Johnson & Mercer (~1996–2008, J. Investing etc.)** [LIT]: monetary
  stance (expansive vs restrictive, real-time constructible from rate direction) —
  cyclical/rate-sensitive sectors earn most of their excess return in expansive regimes;
  defensives flatter and relatively better in restrictive regimes. A MONETARY conditioning,
  not a growth-phase one — keep analytically separate. TEST: most replicable design in
  this section — RBI repo direction × NSE sectoral indices once vaulted.
- **Stangl, Jacobsen & Visaltanachoti (~2009, "Sector Rotation Over Business Cycles")**
  [LIT, qualitative high-confidence]: the skeptical counterweight — the practitioner
  rotation clock's edge is much weaker than claimed, concentrated in a subset of
  cycles/sectors, sensitive to phase-dating choices, shrinks/disappears under costs and
  non-ex-post dating. Standard citation for "sector rotation does not replicate cleanly";
  converges with FUN-D3's one-way kill.
- **Defensive definitions**: utilities/staples have the most stable low-beta profile
  [LIT, consensus] BUT increasingly behave as BOND PROXIES in low-rate regimes (2013
  taper-tantrum selloffs with duration) [LIT, LOW CONFIDENCE, pattern not single paper];
  pharma/healthcare defensiveness least stable (policy risk). Adds the rate-regime
  conditionality caveat to SEC-D6.

## 3. Factors × regime

- **Daniel & Moskowitz (2016, JFE), "Momentum Crashes"** [LIT]: momentum's worst losses in
  an identifiable state — post-crash, high vol, when the short leg has become high-beta
  and the market rebounds. Real-time constructible (trailing drawdown + vol). Barroso &
  Santa-Clara (2015, JFE): vol-managed momentum is the fix — already the program's
  doctrine #4. TEST: check WML in IIMA for the same crash signature.
- **Value's cycle sensitivity** [LIT, LOW CONFIDENCE, diffuse]: value = financial/
  cyclical-heavy; relatively better in recovery/reflation, worse in late-cycle
  growth-scarcity. Asness et al. (2013, JF) "Value and Momentum Everywhere": the −corr
  with momentum is the exploitable fact (already booked, −0.37).
- **Quality/low-vol in recessions**: QMJ (Asness-Frazzini-Pedersen, ~2013/2019) [LIT]
  flight-to-quality in downturns. Low-vol (Ang et al. 2006; Frazzini-Pedersen 2014) [LIT]
  outperforms risk-adjusted in down/high-vol markets, badly lags strong recoveries — the
  failure mode the program's REFUTED low-vol core already hit. Contested as an
  unconditional excess-return anomaly (§8).
- **Size in recoveries** [LIT, e.g. Ilmanen 2011]: small caps rally hardest off troughs
  (leverage torque) — but the UNCONDITIONAL size premium is contested (Crain 2011, van
  Dijk 2011 reviews: weak-to-absent post-publication, micro-cap/January-dependent). TEST
  only the conditional recovery-bounce, event-study style.
- **Betting-against-beta and funding**: Frazzini-Pedersen (2014, JFE) [LIT] — BAB returns
  tied to funding-constraint tightness (TED spread, dealer leverage); anomaly compresses
  when funding easy. TEST: beta-sorted spread on NSE750 vs financing-cost proxies.

## 4. Monetary regime

- **Jensen & Johnson (+Mercer) line (~1996–2003)** [LIT]: rate-direction regimes (hiking
  vs cutting — unambiguous, real-time) — stock AND bond returns materially higher in
  expansive regimes, across size/style/sector cuts. One of the most replicated
  conditionings BECAUSE the label is real-time honest. TEST: RBI repo trend vs
  NIFTY/bond forward returns — the single most defensible India design here.
  [DESK NOTE: FUN-D8 m1 independently reproduced this on JST: falling +8.0% vs rising
  +3.3% next-yr real equity.]
- **Yield curve**: Estrella & Mishkin (1998, REStat) [LIT] — strong for recession
  probability 12–18m out; weaker/inconsistent as a direct equity-return conditioner.
  [DESK NOTE: FUN-D8 m2 found slope DOES rank next-yr equity monotonically on JST —
  flagged NEW, needs India/monthly verification before consumption.]
- **The "Fed model"**: Asness (2003) [LIT] — real/nominal conflation, a disinflation-era
  artifact, no robust OOS value. §8 material.
- **Real rates and gold**: Erb & Harvey (2013, FAJ) [LIT, LOW CONFIDENCE on magnitudes] —
  the gold/real-rate link is real but noisy; long near-zero stretches punctuated by
  regime repricings. The program's own gold doctrine stands; don't re-derive.

## 5. Inflation regime

- **Neville, Draaisma, Funnell, Harvey & Van Hemert (2021, JPM), "The Best Strategies
  for Inflationary Times"** [LIT]: across inflation-surge episodes to the 1920s —
  commodities (esp. energy) the most reliable outperformer; TREND-FOLLOWING the standout
  strategy; gold helps but noisier; nominal bonds worst; equities negative on average with
  energy/materials best within; TIPS muted. TEST: CPI-acceleration event study against
  core/gold/factor sleeves.
- **vs the booked HIGH+RISING killer cell** (gold +9.6 > housing +6.8 >> equity −3.0 >
  bonds −5.6): broadly consonant on ordering; the flagged disagreement — the LIT winner is
  BROAD COMMODITIES and trend-following, not gold specifically; the program only tested
  gold. TEST: re-run the killer-cell ranking with a broad commodity leg before concluding
  gold is THE hedge rather than A hedge.

## 6. Credit regime

- **Schularick & Taylor (2012, AER)** [LIT]: excess private-credit growth = the best
  financial-crisis predictor, 14 countries 1870–2008.
- **Mian, Sufi & Verner (2017, QJE)** [LIT]: HOUSEHOLD credit expansion predicts GDP
  slowdowns with a 3–4 year lag (debt-overhang).
- **Baron & Xiong (2017, QJE)** [LIT]: bank credit expansion predicts bank-EQUITY crash
  risk; the market does not price it ahead.
- **Gilchrist & Zakrajšek (2012, AER)** [LIT]: the Excess Bond Premium leads activity;
  modest equity-return predictability (risk-premium story).
- Consonant with the booked CI doctrine ("crises belong to credit, returns to
  inflation") — extends, not contradicts. [DESK NOTE: FUN-D8 m3 adds the horizon
  separation — credit booms do NOT hurt t+1 returns; the damage is 2–5y crisis risk.]
  TEST: RBI credit growth vs Schularick-Taylor-style excess-credit thresholds (FUN-D6).

## 8. Known failures — do not spend census cells

- **Fed model** (Asness 2003 — debunked).
- **Unconditional size premium** (Crain/van Dijk — weak-to-absent post-publication; only
  the conditional recovery-bounce has standing).
- **Naive practitioner rotation clock** (Stangl et al.; FUN-D3 independently killed the
  India analog).
- **Utilities/staples as universal recession hedges** (bond-proxy effect — defensiveness
  is monetary-regime-conditional).
- **Low-vol as an unconditional excess-return strategy** (contested; the program's own
  REFUTED low-vol core is the live demonstration).
- **Momentum-crash timing as a standalone signal** (mechanism descriptive; no robust
  incremental value beyond vol-managed sizing).
- **Phase dating with fully-revised macro data** — the structural trap of the whole
  clock literature; vintage stamps are non-negotiable for every phase design.
