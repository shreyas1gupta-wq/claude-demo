# The Policy-Created Cities — Amravati and Ayodhya

Agent dossier, IN programme (Half B), the two policy-conferred cases named separately from the
organic-growth city dossiers in this directory. Compiled 2026-09-11. WebSearch only — WebFetch is
EGRESS_BLOCKED for every domain and was not attempted; every figure below is a search-snippet read,
not a re-read primary page, at the same standard as the CN programme's Half B. **Corroboration
tags used throughout**: `[2-SOURCE]` = two independent searches/outlets converged; `[1-SOURCE]` =
one outlet only; `[RECALL — unverified]` = context only, not found this session. Circle/ready-
reckoner rates are marked **[CIRCLE RATE]** explicitly wherever they appear and are never treated
as a market price. 35 searches used against the 32-40 budget.

**Framing (binding on how to read this dossier):** these two cities are analysed together and
apart from the organic-growth cities because both are cases of **administrative/political
privilege conferring value**, not jobs-and-household-formation demand. The China programme's own
finding is the yardstick: provincial-capital status there was worth roughly double the
appreciation through the boom (~+400% vs ~+200%) and worth **zero** protection in the bust —
Nanjing and Wuhan, both provincial capitals, posted among the steepest recent declines nationally
(secondary-market y/y: Nanjing -11.45%, Wuhan -10.89% `[1-SOURCE, this session]`, alongside this
desk's own already-booked CN-programme figures). Every question below is graded against that
frame, not against "is this a good story."

## Headline findings

- **Amravati is the cleaner case, and the fit is close to exact.** A city built almost entirely on
  an administrative designation (capital status conferred 2014, land-pooled 2015) saw land run from
  roughly ₹15-70 lakh/acre (2014-15) to reported unofficial deals near ₹5 crore/acre at the 2018-19
  peak `[1-SOURCE, off-record deals per PARI]` — then the SAME designation was **legally withdrawn**
  for five years (Dec 2019 three-capitals bills) with Vijayawada-area sales falling 40-50% and prices
  ~25%, and Amravati itself going to a transaction "standstill" `[1-SOURCE]`. The city today has
  ~100,000 residents against a 3.5-million 2050 target `[1-SOURCE, World Bank]`, and its permanent
  government buildings are still at groundbreaking/tender stage as of 2025 `[2-SOURCE]` — eleven
  years after the capital decision. **The identical risk is being re-run for 2029 right now**: the
  same party that shelved Amravati once (2019) is campaigning on an alternative capital (MAVIGUN)
  for the next election `[2-SOURCE]`, a fact this dossier judges is not fully priced into the current
  land-price recovery.
- **Ayodhya's price move happened mostly BEFORE the January 2024 event, not because of it.** Land
  in the 10km temple radius went from **[CIRCLE RATE-adjacent market quotes]** ₹600-1,000/sq ft
  pre-Nov-2019 to ₹2,200-3,500/sq ft by 2020 `[2-SOURCE]`, and by 2023 — a full year before
  consecration — actual DEED prices were already running 41% to as much as 1,235% above the
  (stale, 8-years-unrevised) official circle rate `[1-SOURCE, Deccan Herald]`. The consecration
  itself repriced a narrower prime-frontage micro-market further (₹20,000-25,000/sq ft near the
  temple, 2024-25) but the bulk of the multiple was already in the price by the time the ribbon was
  cut — a textbook "buy the political/legal certainty event, not the ceremony" pattern.
- **Circle rates and market prices moved on different clocks in Ayodhya, and conflating them is
  the single most common error in the source material.** The 2025 circle-rate revision (**[CIRCLE
  RATE]** Rs 6,650-6,975/sq metre -> Rs 26,600-27,900/sq metre, up to +200% in places `[2-SOURCE]`)
  was the district administration CATCHING UP to a market that had already moved years earlier
  `[1-SOURCE]` — not a fresh demand shock. Every "circle rate hiked X%" headline found in this
  session needs to be read as a tax-floor reset, not a price move, and several press pieces blur
  the two in the same sentence.
- **Neither city has an independent transaction-volume series** — every price figure sourced this
  session is an asking/quoted/press price, not a sub-registrar deed count or a repeat-sales index.
  The one genuine registration-count found (Amravati: 61,793 LPS returnable plots registered,
  7,628 pending `[1-SOURCE]`) measures farmers receiving what they were promised, not open-market
  resale turnover. This is a named, load-bearing gap for both cities (Q10).
- **Ayodhya has a real, large, verifiable demand-side number that Amravati does not**: tourist/
  pilgrim footfall rose from 2.84 lakh (2017) to ~5.6-5.8 crore (2023) to 16.44 crore (2024) to a
  reported ~23.8-30 crore (2025) `[2-SOURCE on trend, exact full-year 2025 count varies by
  source]`. That is a genuine, growing cash-flow base (hotel occupancy, F&B, retail) that Amravati's
  land-price story has no equivalent for — Amravati's entire case still rests on capital-status
  administrative promise, eleven years in.
- **Both markets are dominated in the search results by promotional/broker/aggregator sites**
  (openplotsinamaravati.com, propertykumbh.com, geospardevelopers.com, toplinerealty.in and
  similar), and the single largest "X-times appreciation" claim found for Ayodhya (15x in 3 years)
  traces to a real-estate developer (Abhinandan Lodha / HoABL) actively marketing a branded project
  in the city `[1-SOURCE, PROMOTIONAL, base price/year unstated]` — flagged per the corroboration
  rule, not treated as a headline number.

## 1-5. AMRAVATI, question by question

**1. Timeline (bifurcation, LPS, 2019 shelving, 2024 revival).** See table above. The core
mechanism: AP's 2014 bifurcation left the residual state with no capital (Hyderabad went to the new
Telangana); Naidu's government chose a greenfield site on the Krishna river and, rather than
acquiring land outright, ran a Land Pooling Scheme — farmers surrendered land in return for
developed returnable plots plus an annuity, betting the state's own capital-city premium would make
the smaller returned plot worth more than the larger surrendered acre `[2-SOURCE on LPS structure]`.
The 2019 change of government reversed the underlying bet by legislative fiat (the three-capitals
bills) rather than by any economic event; the 2022 court ruling restored Amravati's SOLE-capital
status but did not compel construction speed, and construction only resumed in earnest after the
2024 election changed the government back.

**2. Prices across the whole arc.** 2014-15 baseline ~₹15-70 lakh/acre `[1-2 SOURCE, variance by
exact source/village]`; 2016-19 boom to reported unofficial deals near ₹5 crore/acre `[1-SOURCE,
off-record]`; 2019-2024 crash to a reported "standstill" with area-wide sales down 40-50% and
prices down ~25% in the adjoining Vijayawada market `[1-SOURCE]`; 2024-2025 recovery to ₹40,000-
60,000/sq yd in boom pockets (from ₹10,000-15,000/sq yd) `[2-SOURCE]`, i.e., roughly a 3-4x nominal
move in about 18 months on the sq-yd measure, layered on top of institutionally-FIXED (not
market-discovered) prices for CRDA-tendered commercial land (₹8.20cr/acre for hotel plots). No
single, consistent "X times" figure exists across sources for the FULL 2014-2025 arc — per-acre and
per-sq-yd quotes for different micro-locations within the pooled villages differ by an order of
magnitude, and this dossier reports the range rather than picking one number.

**3. The farmers.** Promised: 1,000 sq yd residential + 250-450 sq yd commercial plot per acre
surrendered (the commercial-plot figure varies 250 vs 450 sq yd by source — flagged, not
resolved), annuity ₹30,000-50,000/acre/yr for 10 years with 10% annual escalation, jobs for family
members, education-fee reimbursement, infrastructure (roads/power/drainage), free health cards, and
pensions for landless agricultural laborers `[2-SOURCE]`. Received: annuity was paid most years but
disrupted — 2020's payment required a court order; reporting describes annuity payments and
pensions being STOPPED for thousands of farmers during the 2019-2024 freeze `[1-SOURCE]`; of the
promised RETURNABLE PLOTS, 61,793 have been registered and 7,628 remain pending as of the most
recent report found `[1-SOURCE]`, and even registered Phase-1 plots are reported as still lacking
basic infrastructure (roads, drainage) as the government proceeds to a SECOND round of pooling
`[1-SOURCE]`. Where they trade now: an early (2017) resale of a 1,000-sq-yd residential plot went
for ₹2 crore (~₹20,000/sq yd) `[1-SOURCE]`; current quoted resale range for LPS-origin plots is
₹12,000-22,500/sq yd depending on location `[1-SOURCE]` — meaningfully BELOW the ₹40,000-60,000/sq
yd quoted for the hottest CRDA-administrative-zone pockets, meaning the farmer's actual payoff
depends heavily on which of the 25 pooled villages the specific returnable plot sits in. This is
the cleanest available test the dossier can construct of whether the policy promise capitalised:
the answer is "partially, and unevenly" — real appreciation happened, but with years of delayed/
interrupted cash annuities, unresolved plot delivery for a meaningful minority (7,628 of ~69,000+),
and a resale value visibly below the top-quoted capital-region rate.

**4. Construction status vs funding vs announcement.** BUILT: the interim/temporary secretariat,
assembly and High Court at Velagapudi (functioning since ~2016-17, "Darbar Move" of the majority of
state employees already completed); some trunk roads and layouts. NOT YET BUILT: the PERMANENT
Assembly, High Court and Secretariat towers — Assembly groundbreaking was May 2, 2025 with
completion targeted ~2027-2028; CRDA was still issuing tenders for the permanent HC/Assembly as of
~March 2025. COMMITTED: ~₹65,000cr total project estimate; Centre ₹15,000cr (of which only
~₹1,400cr reported actually released); World Bank + ADB $1.6bn combined (~₹13,000-13,600cr)
approved, with the first World Bank tranche ($340mn) disbursed only around April 2026 under a
milestone-linked (not fixed-schedule) structure; AP state budget line ₹6,000cr for 2025-26. Net:
roughly ₹20,000-25,000cr of the ₹65,000cr full estimate is committed in principle, and actual cash
disbursed to date is a small fraction even of that — eleven years after the capital decision, and
roughly two years into the current revival, the city remains substantially UNBUILT.

**5. The honest risk — 2029.** The 2022 AP High Court ruling that quashed the three-capitals plan
provides a LEGAL floor (Amravati as sole capital cannot be undone by ordinary legislation the way
it was in 2020), but it does not prevent a repeat of the 2019-2024 STYLE outcome, which was achieved
by simply not funding and not building rather than by any court-reviewable act. As of July 2026, the
same party that shelved Amravati once (YSRCP) is already campaigning for 2029 on an alternative
capital plan (MAVIGUN — Machilipatnam-Vijayawada-Guntur) `[2-SOURCE]`, with commentary explicitly
naming the effect on investor confidence: repeatedly floating alternatives signals that "the
project's long-term future depends on who wins the next election" `[1-SOURCE]`. Is this priced? The
evidence says largely NOT: land prices are compounding strongly (roughly 3-4x since the 2024
revival) on what looks like an assumption of continuity, at the same time as the 2029 alternative-
capital campaign is already live in the press — an asymmetry consistent with the China finding
(the market prices the upside scenario, not the historical base rate that has already delivered one
multi-year freeze).

## 6-9. AYODHYA, question by question

**6. Ram temple timeline and infrastructure.** Consecration (Pran Pratishtha) January 22, 2024,
~7,000 guests `[2-SOURCE]`. Associated infrastructure: Maharishi/Maryada Purushottam Shri Ram
International Airport (~8km from the temple, Ramayana-themed terminal), Ayodhya Dham railway
station redevelopment, the 13km Ram Path corridor (Sahadatganj to Naya Ghat), and >200 projects
worth ₹30,923cr reported underway alongside a headline >$10bn (roughly ₹85,000cr) total
infrastructure figure that matches the separately-cited Master Plan 2031 commitment `[2-SOURCE]`.
The Ayodhya Development Authority's Master Plan 2031 adds an industrial corridor along the ring
road, additional bus terminals, and a medical college, explicitly aiming at a "world-class Smart
City" positioning `[1-SOURCE]`.

**7. Prices — pre-2019, post-verdict, post-consecration, circle rate vs transaction.** Pre-2019: sub-
₹1,000/sq ft in the town centre, ₹400-600/sq ft further out, ₹5-20 lakh/acre depending on
agricultural vs residential use `[2-SOURCE]`. Post-Nov-2019 verdict: +30-40% immediately, then a
further doubling after the Aug 2020 Bhoomi Pujan, taking the 10km-radius figure to ₹2,200-3,500/sq
ft `[2-SOURCE]`. By 2023 (one year before consecration): outskirts ₹1,000-1,500/sq ft, town centre
₹2,000-3,000/sq ft, and — the cleanest finding on this question — ACTUAL DEED prices already running
41% to 1,235% above the (then 8-years-stale) official circle rate `[1-SOURCE]`. Post-consecration
(2024-25): near-temple prime commercial frontage ₹20,000-25,000/sq ft, settled residential areas
(Deokali) ₹8,500-10,000/sq ft `[1-SOURCE]`. The 2025 circle-rate revision (**[CIRCLE RATE, NOT A
MARKET PRICE]**) took the official floor from ₹6,650-6,975/sq metre to ₹26,600-27,900/sq metre near
the temple, +150-200% in the 10km radius `[2-SOURCE]` — explicitly the administration's own
described purpose was to bring the STALE circle rate in line with prices the MARKET had already set;
reading this circle-rate jump as new demand would be exactly the conflation this dossier's rules
warn against.

**8. Tourist/pilgrim arrivals and the revenue mechanism.** 2.84 lakh (2017) -> ~5.6-5.8 crore (2023)
-> 16.44 crore (2024, of which >11 crore came in H1 2024 alone, i.e. concentrated around the
consecration) -> a reported ~23.8 crore by mid-2025 rising to ~29.95 crore for the full year (one
source) or ~22 crore by mid-October (another source) `[2-SOURCE on trend and order of magnitude,
exact annual counts vary]`. This is a REAL and large cash-flow base, unlike Amravati's purely
land-price story: IHCL/Taj has committed a 300-room hotel (7.2 acres, targeted complete Dec 2025,
operational Apr 2026) plus Vivanta (100 rooms) and Ginger (120 rooms); ITC is in discussions; other
chains (Marriott, Club Mahindra, Wyndham, Leela) are named as interested `[2-SOURCE on the Taj
deal specifically]`. Actual occupancy data is thin outside the January 2024 peak event itself
(90% occupancy, ₹70,000-100,000+/night that week) `[2-SOURCE, same event]` — one hospitality-
industry planning document ASSUMES 65% marketwide occupancy as a modeling input, not a measured
outcome `[1-SOURCE]`, and no steady-state 2025 occupancy figure was found in this session — a named
gap.

**9. Is the appreciation already done?** The evidence supports YES, substantially, for the BULK of
the multiple, though the near-temple micro-market kept moving after January 2024. The chronology:
prices moved 2.2-3.5x on legal/political CERTAINTY events alone — the November 2019 verdict and
the August 2020 Bhoomi Pujan — years before a single wall of the actual temple was finished; by
2023, a full year before consecration, DEED prices were already 41-1,235% above the stale official
floor. The "15x in 3 years" claim that would extend this further to 2024 comes from a real-estate
DEVELOPER actively marketing a branded Ayodhya project (Abhinandan Lodha/HoABL) and states no base
price or base year `[1-SOURCE, PROMOTIONAL — treat with real caution per the corroboration rule]`.
What is credibly NEW since January 2024 is narrower and smaller in proportional terms: prime
commercial frontage immediately adjacent to the temple (₹20-25k/sq ft) moving further above an
already-elevated townwide level (₹2-3k/sq ft as of 2023), plus — genuinely new — the arrival of
real, measurable footfall and committed branded-hotel capacity that, for the first time, gives the
market a cash-flow basis to test the already-elevated price against. **This dossier's read: the
land story was substantially pre-traded on the legal/political certainty path (2019-2023); the
temple's actual opening is now supplying the revenue data that will determine whether the pre-
traded price was right, not the event that caused the pre-trading.**

## 10. The liquidity question — who is the exit buyer? (both cities)

Neither city has an independent transaction-volume series available to this dossier. Every price
figure sourced this session is an asking/quoted/press price or an administratively-SET price
(Amravati's CRDA tender rates, Ayodhya's circle rate); none is a sub-registrar deed-count or
repeat-sales index — that data (NHB RESIDEX, state registration-office series) is RUNSHEET-gated
per `CONTRACT.md` (free-data-only; a principal-machine pull), not available to WebSearch.

**Ayodhya**: the buyer types named in this session's sources are NRIs (one developer states ~27%
of its own sales are NRI-sourced `[1-SOURCE, developer-self-reported — a sales-mix claim from the
seller, not an independent count]`), "local businessmen," and "middle-class families." No
government transaction-volume figure was found.

**Amravati**: the one concrete registration count located — 61,793 plots registered, 7,628 pending
— measures LPS returnable-plot deeds (farmers receiving what they were promised), not open-market
resale turnover. Buyer types named are NRIs and "institutional investors," with one industry piece
explicitly framing the appeal as "Amaravati today looks like Hyderabad in its early development
phase" `[1-SOURCE]` — an analogy to another city's growth story, not a cash-flow-grounded
valuation argument.

**The honest answer for both**: the buyer being described in the available sources is the SAME
buyer that is driving the current price level — NRIs, developers, and retail speculative investors
betting on a growth narrative — not a distinct, later-stage, larger buyer pool (institutional
developers acquiring completed product, or end-users pricing off realized rents) that would need
to appear to make current sellers whole. No evidence in either city's sourcing this session shows
that second buyer pool. This is the single largest evidentiary gap this dossier surfaces, and it
matters more than any individual price figure above: a market whose only visible characterised
buyer is "more of the same kind of buyer that got in earlier" is a market whose exit liquidity is
unproven, not merely unmeasured.

## Applying the China provincial-capital finding

**China's finding, restated**: administrative privilege (provincial-capital status) bought roughly
double the boom-period appreciation and zero protection in the bust — Nanjing and Wuhan, both
provincial capitals, are among the steepest recent decliners nationally.

**Amravati — close to an exact fit.** The upside was purely administrative: a bifurcation decision
plus a land-pooling scheme, with essentially no organic economic base, drove land from tens of
lakhs to (reportedly) crores per acre inside four years. The downside was not merely economic — it
was a **legal withdrawal of the designation itself** for five years (2019-2024), producing a
transaction standstill, and it is **the same risk being actively re-run for 2029** by the same
political actor. If anything Amravati's case is sharper than China's: the Chinese cities that fell
hardest KEPT their provincial-capital status through the bust (the designation didn't change, the
market simply re-rated); Amravati's designation was itself legally revoked and later restored by a
court, which is a more direct, mechanism-level match to "administrative privilege, zero downside
protection" than the Chinese comparison actually offers.

**Ayodhya — a partial fit, and the dossier should say exactly where it diverges rather than force
the frame.** The UPSIDE mechanism matches closely: a political/legal decision (the 2019 verdict,
the 2020 foundation-laying), not organic job growth, drove most of the repricing, exactly as
China's provincial-capital premium was policy-conferred rather than earned. But the DOWNSIDE
mechanism is NOT the same in kind. Amravati's downside risk is that the designation gets withdrawn
(a discrete, observable, political event with precedent). A consecrated temple is not a relocatable
administrative designation — no government has proposed, or plausibly could propose, un-
consecrating the temple. Ayodhya's real downside risk, per Q9, is a VALUATION risk (the repricing
outran the realized cash-flow base and has to grow into itself, or correct) — a bubble-velocity
risk, not a policy-reversal risk. Pairing the two cities under one frame is right for the UPSIDE
half of the China finding and only partly right for the downside half.

**What would falsify the pattern, named in advance:**
- *Amravati*: if the 2029 MAVIGUN campaign fails to move Amravati land prices at all — i.e., if the
  market has concluded the 2022 High Court ruling makes a second reversal legally foreclosed
  regardless of the election outcome — that would be evidence the downside risk is genuinely
  retired, not merely unpriced. Conversely, any measurable price softening around the 2029
  campaign/election, even without an actual legal reversal, would CONFIRM the pattern extends to
  political uncertainty short of an actual designation change.
- *Ayodhya*: if pilgrim-arrival growth and hotel-occupancy data (once measured beyond the January
  2024 peak) compound enough to retrospectively justify the 2019-2023 repricing on a realized
  cash-flow (RevPAR-type) basis, that would falsify the "pre-traded/overrun" reading of Q9. If
  arrivals plateau — which the 2017-to-2025 curve, starting from a near-zero base, mechanically
  must do at some point — while prices hold at consecration-era highs, that confirms it.
