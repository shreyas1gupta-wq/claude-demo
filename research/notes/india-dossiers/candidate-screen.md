# The Candidate Screen — Five Tier-1/2/3 Cities Outside the Seven Already Covered

Agent dossier, IN programme (Half B), candidate-city leg. Compiled 2026-09-11. Plan of record:
`research/frontier/india-property-plan.md`, §0 read in full before this work started — the CAGR
reading of the principal's "20% for 5yr" bar (=148.8% cumulative) is the one this screen is built
against, per §0a, and the desk's own IN-D1/IN-D2 finding (a sustained 20%/yr REAL move has never
happened post-1970 in the 18-country JST panel; even nominal 20%/yr-clearing windows are ~2.4% of
windows once hyperinflation is excluded) is the governing frame, not a forecast tool. WebSearch
only — WebFetch is EGRESS_BLOCKED for every domain and was not attempted. **40 searches used
against the 36-44 budget.** Corroboration tags used throughout: `[2-SOURCE]` = two independent
searches/outlets converged; `[1-SOURCE]` = one outlet only; `[RECALL — unverified]` = context only,
not confirmed this session. This is a SCREEN, not a set of picks — the desk ranks from the table
and sections below, not from any intuition supplied going in.

**The frame, applied to every candidate**: INFRASTRUCTURE-BACKED (a durable asset — airport,
metro, expressway), POLICY-BACKED (a withdrawable designation — capital status, SEZ/scheme), or
DEMAND-BACKED (jobs/incomes/household formation, slowest but most durable). China's own finding
binds directly: provincial-capital status there was worth ~2x appreciation through the boom and
**zero** protection in the bust; Amravati (already booked in this same directory,
`policy-cities.md`) had its capital designation legally withdrawn for five years, collapsing
transactions 40-50%. That is the yardstick every POLICY-backed candidate below is measured against.

## Headline findings

- **The single biggest finding this session was not on the original candidate list: Visakhapatnam's
  "executive capital" status — the reason the task brief classed it as POLICY-backed — has already
  been LEGISLATED AWAY.** The Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation (Amendment) Bill, 2026, passed by
  Parliament ~April 2026, makes **Amaravati the sole and permanent capital of Andhra Pradesh**, and
  coverage describes the three-capitals plan as abandoned `[2-SOURCE: parliamentary record via
  newsonair.gov.in + Manorama Yearbook/vajiramandravi]`. So Vizag is not a *fragile* policy story
  today, in the Amravati sense — its policy driver has already gone to zero, and its real 2026
  driver has quietly become INFRASTRUCTURE + DEMAND (the Bhogapuram airport, GCC/data-centre jobs),
  which the market appears not to have fully repriced around: land in the IT Hills-Madhurawada
  corridor is still described as rising on old capital-city hopes even as the legal position has
  moved the other way.
- **Every infrastructure catalyst in this screen with a clean price series shows the same shape as
  the sibling MMR finding: the move happens before the ribbon-cutting, not after.** Noida/Yamuna
  Expressway apartments ran **+142.9% to +158%** over five years to reach ~₹10,200/sqft by 2026,
  almost entirely BEFORE Jewar's first commercial flight (15-Jun-2026); Nagpur's Samruddhi-corridor
  land had already run to **~3.7x its 2015 level by 2022-23** — two-plus years before the
  expressway's final stretch opened (5-Jun-2025); Dehradun North's own price series climbed
  **₹7,312→₹9,008/sqft (+23%) between Mar-2025 and Dec-2025**, i.e. in the twelve months immediately
  BEFORE the Delhi-Dehradun Expressway's Phase 1/4 inauguration (14-Apr-2026). Three independent
  city pairs, one pattern.
- **Delivered-vs-announced splits the twelve candidates roughly in half.** DELIVERED and dated:
  Noida/Jewar airport (commercial flights from 15-Jun-2026), Nagpur's Samruddhi Mahamarg (fully
  complete 5-Jun-2025), Visakhapatnam's Bhogapuram/Alluri Sitarama Raju airport (opened
  17-Aug-2026 — three weeks old as of this writing), Dehradun's expressway (Phase 1/4, 14-Apr-2026),
  and Surat's Diamond Bourse (Dec-2023). STILL ANNOUNCED, years out: the Surat-Mumbai bullet train
  (first section 2027, full route 2029), Jaipur Metro Phase 2 (foundation laid Jul-2026, targeted
  2031), Coimbatore Metro (still DPR-stage, no construction started at all), and both of Varanasi's
  named projects (ropeway targeted May-2026 with no confirmation found that it actually opened;
  airport expansion still under construction).
- **The honest negative is Varanasi: the loudest narrative in the whole list, the thinnest file.**
  It is the direct analogue the principal's own task brief names (Ayodhya), carries the biggest
  national-attention footfall numbers of any candidate, and yet: no NHB RESIDEX coverage was found,
  every price figure traces to a single aggregator chain with no second source, neither named
  infrastructure project is confirmed delivered, and the Ayodhya dossier already booked in this
  same directory names circle-rate/market-price conflation as a specific, recurring failure mode of
  exactly this kind of temple-city story — this session's search pass did not turn up data clean
  enough to rule that out for Varanasi.
- **Yields: the corroborated tier-2 premium over tier-1 is real but modest (roughly 4-6% vs
  2-4% gross), not the outlier some portals imply.** Indore's Nipania/Super Corridor (6-8%) and
  Kochi's Kakkanad IT belt (4.6-6%) are the most credible named pockets. A Surat portal's
  Sachin/Surat-North prints of 11-12% gross look like a single-source measurement artifact (they
  are inconsistent with the SAME portal's Vesu/PAL prints of 3-4% a few localities over) and are
  flagged, not adopted.
- **National/city base rates found this session sit far under any 20%/yr CAGR reading**: NHB's
  50-city composite ran **+5.0% y/y in Q3 FY25-26** and Anarock's top-7-city average rose **+8%
  y/y** to ~₹9,260/sqft over the same window (Delhi-NCR's +23% print is the outlier, everything
  else single digits) `[2-SOURCE, general]`. Locality-level 5-year cumulative moves of 100-300% DO
  appear repeatedly in this dossier's own table below, but every one of them is a narrow, catalyst-
  concentrated MICRO-MARKET print from a single portal, not a city-wide or NHB-grade figure — the
  same city-vs-national dispersion the CN programme already established, read onto India.

## Per-candidate notes

### 1. Noida / Greater Noida / Yamuna Expressway
The cleanest infrastructure-backed story in the screen and the best-dated: Jewar's airport
inauguration (28-Mar-2026) and first commercial flight (15-Jun-2026, IndiGo) are both pinned dates
`[2-SOURCE]`. But the price series makes the timing case for caution rather than opportunity — a
+142.9-158% five-year move to ~₹10,200/sqft happened mostly **before** any flight took off, and
NCR-wide, Noida-Greater Noida specifically saw the sharpest correction anywhere in the region in
Q2 2026 (new launches -72% y/y, though inventory itself only edged down 3%, and NCR-wide inventory
was roughly flat y/y at ~89,000 units) `[1-SOURCE, Anarock via zeebiz/storyboard18]` — a market that
looks like it is digesting the story rather than starting to price it. International flights were
reported likely "around September 2026" `[1-SOURCE]` — i.e. plausibly happening right now, which
is exactly the point in the cycle where, per the MMR precedent, most of the discrete re-rating is
typically already behind the price, not ahead of it.

### 2. Indore
The one candidate on this list with no single mega-catalyst at all — its case is a liveability/
cleanliness/administration reputation plus a diversified IT-and-education base, not an asset that
"opens." Growth-corridor localities (Super Corridor, Vijay Nagar, Scheme 140) show solid but not
extreme 5-year moves (+57% to +73%) `[1-SOURCE, 99acres]`, and Nipania's rental yield (up to 8%)
is one of the more credible high-yield prints in the whole screen `[1-SOURCE]`. No population
figure more recent than the (postponed) 2021 projection was found `[1-SOURCE]`.

### 3. Nagpur
Samruddhi Mahamarg is fully complete (final 76km stretch, 5-Jun-2025) `[2-SOURCE]`, linking JNPT
(Mumbai) to MIHAN (Nagpur) and halving travel time to ~8 hours. But land near the corridor had
already run to roughly 3.7x its 2015 level by 2022-23 — two-plus years before completion —
with a further 5.2x by 2035 cited as a developer/broker PROJECTION, not fact `[1-SOURCE]`. Reported
corridor premium over non-corridor land: 200-300bps/yr `[1-SOURCE]`. City-wide price data is
locality-patchwork only (no clean Nagpur-wide index found this session).

### 4. Surat
A split case by design: the Diamond Bourse is a delivered, three-year-old fact (Dec-2023)
`[1-SOURCE, though widely known]`; the Mumbai-Ahmedabad bullet train is a multi-year-out
construction project (~55% physical progress, first section Surat-Bilimora targeted 15-Aug-2027,
full route 2029) `[1-SOURCE]`. Surat's own 5-year price prints (Vesu +18.9%, PAL +16.3%, Palanpur
+13.2%) are notably **lower** than every other candidate in this table — a real signal, not a gap
in the search, since multiple Surat localities were queried directly. The rental-yield figures
found for Surat are internally inconsistent (Surat North 11.25% vs Vesu 3% vs PAL 4%, same portal)
and the high prints are flagged as a likely construction/measurement artifact rather than adopted.

### 5. Varanasi
See the headline finding above — this is the honest negative of the whole screen. Every price
figure found traces to one aggregator family with no second source; the two named catalysts
(India's-first urban ropeway, targeted operational "by May 2026," and an airport expansion
extending the runway from 2,745m to 4,075m and the terminal from 24,500 to 99,500 sqm on a
₹2,870cr sanction) are both **not confirmed delivered** as of today — the most recent evidence
found on the airport was a construction-progress social-media post, not a completion notice
`[1-SOURCE each]`. The already-booked Ayodhya dossier in this same directory names circle-rate vs
market-price conflation as a specific, repeated error in exactly this genre of source material;
this session did not gather enough Varanasi-specific data to know whether the same conflation is
occurring here, which is itself the finding.

### 6. Coimbatore
Strong demand-side case — GCC/manufacturing growth is real and multiply-cited (Bosch expanding
engineering presence; tier-2 GCC hiring overall growing 23% y/y, roughly double the metro pace)
`[1-SOURCE Coimbatore-specific, 2-SOURCE on the tier-2 GCC trend generally]` — attached to the
weakest infrastructure catalyst in the entire list: the metro remains at the DPR stage with no
construction started, years even from a groundbreaking, let alone an opening `[1-SOURCE,
themetrorailguy/metrorailnews]`. Locality land prices (Vedapatti +110.7%, Kovaipudur +130.0% over
5y) run well ahead of flat prices (+31% to +53%) — consistent with land speculation ahead of an
infrastructure event that has not yet been committed to a construction timeline.

### 7. Kochi
The most internally consistent price data in the whole table — no wild single-portal outliers,
a metro timeline with two independent sources roughly agreeing (>40% civil work complete, first
five Pink Line stations targeted June 2026), and the most credible non-outlier high yield found
this session (Kakkanad 4.6%, IT-belt more broadly cited at 5-6%) `[2-SOURCE on the metro schedule,
1-SOURCE on yield]`. No source found this session actually confirms the June 2026 station-opening
target was met, so it is recorded as PARTIAL/announced-with-imminent-delivery rather than
delivered.

### 8. Visakhapatnam
The reclassification finding above is the whole story here. As of the AP Reorganisation
(Amendment) Bill 2026 (~April 2026), Amaravati is the state's sole, permanent, statutorily-named
capital `[2-SOURCE]` — Vizag's "executive capital" designation, which is what the task brief's own
framing assumed made this a POLICY-backed case, has already gone the way Amravati's did in 2019,
except with no five-year interregnum: it simply never converted into the durable status the
three-capitals plan once promised. What is left is genuinely infrastructure- and demand-backed:
the Alluri Sitarama Raju International Airport at Bhogapuram opened 17-Aug-2026 (DGCA licence
7-Jul-2026, Air India validation flight completed, construction >99% at last report) `[1-2-SOURCE]`,
alongside a reported Google data-centre and Cognizant expansion `[1-SOURCE]`. Land in the
IT Hills-Madhurawada-Rushikonda corridor is up 30-50%, and Bhogapuram's own locality print shows
+48.1% over five years and +25% in the most recent year alone `[1-SOURCE each]` — appreciation that,
per the search results, appears to still be substantially anchored to the now-abandoned capital
narrative rather than fully repriced onto the airport/GCC story.

### 9. Jaipur
NCR-adjacency and tourism give it a genuine demand base, but the flagship number found this
session — a 65% rise in "weighted average new-launch price" from ₹4,240 (2023) to ₹6,979
(Oct-2024) cited as making Jaipur "the highest-appreciating Tier 2 city in India" `[1-SOURCE]` — is
a NEW-LAUNCH MIX-SHIFT figure, not a same-stock or repeat-sales move, and is flagged on exactly the
composition-artifact grounds this desk applied to China's land-price reporting in the CN atlas
(different mix of what's being sold, not the same asset revalued). The metro (Orange Line, Cabinet
cleared Apr-2026, foundation laid Jul-2026) is targeted for 2031 completion — the furthest-out
funded infrastructure catalyst in this entire screen bar Coimbatore's unstarted one.

### 10. Bhubaneswar
Every figure found this session, across price, appreciation and Smart City framing, traces to one
aggregator family (99acres and closely-related sites) with no second source located for any single
number — including some of the largest headline 5-year prints in the table (Patia land +160%,
peripheral Pahala +140% over 3y). The Smart City Mission designation is a decade-old administrative
fact (2015-16 selection), not a fresh 2026 catalyst, so there is no dated "delivered vs announced"
question to apply here at all — the honest read is that this candidate has locality-level heat but
no city-level corroboration and no discrete asset to date.

### 11. Raipur
The weakest file of all thirteen groups, and for a specific reason beyond thin sourcing: the one
named infrastructure/policy catalyst found — the Naya Raipur ("Atal Nagar") IT park — was itself
reported to have struggled with basic infrastructure and power-supply problems, i.e. this is a case
where the catalyst did not merely fail to arrive yet, it arrived and reportedly underperformed
`[1-SOURCE]`, ahead of a fresh ₹4,000cr investment push (AI parks, Medicity, EduCity) announced for
2026 `[1-SOURCE]`. Consistent with that read, Naya Raipur's own land (₹750/sqft) prices BELOW older
Raipur suburbs like Kachna (₹5,850/sqft land) — the planned city trading cheaper than the
organic one. Price-change figures found (Kachna flats "+53.6%" cited identically for both the 3-year
AND 10-year windows) look like a stale index cell rather than real data and are not relied upon.

### 12. Chandigarh / Mohali
No discrete named infrastructure asset (airport, metro, expressway) was found driving either city
this session — the story is Tricity spillover and redevelopment. Mohali's headline appreciation
prints (3BHK +149.2%, Aerocity land +173.8%, both 5-year) are among the highest in this entire
table, but every one of them traces to real-estate-marketing-adjacent blogs (Noble Aurellia, Farmer
Estates, Royals Property Consultant) rather than brokerage-research or news sources — exactly the
promotional-material caution this task was instructed to apply — and no Anarock/Knight
Frank/JLL/CBRE coverage of either city surfaced in this session's searches.

### 13. Dehradun
The second-cleanest pre/post price pair in the screen after Noida and Nagpur: Dehradun North's own
series shows ₹7,312/sqft (Mar-2025) rising to ₹9,008/sqft (Dec-2025), a +23% move concentrated in
the nine months immediately before the Delhi-Dehradun Expressway's Phase 1/4 inauguration
(14-Apr-2026) `[1-SOURCE, but the two data points are dated and internally consistent]`. Fastest
3-year movers (Nawada +260.9%, Selakui +163.2%) look like small-base periphery effects rather than
city-wide moves. No rental yield or population/jobs figure was found for Dehradun this session —
named as a gap, not assumed.

## 11. Delivered vs announced — the MMR test applied to every candidate

The sibling MMR dossier (`mumbai-outer.md` §6-7) established the pattern directly: *"connectivity
tends to be priced in ahead of completion, not after, and by the time a line is running, the market
has usually absorbed much of the expected uplift"* — Navi Mumbai airport's own Panvel price index
had reached 176 (2021=100) before the airport's first flight, and property near
airports/expressways can move 30-70% from ANNOUNCEMENT to completion against only 8-15%/yr
increments AFTER actual opening. Applying that same test to this screen's candidates:

**DELIVERED, with dates**: Noida/Jewar (commercial flights from 15-Jun-2026 — ~3 months old);
Nagpur/Samruddhi Mahamarg (complete 5-Jun-2025 — 15 months old); Surat Diamond Bourse (Dec-2023 —
3 years old); Visakhapatnam/Bhogapuram airport (opened 17-Aug-2026 — 3-4 weeks old); Dehradun
Expressway Phase 1/4 (14-Apr-2026 — ~5 months old). In every one of these where a price *series*
(not just a single figure) was found, the bulk of the appreciation predates the delivery date —
Noida's 142-158%, Nagpur's ~3.7x corridor-land move, Dehradun's +23% in the nine months before
opening. **The newer the delivery date, the less post-delivery data exists to test whether the
"confirms rather than discovers" half of the pattern (8-15%/yr ongoing) is holding** — Vizag's
airport and Dehradun's expressway are both too recent (weeks to months) to say anything about the
post-opening trajectory yet; Noida has ~3 months; only Nagpur (15 months) offers any real
post-completion window, and even there the price data found this session is locality-only and does
not cleanly separate pre/post.

**STILL ANNOUNCED, years out**: Surat-Mumbai bullet train (2027/2029); Jaipur Metro Phase 2
(2029-2031); Coimbatore Metro (no construction start at all — the furthest out of any candidate);
Varanasi's ropeway (targeted May-2026, unconfirmed) and airport expansion (under construction, no
date found). Kochi Metro Phase 2 sits in between — partially built, an imminent-but-unconfirmed
opening. **None of these candidates' price data was decomposed into a clean pre/post split this
session because none of them has a "post" yet** — which is itself informative: if the MMR pattern
holds, most of whatever uplift these catalysts will produce should already be visible in current
asking prices, well before the asset exists, and a buyer waiting for the ribbon-cutting to confirm
the thesis is, per the sibling finding, buying the same anticipation that would already have played
out once.

## 12. Base rates — what Indian tier-2 residential has actually compounded at, 2015-2025

No India-specific decadal series was retrieved cleanly this session (NHB RESIDEX's full 2015-2025
city-level time series was not returned by the searches run — a named gap, not a finding). What
WAS found: NHB's 50-city composite housing price index rose **+5.0% y/y in Q3 FY25-26** (46 of 50
cities registering gains) `[1-SOURCE, NHB press release]`, and the index level moved **102.32
(Q4 2025) → 104.51 (Q1 2026)**, a ~2.1% quarter print `[1-SOURCE, tradingeconomics]` — both point to
a national pace in the mid-single-digit-to-high-single-digit %/yr nominal range in the most recent
window, not 20%/yr. Anarock's top-7-city average rose **+8% y/y** (₹8,590→₹9,260/sqft, Q4-2024 to
Q4-2025), with Delhi-NCR's +23% the one outlier and every other major city at 4-9% `[1-SOURCE]`.
**Set against this desk's own already-booked IN-D1/IN-D2 entry** (a 20%/yr REAL move has never
happened post-1970 across 18 advanced economies in 150 years, and even the NOMINAL threshold —
after excluding hyperinflation episodes — clears at only ~2.4% of five-year windows), the national/
city-index evidence gathered here is entirely consistent with that base rate: ordinary Indian
residential markets, city-wide, appear to compound at a pace far under any 20%/yr CAGR reading, and
closer to the "20% CUMULATIVE over 5yr" reading (~3.7%/yr) than to the CAGR one on most years.
**The exception, and it is a real and repeated one, is at the LOCALITY level inside these cities**:
this dossier's own table has multiple micro-market prints of 100-300% cumulative over five years
(Yamuna Expressway flats +142.9%, Bhubaneswar's Patia land +160%, Mohali's Aerocity land +173.8%,
Coimbatore's Kovaipudur land +130%) — CAGRs in the 15-33%/yr range, i.e. squarely inside the
principal's bar. Every one of these is a narrow, single-portal, catalyst-adjacent print, not a
city-wide or NHB-grade average — the same city-vs-national dispersion the CN programme's own JST
work already established (a single Chinese city can and does exceed any national panel figure in
either direction), read directly onto India. The honest statement: **20%/yr CAGR is not what an
Indian tier-2 CITY does; it is what a specific catalyst-adjacent MICRO-MARKET inside one
occasionally prints, on data too thin (single portal, no transaction count, no repeat-sales
control) to certify.**

## 13. Where the rental yield is highest

Corroborated direction: Indian tier-2 cities run a real but modest premium over tier-1 — roughly
**4-6% gross** in tier-2 hotspots (Indore, Jaipur, Coimbatore, Nagpur, Ahmedabad cited together)
against **2-4% gross** in the big metros (Mumbai 3.84%, Bengaluru/Hyderabad 4-5%) `[2-SOURCE
general framing]`. Inside this screen, the highest **credible, non-outlier** prints are **Indore's
Nipania (up to 8%) and Super Corridor (~6%)** and **Kochi's Kakkanad/IT belt (4.6%, with 5-6% cited
more broadly for the corridor)** `[1-SOURCE each, but internally consistent with the general tier-2
range]`. Surat's portal-reported 11-12% (Sachin, Surat North) is **flagged, not adopted** — the same
source's other Surat localities (Vesu, PAL) print 3-4%, a 3-4x internal spread on one portal that
reads as a measurement or small-sample artifact rather than a real citywide number; if genuine it
would be the highest yield found in the whole screen, but it fails this programme's own
corroboration rule. No yield data was found this session for Nagpur (city-specific), Varanasi,
Visakhapatnam, Jaipur, Bhubaneswar, Raipur, Chandigarh/Mohali, or Dehradun — named as gaps.

## 14. The honest negative — weakest evidence behind the strongest narrative

**Varanasi.** Of all thirteen groups screened, it carries the single loudest national narrative —
it is the principal's own named analogue to Ayodhya, it has the largest attention-grabbing
footfall numbers of any candidate (already documented in the sibling `policy-cities.md` dossier:
2.84 lakh in 2017 rising past 16 crore by 2024), and it has two named, government-backed
infrastructure projects (a ₹645cr urban ropeway, a ₹2,870cr airport expansion). Yet on the
evidence actually gathered this session: no NHB RESIDEX coverage was found; every price figure
traces to a single aggregator chain with no independent second source; neither infrastructure
project is confirmed delivered as of today (the ropeway's own "May 2026" target has no completion
notice found, and the airport expansion's most recent evidence is a construction-progress social
post); and no city-wide 5-year price-change figure was found at all. The already-booked Ayodhya
dossier in this same directory names circle-rate/market-price conflation as a specific, recurring
failure mode in exactly this kind of source material — this session did not gather the data needed
to confirm or rule that out for Varanasi, which is itself the finding. Runner-up on thinness (but
without the loud narrative to match, so a different category of problem) is Raipur — see §11 above.
