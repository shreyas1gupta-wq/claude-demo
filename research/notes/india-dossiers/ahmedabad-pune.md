## Headline findings

- **Neither city yielded an asking-vs-registered (IGR) transaction gap comparable to the sibling
  agent's MMR/Nepean Sea Road finding — this is a genuine negative result, not an omission.** Both
  cities' searches surfaced only **jantri (Gujarat) / ready-reckoner (Maharashtra) rates** — the
  government's own assessed *floor* value used for stamp duty — running BELOW market asking prices
  (Ahmedabad: market 10-40% above post-2023 jantri; Pune: market 1.5-3x ready reckoner). That is a
  **different comparison** from the MMR finding (asking price vs. actual registered *transaction*
  price, both market-side numbers) and is reported separately, not substituted for it. No
  IGR-Gujarat or IGR-Maharashtra micro-market-level *transaction* series was found for either city.
- **GIFT City residential has appreciated ~110% since 2021 (~16%/yr CAGR, ₹5,000 → ~₹10,200-10,617/
  sq ft) [2-SOURCE], but delivery lags the price move**: only ~48% of planned space is allotted (25
  operational buildings of a planned 62m sq ft district), and journalistic sources independently
  describe it as "quite a ghost town after working hours" — real, growing office/fintech occupancy
  coexists with thin evening/residential vibrancy. Read as anticipation running ahead of, not purely
  disconnected from, a genuine but partial delivery.
- **Dholera is this dossier's clearest land-banking case.** The Tata-PSMC semiconductor fab is a
  real, dated, ~50%-complete industrial delivery (trial output targeted December 2026, ASML
  lithography deal signed) [2-SOURCE] — but the SIR's residential/land story around it is almost
  entirely anticipation: no operational city or residential-absorption figure was found anywhere in
  this search, land prices are thinly traded with a ₹550-1,667/sq ft spread across sources
  [2-SOURCE, order-of-magnitude only], and multiple independent sources flag active fraud risk
  (a named fake scheme, a Gujarat High Court "concerted fraud" land case).
- **Pune's own Ring Road and Pink Line metro extension show the same anticipation-vs-delivery split
  as Dholera, at a much shorter horizon.** The Ring Road's Western Corridor has ~98% land acquired
  and construction underway (target: operational late 2026-mid 2027); its Eastern Corridor lags to
  2028; and the separately named **Inner Ring Road (HCMTR)** is a distinct, much earlier-stage
  project (land-acquisition fast-tracking only began August 2026) — conflating the two would
  misprice how close any given corridor actually is to delivery. The Pink Line's near-term segments
  (Maan-Balewadi, Dec 2026) are genuinely close to delivery; the Phase-II Chakan corridor is DPR-stage
  only (submitted Feb 2026) — pure anticipation.
- **Ahmedabad's premium west (Satellite/Vastrapur/Bodakdev/Prahlad Nagar) runs roughly ₹8,150-9,300/
  sq ft; Pune's comparable core (Koregaon Park/Kalyani Nagar/Deccan Gymkhana) runs 2-3x higher, roughly
  ₹15,000-21,000/sq ft** — the two cities are not comparable in absolute level, only in gradient shape.
  Ahmedabad's rental yields (~3.9% citywide, the HIGHEST of any major Indian city per one cited
  survey) run ABOVE Pune's IT-corridor yields in the cited ranges for Hinjewadi/Wakad (though sources
  conflict on Pune's own IT-corridor number, see §6), the opposite of the "cheaper city, thinner
  yield" pattern that might be expected.
- **Pune's inventory position is real but the "highest of major cities" framing needs a metric
  check**: Gera's July-2026 PRRR puts Pune's own unsold-stock VALUE at a record ₹92,110 cr (+28%
  YoY) with 11.3 months of overhang citywide (13.5 months in Premium Plus) [1-SOURCE, single named
  report]; on unit COUNT, Pune's 83,491 unsold units (2025) is second only to MMR's 1,79,228 among
  four cities checked, ahead of Bengaluru's 64,863 [2-SOURCE cross-check]. Ahmedabad's own overhang
  (19 months, Q1 2025 — stale, no 2026 print found) is actually WORSE on the months metric than
  Pune's 11.3 — so "among the highest" holds for Pune on value/unit-count trend, not unambiguously on
  months-of-supply against every city in this dossier.

## 1. Inner/west premium — Satellite, Vastrapur, Bodakdev, Prahlad Nagar

**Price level.** This belt is Ahmedabad's most expensive residential quadrant: Bodakdev tops it at
₹9,300/sq ft average (a second cut gives ₹8,800-9,000, individual projects ₹7,200-10,000), Vastrapur
₹8,900, Prahlad Nagar ₹8,250, and Satellite (the largest, most liquid of the four) ₹8,150 — with
individual premium villas/apartments across Satellite and Prahlad Nagar reaching ₹8,000-14,000/sq ft
[2-SOURCE for the cluster as a whole; each individual point figure only 1-2-SOURCE]. No source
returned in this search stated whether these figures are carpet, built-up, or super-built-up — treat
every number in this section as basis-unknown.

**Trajectory.** Satellite is the one name in this belt with a clean, multi-horizon percentage series:
+5.8% (1y), +27.3% (3y), +33.6% (5y), +58.3% (10y) [2-SOURCE, Square Yards/Commonfloor family and
99acres roughly consistent]. Back-solving the 5-year figure against the current ₹8,150 gives a
DERIVED 2021 level near ₹6,100/sq ft — not a directly quoted number. Vastrapur's +14.8% is stated as
a 1-year figure by a single source and was not independently corroborated; treat it as an outlier
until a second source confirms it. No absolute 2019 level was found for any of the four names.

**Rental yield.** No source gave a Satellite/Vastrapur/Bodakdev/Prahlad-Nagar-SPECIFIC yield number.
The one hard figure available is a citywide one: Ahmedabad has the HIGHEST rental yield of any major
Indian city surveyed, at 3.9% (Business Standard, citing a CREDAI/industry-body survey, Nov 2024)
[1-SOURCE for the specific number, though it is a named, widely-repeated industry figure]. A rough
back-of-envelope cross-check for Prahlad Nagar specifically (a 3BHK renting at a cited ₹40,000/month
against an ₹8,250/sq ft x ~1,600 sq ft ≈ ₹1.32 cr unit) implies roughly 3.6% — broadly consistent
with the citywide 3.9%, though this cross-check is this dossier's OWN arithmetic, not a sourced
figure, and should not be cited as an independent data point.

**Inventory.** No micro-market-specific figure was found; the only figure available is citywide (see
§ Ahmedabad Inventory/GIFT/Dholera notes and the closing gaps section) — Ahmedabad's own inventory
overhang stood at 19 months in Q1 2025, with no 2026 print located.

**What drives it.** This is Ahmedabad's established, already-realized premium belt — the SG
Highway/BKC-style commercial spine (see §2) sits immediately adjacent and the four names function as
its primary residential catchment, analogous in role (not in absolute price) to Bandra/BKC in Mumbai.

## 2. Middle — SG Highway corridor, Thaltej, Science City

**Price level.** SG Highway itself averages ₹7,350/sq ft (range ₹6,050-9,100; a separate cite gives a
weighted average of ₹7,500), materially cheaper than the Satellite/Bodakdev belt it borders. Thaltej
spans a wide ₹6,000-10,000/sq ft as part of the broader SG Highway corridor, with a more granular
July-2026 cut isolating "inner Thaltej, SG Highway edges, and Science-City-facing pockets" at
₹7,000-12,500+/sq ft. Science City's own average is stated as ₹6,900/sq ft — but the SAME source
gives Science City's range as ₹7,350-11,100, which does not bracket its own quoted average; this
internal inconsistency is flagged rather than resolved. [2-SOURCE for SG Highway; 1-SOURCE, and
internally inconsistent, for Science City.]

**Trajectory.** The corridor-wide figure is +25-35% over 5 years (not attributed to a single point
name) [1-SOURCE, though a second source's Bopal-specific +28.6%/5y — see §3 — sits squarely inside
this same range and is treated as loosely corroborating the corridor's general order of magnitude].
DERIVED 2021 levels off SG Highway's own current ₹7,350 and the corridor's own 25-35% band: roughly
₹5,440-5,880/sq ft — a range, not a point estimate, and explicitly approximate.

**Rental yield and inventory.** No corridor-specific figures were found for either; use the citywide
3.9% yield and the citywide (stale, Q1 2025) 19-month overhang as the only available proxies.

**What drives it.** GIFT City, metro expansion, and SG Highway's own connectivity are the three
drivers cited for this corridor's "high-growth hub" framing [1-SOURCE] — i.e. this middle ring is
explicitly positioned (by the sources themselves) as capturing anticipation of infrastructure still
completing, the same anticipation-vs-delivery dynamic flagged for GIFT City and Dholera below.

## 3. Outer growth — Shela, Bopal, South Bopal, Sanand

**Price level.** This is Ahmedabad's affordable growth ring, running roughly ₹5,000-7,500/sq ft for
Shela/Bopal/South Bopal — Shela ₹5,050 average, Bopal ₹5,400, South Bopal ₹5,100 (as of March 2026,
recently softening at -2.88%) — though a separate citation directly contradicts the Shela-vs-Bopal
ranking, giving Shela ₹5,400 and a differently-spelled "Shila" ₹6,050, which this dossier does not
attempt to reconcile [2-SOURCE overall, internally conflicting at the individual-name level]. Sanand
is this dossier's single least trustworthy price cell: cited variously at "up to ₹4,500/sq ft"
(comparable to Shela/Bopal), a separately stated average of just ₹680/sq ft for plots, and a raw
listing spread of ₹400-6,500/sq ft — a roughly 10x internal range that most plausibly reflects a mix
of raw agricultural-adjacent land and NA-converted developed plots being quoted together, or a
unit-basis error in one of the citations, neither of which this dossier can resolve from search
snippets alone.

**Trajectory.** Bopal's own +28.6%/5-year figure is the one clean number in this micro-market
[1-SOURCE]; South Bopal and Shela have no comparable point-figure historical series, only the recent
(2026) softening noted above for South Bopal. No 2019 level was found for any of the four names.

**Rental yield.** No area-specific figures; one source describes Sanand qualitatively as offering
"great ROI from property investments" without a number — treated as non-evidence-grade.

**What drives it.** Sanand's residential story is inseparable from its industrial one: Tata Motors'
original plant acquisition there (historically ~$81,000/acre, a legacy figure with no current
bearing on today's residential land price) anchored the belt's initial development narrative, and the
area continues to be marketed on industrial-employment spillover rather than any demonstrated
residential-absorption metric this search could find.

## 4. East/old city and the Sabarmati riverfront redevelopment

**Price level.** Ahmedabad's east/old city is its cheapest quadrant: Maninagar averages ₹6,050/sq ft
(range ₹4,800-7,250), while Naroda/Nava Naroda/Vastral/Bapunagar run ₹2,500-3,500/sq ft — the
cheapest figures found anywhere in this dossier's Ahmedabad table. The Sabarmati riverfront's western
bank (Ashram Road) sits meaningfully higher, averaging ~₹7,250/sq ft, with a cited OFFICE rental
yield of 9.77% on that stretch — explicitly a commercial, not residential, figure and reported here
only to flag that distinction sharply, not as evidence of residential yield.

**Riverfront redevelopment — anticipation vs delivery, directly testable.** The state's own auction
base prices for riverfront plots (western ₹23,676/sq m, eastern ₹19,982/sq m — converting at 10.7639
sq ft per sq m gives approximately ₹2,200/sq ft western and ₹1,857/sq ft eastern, a conversion this
dossier performed itself, not independently sourced) sit far BELOW Ashram Road's own ~₹7,250/sq ft
market average — consistent with these being government floor/reserve prices for raw or
under-developed riverfront land, not finished residential product, and therefore not a like-for-like
comparison to the Ashram Road figure. Separately, the AMC's FY2026-27 draft budget proposes ~₹853 cr
of NEW riverfront infrastructure (a Water Metro feasibility study, an exhibition-cum-convention
centre, promenade and Phase-3 extension toward the Narmada Canal) — this is a **budget proposal, not
yet delivered infrastructure**, and riverfront-area properties' own cited +30-40% appreciation over
the PAST decade is therefore a delivery-confirmed historical read, while the ₹853 cr FY27 push is
this section's clearest still-anticipatory element. [1-SOURCE for each figure in this paragraph.]

**Rental yield and inventory.** No residential yield or inventory figure specific to the old
city/riverfront belt was found.

**What drives it.** Structurally, this belt's cheapness reflects its industrial/working-class
origins (echoing, at much lower absolute prices, the mechanism the Mumbai dossier documents for
Chembur and Wadala) — the riverfront redevelopment is the belt's one active re-rating catalyst, and
it is explicitly still mid-build (Phase 3 not yet complete, the ₹853 cr FY27 spend not yet
disbursed).

## 5. GIFT City — occupancy, residential demand, and whether IFSC status has translated to residential absorption

**Price level and trajectory.** GIFT City residential has appreciated sharply: a directly-quoted 2021
level of ₹5,000/sq ft rising to ₹6,500 (2023) → ₹8,800 (2024) → ₹9,550 (2025) → ₹10,200 (start of
2026) → ~₹10,617 (Q2 2026, Magicbricks) [2-SOURCE, the two citation families broadly agree on both
the trajectory shape and the current level]. That is roughly **+110% cumulative, ~16%/yr CAGR** —
independently consistent with a separate source's own "+110.5% in the last 5 years" claim
[2-SOURCE on the cumulative figure specifically]. Current listings: standard residential ₹10,500-
13,000/sq ft, premium/high-rise ₹15,000-18,500/sq ft. No source stated carpet vs. built-up basis.

**Rental yield.** Cited at 3-5% depending on location/furnishing/tenant profile [1-SOURCE], with
qualitative support that occupancy is "steady" given a mixed corporate-tenant/HNI/NRI demand base.

**Occupancy — the delivery side, tested directly.** Official figures put roughly 29.5 million sq ft
allotted against a planned 62 million sq ft district (~48%), with 25 operational buildings and 37
still under construction [1-SOURCE]. Independently, journalistic coverage repeatedly describes GIFT
City as "quite a ghost town after working hours," with "entire rows of workstations" unoccupied by day
despite a headline >100 fintech firms present and a workforce target of 150,000 by 2030 [1-2 SOURCE,
qualitative but from more than one independent outlet]. These two reads are NOT contradictory: they
describe genuine, growing DAYTIME office occupancy (the source of the fintech/BFSI residential-demand
narrative) sitting alongside a still-thin EVENING/residential social ecosystem — i.e. the office side
of GIFT City's IFSC bet is real and delivering; the residential/urban-life side lags it.

**Verdict.** GIFT City is not a pure anticipation play like Dholera — it has a real, growing,
identifiable tenant base and a documented office buildout — but its ~110% five-year residential price
run has outpaced the "ghost town" delivery reality on the ground, making it a case of **anticipation
running ahead of a genuine but partial delivery**, not anticipation running ahead of nothing.

## 6. Dholera — the greenfield SIR and the Tata semiconductor fab: status, land prices, investable or land-banking?

**The fab — real, dated, delivery-stage.** Tata Electronics, in partnership with Powerchip
Semiconductor Manufacturing Corporation (PSMC), is building India's first advanced-node fab at
Dholera at a stated cost up to ₹91,000 cr (~US$11bn), targeting up to 50,000 wafers/month capacity.
As of 2026 the project is reported ~50% complete, with foundations finished and cleanroom
installation/equipment calibration underway; trial production is targeted for December 2026. Tata
Electronics has separately signed with ASML for lithography systems. [2-SOURCE: Tata's own newsroom,
gasworld/industry trade press, and multiple Dholera-focused property-marketing sites converging on
the same ~50%-complete, Dec-2026-trial-output figures — the fab-specific claims are corroborated
across a genuinely independent trade-press source, not only promotional real-estate sites.]

**Land prices — thin, wide, and internally unreconciled.** Per-square-foot aggregator figures put
Dholera land at ₹550-1,000/sq ft; a separate, more granular zone-wise per-square-yard breakdown gives
residential ₹7,500-15,000/sq yd, commercial ₹12,000-20,000/sq yd, and industrial/Activation Area
₹5,000-10,000/sq yd — converting the residential band at 9 sq ft per sq yd gives roughly
₹833-1,667/sq ft, an order-of-magnitude match to the aggregator figure but not an identical range
[2-SOURCE, consistent in magnitude, not reconciled to a single number]. Pricing is explicitly stated
to depend heavily on Town Planning (TP) zoning, proximity to the Ahmedabad-Dholera Expressway, and
NA (non-agricultural) title clearance — i.e. this is not a homogeneous, liquid asset class but a
zone-by-zone, title-quality-dependent one.

**Investment risk — independently corroborated.** Multiple sources warn of fake schemes (a named
"Nexa Evergreen" scheme offering guaranteed returns with no official government connection) and
document a Gujarat High Court case describing a "concerted fraud" involving land tied to the
Ahmedabad-Dholera Expressway itself. Investment-guide sites (self-interested, but consistent on this
point) explicitly counsel that Dholera "is better suited to patient capital than short-term
speculation" given delay risk across roads, utilities, and residential build-out. [2-SOURCE for the
fraud/delay risk framing.]

**Verdict — the clearest anticipation-only case in this dossier.** The industrial anchor (the fab) is
real, dated, and roughly half-delivered. The residential/SIR land story built around it is almost
entirely anticipatory: no operational city, no population figure, and no residential-absorption
metric of any kind was found anywhere in this search. Combined with the wide, unreconciled price
spread and the independently-documented fraud risk, Dholera today reads as **a land-banking bet on
future urbanization around a real industrial catalyst, not yet an investable, liquid residential
market** — the fab's own delivery timeline (trial output Dec 2026) is the one concrete date against
which that bet can eventually be tested.

## 7. Inner/central — Koregaon Park, Kalyani Nagar, Boat Club Road, Deccan

**Price level.** This is Pune's most expensive belt and also its most internally inconsistent one in
the sources returned: Koregaon Park alone is cited anywhere from ₹8,566 to ₹27,500/sq ft depending on
the cut, with central-tendency figures clustering ₹15,050-19,150/sq ft. Deccan Gymkhana is the one
name in this belt with a cleaner set of figures — ₹19,400 average, ₹17,250-21,300 range, a
transaction-rate cut of ₹16,206, and a named top project (Fortune Yashodhan) at ₹30,674/sq ft.
Kalyani Nagar and Boat Club Road are consistently grouped with Baner/Aundh/Koregaon Park in a
₹12,000-22,000/sq ft premium band but were not isolated as standalone point figures by any source
returned. [1-2 SOURCE per name; the wide KP dispersion is flagged rather than resolved.]

**Trajectory.** Koregaon Park's own 5-year change is directly CONFLICTING between sources: 43.3% vs
25.2% — both reported here, neither preferred, and DERIVED 2021 levels off either rate (₹13,364 or
₹15,290 from a ~₹19,150 base) are given only as illustrative bounds, not as a resolved figure. No
absolute 2019 level, and no 5-year figure at all, was found for Kalyani Nagar, Boat Club Road, or
Deccan.

**Rental yield.** Koregaon Park: 4.31%, 4.2%, and 3.5-4.5% cluster together reasonably, but one
source states a markedly lower 1-3% band ("among the lower yields in Pune despite the prestige") —
flagged as a genuine conflict, plausibly reflecting the gap between very-high-ticket luxury stock
(low yield) and smaller/older KP stock (higher yield) being blended differently across citations.
Kalyani Nagar: 2.97% and 3.5-4.5%, similarly spread.

**Inventory.** No belt-specific figure found; see §12 for the citywide Pune position.

**What drives it.** Koregaon Park and Kalyani Nagar are Pune's original colonial/cantonment-era elite
addresses (paralleling South Mumbai's role in that city's hierarchy), with Deccan Gymkhana serving a
similar old-money/institutional (university-adjacent) function; none of the three carries a
comparable "single infrastructure catalyst" story to the corridors in §8-9 below.

## 8. IT-driven west — Hinjewadi, Baner, Balewadi, Wakad

**Price level.** Hinjewadi averages ₹9,000/sq ft with a clear phase gradient (Phase 1 ₹9,000-11,500,
Phase 2 ₹7,500-9,800, Phase 3 ₹6,200-7,900 — prices falling as one moves further from the original,
most-established IT park). Baner is the belt's most expensive name at ₹11,700, grouped with the
premium ₹12,000-22,000 band alongside Koregaon Park. Balewadi runs ₹9,400-16,000 (flat-specific cut
₹10,550-13,000), and Wakad ₹9,050. [1-SOURCE per individual name; the cluster as a whole is broadly
internally consistent in level.]

**Trajectory.** No 5-year (or 2019/2021 absolute) figure was found for any of the four names in this
search — a genuine gap for what is arguably Pune's single most economically important corridor
(Hinjewadi alone is cited elsewhere in this dossier at an estimated 3-4 lakh employed workers). This
absence is flagged explicitly in Gaps & Cautions rather than papered over with a proxy.

**Rental yield — directly conflicting.** One source cluster puts Hinjewadi specifically at an
unusually high 5.5-6% and Wakad at 4.8-5.2%, attributing it to "low vacancies and strong workforce
demand" from an estimated 3-4 lakh IT professionals. A SEPARATE, more general "Pune IT corridor"
citation puts the same corridor at a more modest 3.5-4.5%. Both are reported; this dossier does not
adjudicate between them, but notes the higher figures come from sources with an explicit
promotional/investment-guide framing and should be weighted accordingly.

**Inventory.** No corridor-specific figure found; see §12.

**What drives it.** Unambiguous: the Rajiv Gandhi Infotech Park at Hinjewadi and its spillover into
Baner/Balewadi/Wakad is the corridor's entire demand story, directly analogous in mechanism (not
scale) to Bandra-BKC in the Mumbai dossier. The Pune Ring Road's western corridor (see §11) runs
directly through/adjacent to this belt and is the corridor's next anticipated catalyst.

## 9. East — Kharadi, Viman Nagar, Magarpatta/Hadapsar

**Trajectory — the strongest 5-year numbers in either city's table.** Magarpatta City +73.42%,
Hadapsar +72.7%, and Kharadi +49.7% over 5 years [1-SOURCE cluster per name, though all three figures

**Rental yield.** Kharadi 4.5-5.5% (same higher-yield source cluster as Hinjewadi/Wakad in §8) versus
the general "IT corridor" 3.5-4.5% citation that also covers Viman Nagar; no yield figure was found
specifically for Magarpatta or Hadapsar.

**What drives it.** Kharadi and Magarpatta's IT-park anchoring (EON IT Park, Magarpatta's own

RECENTLY-developed IT-employment node, still capturing a steeper part of its re-rating curve — though
this dossier notes the pattern rather than proving the mechanism from these sources alone.

## 10. Outer/affordable — Ravet, Punawale, Chakan, Talegaon, Wagholi

**Price level.** This is the cheapest ring in either city's table: Chakan/Talegaon Dabhade/Alandi/
Charholi Kurd are cited only as "up to ₹5,000/sq ft" (grouped, not individually broken out); Wagholi
spans ₹5,000-8,000 (one cite) or a narrower ₹6,000-6,500 (another); Punawale averages ₹7,400 with a
transaction-rate cut of ₹9,549 (an unusually wide internal spread for a single name) and an
alternative citation of ₹7,200-7,800. **Ravet returned no standalone price figure at all** in this
search — only grouped references to it as a "mid-income" area alongside Wakad/Hinjewadi/Bavdhan — a
genuine gap, not a zero.

**Trajectory.** No 5-year or absolute historical figure was found for any of the five names — this
entire micro-market has the thinnest historical data of any covered in this dossier.

**Rental yield.** Wagholi 4.3-5% (average ~5%) and Punawale 5-5.5% — both HIGHER than the premium
belts' yields, consistent with the general (though not universally confirmed in this dossier's other
sections) pattern of yield compressing toward city centres and expanding toward the periphery.

**What drives it — explicitly anticipation-priced on the Ring Road.** One source states plainly that
"the upcoming Pune Ring Road is opening up entirely new corridors — Ravet, Punawale, Chikhali, and
Moshi — as the next wave of 'affordable but improving' markets, similar to what Wagholi and Tathawade
looked like five years ago" [1-SOURCE, explicitly promotional framing, but a clean statement of the
anticipation mechanism at work]. Given the Ring Road's own Western Corridor is still pre-operational
(§11), any current price premium in this belt tied to Ring Road access is, by construction, an
anticipation premium, not a delivery-confirmed one.

## 11. Pune Ring Road and Metro — status, and separating anticipation from delivery

**Ring Road — two distinct projects, easily conflated.** The (outer) **Pune Ring Road**'s Western
Corridor has land acquisition ~98% complete and is the fastest-moving segment, targeted for
operational status late 2026 to mid-2027; heavy construction (tunnels, viaducts) began December 2024.
The Eastern Corridor lags to a 2028 target. Separately, and at a MUCH earlier stage, the **Inner Ring
Road (HCMTR)** is a distinct project: as of August 2026, Pune's district collector and municipal
commissioner were only directing officials to "expedite" its land-acquisition process, with proposals
for eight villages submitted and joint measurement/valuation just completed [2-SOURCE for the outer
Ring Road's status; 1-SOURCE for the Inner Ring Road/HCMTR being a separate, earlier-stage project —
this distinction is flagged explicitly because "Ring Road" is used loosely across property-marketing
sources and conflating the two would materially overstate how close any given corridor is to
delivery].

**Metro.** Pune Metro's existing Purple/Aqua lines carry a genuinely delivered, growing ridership:
~1,87,299 average daily riders (March 2026), ₹110-111 cr FY2025-26 revenue, and a cumulative 10 crore
passengers by October 2025 [2-SOURCE]. The Pink Line (Line 3) is under active construction with its
Maan-Balewadi segment targeted for December 2026 and the Balewadi High Street-District Court segment
for March 2027 — close enough to delivery that daily ridership is projected to rise to ~2,75,000 once
it opens. By contrast, Phase 1A's extension to Nigdi/Katraj/Chandni Chowk/Wagholi (Cabinet-approved
October 2023, ₹910 cr for the PCMC-Bhakti Shakti stretch) and Phase II's Chakan corridor (a revised
₹12,937 cr DPR for a 45.29 km Bhakti Shakti-to-Chakan line via Nashik Phata, submitted to PCMC only
in February 2026) are both still pre-construction/approval-stage — genuinely anticipatory relative to
the operational Purple/Aqua lines and the near-complete Pink Line segment.

**Reading the two together, per the brief's own test.** Any current Wagholi, Chakan, or outer-west
(Ravet/Punawale) pricing premium attributable to "the metro/ring road is coming" should be read as
**anticipation** for corridors still at DPR/land-acquisition stage (Chakan metro, Inner Ring Road,
Ring Road Eastern Corridor), and as **approaching delivery confirmation** only for the Pink Line's
imminent Balewadi-area segments and the Ring Road's Western Corridor — the two genuinely close-to-
operational pieces of this infrastructure story.

## 12. Pune's unsold inventory position

**The headline figure.** Gera's Pune Residential Realty Report (PRRR), July 2026, covering 2,900+
active projects and 3.4 lakh+ under-construction homes: unsold-stock VALUE hit a record ₹92,110 cr
(+28% YoY), inventory overhang rose to 11.3 months citywide (from 10.8 months a year earlier), with
the Premium Plus segment worst-hit at 13.5 months. New launches climbed 14% to 101,085 units, outrunning
a 7% sales recovery. [1-SOURCE — this is a single named, proprietary report reprinted across several
outlets, treated as one source family per this dossier's own corroboration convention.]

**Cross-city check.** On unit count (2025 data, four cities checked): MMR 1,79,228 unsold units
(highest), Pune 83,491 (second), NCR 90,455, Bengaluru 64,863 (lowest of the four) [2-SOURCE cross-
referenced across outlets citing the same underlying Anarock-style count]. This supports "Pune among
the highest" on unit count and on the value trend (+28% YoY, an all-time high), but this dossier
could NOT find a clean, directly-comparable 2026 MONTHS-OF-SUPPLY ranking across all four cities —
Ahmedabad's own 19-month figure (Q1 2025, stale) is actually WORSE than Pune's 11.3 months on that
specific metric, which complicates a literal "Pune has the highest inventory of major cities" claim
if months-of-supply, rather than value or unit count, is the intended metric. Both readings are
reported; neither is resolved into one ranking.

**What it implies.** The combination of record unsold value, rising overhang, and launches
outpacing sales is consistent with builders continuing to add supply into a market that has not
fully absorbed the prior cycle — directly relevant to how much of the 5-year price appreciation
documented in §7-10 (especially the east belt's +49-73% figures) should be read as durable versus a
launch-driven, inventory-building phase that has not yet been tested by a demand slowdown.

   gap, not papered over with the wrong proxy.

   sources returned in this search — a sharper gap than the Mumbai dossiers, where at least the

   above should be treated as basis-unknown unless a future pull resolves it.
