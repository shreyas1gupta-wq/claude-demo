# Mumbai's outer micro-markets and the MMR satellites — dossier

Snippet-sourced (WebSearch only, WebFetch egress-blocked), per `research/frontier/india-property-plan.md`
§0. Scope: Andheri-Goregaon-Malad-Borivali, Mulund-Ghatkopar-Vikhroli, Thane, Navi Mumbai
(Vashi/Nerul/Kharghar/Ulwe), and Panvel/Karjat/Dombivli-Kalyan. South Mumbai, Worli, Bandra West
and Powai are a sibling agent's scope and are cited here only where an infrastructure story
(coastal road, MTHL) crosses into them. As-of dates are stated per figure; "today" for this
dossier is 2026-09-11. Corroboration tags: **[2-SOURCE]** / **[1-SOURCE]** / **[RECALL —
unverified]**, per the binding rule; no price-per-sq-ft figure below was invented — where a
number could not be found it is named as a gap, not filled in.

## Headline findings

- **The price gradient from Andheri to Karjat spans roughly 8x**: ~₹35-40k/sqft (Andheri) down to
  ~₹5,000-5,300/sqft (Karjat), with Navi Mumbai's core (Vashi/Nerul, ~₹28-33k) sitting closer to
  the western suburbs than to its own southern end (Ulwe/Panvel, ~₹13-17k) — the region is not one
  gradient but two, hinged at the harbour crossing.
- **Vashi and Nerul, not Ulwe, show the largest 5-year percentage moves** in this set (+47.5% and
  +48.8% [1-SOURCE each, squareyards/99acres-style portal data]) — a low-base, metro- and
  MTHL-linked catch-up in already-built-out Navi Mumbai, not the airport corridor.
- **Navi Mumbai International Airport (NMIA) is COMPLETE, not pending**: commercial ops began
  25-Dec-2025, 24-hour ops from Feb-2026, international flights (Air India Express to Abu Dhabi)
  from 15-Jul-2026 [2-SOURCE]. The metro rail spur to the terminal (Line 1A/Line 2, ~28km,
  ₹5,575cr) was only approved Jun-2026 and is 2-3 years from completion [2-SOURCE] — the airport
  itself is delivered, the connectivity that would make it a daily-commute asset is not.
- **A large share of the airport's expected uplift looks already priced in**: Panvel's Anarock
  residential price index reached 176 (2021=100) by H1 2026 — +76% cumulative — accrued mostly
  *before* the airport opened [2-SOURCE]; Ulwe's asking prices (₹13,500-14,850/sqft) already run
  well above what actually closes (₹10,500-12,500/sqft on IGR-linked deal tracking) [1-SOURCE,
  explicit asking-vs-closing analysis], a classic late-cycle-anticipation signature.
- **Completed infrastructure delivers a smaller, slower re-rating than the announcement did**: MTHL
  (complete Jan-2024) and Metro 2A/7 (complete Apr-2022) both show continuing 8-15%/yr
  micro-premiums near stations/interchanges post-completion, well below the 30-70%
  announcement-to-completion moves reported for airport/expressway corridors [1-SOURCE each,
  broker-tracker data — treat as directional, not audited].
- **Unsold inventory is concentrated in volume, not value, at the far outer edge**: 91% of
  Panvel's current stock is still under construction (only 9% ready) and Panvel alone now carries
  ~42-46% of Navi Mumbai's total residential supply pipeline [1-SOURCE, Anarock micro-market
  report]; MMR's luxury-inventory buildup (+6% QoQ) is comparatively contained next to Delhi-NCR
  (+78%), Bengaluru (+57%) and Kolkata (+96%) [1-SOURCE, Anarock Q1 2026].

## 1. Andheri / Goregaon / Malad / Borivali (western outer suburbs)

Andheri West sits at the top of this belt: ~₹37,700-41,500/sqft through 2026 depending on the
portal, with Lokhandwala at a premium (~₹43,750) [1-SOURCE, squareyards + 99acres both quote
similar ranges but neither states carpet vs built-up — **basis unknown**]. Andheri East is
materially cheaper (~₹31,650-35,100) and has printed a clean 1y/3y/5y ladder — +4.8% / +15.8% /
+23.9% [1-SOURCE, squareyards]. A quarterly print for Andheri West actually showed -0.71%
(Jun 2026), consistent with a market pausing after a multi-year run rather than one still
accelerating.

Goregaon shows the clearest ASKING-vs-REGISTERED gap found in this belt: Goregaon West asking
₹32,500-45,000 against a "transaction rate" cited at ₹26,010/sqft, and Goregaon East asking
₹35,750 against transacted ₹32,685 — a 20-30% asking premium in the West, a much narrower ~8% gap
in the East [1-SOURCE for both pairs; the source does not name IGR directly but describes itself
as transaction-based, so treat as directional evidence of the asking/registered wedge rather than
an audited IGR pull].

Malad and Borivali are the belt's value end: Malad ~₹28,600/sqft (+6.9% 1y, +18.7% 3y), Borivali
West/East ₹30,650/₹32,150 (+22.6%/+25.3% 5y) [1-SOURCE each]. The Malad-Borivali corridor is the
one part of this micro-market with a genuinely corroborated yield: **4-5% gross**, cited both by a
dedicated news report on the corridor and independently by a rental-yield guide naming
Malad/Goregaon/Kandivali as the highest-yield pocket in the western suburbs (3.5-4%+) [**2-SOURCE**
on the yield band, though not on the exact point estimate]. Andheri West's own yield prints lower,
2.8-3.4% [1-SOURCE] — consistent with it being the most expensive, most capital-appreciation-driven
corner of the belt. Unsold inventory here reads as *controlled* rather than *overhung*: search
turned up qualitative confirmation ("developers paced launches... kept unsold inventory under
control," "quality inventory in prime zones is reducing") but no area-specific unsold-units or
months-of-supply figure for Andheri/Goregaon/Malad/Borivali individually — a genuine gap, named
rather than estimated.

## 2. Mulund / Ghatkopar / Vikhroli (central line outer belt)

This is the most internally consistent — and cheapest — of the two "outer suburb" belts. Mulund
prints ~₹28,650-28,750/sqft with a clean ladder: +4.0-5.1% (1y), +10.2-10.8% (3y), +19.8-19.9%
(5y) [1-SOURCE, but the West/overall figures corroborate each other closely]. Ghatkopar is
higher-priced on one portal (₹31,800 avg, +9.7% 1y) but a different portal shows Ghatkopar East at
only ₹28,100 and *falling* (-4.68% quarterly) [1-SOURCE each, and they disagree — flagged, not
reconciled]. Vikhroli is cheapest of the three at ~₹24,550/sqft.

Yield data on this belt is the noisiest in the dossier: one source gives Ghatkopar East 8% against
Ghatkopar West 2% — an implausible 4x split for adjoining halves of one suburb that most likely
reflects a small, skewed sample (redevelopment-society rentals mixed with new-build asking rents)
rather than a real structural gap; the same source's Vikhroli print (5%) is similarly out of line
with Mulund's more plausible 3.4% [1-SOURCE, treat all three yield figures here as low-confidence].
No 2019/2021 base, no 5-year figure for Ghatkopar or Vikhroli, and no unsold-inventory figure for
this belt were located — this micro-market is the thinnest-covered of the five in searchable
secondary sources, likely because it draws less investor/broker-content attention than the
airport corridor or the western suburbs.

## 3. Thane (and Ghodbunder Road)

Thane's city-average print rose across 2026 — ₹14,800 (Sep'25) → ₹15,800 (Dec'25) → ₹15,350
(Mar'26) → ₹16,050 (Jun'26) — a noisy but net-rising series [2-SOURCE, squareyards series
corroborated in direction by 99acres]. Thane West's 5-year change is +26.4%, with named pockets
(Patlipada, Pokhran 2, Pokharan Road, Louis Wadi, Ghodbunder Road) reaching up to +35% over the
same window [1-SOURCE]. Ghodbunder Road itself averages ₹14,500-15,000/sqft with a wide ₹9,000-
16,000 range and one outlier print at ₹10,900 [1-SOURCE, dispersion reflects the corridor's own
length — Manpada end vs the Bhiwandi end price very differently]. Anarock published Thane
specifically as "the Rising Star of MMR Real Estate" (Jul 2025) [1-SOURCE], consistent with the
belt's above-average 5-year print relative to the central-line belt in §2.

Yield is comparatively well corroborated at ~3.0-3.5% [2-SOURCE, squareyards + nobroker/
rentvsbuyindia-style guides converge], with a caveat that specific micro-pockets (Kausa ~9.9%,
Mumbra ~6.7%) print far higher — these are lower-price, higher-turnover, more informal segments
of Thane and should not be read as representative of the ₹15-16k/sqft mainstream corridor. Unsold
inventory: 97,117 units at end-Dec-2025, down 11% YoY [1-SOURCE, cited via a search snippet
attributing the figure to a property-data provider] — a large absolute base even after the decline,
and larger in absolute units than any other single micro-market in this dossier.

## 4. Navi Mumbai (Vashi / Nerul / Kharghar / Ulwe)

Vashi and Nerul are the belt's established core: Vashi ₹31,500-33,350/sqft, Nerul ₹28,650/sqft,
both with the largest 5-year moves found anywhere in this dossier — +47.5% and +48.8% respectively
[1-SOURCE each]. Kharghar sits at ₹18,500/sqft on the most recent print (Aug 2026) but the sector
range is wide (₹13,350-25,200) and one other source cites a materially lower ₹9,000-18,000 band —
the gap is large enough (roughly 2x at the low end) that it most plausibly reflects different
carpet/built-up/super-built-up conventions across portals rather than a real price move, and is
flagged rather than resolved. Kharghar's yield (3.1-3.4%) is unremarkable and close to Ulwe's.

**Ulwe is the sharpest asking-vs-closing case in this dossier.** Asking prices cluster
₹13,500-16,600/sqft (up to ~₹17.9k in airport-proximate sectors), but IGR-linked deal tracking
puts actual closing prices at ₹10,500-12,500/sqft — a 25-30% asking premium over what transacts
[1-SOURCE, but the source explicitly frames itself as an asking-vs-closing reconciliation, which
is exactly the distinction the task requires]. Layered onto this is a genuine carpet/built-up
trap: a builder project (Villa Evara) prices at ₹10,135-10,339/sqft on an explicitly **carpet**
basis, while the "Ulwe average ₹14,700" figures elsewhere do not state their basis — the two
numbers are not necessarily in conflict, they may simply be measuring different things. Ulwe's
2021 level is cited once at ~₹12,300/sqft, rising to ₹14,500-16,600 by 2026 (roughly +18-35%
corridor-wide) — but airport-proximate Sectors 1-10 specifically are cited as having *more than
doubled* since 2021, and as having risen 200-300% over an ~8-year window from an unspecified
earlier base [1-SOURCE for each claim, and they are not fully reconcilable with each other —
presented as a range, not a point estimate]. Ulwe's yield (3.5-3.8%) sits slightly below the
Navi Mumbai average.

Panvel is discussed in full in §5, since it belongs equally to the "airport corridor" reading and
the "far outer affordability frontier" reading; its numbers are tabled there and above.

## 5. Panvel / Karjat / Dombivli-Kalyan (far outer affordability frontier)

Panvel's own portal-level asking price (₹13,750-13,800/sqft, range ₹9,000-14,500) implies roughly
+26-31% appreciation over five years on a like-for-like flat-rate basis (New Panvel +26.2%, Old
Panvel actually -4.7% over the same window) [1-SOURCE]. This sits starkly against Anarock's own
published residential price INDEX for Panvel, which reached 176 against a 2021 base of 100 — a
+76% cumulative move — and against Navi Mumbai's own index of 164 (+64%) over the same period
[**2-SOURCE**, freepressjournal and topnews/pninews both cite the same underlying Anarock report
with matching figures]. These two readings of "Panvel's five-year change" — roughly +30% on one
methodology, +76% on another — **do not agree**, and this dossier does not attempt to resolve
which is right; the honest read is that Anarock's index may be weighted toward new-launch pricing
or blend in the area's substantial land-parcel transaction activity (6,400 acres transacted in
five years, per the same report), while the flat-rate portals track existing secondary listings —
a methodology gap, not a typo, and a caution against quoting either number as *the* Panvel figure
without naming its source.

What is not in dispute: Panvel has absorbed the large majority of Navi Mumbai's *new* housing
supply. Panvel's share of Navi Mumbai's total residential launches rose from 28% (2021) to a peak
46% (2024), moderating to 42% (2025) [1-SOURCE], with 42,330 new homes added 2021-H1 2026 and 47%
of that supply priced ₹50L-1cr (1BHK+2BHK = 85% of total supply) — Panvel is functionally Navi
Mumbai's affordable-to-mid-market overflow valve, not a luxury story. As of H1 2026, 91% of
Panvel's current inventory is still under construction and only 9% is ready for possession
[1-SOURCE] — this is a genuine future-supply overhang rather than a stock of already-built,
unsold homes, and it matters for how much of the airport thesis is still "supply not yet delivered"
versus "demand not yet arrived."

Karjat has no usable time series: current transacted averages cluster ₹5,257-5,322/sqft (with
builder-floor and independent-house variants slightly higher, ₹6,527-6,776), but the quoted overall
range (₹222-18,708/sqft) is wide enough to suggest it mixes farmland/NA-plot rates in with built
housing, and **no 2019 or 2021 level, and no clean 5-year change, was located in this search pass**
— named here as a gap rather than filled with a recalled figure. Karjat's stated catalysts are the
Panvel-Karjat rail corridor (targeted for completion around Dec 2025, status not independently
confirmed in this pass) and NMIA at ~40km distance — both still more narrative than priced fact at
this level of data availability.

Dombivli and Kalyan are the cheapest markets in the dossier: Dombivli ~₹9,700-9,750/sqft (East
+18.3% 5y), Kalyan ~₹10,115/sqft (Aug 2026). One source's Dombivli print of "+1.6% 1y / -19.1% 3y"
does not fit the rest of the series and looks like an index-construction artifact (a small sample
or a base-period change) rather than a real 3-year decline — flagged as noisy, not corroborated,
and not used in the headline comparison. The broader Kalyan-Dombivli range (₹5,230-15,000/sqft) is
too wide to summarize as one number; it most likely spans old low-rise redevelopment stock at the
low end and new RERA-registered towers along Metro Line 5/12 alignments at the high end.

## 6. Navi Mumbai airport: dated status and how much is already priced in

**Status, dated.** NMIA began commercial operations 25-Dec-2025 (domestic only, IndiGo/Akasa
Air/Air India Express, ~16 destinations) [2-SOURCE]. It moved to round-the-clock operations in
Feb-2026 (34 daily departures at that point) [1-SOURCE, corroborated in direction by a second
source describing "24-hour operational" from February]. International passenger service began
15-Jul-2026 with Air India Express's first Abu Dhabi flight [2-SOURCE]. Early traffic: 109,917
passengers in the first 19 days (through 12-Jan-2026), a single-day peak of 7,345 (10-Jan-2026)
[1-SOURCE]. NMIAL's own target is >1 million passengers/month from Nov-2026, against a Phase 1
capacity of ~20 million passengers/year and an eventual 50 million/year by 2030 [1-2-SOURCE mixed].
The metro spur that would connect the terminal to the existing network (Line 1A, Belapur-Sagar
Sangam, plus Line 2, Pendhar-NMIA Terminal 4, ~28km combined) was approved by the Maharashtra
government only in Jun-2026, with the extension itself separately described as "2-3 years" out
[2-SOURCE on the approval and separately on the timeline] — so as of today (2026-09-11), the
airport is a real, operating asset, but it is not yet rail-connected to the corridor whose land
values are supposed to benefit most from it.

**The pricing-in argument, with figures, not adjectives.** Three separate pieces of evidence point
the same way: (i) Panvel's residential price index had already reached 176 (2021=100) by H1 2026
— the bulk of that climb occurred while the airport was still under construction, not after it
opened, since the index's 2021 base predates first commercial flight by four years [2-SOURCE on
the index print]; (ii) Ulwe's asking prices (₹13,500-16,600/sqft) already run 25-30% above what
IGR-linked data shows actually closes (₹10,500-12,500/sqft) — a textbook sign of a market where
sellers are pricing a story that buyers are not yet fully paying for [1-SOURCE, but the framing
of the analysis is explicit and internally consistent]; (iii) Maharashtra's own Ready Reckoner
(government floor) rates in Ulwe/Taloja/Panvel sit 40-80% *below* actual market rates — meaning
the state's own valuation machinery, which typically lags a hot market, has already been badly
outrun in this specific corridor, itself evidence that the market has moved well ahead of any
official confirmation of value [1-SOURCE]. Broker commentary in this same search set is candid
about the same point in places: one source states plainly that "the easy upside that came from
pure anticipation may already be behind the market in many sectors" of Ulwe, while judging Panvel
to have "more room… because its story is not limited to airport nearness alone" [1-SOURCE,
explicitly an analyst judgment, not a hard number — flagged as interpretation].

**What is not yet priced.** Two things remain genuinely forward, not retrospective: the metro rail
connection (2-3 years out, and rail-adjacency premia in this dossier's own data — Metro 2A/7,
§7 — run 8-15%/yr, i.e. a real but modest increment, not a doubling) and the traffic/jobs ramp
itself (109,917 passengers in 19 days is a rounding error against the 1 million/month Nov-2026
target and the 50 million/year 2030 design capacity, and the frequently-cited "2 lakh jobs" figure
is a projection, not yet an observed count). **Verdict, stated plainly: the DISCRETE re-rating from
"an airport is coming here" has very largely already happened — Panvel's own index shows most of
a 76% five-year move landing before the first flight — while the SLOWER compounding from actual
operating scale (traffic, jobs, rail linkage) has not yet arrived and is where any remaining upside
would have to come from.** Every forward number cited above (8-12%/yr next five years; a further
15-20% from the Metro Line 1 extension) is sourced to broker/developer material and is labelled
here as a PROJECTION, not an achieved fact — per the task's own instruction to be sceptical of
exactly this kind of claim.

## 7. Metro, coastal road, Atal Setu/MTHL, and the Virar-Alibaug corridor: completed vs announced

**Completed:**
- **Atal Setu (MTHL)** — opened Jan-2024, 5.96km, cuts South Mumbai-Navi Mumbai travel from
  90-120 minutes to 20-25 minutes [2-SOURCE on the opening date and travel-time claim]. Post-
  completion price response: 12-15%/yr near the MTHL interchange (Ulwe/Panvel) [1-SOURCE, and the
  source is broker-affiliated — treated as directional, not audited].
- **Mumbai Coastal Road Phase 1** (Marine Drive to the Bandra-Worli Sea Link) — completed and
  fully 24/7 operational since Aug-2025 [1-SOURCE]. Post-completion price response cited at
  Worli Sea Face: +20-38% since 2024 [1-SOURCE, broker-sourced — Worli itself is the sibling
  agent's scope, cited here only for the completed-vs-announced comparison].
- **Metro Lines 2A & 7** (Dahisar-Andheri-Aarey/Gundevali) — Phase 1 operational since Apr-2022.
  Post-completion price response: flats within 500m of a station show an 8-10% premium over flats
  2km away; Dahisar East is cited at +33% over three years (a figure that plausibly reflects the
  area's own general run-up as well as the metro, not the metro alone) [1-SOURCE].

**Pending/under construction:**
- **Mumbai Coastal Road Phase 2** (Bandra-Dahisar) — under construction, targeted for completion
  Dec-2028 [1-SOURCE]. This is the phase most relevant to Malad/Borivali (§1); its price effect, if
  any, has not yet happened by construction, only by anticipation.
- **Navi Mumbai Metro Line 1A/Line 2** (airport spur) — approved Jun-2026, 2-3 years to completion
  (§6).
- **Virar-Alibaug Multi-Modal Corridor** — a 126km access-controlled expressway (Phase 1: ~96.4km,
  Navghar-Balavali). As of 2026: land acquisition ~90% complete, preliminary site-clearing and
  access-road work started Feb-2026, fresh BOT tenders expected by Apr-2026, Phase 1 targeted for
  2027, full corridor 2030 [1-2-SOURCE across these dated milestones]. This is squarely an
  ANNOUNCED, not completed, asset — construction has barely begun.

**The completed-vs-announced price finding, which is the whole question for an investor.** The
same search pass that produced the airport analysis in §6 surfaced this directly: "connectivity
tends to be priced in ahead of completion, not after, and by the time a line is running, the
market has usually absorbed much of the expected uplift" — and separately, that property near
airports/expressways can move 30-70% from ANNOUNCEMENT to completion, against the 8-15%/yr
continuing increments observed AFTER MTHL and Metro 2A/7 actually opened [1-SOURCE for the explicit
framing, but it is directly consistent with this dossier's own numbers: Alibaug land near Mandwa/
Zirad/Awas is already up 30-35% over the last 2-3 years purely on the ANNOUNCED Virar-Alibaug
corridor and RORO ferry upgrades — before a single km of the access-controlled expressway is open
[1-SOURCE] — while Ulwe/Panvel's post-MTHL, post-airport-opening increments (12-18%/yr) look like
a continuation of an existing trend rather than a fresh step-change. **The pattern is consistent
across every pair in this dossier: the announcement moves the price; completion mostly confirms
it and adds a smaller, ongoing increment.** An investor buying on a COMPLETED catalyst (Atal Setu,
Metro 2A/7, Coastal Road Phase 1) is buying the confirmation, not the discovery; an investor buying
on an ANNOUNCED one (Virar-Alibaug, the NMIA metro spur) is buying the same kind of anticipation
that has already largely played out once in Panvel and Ulwe since ~2018-2021 — and per §6, may
already be reflected in current asking prices before a shovel goes further into the ground.

## 8. Where MMR's unsold inventory is concentrated

Pan-India context first, because the two headline aggregates found in this search pass do not
match and should not be conflated: Anarock's Q1 2026 release puts top-7-city unsold inventory at
601,210 units; a separately-cited Knight Frank H1 2026 figure (a different reporting date and,
per its own text, an 8-city rather than 7-city panel) gives 525,695 units, +4% YoY [both
1-SOURCE, and they are not reconcilable without their underlying methodology — presented as two
separate reads, not averaged].

Within that pan-India picture, **MMR's own inventory build is comparatively luxury-light**: MMR
luxury (unsold) inventory rose a modest 6% QoQ in Q1 2026, against NCR +78%, Bengaluru +57% and
Kolkata +96% over the same quarter [1-SOURCE]. Pan-India, the 12% QoQ rise in overall inventory is
concentrated in the ₹1.5cr+ bracket, where ~70% of new launches sat in Q1 2026 [1-SOURCE] — so the
national inventory story is a luxury-supply story, and MMR is a comparatively minor contributor to
it.

Inside MMR, the concentration this dossier can actually locate sits at the **far outer, affordable-
to-mid segment, and specifically in future (under-construction) supply rather than completed,
unsold stock**: Panvel alone carries 42-46% of Navi Mumbai's total residential launch pipeline
(2024-2025), 91% of its current inventory is unbuilt, and 85% of its 2021-H1 2026 supply is
1BHK/2BHK in the ₹50L-1cr bracket [1-SOURCE throughout, one Anarock micro-market report]. Thane
carries the largest *absolute* unsold-unit count found in this dossier (97,117 units, end-Dec-2025,
down 11% YoY) [1-SOURCE]. By contrast, MMR-wide H1 2026 registration data shows the ₹1-2cr bracket
now at 38% of all registrations (up from 32% a year earlier) and ₹5cr+ luxury at 7% [1-SOURCE,
Knight Frank] — but this registration mix is dominated by South Mumbai/inner-suburb transactions
(the sibling dossier's scope) and should not be read as describing the outer belt covered here; it
is cited only to show that MMR's overall market is drifting upmarket even as the outer suburbs and
Navi Mumbai satellites remain skewed toward volume, affordability, and (in Panvel's case
specifically) a large pipeline of homes not yet built. **Segment-split verdict: MMR's inventory
overhang, where it exists in this belt, is a unit-volume problem in the ₹50L-1.5cr band concentrated
in Panvel and Thane, not a value problem in luxury stock** — the opposite of the pattern in most
other large Indian cities in this same quarter.

## Gaps and cautions

- **No India property series exists in the desk's own vault** (per the plan's §0b); every figure
  above is snippet-sourced from real-estate portals, broker blogs, and secondary citations of
  Anarock/Knight Frank/PropEquity reports — never a primary NHB RESIDEX or IGR Maharashtra pull.
  RUNSHEET already carries the pull that would upgrade this.
- **Carpet vs built-up vs super-built-up basis is unstated on the large majority of portal figures
  in this dossier** — flagged wherever found, but the reader should assume every "average psf"
  number above may sit on an unknown, possibly inconsistent, basis across sources; the Ulwe/
  Kharghar internal conflicts (§4) are the clearest evidence this is a live problem, not a
  hypothetical one.
- **Many apparent "2-source" corroborations in Indian property journalism trace back to one
  primary report** (an Anarock or Knight Frank release covered by several portals with matching
  numbers). Where that is visible (Panvel's price-index figure, the NMIA operational dates) it is
  noted; where it is not visible, the [2-SOURCE] tag should be read as "two independent search
  hits agreed," not "two independent data-collection efforts."
- **No usable 2019 level was found for ANY micro-market in this dossier.** Every "2019" cell in the
  table is a genuine gap, not an omission — pre-pandemic portal archives are not indexed the way
  current listings are, and this search pass (WebSearch only, no page-level fetch) could not reach
  them. The 2021 column is populated only for Ulwe and Panvel (via the Anarock index base), and is
  otherwise equally thin.
- **Ghatkopar/Vikhroli yield figures and the Dombivli "-19.1% 3y" print look like data artifacts**
  (small-sample or index-construction noise) rather than real market moves; they are reported
  because the rule is to tag and flag, not to silently drop inconvenient numbers, but they should
  not be relied on for any decision.
- **Karjat has no time series at all** in what this search pass could reach — only a current-level
  average, itself likely contaminated by land/plot listings given its implausibly wide quoted
  range. Any Karjat view should be treated as directional narrative (rail corridor + airport
  proximity) rather than a priced fact.
- **Panvel's own two price-change readings actively conflict** (+76% Anarock index vs +26-31%
  portal flat-rate) and this dossier deliberately does not pick a winner — an honest reader should
  treat "Panvel is up about a third to three-quarters over five years" as the true confidence
  interval, not a single number.
- **All infrastructure-linked price claims in this dossier are broker- or developer-adjacent in
  origin** (Square Yards, Housivity, Piramal/K Raheja/Godrej blogs, and similar), consistent with
  the task's own warning that this industry systematically reports expected uplift as achieved
  uplift; every such figure is labelled [1-SOURCE] or explicitly flagged as promotional-source
  above, and none should be read as an independently audited number.
