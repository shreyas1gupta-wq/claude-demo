Agent dossier, India property programme (Half B), one-agent-per-city leg (`research/frontier/india-property-plan.md`
§2). Compiled 2026-09-11. WebFetch is EGRESS_BLOCKED for every domain and was not attempted — every figure below
is a WebSearch snippet read, not a re-opened primary page, at the same standard as the CN programme's Half B and
this directory's Mumbai/policy-cities dossiers. **Corroboration tags used throughout**: `[2-SOURCE]` = two
independent outlets/searches converged on the same number or range; `[1-SOURCE]` = one outlet only; `[RECALL —
unverified]` = context only, not found this session. Circle rates, government e-auction prices and LDA
allotment/land-pooling rates are marked **[NOT A MARKET PRICE]** wherever they appear and are never averaged into
a market figure. 36 searches used against the 34-42 budget.

**Tooling/basis note (binding on how to read this dossier):** unlike the Mumbai dossiers, essentially none of the
Hyderabad/Lucknow aggregator snippets returned this session stated "carpet" or "built-up" explicitly for these two
cities — basis is recorded as **unstated** for nearly every row below. Treat every number as a portal-quoted
average of unknown area basis unless a row says otherwise; the 20-35% carpet/built-up/super-built-up swing named
in the brief is therefore a live, un-neutralised risk on every price comparison in this file, not just an
academic caveat.

## Headline findings

- **Hyderabad's asking-vs-transacted gap is the starkest found anywhere in this programme so far, though it is
  segment-mix, not a clean like-for-like pair.** Banjara Hills asking rates for luxury product run
  **₹15,000-35,000/sq ft** (2026) while the locality's own blended-transaction average (99acres, all unit types)
  sits at **₹7,748/sq ft** — a ~2-4x spread. This is NOT the MMR-style 25-30% asking-over-registered gap (that
  compares the same unit at two points in the sale process); it compares a luxury-asking subset against an
  all-segment transaction average, so the honest read is "Banjara Hills is bimodal (a mass mid-tier plus an
  ultra-luxury KBR-Park-facing sleeve), not a single market," not a quantified asking/registered premium
  `[2-SOURCE on both numbers, comparison itself is this dossier's construction]`.
- **Hyderabad's oversupply story is real but the "biggest pipeline in India" headline needs a qualifier.**
  Hyderabad does NOT have the largest absolute new-launch volume (MMR ~40,000 units / 32% share of the top-7
  cities in Q1 2026 vs Hyderabad's ~19,300 units / 15% share) — but it has the **fastest-growing** new supply
  (+46% QoQ, the highest growth rate of any top-7 city) stacking onto **India's largest unsold-inventory count**:
  142,722 unsold units citywide (H1 CY26, +21% YoY) against an inventory overhang of **~29 months** — nearly two
  years to clear at the current sell-through rate `[2-SOURCE]`. Only ~21% of that unsold stock is ready-or-2026;
  the rest completes 2027-28+, so the overhang is partly a construction-timeline artifact, not all dead stock
  `[1-SOURCE]`.
- **The Kokapet/Neopolis government e-auctions are a real, escalating benchmark and explicitly NOT a market
  price**: HMDA's per-acre bids ran ₹35cr upset → ₹73-101cr actual (Aug 2023) → ₹99cr upset → **₹137.25cr actual**
  (Nov 2025, record) → ₹120cr upset fixed for the next scheduled 2026 round `[2-SOURCE]`. Converted at 43,560 sq
  ft/acre, the Nov-2025 record implies a **raw land cost of ~₹31,500/sq ft** before any FSI credit — against a
  built Kokapet flat asking price of only ~₹11,900/sq ft today `[DERIVED, this dossier]`. Even at a generous 3.5x
  FSI multiplier that is ~₹9,000/sq ft of land cost alone embedded in every constructed square foot — i.e. land
  under Neopolis is now pricing at levels that make the surrounding built product look **cheap relative to its
  own dirt**, a genuine implied-oversupply-of-buildable-land-value signal worth flagging Tier-C, not a forecast.
- **Hyderabad's political-risk chain (2023 govt change → HYDRAA → Musi riverfront) reads as sentiment damage with
  no clean price break yet identified.** Registration REVENUE fell from ₹14,588cr (FY23-24) to ₹14,214cr
  (FY24-25) to ₹12,443cr (through Jan 2026 of FY25-26) even though Jan-2024's very first month under the new
  government printed +24% YoY `[2-SOURCE]` — i.e. the initial reaction was positive, the slide is a slower,
  multi-quarter erosion coincident with (not proven caused by) HYDRAA's lake-buffer demolitions and Musi's
  ~1.3-lakh-person displacement estimate, both of which press coverage repeatedly ties to buyer "apprehension"
  and a "wait-and-watch" posture, while developers simultaneously insist the market is "resilient" `[2-SOURCE,
  directionally consistent, magnitude not independently priced]`.
- **Hyderabad's office story is the one clean tailwind for the residential thesis**: 8.5 mn sq ft net absorption
  in 2025 + 5.2 mn sq ft in H1 2026, vacancy falling 26.3% → 23.5%, and GCCs alone now supply 50% of all
  absorption (the highest GCC share of any Indian city), with Anarock flagging 8-12 mn sq ft of further GCC
  demand over 3-5 years `[2-SOURCE]`. This is the fundamental the western-corridor micro-markets (Gachibowli,
  Kokapet, Financial District) actually rest on, per the brief's own framing.
- **Lucknow's growth reads as government/policy-orchestrated supply meeting real but smaller private demand, not
  the reverse.** The LDA runs the newest large-scale townships itself (Anant Nagar, Udyog Nagar, Naimish Nagar,
  IT City) at allotment prices (₹2,045-4,522/sq ft flats; ₹3,252-4,180/sq ft plots) **well below** the open-market
  ₹7,000-8,200/sq ft. band these same corridors fetch on resale `[2-SOURCE]` — a subsidised-supply gap, not a
  market clearing price. The Defence Corridor's real-estate case rests entirely on government/developer messaging
  (96% land acquired, 32 MoUs signed, a projected 10-20% appreciation) with **no independent psf figure found this
  session tied specifically to Defence Corridor land** `[1-SOURCE, government/promotional framing only]` — against
  this, IT/GCC entrants (Genpact, Deloitte) are a real but still-small private-demand signal `[1-SOURCE]`. Verdict:
  **government-employment/administrative-hub demand and LDA-orchestrated supply are the dominant, provable driver;
  private-sector/IT demand is real but the smaller, less-evidenced leg** — structurally closer to this programme's
  "policy-backed" category than its "demand-backed" one.

## HYDERABAD — micro-market notes

### 1. Inner premium: Banjara Hills, Jubilee Hills, Somajiguda
Banjara Hills is Hyderabad's most expensive address and structurally bimodal: KBR-Park-facing Road No. 1/2 asking
rates (₹22,000-28,000, up to ₹35,000 for top product) sit far above the locality's own blended transaction
average (₹7,748) `[2-SOURCE]`. Jubilee Hills has grown faster over 5 years (+65.9% vs Banjara's +17.6% on flats)
even though its absolute level is currently quoted lower (₹10,350-16,700 vs Banjara's wider ₹6,600-13,700+ band)
— the two rankings are not the same question, and the sources do not agree on which is "more expensive" once
segment-mix is accounted for `[2-SOURCE]`. Somajiguda shows the fastest 1-year move of the three (+36.1%) but no
5-year print was recovered `[1-SOURCE]` — treat its trend as directional only.

### 2. The IT corridor: Gachibowli, Madhapur/HITEC City, Kondapur, Nanakramguda (Financial District)
This corridor is the commercial and residential core of the western-Hyderabad growth story and it shows the
programme's clearest **internal conflict cluster**: Gachibowli's 5-year change is reported as either +49.7%
(99acres) or +78% cumulative 2021-24 (a different, more recent secondary source) — both numbers describe growth
in the same locality over overlapping periods and are NOT reconciled here; and the Financial District/Nanakramguda
pairing shows a ₹7,200-10,950 spread depending on whether ready or under-construction stock is being quoted
`[2-SOURCE each, unreconciled]`. Kondapur and Madhapur/HITEC City lack any 5-year series in the snippets returned
this session — both are DATA GAPS on the trend axis even though current levels are well covered `[1-2 SOURCE on
level, 0-SOURCE on trend]`. This corridor's residential story is explicitly the one this dossier ties to office
absorption (city-section 4, below) rather than to any standalone residential demand story.

### 3. West/outer growth: Kokapet, Tellapur, Narsingi, Mokila
Kokapet is the corridor's best-documented micro-market: current ₹11,900 (Aug'26), +77.2% over 5 years, yield
2.5-4.2% `[2-SOURCE]` — and it is also the site of the Neopolis land-auction escalation discussed in city-section
2. Tellapur shows a severe, unreconciled internal conflict (₹3,550 vs ₹6,500-11,150 for what is nominally the same
locality) that this dossier could not resolve from search snippets alone — likely reflects a stale/outlying page
or a plot-vs-flat mismatch, but the gap is too large (2-3x) to average or silently pick a side `[2-SOURCE,
contradictory]`. Narsingi returned NO psf figure despite being explicitly named in the brief and repeatedly
described qualitatively as "strategically located" and "rising" — a clean data gap, not a null finding. Mokila
(₹5,600-7,050 flats) is the cheapest of the four and the least documented on trend.

### 4. East and north: Uppal, Kompally, Medchal, Shamirpet
Uppal (+85.0% flats/5y) and Kompally (+39.1% flats / +82.2% land /5y) are both real, decently-documented
micro-markets on the cheaper end of the city (~₹5,800-7,400 current) with strong 5-year appreciation — consistent
with a "catching up from a low base" pattern rather than a premium-corridor story `[2-SOURCE each]`. Medchal and
Shamirpet returned essentially no organized locality-level pricing — both are effectively peripheral/rural
markets as covered by the major portals today, and the one figure found (a farmland project at ~₹889/sq ft
equivalent) is not comparable to the built-flat figures used elsewhere in this table. **Flag**: the brief named
these four as one micro-market band, but the evidence base for the first two (Uppal/Kompally) and the last two
(Medchal/Shamirpet) is not remotely comparable in depth — treat this row as two different confidence tiers, not
one.

### (a) The oversupply question
Hyderabad's unsold inventory is **142,722 units citywide (H1 CY26), +21% YoY**, with an inventory overhang of
**~29 months** — among the worst of any major Indian city and the reason for the "Hyderabad has India's biggest
pile of unsold flats" headline `[2-SOURCE]`. A North-West Hyderabad-specific cut shows 113,016 units / ~30 months
overhang, i.e. that one sub-market alone carries the large majority of the citywide count `[1-SOURCE]`. Anarock
separately flags the >₹2cr segment: 30,320 unsold units (Q1 2025, +6% YoY) `[1-SOURCE]`. On new supply, Hyderabad
recorded ~19,300 units in Q1 2026 (15% of top-7-city launches, +46% QoQ — the fastest growth rate of any major
city) but this is still well below MMR's ~40,000 units (32% share) in absolute terms `[2-SOURCE]` — **the
"largest pipeline" claim is best stated as "fastest-growing pipeline stacking onto the largest unsold base," not
"largest pipeline" outright**. One partially offsetting, less-corroborated data point: a separate cut shows a
13% YoY DROP in unsold stock as of Q4 2024 `[1-SOURCE, bizzbuzz/business-standard-linked]` — this is an earlier
and different snapshot than the H1 CY26 print above, so the two are not strictly contradictory (inventory can have
dipped in late 2024 and risen again through 2026), but it is flagged because it complicates any simple
"monotonically worsening" narrative.

### (b) The Kokapet and Neopolis government land auctions
See headline finding #3 above for the full number sequence (₹35cr upset → ₹137.25cr record actual → ₹120cr next
upset, `[2-SOURCE]`) and this dossier's own derived land-cost-per-built-sqft calculation (~₹31,500/sq ft raw,
~₹9,000/sq ft even at 3.5x FSI). **These are HMDA e-auction clearing prices for bare government land parcels —
explicitly NOT a market price for built residential or office product**, and this dossier does not treat them as
one. What they DO imply, read carefully: (i) the state government is extracting maximum land value at exactly the
same moment private developers are complaining of a 29-month unsold overhang a few kilometres away — a genuine
tension between the state's own revenue incentive and the market's absorption capacity that is worth naming as a
risk, not resolving; (ii) the gap between HMDA's escalating auction floor and the pre-2026 government REGISTRATION
value (~₹3,000/sq ft before the 2026 land-value revision, against ₹12,000/sq ft of developer asking price on
built Kokapet product) is exactly the circle-rate-lag mechanism this programme flagged in the Ayodhya dossier —
official values catching up to a market that had already moved `[1-SOURCE for the pre-revision ₹3,000 figure,
2-SOURCE for the revision itself]`.

### (c) The political-risk question: 2023 government change, HYDRAA, Musi riverfront
Covered in headline finding #4. To add the mechanism detail: HYDRAA's demolitions specifically target lake
full-tank-level (FTL) and buffer-zone encroachments, and developers have responded by publicly displaying FTL/
buffer-zone certificates at sales offices — a direct, visible admission that buyer due-diligence behavior has
changed `[1-SOURCE]`. The Musi riverfront project (₹56,000cr official estimate, reportedly up to ₹1.5 lakh cr) is
still at DPR stage as of mid-2026, faces organized opposition (Musi Jan Andolan) over an estimated 10,600-10,770
homes surveyed for demolition and ~1.3 lakh potential displacement, and over 36% of its budget is allocated to
roads/bridges rather than river restoration per one critical source `[1-2 SOURCE, contested — pro-development
(Godrej blog, government messaging) and critical (Question of Cities, Musi Jan Andolan, Countercurrents) framings
diverge sharply and neither should be read as neutral]`. **No price series isolates the Musi corridor specifically
this session** — its effect, if any, is not yet separable from the broader HYDRAA sentiment effect in the data
available.

### (d) Office absorption
Covered in headline finding #5. To add: the western corridor (HITEC City-Madhapur-Gachibowli-Financial
District-Kokapet) is repeatedly named as "the principal commercial cluster" `[2-SOURCE]`, directly overlapping
micro-markets 2 and 3 above — i.e. the residential price action in Gachibowli/Kokapet/Financial District and the
office-absorption story are the same corridor, not two separate facts to be combined later. Gachibowli Grade-A
office rents of ₹60-90/sq ft/month at 15-25% below HITEC City/Financial District pricing is the stated reason GCCs
are choosing it specifically `[1-SOURCE]`, which is a plausible mechanism for why Gachibowli's residential
5-year change (+49.7 to +78%, see corridor note above) has run as hot as it has.

**Hyderabad Regional Ring Road and metro Phase 2 — anticipation vs delivery, as the brief asked**: Phase 1 metro
data shows a 15-25% premium already realized within 500m of stations `[1-SOURCE]`; the western-corridor
localities (Kokapet, Neopolis, Narsingi, Financial District) are cited as already up >50% over 5 years, with
an ADDITIONAL 10-15% still expected specifically upon Phase 2 completion `[1-SOURCE]` — i.e. this is a case where
both the announcement AND a meaningful further delivery-linked re-rating are claimed, not one or the other. RRR is
earlier-stage and more purely anticipatory: one source explicitly cautions that "sustained appreciation is
possible only when the project is actually being built," while a different source in the same search claims
pockets (Shankarpally, Sangareddy) have already seen "two-to-three-fold appreciation since the project
announcement" `[2-SOURCE, in tension with each other]` — a clean within-programme illustration of the
announcement-vs-completion distinction the brief asked to test, with the two cited sources themselves disagreeing
on how much of the RRR move is already in the price.

## LUCKNOW — micro-market notes

### 5. Inner/established: Gomti Nagar, Hazratganj, Aliganj
Gomti Nagar is Lucknow's best-documented locality (₹7,450 avg, +29.6%/5y, ~3% yield, `[2-SOURCE]`) and shows a
clean deceleration pattern across horizons (10y +60.2% → 5y +29.6% → 3y +12.0% → 1y +8.0%) — a maturing, not
accelerating, market. Hazratganj and Aliganj are both **DATA-QUALITY PROBLEM ROWS**: Hazratganj alone returned
three unreconciled current-price cuts (₹6,600 / ₹7,150 / ₹9,200, the last carrying its own +21.1% YoY claim), and
Aliganj's stated range (₹4,100-7,450) sits well below a separately-quoted "weighted average" of ₹10,000 — in both
cases this dossier reports the spread rather than picking a number, because nothing in the retrieved snippets
explains which cut is authoritative.

### 6. Middle: Gomti Nagar Extension, Vibhuti Khand, Jankipuram
Gomti Nagar Extension is the fastest-appreciating micro-market in either city in this dossier on a like-for-like
basis (+68.4% to +72.6%/5y, two independently-cited cuts that are close enough to treat as corroborating rather
than conflicting) `[2-SOURCE]` — consistent with it being the newest LDA-linked growth node along Shaheed Path.
Vibhuti Khand (technically a sector within core Gomti Nagar, not a separate locality) shows an internal YoY
conflict this dossier could not resolve (+7.9% vs +24.4% across two searches for what appears to be the same
₹8,200 current level) `[1-SOURCE, contradictory]`. Jankipuram is the cheapest of the three (₹4,550-6,100) with no
trend data recovered.

### 7. Outer/growth: Sultanpur Road, Shaheed Path corridor, Amar Shaheed Path
Sultanpur Road's plot-zone breakdown (Zone 1/GNX ₹3,000-6,900 → Zone 2/Wellness City ₹3,500-5,000 → Zone 3
₹900-2,500) is the cleanest illustration in this dossier of a pure distance/infrastructure-maturity gradient
within one corridor `[1-SOURCE, internally consistent]` — plots near Amity University are separately flagged for
an expected 10-15% near-term rise `[1-SOURCE, promotional framing, unverified]`. Amar Shaheed Path / Shaheed Path
proper (₹7,000-8,200, +13.9%/1y) is more built-out and shows the strongest recent momentum of the three named
sub-areas `[2-SOURCE]`.

### 8. Industrial/periphery: Sitapur Road, Raebareli Road, Lucknow-Kanpur expressway side
Sitapur Road is the one micro-market in either city with a NEGATIVE recent print (-2.8% over 1 year) `[1-SOURCE]`
— worth flagging as the one visible soft spot in an otherwise universally-positive dataset, though a single
1-year cut from one source is thin evidence for a turn. Raebareli Road shows the widest internal plot-price
gradient found in either city (₹900 in Mohanlalganj-Kisan Path vs ₹5,500 in the PGI belt, a 6x spread within one
named corridor) `[1-SOURCE]`. The Lucknow-Kanpur expressway itself is still "on the cards" / under planning — this
is a pure **anticipation-only** case with zero delivery yet, and no source isolated an expressway-specific psf
figure; the only claims found are generic "will benefit" statements for the roads it will eventually parallel
`[1-SOURCE, non-quantified]`.

### LDA scheme launches and land allotment prices
The LDA runs its own townships (Anant Nagar, Udyog Nagar, Naimish Nagar — jointly projected to house 300,000+
people, `[1-SOURCE]`) at allotment prices well below the open-market comparables this dossier found for nearby
resale corridors: flats ₹2,045/sq ft (PMAY EWS/LIG, Basant Kunj) to ₹4,522/sq ft (Park View premium 3BHK, same
scheme); plots ₹3,252/sq ft (Varun Vihar) to ₹4,180/sq ft (IT City); Anant Nagar Yojana Phase 4 land at ~₹44,731/
sq m (≈₹4,155/sq ft) `[2-SOURCE for the price points]`. **These are administrative allotment prices, not market
discovery** — many are tied to land-pooling compensation to the farmers who originally supplied the land (the IT
City Scheme's Phase-2 lottery, May 2026, allotted 415 plots specifically to land-pooling participants
`[1-SOURCE]`) — and should never be read across to the resale market rates in the tables above, exactly as circle
rates and HMDA auction prices should not be for Hyderabad.

### The Defence Corridor node
The UP Defence Industrial Corridor's Lucknow node has 96% land acquisition complete and 32 signed MoUs across its
six statewide nodes `[1-SOURCE]`, with government messaging citing a generic "10-20% appreciation" from
infrastructure corridors of this type `[1-SOURCE, non-Lucknow-specific benchmark applied generically]`. **No
independent price series tied specifically to Defence-Corridor land was found this session** — every mention
routes back to the same government/promotional framing, with the LDA's Udyog Nagar/Naimish Nagar townships (see
above) as the actual vehicle through which any Defence-Corridor-linked demand would show up in housing prices.
This is a genuine, named evidence gap, not a quiet omission.

### Demand-backed or government-employment-backed?
See headline finding #6 for the verdict. Supporting detail: Lucknow's own boosters name government employment
("high concentration of state headquarters, judicial bodies, research institutions") in the same breath as IT-
sector growth (Genpact, Deloitte GCCs) as its two demand pillars `[1-SOURCE]` — but the SUPPLY side of the market
(LDA townships, Defence Corridor land pooling, Gomti Nagar Extension itself originating as an LDA scheme) is even
more clearly government-orchestrated than the demand side is government-employed. No source this session
quantified what share of Lucknow's housing demand is state-government-salary-funded versus private-sector, so
the "government-BACKED" framing here rests on the supply-side evidence (LDA's dominant role in new-node creation)
being much harder than the demand-side evidence — a distinction worth preserving rather than collapsing into one
verdict.

## Gaps and cautions

1. **Basis (carpet/built-up/super-built-up) is unstated for nearly every row in both tables** — the single
   biggest, most pervasive gap in this dossier. Every price comparison here, including every "5-year change" and
   every cross-locality ranking, carries an un-neutralised 20-35% area-basis risk per the brief's own framing.
2. **No clean asking-vs-registered (sub-registrar/IGR) pair was found for Lucknow** — only a qualitative "market
   rate typically exceeds circle rate" statement and the 2026 circle-rate revision (+10-15%). Hyderabad fared
   better here (the pre-revision Kokapet ₹3,000 circle-rate vs ₹12,000 asking gap, `[1-SOURCE]`), but even that
   is a circle-rate-vs-asking gap, not a true registered-transaction-vs-asking gap of the kind found in the MMR
   Nepean Sea Road example this programme's sibling dossier reports.
3. **No citywide 2019/2021 absolute price-level benchmark (Knight Frank/Anarock style) was recovered for either
   city** — every 2021 figure in the tables above is [DERIVED] by dividing today's price by a stated 5-year %
   change, never an independently-quoted historical print. This is a materially weaker evidentiary basis than a
   directly-sourced historical figure and should not be treated as equivalent.
4. **Internal, unreconciled conflicts were found and reported rather than resolved** in: Gachibowli's 5-year
   change (+49.7% vs +78%), Tellapur's current level (₹3,550 vs ₹6,500-11,150, a 2-3x spread), Financial
   District's current level (₹7,200-8,100 vs ₹10,950), Hazratganj's current level (three cuts spanning ₹6,600-
   9,200), Aliganj's current level (₹4,100-7,450 range vs a ₹10,000 "weighted average"), and Vibhuti Khand's 1-
   year change (+7.9% vs +24.4%). None of these were adjudicated by this dossier — a follow-up leg with primary-
   page access (WebFetch, currently blocked) would be needed to resolve them.
5. **Narsingi and the Lucknow-Kanpur expressway corridor returned essentially no quantitative data** despite being
   explicitly named in the brief; Medchal/Shamirpet and, to a lesser extent, Sitapur Road/Raebareli Road are
   thinly covered relative to the inner/middle rings of both cities. Portal aggregator coverage density itself is
   informative here — it roughly tracks how "arrived" a micro-market is as an organized resale market versus a
   still-informal/under-construction one.
6. **Yield data is sparse outside a few IT-corridor/prime localities** — Gachibowli, Kokapet (Hyderabad) and
   Gomti Nagar/Gomti Nagar Extension (Lucknow) are the only micro-markets in this dossier with sourced gross
   rental yields; every other row is a flagged gap, not a zero.
7. **Every political-risk and infrastructure-anticipation claim in this dossier is directional, not quantified
   against a counterfactual** — no source attempted to isolate, e.g., "how much of Hyderabad's registration
   revenue decline is HYDRAA/Musi-attributable versus a broader national/rate-cycle slowdown," and this dossier
   does not manufacture that attribution either.
8. **The Kokapet auction land-cost-per-built-sqft calculation (~₹31,500 raw / ~₹9,000 at 3.5x FSI) is this
   dossier's own derivation**, not a sourced figure — the FSI multiplier used (3.5x) is illustrative, not
   confirmed against Hyderabad's actual GHMC/HMDA FSI norms for the Neopolis layout, and should be treated as an
   order-of-magnitude flag rather than a precise cost-buildup.
