# Mumbai's Inner Micro-Markets — South Mumbai, the Mill-Land Belt, Bandra-Khar-Santacruz, Powai-Chembur-Wadala

Agent dossier, India property programme (Half B), Mumbai island-city + inner-suburb leg. Compiled
2026-09-11. Companion piece to `research/notes/india-dossiers/` outer/MMR-satellite dossiers (see
`research/frontier/india-property-plan.md` for the full seven-micro-market structure and the
programme's evidence standard, §0).

**Tooling note (binding on how to read this dossier):** WebFetch was not attempted — the task brief
states it is EGRESS_BLOCKED for every domain, confirmed independently by the CN programme in this
same session. Every figure below is drawn from WebSearch's indexed snippets only; none were opened
and re-read as a primary page. URLs are real and specific (returned against real indexed content)
but not independently re-verified by fetching the page. This is a step below the corroboration
rule's own bar in rigor and is flagged again in Gaps & Cautions. 37 searches used (budget 30-38).

## Headline findings

- **The carpet/built-up trap is pervasive and mostly unresolved by the sources themselves.** Recent
  (2025-2026 vintage) portal aggregator pages (Square Yards, 99acres, Property Butler) increasingly
  state "carpet area" explicitly, consistent with RERA's 2017 carpet-area mandate. Older figures
  (2019-2021 Knight Frank/Anarock citywide averages, most blog "5-year change" percentages) do
  **not** state a basis at all — treated here as unknown-basis and flagged, never silently assumed
  carpet.
- **Asking vs. registered is not a small gap on Mumbai's ridge.** Nepean Sea Road's own IGR-derived
  IGR (government-registered) rate is **₹64,600/sq ft** (6 transactions, ₹57 cr total, Apr 2025-Mar
  2026) against **₹92,350-96,950/sq ft** asking (Dec 2025-2026) on the same street — a ~35-40% gap,
  the cleanest single illustration of the asking/registered distinction the brief asked for.
  [2-SOURCE for the asking side, 1-SOURCE for the IGR-derived figure — see §1.]
- **The "flat 2014-2021" claim is directionally CONFIRMED for the prime segment, not a myth**: prime
  residential appreciated only **+12.7% cumulative over the decade to 2019** (Knight Frank, ~1.2%/yr
  nominal — negative real against ~5-6% CPI), Mumbai's Q1-2019-to-Q1-2020 change was **+0.1%**, and
  Knight Frank's own Prime Global Cities print for Mumbai was still **-1.1% YoY at ₹63,697/sq ft in
  Q2 2021**. The regime changes sharply AFTER 2021: MMR's Anarock-tracked average rose **+48%** from
  ₹10,610/sq ft (2019) to ₹15,650/sq ft (Jan-Jun 2024), and every micro-market's 5-year change column
  in §Table below is dominated by 2021-2026 gains, not 2014-2019 ones. [2-SOURCE core claim.]
- **Mill-land redevelopment is the one true structural supply unlock inside the inner ring**, and it
  shows up as the fastest price growth of the four micro-markets: Lower Parel **+42.5%/5yr**, Worli
  **+44.8%/5yr** (Square Yards trend series) — both outrunning South Mumbai and Bandra — while
  simultaneously carrying by far the highest unsold-inventory VALUE share (Central Mumbai = 32% of
  Mumbai's unsold units but ~55% of unsold value, ICRA, Aug 2019 — dated, flagged 1-SOURCE-stale).
- **South Mumbai's redevelopment potential is real on paper (≈15,000 cessed buildings, FSI 3.0) and
  structurally throttled in practice** (heritage controls, fragmented ownership, narrow roads) — the
  mechanism that keeps the island city supply-constrained and price-resilient despite decades of
  redevelopment policy.
- **Gross rental yields compress toward the centre, not the periphery**: Powai/Chembur ~3.0-3.5%,
  Bandra West ~2.0-2.5% (Bandra East/BKC-facing higher at 2.5-3.6%), Worli/Prabhadevi ~2.5-4%, South
  Mumbai/Malabar Hill ~2-4% (best case Malabar Hill cited at 4%) — a narrow 2-4% band throughout,
  confirming these are capital-preservation/appreciation plays, not yield plays, everywhere in the
  inner ring.
- **IGR Maharashtra registration data is the one genuinely point-in-time series and it shows a clean
  multi-year uptrend**: Mumbai city registrations 80,746 units (2018) → Sept 2021 +87% YoY vs Sept
  2019 (stamp-duty-cut effect) → 1,22,035 (2022) → 1,26,907 (2023) → 1,41,202 (2024, 13-yr high) →
  2025 a 14-yr high → H1 2026 80,221 registrations / ₹6,968 cr revenue (strongest H1 since 2013) →
  Aug 2026 12,503 registrations (+11% YoY, 14-yr high for the month), ₹1,123 cr stamp duty (+12%
  YoY). [2-SOURCE.]

## 1. South Mumbai — Malabar Hill, Cumballa Hill, Altamount/Carmichael Road, Colaba, Nepean Sea Road

**Price level and basis.** Asking rates cluster ₹55,000-1,20,000/sq ft across the belt as a whole
[2-SOURCE], with Malabar Hill/Altamount/Carmichael Road forming the top tier: Malabar Hill averages
₹86,650-95,700/sq ft asking with premium projects (Birla Anayu, Petit Hall, Lodha Malabar) at
₹1.3-1.41 lakh/sq ft; Altamount Road averages ₹98,200-1,21,450/sq ft with a ₹70,000-1,80,000/sq ft
carpet-area range; Carmichael Road tops the whole city on asking psf (Carmichael Residences and
Windsor Morena House both near ₹1,80,000-1,80,500/sq ft), the ₹50-150 cr apartment tier that makes
it "India's priciest residential street by asking psf" [2-SOURCE: Sobha, Ghar.tv]. Colaba is the
belt's relatively cheaper end at ₹39,685-47,800/sq ft, with a -2.1% 1-year move [2-SOURCE, wide
dispersion between cuts — likely reflects different building-age mixes rather than one true number].
Cuffe Parade sits ₹69,700-71,750/sq ft avg (₹90,000-1,20,000 for premium stock).

**The registered-vs-asking gap, directly measured.** Nepean Sea Road is the one address in this
dossier where an IGR-style registered number surfaced directly: **₹64,600/sq ft**, based on 6
transactions totalling ₹57 cr between April 2025 and March 2026 — against asking quotes of
₹92,350/sq ft (Dec 2025) rising to ₹96,950/sq ft (2026) on the same street. That is a ~35-40% asking
premium over registered value on an n=6 sample — directionally consistent with the "asking runs hot"
pattern expected on ultra-thin-volume ultra-luxury streets, but not statistically dispositive at
n=6. [1-SOURCE for the IGR figure specifically; 2-SOURCE for the asking range.]

**Trajectory.** No micro-market-specific 2014 or 2019 absolute level was found for South Mumbai
(searches returned only city-prime aggregates). What IS directly sourced: (i) Knight Frank's own
Prime Global Cities Index put Mumbai prime residential at ₹63,697/sq ft in Q2 2021, down 1.1% YoY —
i.e. still falling in mid-2021; (ii) the SAME Knight Frank research line separately states prime
residential appreciated only +12.7% cumulative over the "last decade" as of 2019 — roughly 1.2%/yr
nominal, negative in real terms; (iii) Knight Frank's Dec-2019 forecast named Cuffe Parade, Nepean
Sea Road, Colaba, Lower Parel, Worli, Tardeo, Juhu, BKC, Santacruz West, Bandra West, Khar West and
Prabhadevi as the specific addresses expected to see prices fall ~1-5% into 2020 — i.e. this dossier's
entire universe was flagged as softening at exactly that moment. [2-SOURCE on the decade-flat claim;
2-SOURCE on the 2020 softness forecast.]

**Rental yield.** Gross yields 2-3% for South Mumbai/Malabar Hill generally, with one source naming
Malabar Hill specifically at 4% — "one of the best yields in premium South Mumbai" — and luxury
sea-facing stock city-wide at 1.6-3%. [2-SOURCE for the 2-4% band; 1-SOURCE for the Malabar-Hill-
specific 4% figure.] All sources agree these are appreciation plays, not income plays.

**Inventory.** No micro-market-level unsold-stock or months-of-inventory figure was found. The one
directly relevant qualitative print: Knight Frank headlined "South Mumbai's luxury housing faces slow
sales despite limited unsold inventory" — i.e. low absolute stock but also low absorption velocity.
[1-SOURCE, qualitative only — flagged, not quantified.]

**What drives it.** Structural land scarcity is the first-order driver: South Mumbai holds roughly
15,000 "cessed" (rent-controlled, pre-1969) buildings theoretically eligible for redevelopment at an
FSI of 3.0 under state policy, but actual delivery is throttled by heritage-conservation rules,
fragmented multi-family ownership, and narrow road-width restrictions on construction — so the
supply unlock exists on paper far more than on the ground [2-SOURCE]. Second, the Mumbai Coastal
Road is a live re-rating catalyst: Worli and Marine Drive-adjacent stretches are reported up 20-30%
over the past two years, and interchanges near Breach Candy/Haji Ali are cited as lifting Nepean Sea
Road/Malabar Hill specifically, with full completion promising a 10-minute Marine-Drive-to-Bandra
commute [2-SOURCE]. Third, HNI/UHNI capital-preservation demand (not yield-seeking) sustains the
top of the market largely independent of rental economics.

## 2. Worli / Prabhadevi / Lower Parel — the mill-land redevelopment belt

**Price level and basis.** Worli averages ₹71,000-74,050/sq ft asking (Square Yards' own July 2026
cut: ₹74,050, +4.56% recent monthly move), median ₹73,529/sq ft (Property Butler, Aug 2026), with a
₹65,000-100,000+ range and ultra-luxury sea-facing towers above ₹1,20,000/sq ft. Separately, 30
deals above ₹40 cr worth a combined ₹4,862 cr were signed in Worli across 2023-2024, alongside a
reported average psf "surged to ₹75,000." Prabhadevi averages ₹62,850-66,650/sq ft. Lower Parel
averages ₹54,867-60,000/sq ft (range ₹45,000-75,000), and Parel itself (same belt, slightly cheaper)
averages ₹45,300/sq ft with ultra-luxury towers to ₹92,000/sq ft. [2-SOURCE throughout; basis stated
as carpet on the 2026-vintage pages, unverified/unstated on the 2023-2024 deal-value citations.]

**Trajectory — the fastest 5-year appreciation of the four micro-markets.** Square Yards' own trend
series gives Worli +7.0% (1yr) / +32.6% (3yr) / +44.8% (5yr); Lower Parel +15.7% (1yr) / +23.8%
(3yr) / +42.5% (5yr); Prabhadevi +1.7% (1yr) / +27.2% (3yr) / +39.9% (5yr). These three 5-year
figures (39.9-44.8%) are the highest in this dossier's table, ahead of South Mumbai's own 2019-2021
pattern (flat-to-down) and comparable to or ahead of Bandra/Powai. No absolute 2014 or 2019 level was
directly sourced for any of the three names; the belt was named among the specific addresses Knight
Frank flagged for a 2020 price DECLINE (see §1), so the belt's own trajectory likely mirrors South
Mumbai's flat-to-down 2019-2021 phase before its post-2021, redevelopment-driven acceleration.

**Rental yield.** Worli 2.5-4% gross (3.89% cited on a specific configuration; a ₹20 cr Worli flat
renting ₹3-4.5 lakh/month implies 1.8-2.7%); Prabhadevi 3.82% average (2.9-4.2% band by
configuration). [2-SOURCE for Worli's range; 1-SOURCE for the specific 3.89%/3.82% point figures.]

**Inventory.** The clearest quantified inventory figure in this whole dossier belongs to this belt,
via the "Central Mumbai" zone definition (which substantially overlaps Worli-Prabhadevi-Lower Parel-
Parel): **Central Mumbai holds 32% of Mumbai's unsold UNITS but ~55% of unsold VALUE** — ₹45,000 cr
of unsold stock as of June 2019 — per an ICRA report dated 2019-08-14. This is a single-source,
now-stale (7-year-old) figure and is flagged as such; it is NOT re-verified for 2026. A separate,
more recent but differently-scoped data point: Mumbai's citywide unsold LUXURY housing stock rose
36% in Q1 2025 (BusinessToday/Anarock) — directionally consistent with a still-heavy high-value
overhang in this belt but not a like-for-like re-measurement of the same "32%/55%" split.
[1-SOURCE, both explicitly flagged for staleness/non-comparability.]

**What drives it — mill-land redevelopment as the belt's defining mechanism.** This is the one
micro-market in the dossier where the driver IS the supply story. Cotton-mill plots across Parel,
Lower Parel, Mahim, Kalachowki and Lalbaug — hundreds of acres of formerly industrial land in the
heart of the island city — have been progressively rezoned and redeveloped into commercial towers,
malls and premium residential stock since the 1990s-2000s, which is the structural reason this belt
converted from a working-class mill-worker district into one of Mumbai's most expensive addresses
within roughly two decades. Live 2026 examples: BMC selected Peddar Realty's ₹1,351 cr bid for the
Century Mills plot in Lower Parel (23,822 sq m, 30-year lease extendable by another 30, a
PPP/MHADA-style structure with rehabilitation flats plus open-market sale units); a Sattva
Group-ORA Group joint redevelopment in Parel with ~1.3 million sq ft of development potential and an
estimated ₹5,500 cr revenue potential launched sales on 2026-03-16. Beyond the mill-land mechanism,
Coastal Road proximity (Worli gains direct, fast access to Marine Drive/Cuffe Parade/Nariman Point)
and BKC-adjacent commercial job growth (Lower Parel's own Peninsula/One Indiabulls office clusters)
add a second, independent demand leg.

## 3. Bandra West / Khar / Santacruz West — the BKC-adjacent premium belt

**Price level and basis.** Bandra West averages ₹65,650/sq ft asking (2026, +7.7% 1yr), with a wide
₹55,000-2,00,000/sq ft range reflecting large internal dispersion: Pali Hill is the most expensive
micro-pocket at ₹95,000-1,80,000/sq ft, Linking Road/Hill Road the cheaper end at ₹45,000-65,000/sq
ft, and sea-facing stock up to ₹90,000/sq ft. Khar West averages ₹61,100/sq ft (March 2026, range
₹70,000-1,10,000 carpet on a separate cut). Santacruz West averages ₹56,950/sq ft (July 2026) — but
a SEPARATE source gives Santacruz West at only ₹28,000-42,000/sq ft, described as "a 20% discount to
Khar West and 40% below Bandra." These two Santacruz figures cannot both be describing the same
carpet-area basis in the same period; this is flagged explicitly as the dossier's clearest live
example of the carpet/built-up (or vintage/methodology) trap the brief warned about, and neither
figure is preferred over the other here. [2-SOURCE but internally conflicting.]

**Trajectory.** Bandra West's own history is similarly conflicted: one source (Homebazaar) gives
₹52,000/sq ft (2020) rising to ₹53,000/sq ft (2021) and separately states "~10% growth in the last 5
years" — but the SAME 2026 current figure of ₹65,650/sq ft implies roughly +24-26% growth from that
2021 base, not 10%. Both figures are reported rather than reconciled; the discrepancy is plausibly a
basis change (carpet vs. built-up) or a different property-quality mix between the two citations,
but neither source states enough methodology to resolve it. [1-SOURCE for the 2020-21 baseline
figures; flagged internal conflict.] Khar West's own trend line is cleaner: ₹59,000/sq ft (Sept
2025) → ₹61,100/sq ft (March 2026), a steady near-term climb. No 2014 or 2019 absolute level was
found for any of the three names.

**Rental yield.** Bandra West gross yields run 2.0-2.5%; Bandra East (the BKC-facing side of the
same suburb, walking distance to the business district) runs meaningfully higher at 2.5-3.5%, with
specific BKC-facing 3BHK resales (₹9-10 cr, renting ₹2.4-3.2 lakh/month) implying 3.0-3.6%. [2-SOURCE
for the West/East split.] This is the one place in the dossier where an intra-suburb yield gap this
size (roughly 1 full percentage point) is directly attributed to job/commute proximity rather than
to building quality.

**Inventory.** No Bandra/Khar/Santacruz-specific unsold-stock or months-of-inventory figure was
found; only MMR-wide aggregates exist in the sources retrieved.

**What drives it.** BKC itself — home to the NSE, SEBI, RBI's Mumbai regional offices, and dozens of
domestic and multinational financial-services and corporate headquarters, cited at "more than two
lakh jobs" — is the single dominant demand driver, and it was deliberately built to relieve pressure
on South Mumbai's own commercial core. Bandra West captures BKC-adjacent demand without BKC's own
commercial zoning constraints, while Bandra East captures the walk-to-work premium directly (hence
its higher yields). Pali Hill's premium is a separate, older celebrity/old-money residential draw
unrelated to BKC. Khar West and Santacruz West are progressively cheaper westward/southward
extensions of the same demand pool, priced at a discount for marginally longer commutes to BKC.

## 4. Powai / Chembur / Wadala — the central-eastern inner ring

**Price level and basis.** Powai averages ₹40,850-42,650/sq ft (August 2026, +4.07% recent 1yr
move), with Hiranandani Gardens (its flagship integrated township) at ₹45,000/sq ft carpet, and a
wide range from ₹20,000-30,000/sq ft (standard stock) to ₹35,000-60,000+/sq ft (premium lake-facing
Hiranandani/Oberoi stock). Chembur averages ₹32,650/sq ft (2026), range ₹35,000-55,000/sq ft, with
select premium micro-pockets crossing ₹45,000/sq ft. Wadala is the cheapest and most internally
dispersed of the three: a general range of ₹25,000-40,000/sq ft, an average cut of ₹31,550-38,600/sq
ft, an asking-price cut of ₹34,900-35,000/sq ft, and GTB Nagar specifically (an older, MHADA-heavy
pocket now under active redevelopment) at only ₹23,000-23,800/sq ft — the cheapest single number
found anywhere in this dossier's four micro-markets. [2-SOURCE for Powai and Chembur; 1-SOURCE, wide
internal spread, for Wadala.]

**Trajectory.** Powai: +7.9% (1yr), +21.6% (3yr), +32.2% (5yr), +54.2% (10yr) per Square Yards' own
trend cut — no absolute 2019/2021 level was directly quoted, but back-solving from the stated 5-year
change against the current ₹42,650/sq ft figure implies a 2021 level near ₹32,270/sq ft; this is
explicitly a DERIVED number, not a directly sourced one, and is flagged as such rather than presented
as fact. Chembur is the one micro-market in this dossier with a genuinely long, directly-quoted
history: ₹12,000-18,000/sq ft (2015-2018) → ₹25,800/sq ft (2021, directly stated as "risen from
around ₹25,800 per sq ft in 2021 to current levels") → ₹32,650/sq ft (2026) — a real, multi-year
trajectory rather than a reconstructed one, giving roughly +27% from 2021 to 2026 and roughly +80-170%
from the 2015-18 range to 2021 alone (reflecting Chembur's earlier, steeper re-rating cycle tied to
the Eastern Freeway). No comparable long series was found for Wadala.

**Rental yield.** Powai 3.0-3.52% gross; Chembur 3.0-4.5% (most citations cluster near 3%, with one
outlier range up to 4.5% for specific configurations). [2-SOURCE for both, generally consistent.]

**Inventory.** No micro-market-specific figure found.

**What drives it.** Powai's economy is largely self-contained: the Hiranandani Business Park and
wider Powai IT hub (accessible via JVLR and the Eastern Express Highway) function as a genuine
secondary employment node, reducing commute dependence on South Mumbai or BKC, with a further catalyst
in the upcoming Metro Line 6 (Pink Line) station. Chembur was historically an industrial/chemical-
belt suburb with a poor address reputation; the Eastern Freeway plus Monorail Line 1 plus the Harbour
Line suburban rail connection are consistently cited as the mechanism that "transformed Chembur into
a premium residential address," directly explaining its steep 2015-2021 re-rating. Wadala's driver is
almost entirely forward-looking and redevelopment-based: it sits at the convergence of the Eastern
Freeway, the Mumbai Trans-Harbour Link (MTHL), and multiple Metro corridors, is being actively
marketed as Mumbai's prospective "third CBD," and its GTB Nagar pocket is the site of a live
MHADA/Keystone Realtors cluster-redevelopment project (11.19 acres, ~20.7 lakh sq ft of saleable area,
~₹4,521 cr Gross Development Value) — i.e. Wadala's current cheapness (₹23,000-38,600/sq ft) is
explicitly a bet on infrastructure and redevelopment delivery still largely ahead of it, in contrast
to Powai (an already-realized job-hub premium) and Chembur (an already-realized connectivity premium).

## 5. Mumbai's overall trajectory: was 2014-2021 really flat in real terms?

**Verdict: broadly CONFIRMED for the period through 2021, with a clear post-2021 regime break —
and confirmed specifically for the PRIME segment, which is what most "Mumbai flat" commentary is
actually describing.**

Four independent prints point the same direction for the pre-2021 window:
1. Knight Frank: prime residential appreciated only **+12.7% cumulative over the decade to 2019** —
   roughly 1.2%/yr nominal, unambiguously negative in real terms against India's ~5-6%/yr CPI over
   that decade.
2. Knight Frank's Prime Global Cities Index: Mumbai's Q1-2019-to-Q1-2020 change was **+0.1%** —
   essentially zero.
3. The same index: Mumbai prime residential was **still -1.1% YoY at ₹63,697/sq ft in Q2 2021** —
   i.e. the softness persisted through the first half of the very year in which the citywide
   registration boom (stamp-duty-cut-driven) began.
4. A contemporaneous Business Standard headline from June 2019 explicitly describes "stagnant
   property prices" as the prevailing Mumbai narrative at that time — a qualitative fourth
   corroboration, not counted toward the 2-source numeric bar but consistent with all three above.

**What changed after 2021**: MMR's Anarock-tracked average residential price rose from ₹10,610/sq ft
(2019) to ₹15,650/sq ft (Jan-Jun 2024) — **+48% cumulative, ~8.1%/yr CAGR** — and a separately cited
RBI House Price Index figure gives Mumbai a 9.58%/yr CAGR over "the last five years" (as of roughly
2024). This is a genuine regime break, not a continuation of the 2014-2021 pattern, and it lines up
exactly with what shows up at the micro-market level in the table above: every 5-year change column
in this dossier (Worli +44.8%, Lower Parel +42.5%, Prabhadevi +39.9%, Powai +32.2%, Chembur +23-24%)
is a number generated almost entirely by 2021-2026 gains, since the pre-2021 base (where measurable
at all — Chembur is the clearest case, ₹25,800/sq ft in 2021) was itself only modestly above its
2015-2018 level. **One large, unresolved discrepancy is flagged rather than resolved**: Knight
Frank's own H1 2026 Mumbai weighted-average figure of ₹36,881/sq ft sits more than double Anarock's
MMR figure of ₹15,650/sq ft (Jan-Jun 2024) — almost certainly a scope/methodology difference (Knight
Frank's own-launches-weighted city figure vs. Anarock's broader MMR-wide stock average, and possibly
a narrower or more premium-skewed sample), but the two cannot be reconciled from the search snippets
available and should not be blended into one "the Mumbai price" number.

## 6. The redevelopment supply pipeline (SRA, cluster redevelopment, mill land, Dharavi)

**Scale, in aggregate unit counts:**
- **SRA (citywide, all of Mumbai, not just the inner ring)**: 2,83,955 families rehabilitated since
  the scheme's inception through December 2025, across 2,545 completed projects over roughly three
  decades — implying a historically slow multi-decade pace (an independent qualitative source,
  Citizen Matters, frames this explicitly as "27 years on... failed to deliver" at scale). The pace
  has visibly accelerated recently: FY2024-25 alone delivered 16,673 units, with 3.34 lakh more
  under construction and a stated target of 5+ lakh homes by 2030 (which would roughly double the
  entire scheme's 29-year output in five years) — a target, not yet a delivered fact. Separately,
  as of 2020 roughly 3.97 lakh units were reported "stuck" on procedural/financing grounds, which the
  state's new MahaWas Nidhi fund and central-government-land joint ventures are aimed at unblocking.
- **Cluster redevelopment (DCPR 33(9), applicable across the inner ring including Wadala/Chembur, not
  only South Mumbai)**: minimum eligible plot size 4,000 sq m within Mumbai city / 6,000 sq m
  elsewhere, unlocking an FSI of up to 4 (well above what individual-building redevelopment can
  access) — with a further 50% one-year premium concession recently announced to accelerate uptake.
- **Mill land**: concentrated in Parel, Lower Parel, Mahim, Kalachowki and Lalbaug. Individually-cited
  live projects: the Century Mills plot (Lower Parel, 23,822 sq m, ₹1,351 cr BMC-PPP bid) and the
  Sattva-ORA Parel project (~1.3 million sq ft saleable, ~₹5,500 cr revenue potential, sales launched
  2026-03-16) — see §2 for detail.
- **Dharavi (Adani-led, the single largest individual project in or near this dossier's geography)**:
  ~225 million sq ft total redevelopment scope, of which ~130 million sq ft is FREE-SALE (open
  market) commercial/residential space located roughly 5 km from BKC; 125,000 total homes planned
  (half inside Dharavi, half elsewhere across MMR); first building handover targeted December 2026;
  ~30,000 units to begin construction in FY27; the rehabilitation phase alone is expected to take
  7-8 years.
- **GTB Nagar / Wadala-Sion (Keystone Realtors + MHADA)**: 11.19 acres, ~20.7 lakh sq ft of saleable
  area unlocked, ~₹4,521 cr Gross Development Value.

**What it does to price — the central mechanism, stated plainly:** the headline unit counts above
look enormous (SRA alone targets 5+ lakh homes; Dharavi alone plans 125,000), but these are
overwhelmingly REPLACEMENT schemes — a rehabilitation component (free, in-kind, for existing
occupants) cross-subsidised by a smaller open-market "sale" component that is what actually reaches
buyers and shows up as tradeable new supply. The genuinely new open-market supply this pipeline adds
is therefore much smaller than the headline unit counts, phased over years-to-decades (Dharavi's own
stated 7-8-year rehabilitation timeline; SRA's 29-year, 2,545-project history to date), and
geographically concentrated in specific corridors rather than spread evenly across the inner ring —
mill land supply lands almost entirely in the Worli-Prabhadevi-Lower Parel-Parel belt (§2), Dharavi's
free-sale component lands specifically in the Bandra-Kurla-adjacent zone (relevant to §3), and GTB
Nagar's supply lands in Wadala-Sion (§4). This is the direct, mechanical answer to why inner-Mumbai
prices have kept rising through a period of headline-scale redevelopment activity: the pipeline is
real, but its market-facing supply is smaller, slower and more geographically concentrated than the
unit counts imply, and South Mumbai proper (§1) captures almost none of it (cessed-building
redevelopment there is separately and more severely throttled — see §1's driver discussion).

## 7. Stamp duty, ready-reckoner rates, and IGR Maharashtra registration data

**Ready reckoner (RR) rates** are the state government's own assessed minimum values, used as the
stamp-duty floor rather than as a market-price series, but their movement is itself informative:
Maharashtra raised Mumbai's RR rates by roughly **+3.39% to +4.39%** for FY2025-26 (effective April
2025), then **held them flat (0% change) for FY2026-27** — a move industry participants had not
expected, having anticipated a further 4-5% rise.

**IGR Maharashtra registration data — the one genuinely point-in-time dataset — shows a clean,
multi-year volume uptrend, not the flat/stagnant pattern seen in price data over the same span**:
- 2018 (full year): 80,746 units.
- September 2021 registrations were **+87% YoY vs. September 2019** — the direct, dated fingerprint
  of the 2020-2021 stamp-duty-cut stimulus, and the clearest evidence that the 2021 price softness
  described in §5 coexisted with (rather than caused, or was caused by) a volume boom.
- 2022 (full year): 1,22,035 units.
- 2023 (full year): 1,26,907 units; ₹10,869 cr stamp-duty revenue (+22% YoY).
- 2024 (full year): 1,41,202 units — a 13-year high.
- 2025: described as a 14-year high (full-year figure not independently isolated from the citation).
- H1 2026: 80,221 registrations, ₹6,968 cr revenue — Mumbai's strongest first half since 2013.
- May 2026: 12,315 registrations (+7% YoY, best May in 14 years), ₹1,051 cr stamp duty.
- June 2026: 13,302 registrations (+15% YoY).
- July 2026: 13,824 registrations, ₹1,255 cr stamp duty.
- August 2026: 12,503 registrations (+11% YoY, best August in 14+ years), ₹1,123 cr stamp duty (+12%
  YoY) — a -10% MoM volume dip and -11% MoM revenue dip vs. July, read as normal month-to-month
  variation rather than a trend break given the YoY figures above it.

Registration COUNT and stamp-duty REVENUE are point-in-time by construction (they record an actual
executed transaction, not an asking quote), but the series as published is citywide/BMC-jurisdiction
and does not itself decompose into the four inner micro-markets covered here — the one place in this
dossier's search results where an IGR number was pinned to a specific micro-market was Nepean Sea
Road (§1), and that required a third-party broker/analytics site to have already done the ward-level
aggregation from raw registration records. This is the dataset's real value and its real limit
simultaneously: it is the most trustworthy price/volume signal available for Mumbai as a whole, but
building a genuinely micro-market-level, transaction-based (not asking-price-based) time series from
it would require a direct IGR data pull — which is a principal-machine task (IGR/registration access
is not on this session's open-egress list), not something achievable via WebSearch snippets alone.

## Gaps and cautions

1. **Every figure in this dossier is WebSearch-snippet-sourced, one level below the corroboration
   rule's preferred standard.** No URL was independently opened and re-read; titles and summaries
   were taken as returned. Treat every number as provisional pending a direct pull.
2. **Carpet vs. built-up/super-built-up is only inconsistently disclosed.** Recent (2025-2026) portal
   pages mostly state "carpet," consistent with RERA; older citations (2019-2021 aggregates, most
   "5-year/10-year change" percentages, the Homebazaar Bandra baseline, the conflicting Santacruz
   West figure) do not state a basis and may mix carpet and built-up across the same table row. Two
   explicit internal conflicts are flagged rather than resolved: Bandra West's 2020-21 baseline
   (implying two different 5-year growth rates from the same source family) and Santacruz West's
   current price (a 2x spread between two citations).
3. **No micro-market-level unsold-inventory or months-of-inventory figure was found for Bandra/Khar/
   Santacruz or Powai/Chembur/Wadala at all**, and only one (dated, single-source) figure was found
   for the mill-land belt (Central Mumbai's 32%/55% unit/value inventory split, ICRA, August 2019).
   South Mumbai's inventory picture is qualitative only ("limited unsold inventory," no number).
   Anything on inventory in this dossier should be read as a significant, acknowledged gap, not a
   settled picture.
4. **The Knight Frank vs. Anarock Mumbai average-price discrepancy (§5) is left unresolved** — the
   two series differ by more than 2x in H1 2026/2024 and cannot be reconciled from search snippets;
   using either series's absolute LEVEL for anything beyond its own directional trend would be a
   methodology error.
5. **The "20% cumulative vs. 20% CAGR" ambiguity from `india-property-plan.md` §0a is not directly
   engaged by this dossier** — this is a description-and-trajectory piece, not a forward-return
   forecast, and none of the historical growth rates quoted here (even Worli's +44.8%/5yr, ~7.7%/yr
   CAGR) should be read as evidence for or against either reading of that bar without the plan's own
   JST-panel base-rate work (IN-D1/IN-D2), which this dossier does not duplicate.
6. **2014 absolute price levels were not found for ANY of the four micro-markets.** Every "5-year" or
   "10-year change" figure quoted is the SOURCE's own stated percentage, not independently computed
   from two absolute levels this dossier verified; Powai's 2021 level is explicitly flagged as
   back-solved (derived), and readers should not treat it as directly sourced.
7. **Rental yield figures are gross, pre-tax, pre-vacancy, pre-maintenance** throughout, as is
   standard for this kind of citation — no source in this dossier reported a net yield, and none
   should be inferred without an explicit expense assumption.
8. **This dossier is Half-B/agent-research-grade under the plan's own evidence tiers** (§0b of
