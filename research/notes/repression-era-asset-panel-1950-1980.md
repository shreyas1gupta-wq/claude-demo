# The financial-repression era, 1950-1980 — what won and what was liquidated

**Status: CONTEXT NOTE, descriptive. No hypothesis, no bars, no census consumption. The
country×asset table is DESK-COMPUTED from the JST vault
(`scripts/analyze_repression_panel.py`, run 2026-09-03); the US sector table is
LITERATURE-GRADE (Fama-French industry portfolios / Siegel), approximate ±1-2pp, ordering
robust. Written on principal request following the DS1 briefing.**

Premise confirmed by the vault's own debt column: US debt/GDP 113%→32%, UK 235%→45%,
Canada 155%→46%, Australia 90%→21% across 1945→1980 — achieved not by surpluses but by
holding nominal rates below inflation while captive institutions (banks under rate caps,
insurers and pension funds under investment mandates) held the bonds. Panel median
inflation 1950-1980: 6.2%/yr; median bill rate: 0.4pp BELOW it.

**Best asset: housing** (median +7.7 real, positive in ALL 16 countries with data — the one
asset that beat repression everywhere; JST housing returns include imputed rents and are
argued by some critics to be overstated ~1-2pp — even so it wins). **Then equities** (+4.2
median, but with a −60%+ real mid-era drawdown in the US/UK and three countries NEGATIVE
over 30 years). **Worst: the repressed instruments themselves** — government bonds median
−1.9%/yr real (UK −2.8%/yr for 30 years ≈ −57% cumulative real; Spain −4.3, Portugal −4.9)
and cash/bills −0.4%/yr. A US long-bond holder 1950-1980 lost ~40% of purchasing power
while "safe."

## Program relevance (context only)
The repression playbook's return ordering — real assets > equities >> cash > bonds, with
the equity return front-loaded BEFORE inflation peaks and gold's entire return arriving in
one decade — is the DS1/monograph-0.1 descent scenario made concrete. It is why Tier-C is
reduce-only, why the gold book exists, and why the atlas treats "hold nominal claims on a
repressing sovereign" as the one position the whole stack is built never to be trapped in.
Changes no verdict; enters no design.
