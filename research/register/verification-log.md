## 1. Lopez-Lira & Tang (2023) and Glasserman & Lin (2023) — D10

**Lopez-Lira & Tang, "Can ChatGPT Forecast Stock Price Movements?"** (arXiv 2304.07619; SSRN
4412788) — **CONFIRMED**, with sharper figures than the dossier had. GPT-4-based sentiment strategy:
annualized Sharpe declining from **6.54 (late 2021) → 3.68 (2022) → 2.33 (2023) → 1.22 (Jan–May
2024)** as the strategy gets arbitraged away; a separately reported GPT-4 vs GPT-3.5 comparison gives
Sharpe **3.8 vs 3.1**; short leg alone reached annualized Sharpe 2.01 vs the long leg's 0.78. The
dossier's [VERIFY] on "exact Sharpe ratio and sample period" is now resolved — decay-over-time IS the
paper's own headline point, which strengthens (not weakens) the design's use of it as the illustrative
failure case in §8.5.

**Glasserman & Lin, "Assessing Look-Ahead Bias in Stock Return Predictions Generated By GPT Sentiment
Analysis"** (arXiv 2309.17322, Sep 2023) — **CONFIRMED** exactly as the task brief stated: Paul
Glasserman & Caden Lin. Mechanism has two distinct legs: (i) **look-ahead bias** — the LLM may
"recall" the actual outcome from training data overlap; (ii) a **distraction effect** — general
knowledge of the named company interferes with sentiment measurement, independent of any date leakage.
Their anonymization test found **anonymized headlines outperform** non-anonymized ones, which the
authors read as evidence the **distraction effect is the larger of the two biases**, not look-ahead
per se. **This nuance is not currently in DESIGN.md §8.5**, which states only "training corpora
postdate the events... this contamination lives in weights the researcher cannot audit or fix" —
true as far as it goes, but it undersells the finding: the paper's own evidence suggests bias
correction (anonymization) is *possible* and *net-positive*, which is in tension with DESIGN's
"inadmissible evidence at any tier, regardless of significance" absolutism. The design's rule is still
defensible as a conservative default, but the argument as written slightly overstates what F10 actually
shows.
**Source URLs**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17322

## 3. SEBI IPO-flipping study; Oct-2024 index-derivatives circular; weekly-expiry weekdays; anchor 30/90 circular — D12

**SEBI IPO-flipping study** — **CONFIRMED, fully resolved** (dossier's "single most important
verification item"). Exact title: **"SEBI study shows 54% of IPO Shares allotted to Investors
(excluding anchor investors) are sold within a week"** — an official SEBI press release, published
**2 September 2024** (sebi.gov.in), not a DERA working paper. Sample: **144 IPOs listed April
2021–December 2023**. Key figures: **54%** of shares (by value, ex-anchor) sold within a week;
individual investors 50.2% within a week / 70% within a year; NIIs 63.3% within a week; retail 42.7%;
disposition effect confirmed (67.6% sold within a week when listing gain >20%, vs 23.3% for negative
returns). The dossier's "~50% within one week" recollection is **CONFIRMED**.

**Anchor-investor post-lock-in selling — CONFIRMED w/ CORRECTION, and this is the most important
finding in this audit for D12.** The dossier asserts (F3) that anchor investors, "once their lock-in
tranche opens, sell a material share of their allotment promptly rather than holding as 'long-term'
institutional capital." A **second, separate SEBI study — released ~August 2026, entirely outside
both analysts' Jan-2026 knowledge cutoff** — directly contradicts the "promptly" framing: across 242
mainboard IPOs (April 2022–October 2025), weighted anchor exit was only **3.2% at the first (30-day)
unlock, ~8% by 60 days, 17.3% by 90 days**; across a 167-IPO one-year subset, cumulative exit reached
**34.4% by 180 days and 50.7% by 365 days**; **FPIs sold ~60% of their allocation in a year vs. ~38%
for mutual funds** (MFs did not exit at all beyond day 30 in >100 of the 242 IPOs). Smaller IPOs
(≤₹250cr) saw much sharper exits (72.5% within a year) than larger ones. Press coverage of this study
is explicitly headlined **"SEBI Study Finds Gradual Anchor Investor Exit"** — the opposite of
"promptly." **Design implication**: DESIGN.md §6.4's "risk-reduction only, no adds ±5–10 days around
disclosed unlocks" rule is still a reasonable conservative default, but its stated survival argument
("Field-Hanka mechanism... adapted") is **weaker than assumed** for mainboard India anchor unlocks —
actual selling pressure in the first 90 days is modest (mid-single-digit to high-teens %, not a
"material share promptly"), concentrated later and in smaller issues. This should be flagged in the
registry as a lower-confidence mechanism than the design currently implies, now that a primary source
exists to test against directly.

**SEBI anchor lock-in 30/90 split** — **CONFIRMED w/ CORRECTION on the date.** The dossier recalled
"a 2022 circular." Correct: SEBI's ICDR (amendment) was notified **14 January 2022**, effective for
IPOs opening **on or after 1 April 2022**; it extended the second anchor tranche from 30 to 90 days,
producing the current **50% released at 30 days / 50% at 90 days** split (governed by Schedule XIII,
Part A, ICDR Regulations 2018). "2022" is right; the dossier didn't have the specific 14-Jan-2022
notification / 1-Apr-2022 effective date, now confirmed.

**SEBI October-2024 index-derivatives circular** — **CONFIRMED**, exact reference resolved: **SEBI/
HO/MRD/TPD-1/P/CIR/2024/132, dated 1 October 2024**, titled "Measures to strengthen equity index
derivatives framework for increased investor protection and market stability" — matches the dossier's
recalled title closely. Confirmed phase-in details: (a) one weekly-expiry benchmark index per
exchange, effective **20 November 2024** (NSE keeps Nifty 50 weekly; BSE keeps Sensex weekly; Bank

raised to **₹15–20 lakh**, effective 20 Nov 2024 (up from ₹5–10 lakh); (c) **upfront full options-

margin benefit on expiry day; (e) **intraday position-limit monitoring** (≥4 random snapshots/day),

**Weekly-expiry weekday assignment as of 2026** — **CONFIRMED**, and the dossier's lowest-confidence

**Bailey & López de Prado (2014), Deflated Sharpe Ratio — CONFIRMED, formula constants recovered.**
V[ŜR] = (1/T)·(1 − γ₃·ŜR + ((γ₄−1)/4)·ŜR²); DSR = Φ((ŜR − SR₀)/√V[ŜR]), where γ₃ = skewness, γ₄ =

conventional 5%-level multiple-testing-corrected bar. This resolves the dossier's [VERIFY] on "exact
formula constants."

## Bonus (4th) spot-check surfaced while cross-checking D08 context: election-day equity moves

DESIGN.md §4 (cycle ladder, L5) states: **"2004/2009/2024 election days moved ∓10–17% on surprise,
not schedule."** Checked all three against primary news coverage:
- **17 May 2004**: Sensex **closed down 15.52%** (its biggest one-day fall ever at the time), having
  hit both the 10% and 15% circuit breakers; Nifty plunged **17.47% intraday**. The dossier D08's own
  hedge ("closing roughly 11%, VERIFY") **understates this — actual close was ~15.5%, not ~11%.**
  **CONFIRMED w/ CORRECTION** (design doc's own "10-17%" range is fine; the dossier's specific "~11%"
  recollection was too low).
- **18 May 2009**: Sensex surged **10.73% intraday to 13,479.39** before the first upper-circuit halt;
  multiple sources describe a further move to a ~17%+ record gain before the market closed for the day
  under the 20% circuit tier. **CONFIRMED**, consistent with "10–17%."
- **4 June 2024**: Sensex closed **down 5.74%** (intraday low −7.40%); Nifty closed **down 5.93%**.
  **This is below DESIGN.md's stated "10-17%" floor** — the most recent of the three episodes was
  meaningfully milder than the range the design's summary sentence implies. **Minor finding**: the
  design's one-line generalization slightly overstates the "typical" magnitude of a surprise-election
  move by omitting that the most recent (2024) instance was roughly half the size of 2004/2009. This
  does not change any design decision (L5 is capped at ≤5% of the regime-score budget and is
  timing/vol-scheduling-only, never a directional bet), but the registry entry for L5 should record
  the 2024 figure alongside 2004/2009 rather than folding it into a single "∓10-17%" band.

business-standard/upstox 4-June-2024 coverage.
