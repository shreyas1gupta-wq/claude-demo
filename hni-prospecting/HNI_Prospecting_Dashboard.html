No proposition, no durable conversion

Charm gets the meeting. The product keeps the money.

You can buy a first meeting — a good event, a warm introduction, a sharp piece of research. What you cannot do is retain an HNI on charm. They will test you.

The first meeting, and the next

Earn the second, then the third

1

Understand

First meeting · earn the right to a second

Prepare like you mean it. Know his sector and two or three real things moving in it right now — a policy shift, a deal, a margin trend. Walk in with a view, not a brochure.

Open on his world. Where did the money come from, what keeps him up, what does his current setup get wrong? Offer to review what he has, and position yourself as an advisor rather than a distributor.

A reference from a willing client in his own sector or community is worth more than any pitch you can make.

2

Show

Second meeting · show, don't tell

Bring the review. Using his own numbers, show how your products would have improved the journey: more return, less risk, fewer blind spots he didn't know he had.

Find the low-friction entry. Look for a fund he's had a bad experience with that also sits on your sell list, and replace it with the single best product from your curated set. Small, obvious, winnable — a first yes that's easy to say.

3

Raise the relationship

By the third meeting

Talk about what he wants to talk about: travel, sport, food, politics. Be genuinely well-read in at least two of these.

At this level that isn't small talk — it's how trust actually gets built.

One rule above all others: never sell a product in the first meeting. Everyone knows it. Almost everyone breaks it.
In discovery, chase the feeling under the number. When he mentions a worry, the useful follow-up isn't “how much?” — it's “what is it about this that worries you most?” Let him arrive at the answer himself, and take it at his pace, not yours.

Timing beats targeting.

HNIs change advisors around events — an exit, an IPO, a business sale, a succession, an inheritance. Liquid, investable assets matter far more than a headline net-worth figure. Be present at the event, not six months after it.

Centres of influence do the heavy lifting.

The CA, the lawyer, the private banker, the founder's first investor — each sits on top of dozens of relationships. One good centre of influence out-produces a hundred cold calls.

Watch-outs

Five ways advisors quietly lose HNI deals

1

Pitching before you understand

Leading with a product before you know what he's actually worried about. The fastest way to look like everyone else.

2

Walking in unprepared

No view on his sector, nothing to say that a brochure couldn't. HNIs read this in the first two minutes.

3

Talking more than you listen

The first meeting is his to talk. If you're doing most of the talking, you're not learning anything you can use later.

4

Chasing paper wealth

Going after a big net-worth number when almost none of it is liquid. Investable, deployable money is the only kind you can advise on this quarter.

5

Going quiet after you win

HNIs don't leave over one bad quarter; they leave over silence. The advisor who disappears between reviews gets replaced by the one who doesn't.
